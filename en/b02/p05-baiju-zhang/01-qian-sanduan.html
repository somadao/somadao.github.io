Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter, Explained in Plain Language · 前三段：见性、筑基、真铅

The First Three Sections: Seeing the Nature, Laying the Foundation, True Lead

Hundred-Line Chapter, first three sections · Seeing the nature, laying the foundation, true lead — only without thoughts can one be still, the way back must be clear, first watch the third night of the moon

Note (translator): The third section, on “true lead”, uses the phases of the moon and images such as “the first menses of a girl of fourteen” to describe fire phasing. These are common metaphors in alchemical books, and both the Daoist Fool and Qiu Ling explain them as the yin and yang within a single body (compare Qiu Ling’s note on the third River Chariot in the previous part, the Secret Principles of the Three Carts). The lines from “only after ten months is the embryo complete” onward, on sending out the spirit and facing the wall, are for understanding only.

About This Chapter

The first three sections of the Hundred-Line Chapter are what the text itself calls “the instructions for the work in three sections”:

  • Seeing the nature: no thoughts → stillness → the breath becomes even → the breathing rests → returning to the root → seeing the nature. The Daoist Fool’s plain-language explanation is very simple: do not think of this and that; the breathing becomes of itself neither large nor small, neither slow nor hurried, and this calls forth the breathing one had in the mother’s womb (the True Breath); when the breathing rests, spirit and breath forget each other and return to the root from which one’s body was born, and one sees the original face of the single opening of the Mysterious Pass — “the nature that is Heaven’s mandate”.
  • Laying the foundation: among all things there is not the slightest sound or smell (stillness at its extreme), and suddenly thunder is heard below the belly — this is the stirring of true yang long accumulated. With the true mind and true intention as the Magpie Bridge, one leads it through the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Jade Pillow up to Kunlun; then one makes the way back clear, curls the tongue to receive it and sends it down to the Yellow Court, where it turns left and right, evenly, fifty-five times. But “lone yin can hardly rise”.
  • True lead: forging the divine sword, three years of refining the self; thoughts stopped and feelings forgotten, the spirit unmoved at the furnace; watching the moth-eyebrow moon of the third day and gathering the true lead; fire phasing starting from the first day of the month, withdrawing the yin tally from the sixteenth, ablution at mao and you to guard against danger; after ten months the embryo is complete, the spirit is sent out, and one faces the wall for nine years.

The Daoist Fool’s explanation of the third section is the longest: he decodes “forging the sword”, “the phases of the moon” and “the menses at fourteen” one by one, and adds his “Diagram of Performing the Changes”.

Original Text and Translation

§1Seeing the nature (first section)
Original (Chinese)

无念方能静，静中气自平。气平息乃住，息住自归根。归根见本性，见性始为真。

Only without thoughts can one be still; in stillness the breath becomes even of itself. When the breath is even, the breathing comes to rest; when the breathing rests, it returns to the root of itself. Returning to the root, one sees one’s original nature; only when one sees the nature is it real.

The Daoist Fool's plain-language explanationon §1
Original note (Chinese)

修行人首重这方寸的心，不要去东思西想。概无一点念头，就是真正清静了。心能清静，那虚无一窍之中，呼吸往来之气，自然就不大不小，不缓不急，和平如水之无波。由是口鼻后天呼吸既调，乃能引起那在母腹时的呼吸，此名先天真息，亦于此凝住其中而不散，是为真息一住。息住则神息相忘，神气融和，自然不出不入，归我生身的本根发源地。息气既归根，就能看见丹经上说的玄关一窍的本来面目，即《中庸》所谓天命之性。然亦必须看见这个性，才算是真性。不仅清清静静之所谓性也，果能照此一步步的工夫继续做去，时常得见此性，筑基的功程自有长进，自能稳固。

What a practitioner values first of all is the mind of this Square Inch: do not think of this and that. Having no thought at all is true clarity and stillness. When the mind can be clear and still, the breath going in and out in the opening of emptiness becomes of itself neither large nor small, neither slow nor hurried, as calm as water without a ripple. When the Posterior Heaven breathing of mouth and nose is thus regulated, it can call forth the breathing one had in the mother’s womb; this is called the Prior Heaven True Breath, and it too congeals here and does not scatter — this is “the True Breath coming to rest”. When the breathing rests, spirit and breath forget each other, spirit and breath merge, and of itself there is neither going out nor coming in; it returns to the root and source from which my body was born. When the breath has returned to the root, one can see the original face of what the alchemical scriptures call the single opening of the Mysterious Pass — what the Doctrine of the Mean calls “the nature that is Heaven’s mandate”. But one must actually see this nature for it to count as the true nature; mere clarity and stillness is not what is meant by the nature. If one really keeps on working step by step like this, and often sees this nature, the work of laying the foundation will naturally progress and naturally become stable.

§2Laying the foundation (second section)
Original (Chinese)

万有无一臭，地下听雷鸣。升到昆仑顶，后路要分明。下山接鹊桥，送下至黄庭。庭中演易卦，五十五堪均。气卷施四大，坐卧看君行。此是筑基理，孤阴难上升。

Among all things, not a trace of sound or smell — underground, thunder is heard. It rises to the summit of Kunlun; the way back must be clear. Coming down the mountain, connect the Magpie Bridge, and send it down to the Yellow Court. In the court, perform the hexagrams of the Changes; fifty-five times, and it can be made even. The breath, rolled up and spread out through the four elements — sitting or lying, it depends on how you practice. This is the principle of laying the foundation; lone yin can hardly rise.

The Daoist Fool's plain-language explanationon §2
Original note (Chinese)

修行人既见了性，神抱住气，气系住息，在丹田中宛转悠扬，聚而不散，外面万有的大地山河，人相众生相，于我心神，无知无识，毫没一声臭之沾染，可谓清静之极了。不觉在我腹下忽然听到有声如雷鸣一样。这是什么？就是身中久积的真阳发动。它要往下走，就要把它收回，用真心真意的鹊桥，让它度过尾闾骨尖两孔中，从此四步四步的像持剑踏罡，升到夹脊，至脑后玉枕，直至昆仑顶上，方才休息一下。又要分明后路，把阳气送下昆仑山。用真心真意的鹊桥，卷舌接这阳气，送下脐中黄庭。在黄庭中又要真心真意引阳气左旋右转，右旋左转，演天地两数，均平五十五次以成其变化。此时的阳气，卷收则退藏于密，施放则弥满四大。无论或坐或卧，看君的行功勤惰如何。这就是交通任督二脉，为丹道下手筑基之事。但孤阴寡阳，是不能上升玉京的。

Once a practitioner has seen the nature, the spirit embraces the breath and the breath binds the breathing; they circle gently and freely in the elixir field, gathered and not scattering. As for the great earth, mountains and rivers of all things outside, the appearances of self and of beings — to my mind and spirit they are unknown and unperceived, without the slightest taint of sound or smell: one may say this is clarity and stillness at its extreme. Without noticing it, below my belly I suddenly hear a sound like thunder. What is it? It is the stirring of the true yang long accumulated in the body. If it wants to go downward, you must take it back: with the true mind and true intention as the Magpie Bridge, let it cross the two holes at the tip of the coccyx, and from there, step by step, like holding a sword and pacing the stars of the Dipper, rise to the Spine-Straddling Pass, reach the Jade Pillow at the back of the head, and go straight up to the summit of Kunlun, and only then rest a moment. Then you must make the way back clear and send the yang breath down Mount Kunlun: with the Magpie Bridge of the true mind and true intention, curl the tongue to receive this yang breath and send it down to the Yellow Court in the navel. In the Yellow Court, again with the true mind and true intention, lead the yang breath to turn left and right, right and left, performing the two numbers of Heaven and Earth, evenly fifty-five times, to complete its transformations. At this time the yang breath, when rolled up, withdraws and is stored in secret; when spread out, it fills the four elements of the whole body. Whether sitting or lying, it depends on how diligent you are in practice. This is opening the Conception and Governing vessels, the work of laying the foundation when one begins the alchemical Way. But lone yin without yang cannot rise to the Jade Capital.

(At this point the original book has the “Diagram of Performing the Changes” drawn by the Daoist Fool: a Daoist sitting cross-legged, with a circle drawn in front of his belly and the character “court” written in its center; the left and right of the circle are marked “turning left” and “turning right”, the top and bottom “fifty-five turns”, and on his chest is written “performing the hexagrams of the Changes” — an illustration of “in the court, perform the hexagrams of the Changes; fifty-five times, and it can be made even”.)

§3True lead (third section, part one)
Original (Chinese)

更要铸神剑，三年炼己成。念止情忘极，临炉不动神。觅买丹房器，五千四八春。先看初三夜，蛾眉始见庚。要见庚花现，反向蛾眉寻。如此采真铅，口口要知音。

Further, you must forge the divine sword; three years of refining the self, and it is accomplished. Thoughts stopped, feelings forgotten to the extreme; at the furnace, the spirit does not move. Seek and buy the vessels for the elixir chamber: five thousand and forty-eight springs. First watch the third night of the month: the moth-eyebrow moon first appears at geng. To see the geng flower appear, turn back and seek it in the moth-eyebrow. Gather the true lead in this way — mouth to mouth, you need one who truly understands.

§4True lead (third section, part two)
Original (Chinese)

火候从初一，一两渐渐生。十六退阴符，两两不见增。沐浴逢鸡兔，防失防险倾。霎时风云会，金气自熏蒸。龙吟并虎啸，体上汗淋淋。

Fire phasing starts from the first day; ounce by ounce it gradually grows. On the sixteenth, withdraw the yin tally; ounce by ounce, no increase is seen. For ablution, at the Rooster and the Hare, guard against loss, guard against danger and toppling. In an instant, wind and cloud meet; the metal breath steams up of itself. The dragon sings and the tiger roars; the body is drenched in sweat.

§5True lead (third section, part three)
Original (Chinese)

十月胎方就，顶门要出神。还须面壁九，飞升上玉京。三段功夫诀，明明说与君。我今亲手释，成书体诀行。传与修行子，玉京之法程。丹诀真师诀，须与神仙论。

Only after ten months is the embryo complete; through the gate at the top of the head, the spirit must go out. You must still face the wall for nine years, and fly up to the Jade Capital. These instructions for the work in three sections I have told you clearly. Now I explain them with my own hand, writing a book to embody the instructions and put them into practice. I pass it on to those who cultivate, as the path to the Jade Capital. The elixir instructions are a true master’s instructions; they should be discussed with immortals.

The Daoist Fool's plain-language explanationon §3–§5
Original note (Chinese)

铸剑炼己功法，祖师未言，间尝窃取其意以补之。所谓己就是自己心中元神，剑就是元神的灵慧。铸之炼之者，就是由筑基补漏的工夫，做到那灵根充实的地步。那坎中一阳的肾气，从此上升。离中一阴的心液，从此下降。是为坎水离火交媾。炼成玉液，发运四肢，流通百脉，赶退三尸五贼，消磨六欲七情，这就是神剑了。还要逆挽漕溪水，磨砺灵剑的锋芒。倒卷黄河水，洗涤用剑的手眼。又将神剑化作雌雄两口，插入真土。雌剑用以补正除邪，雄剑用以斩妖诛邪。功夫到此，以铅伏汞，对境忘情，在欲无欲，居尘出尘，骨气俱是金精，肌肤皆成玉质，这就是铸剑功成，炼己功纯了。所谓三年者，不过指其大致程限，如念止情忘以后。采铅的功法，口口相传，必要知音。谓真正丹诀，难与讨论，故以此喻示之。

以十四岁女子潮信初来，比金液丹成的定候。要五千四十八日归黄道，恰合一部《大藏真经》之数。非然者，若未到十四岁潮信便来，就是不及。已逾十四才来，就是太过。过不及俱非坤元固体。三年炼己非做到三全时，天地灵宝不易得手。不待蛾眉圆光透出三现的时候，药未纯乾，就是不及。已逾三现四现亢龙有悔，就是太过。过不及均不合返还生成之数，决不能成大丹。

以天上月轮，阴阳升降，盈虚消长，比金液运行火候。自初一朔日阳中纯阴的子半起，好比冬至节，一阳在此复生。就是那邵子诗所谓地逢雷处，正当进阳火。所谓时之子妙在心传者此也。由是三日出于庚方，蛾眉阳光现相，在身中自有可验之景。由八日上弦至十五圆甲，金水盈满，就如身中金液腾腾壮盛，正当采药之时。所谓月之圆存乎口诀者此也。又自十六阳渐消而阴渐长，就是那邵子诗所云乾遇巽时，正当退阴符。又由二十三下弦以至三十入地为晦，纯阴坤卦主事，也是温养之时。其中圆半缺半，犹如乌肝八两，兔髓半斤。虽阴阳平均，却是春秋二分，阴阳争，死生分，故卯酉沐浴，正是防危虑险的时候。明此事理，虽未直示，却已显露，故曰三段工夫诀，明明说与君也。以后出神面壁法，当日亲释成书已传修行之人。至阴阳升降，盈虚消长，详易卦图中。

The method of forging the sword and refining the self the Patriarch did not describe; I have ventured to take what I understand of his meaning to fill it in. The “self” is the original spirit in one’s own heart; the “sword” is the spiritual wisdom of the original spirit. “Forging it and refining it” means working, through the practice of laying the foundation and repairing the leaks, to the point where the spiritual root is full. The kidney breath, the one yang within Kan, then rises; the heart liquid, the one yin within Li, then descends — this is the intercourse of Kan water and Li fire. Refined into jade liquid, it is carried to the four limbs and flows through the hundred vessels, driving out the three corpses and five thieves and wearing away the six desires and seven emotions — this is the “divine sword”. Further, one must “pull back the water of Caoxi” to whet the edge of the spiritual sword, and “roll back the water of the Yellow River” to wash the hands and eyes that wield it. Then the divine sword is turned into a female and a male blade and planted in the true earth: the female sword is used to restore what is right and remove what is wrong, the male sword to slay demons and destroy evil. When the work reaches this point — lead subduing mercury, forgetting feelings when facing circumstances, without desire in the midst of desire, out of the dust while living in the dust, bones and breath all become golden essence and flesh and skin all become jade — then the forging of the sword is accomplished and the work of refining the self is pure. The “three years” only indicate a rough time limit, for instance after thoughts have stopped and feelings are forgotten. The method of gathering the lead is transmitted mouth to mouth and requires one who truly understands — that is, the true alchemical instructions are hard to discuss openly, so they are hinted at with this metaphor.

“The first menses of a girl of fourteen” is used as a metaphor for the fixed moment when the golden-liquid elixir is complete; “five thousand and forty-eight days to return to the Yellow Path” matches exactly the number of scrolls in a set of the Great Treasury of Scriptures. If not — if the menses come before fourteen, that is “falling short”; if they come only after fourteen, that is “going too far”; and neither is the firm body of the Kun origin. If three years of refining the self have not reached the point of the “three completions”, the spiritual treasure of Heaven and Earth is not easily obtained. If one does not wait for the round light of the moth-eyebrow to shine through and appear three times, the medicine is not yet pure Qian, and that is “falling short”; if it has already appeared more than three times, a fourth time, that is “the arrogant dragon will have cause to repent” — “going too far”. Neither falling short nor going too far matches the number of reversion and generation; with them one can never accomplish the great elixir.

The rising and falling, filling and emptying, waxing and waning of yin and yang in the disc of the moon in the sky are used as a metaphor for the fire phasing of the golden liquid’s circulation: it starts from the first day of the month, the new moon, at the middle of the zi hour of pure yin within yang, like the winter solstice, when one yang is born again — this is what Master Shao’s poem calls “where Earth meets Thunder”, just the time to advance the yang fire; “the zi of time — its wonder lies in mind-to-mind transmission” refers to this. From here, on the third day the moon comes out at geng: the yang light of the moth-eyebrow shows its form, and there are signs in the body that can be verified. From the upper quarter on the eighth to the full moon at jia on the fifteenth, metal and water are full, like the golden liquid surging and flourishing in the body, just the time to gather the medicine — “the roundness of the moon lies in the oral instructions” refers to this. Then from the sixteenth yang gradually wanes and yin gradually grows — this is what Master Shao’s poem calls “when Qian meets Xun”, just the time to withdraw the yin tally. Then from the lower quarter on the twenty-third to the thirtieth, when the moon goes into the earth and it is the dark of the moon, the pure yin Kun hexagram presides, and this too is a time of warm nourishing. In between, half round and half lacking, it is like “eight ounces of crow’s liver, half a pound of hare’s marrow”: though yin and yang are equal, these are the spring and autumn equinoxes, when yin and yang contend and life and death part; so ablution at mao and you is precisely the time to guard against danger. When these matters and principles are understood — though not shown directly, they are already revealed — that is why it says “these instructions for the work in three sections I have told you clearly”. As for the later methods of sending out the spirit and facing the wall, the Patriarch at that time explained them himself and wrote them into a book already passed on to practitioners. As for the rising and falling, filling and emptying, waxing and waning of yin and yang, see the details in the Diagram of the Hexagrams.

Key Terms

  • Square Inch: the mind; see Square Inch.
  • Prior Heaven True Breath, “the True Breath coming to rest”: the breathing one had in the mother’s womb; see True Breath.
  • Returning to the root, “the nature that is Heaven’s mandate”: the root from which one’s body was born; the Doctrine of the Mean says “what Heaven mandates is called the nature”. See inner nature / life.
  • Thunder heard underground: the stirring of true yang long accumulated; see Living Zi Hour.
  • The way back must be clear: after rising to the Muddy Pellet, one must make the path of descent clear and send it back to the Yellow Court.
  • Holding a sword and pacing the stars of the Dipper: a metaphor for rising step by step.
  • Performing the hexagrams, fifty-five: “the numbers of Heaven and Earth are fifty-five” (Appended Statements), a metaphor for even turning left and right.
  • Four elements: the body.
  • Lone yin can hardly rise: with yin but no yang, nothing can be accomplished.
  • Divine sword: the spiritual wisdom of the original spirit; the female sword restores what is right and removes what is wrong, the male sword slays demons and destroys evil.
  • Caoxi, Yellow River: “pulling back the water of Caoxi” and “rolling back the water of the Yellow River” are metaphors for the reverse flow along the Governing vessel.
  • The spirit unmoved at the furnace: when the work reaches its critical point, the spirit does not waver.
  • Moth-eyebrow, geng flower: the new moon of the third day appears in the west, at the geng position, a metaphor for the first appearance of one yang.
  • The menses of a girl of fourteen: the Daoist Fool uses this as a metaphor for the “fixed moment” — neither falling short nor going too far succeeds.
  • Five thousand and forty-eight: see the previous part, the Secret Principles of the Three Carts.
  • Rooster and Hare: you and mao, i.e. ablution at mao and you; see ablution.
  • “Eight ounces of crow’s liver, half a pound of hare’s marrow”: a metaphor for the balance of yin and yang.
  • “The arrogant dragon will have cause to repent”: the top line of the Qian hexagram in the Book of Changes, a metaphor for going too far.
  • “Where Earth meets Thunder”, “when Qian meets Xun”: lines from a poem by Shao Yong, referring respectively to the return of one yang and the first birth of one yin.
  • “The zi of time — its wonder lies in mind-to-mind transmission; the roundness of the moon lies in the oral instructions”: common sayings in alchemical books.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Learn the six lines of “seeing the nature” by heart: no thoughts → stillness → the breath becomes even → the breathing rests → returning to the root → seeing the nature. This is the whole course of a session of quiet sitting from shallow to deep; each step is brought about naturally by the one before, and none needs to be forced.
  2. “The way back must be clear” — after the movement of breath rises, one must be able to bring it back and return it to the root. For ordinary practitioners, gently bringing the attention back to the lower belly at the end of a sitting is “making the way back clear”.
  3. “Thoughts stopped, feelings forgotten to the extreme; at the furnace, the spirit does not move” — the more critical the moment, the more the mind must stay unmoved.
  4. Neither falling short nor going too far succeeds. What the Daoist Fool says again and again through all these metaphors is one thing: the right measure of fire phasing.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Fifty-five times, and it can be made even”: the Appended Statements of the Book of Changes say “the numbers of Heaven are twenty-five, the numbers of Earth thirty; all the numbers of Heaven and Earth together are fifty-five”. The Daoist Fool explains this as turning left and right evenly fifty-five times.
  2. The “true lead” section is a single passage in the original; we have divided it into three smaller passages (§3–§5), with the Daoist Fool’s explanation placed after all three.
  3. “Eight ounces of crow’s liver, half a pound of hare’s marrow”: the crow is the sun (yang) and the hare the moon (yin); eight ounces and half a pound are equal (sixteen ounces to the old Chinese pound), a metaphor for the balance of yin and yang.
  4. “The Patriarch at that time explained them himself and wrote them into a book already passed on to practitioners”: this refers to Patriarch Lü’s own lines “Now I explain them with my own hand, writing a book to embody the instructions and put them into practice”.
  5. The “Diagram of Performing the Changes” was drawn by the Daoist Fool; for now this site describes its content in words.

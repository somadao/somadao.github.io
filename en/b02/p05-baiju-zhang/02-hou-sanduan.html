Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter, Explained in Plain Language · 后三段：妙法、假道、易卦

The Last Three Sections: The Wondrous Method, False Ways, the Hexagrams

Hundred-Line Chapter, last three sections · The wondrous method, false ways, the hexagrams — playing the zither together with the lute, all methods of deliberate action, holding to Heaven’s course

About This Chapter

After the instructions for the work in three sections, the Hundred-Line Chapter adds three more sections:

  • The wondrous method: “Playing the zither together with the lute, husband and wife live in peaceful harmony” — the Daoist Fool says this means “playing the stringless zither and the lute of loving union, treating the one yin and the one yang as husband and wife, one singing and the other following, never apart for a moment”; when stillness reaches the dim and obscure, suddenly a disc of golden-bright moon rises from the Winding River to Kunlun; then one waits for the red sun to rise within the moon (the sun and moon joined like a jade disc), and something like a vermilion tangerine appears, which is met with “quicksilver” — “never using any implement, but following Qian and Kun in reverse”.
  • False ways: Patriarch Lü criticizes people of the world who do not understand the principle: some practice so-called sexual “battle-gathering”, some only talk about the three passes or “entering the furnace”, and there are other practices too foul to hear — “all methods of deliberate action belong to people of hell”; another kind of person clings to “dead quietism” and sits until the hair turns yellow and the teeth fall out, the body withered like a crane’s. Both take the false for the true.
  • The hexagrams: observe the great Way of Heaven, hold to Heaven’s course — the moon hanging over Xichuan, the rosy clouds over the shores of southern Chu; the dark of the moon, the new moon, the upper quarter, the sixteenth, the twenty-third, matched with the twelve sovereign hexagrams. Only when one has seen through them does one know the true nature of the great Way.

Original Text and Translation

§1The wondrous method (fourth section)
Original (Chinese)

更有妙丹法，予恐太泄轻。弹琴并鼓瑟，夫妻和平情。霞光照曲水，红日出昆仑。恍恍并惚惚，渺渺与冥冥。此中真有信，信到君必惊。一点如朱橘，要使水银迎。绝不用器械，颠倒法乾坤。

There is a further wondrous elixir method, which I fear to reveal too lightly. Playing the zither together with the lute, husband and wife live in peaceful harmony. Rosy light shines on the winding water; the red sun rises from Kunlun. Dim and indistinct, vast and obscure — within this there truly is a sign, and when the sign comes, you will surely be startled. A dot like a vermilion tangerine: you must let quicksilver meet it. Never use any implement; only follow Qian and Kun in reverse.

The Daoist Fool's plain-language explanationon §1
Original note (Chinese)

修行至炼己还丹、怀胎出神的时候，更要重立性命，再造乾坤。妙法为何？就是弹无弦琴，鼓好合瑟，把那一阴一阳当成夫妻，唱随不离一刻。静定到恍恍惚惚，渺渺冥冥，忽有一轮金光皓月，如车轮大，由曲江上升于昆仑。这个信实在惊人，就要收摄性中，留而待之，以为化形之本。又待一轮红日升于月中，就是日月合璧。急将法身跃入光中，吞吸中藏，静极而动，有个物件像朱橘一般，要使水银朱里汞，迎它上合于道胎，则河车重转，自然合乾坤颠倒之妙用，而绝无所谓器械也。

When cultivation reaches the time of refining the self and reverting the elixir, conceiving the embryo and sending out the spirit, one must further re-establish nature and life and remake Qian and Kun. What is the wondrous method? It is playing the stringless zither and the lute of loving union, treating the one yin and the one yang as husband and wife, one singing and the other following, never apart for a moment. When stillness and concentration reach the dim and indistinct, vast and obscure, suddenly there is a disc of golden-bright moon, as large as a cartwheel, rising from the Winding River to Kunlun. This sign is truly startling; one must gather it into the nature, keep it and wait, as the basis for transforming form. Then one waits for a disc of red sun to rise within the moon — this is the sun and moon joined like a jade disc. Quickly let the Dharma-body leap into the light, swallow it and store it within; stillness reaches its extreme and turns to movement, and there is something like a vermilion tangerine. One must use “quicksilver” — the mercury within cinnabar — to meet it and bring it up to join the embryo of the Way; then the River Chariot turns again, naturally matching the wondrous function of Qian and Kun in reverse, and there is no implement whatsoever.

§2False ways (fifth section)
Original (Chinese)

世人不悟理，山峰采战行。也有说三关，也有入炉临。又以口对口，丑秽不堪听。一切有为法，都是地狱人。有等执着者，信死清静因。发黄并齿落，鹊体似鹤形。他未知吾道，分明假作真。

People of the world do not understand the principle and practice “mountain-peak battle-gathering”. Some talk about the three passes, some about entering and approaching the furnace. Others again practice mouth to mouth — foul and filthy, unbearable to hear. All methods of deliberate action belong to people of hell. There are also those who cling and believe in the cause of dead quietism: their hair turns yellow and their teeth fall out, their bodies thin as a magpie, shaped like a crane. They do not know my Way; plainly they take the false for the true.

The Daoist Fool's plain-language explanationon §2
Original note (Chinese)

自三教鼎立以来，异说声张，邪法不少。即如此章所指，亦有几种。彼等盲修瞎炼，良可慨也！故祖师略指假道之非，以救错行之失。

Ever since the three teachings — Confucianism, Buddhism and Daoism — have stood side by side, strange doctrines have been noisily proclaimed and there have been many deviant methods. Just in what this section points to, there are several kinds. Those people practice and refine blindly — truly lamentable! So the Patriarch briefly points out what is wrong with the false ways, to rescue those who have gone astray.

§3The hexagrams (sixth section)
Original (Chinese)

观天之大道，执天之运行。月挂西川上，霞临南楚滨。三日前为晦，阳中之纯阴。三日后为朔，阴中之阳精。亦如逢冬至，和景好阳春。八日是上弦，一向卯兔门。十六方为姤，廿三是酉门。以此参易卦，方知大道情。百句章中字，字字要寻文。此书雷将守，得者慎勿轻。

Observe the great Way of Heaven; hold to Heaven’s course. The moon hangs over Xichuan; rosy clouds come to the shores of southern Chu. Before the third day is the dark of the moon, pure yin within yang; after the third day is the new moon, the yang essence within yin. It is also like meeting the winter solstice, a mild scene like a fine spring. The eighth day is the upper quarter, facing all the way toward the gate of the Hare, mao. Only on the sixteenth is it Gou; the twenty-third is the gate of you. Use these to contemplate the hexagrams of the Changes, and only then will you know the true nature of the great Way. The characters in the Hundred-Line Chapter — seek the meaning of every one. This book is guarded by thunder generals; whoever obtains it must not take it lightly.

The Daoist Fool's plain-language explanationon §3
Original note (Chinese)

易卦以复、临、泰、大壮、夬、乾六卦为阳升，姤、遯、否、观、剥、坤六卦为阴降。修丹者观天道，执运行，参考易卦，才晓得身中阴阳消长，恰合这十二卦爻。身中阴阳升降，恰合这太阴运行。故邵康节先生诗云：“耳目聪明男子身，鸿钧赋予不为贫。因探月窟方知物，未蹑天根岂识人。乾遇巽时观月窟，地逢雷处见天根。天根月窟闲来往，三十六宫都是春”。又云：“冬至子之半，天心无改移。一阳初动处，万物未生时。玄酒味方淡，太音声正稀。此言如不信，更请问庖羲。”二诗固可为天道之一证。然而晦朔弦望，盈虚消长，概实包涵于十二卦中，能将卦图融会贯通，则大道之情，无不咸知矣。祖师不惜一字一珠，教人寻文悟道，慈悲至矣！又恐人轻视亵渎此书，故所在之处，自称特遣雷将护守，以昭珍重。学者曷共勉诸。

Among the hexagrams of the Changes, the six hexagrams Fu, Lin, Tai, Dazhuang, Guai and Qian are the rising of yang, and the six hexagrams Gou, Dun, Pi, Guan, Bo and Kun are the descent of yin. Only when those who cultivate the elixir observe the Way of Heaven, hold to its course and consult the hexagrams do they understand that the waxing and waning of yin and yang in the body exactly match the lines of these twelve hexagrams, and that the rising and falling of yin and yang in the body exactly match the course of the moon. That is why Master Shao Kangjie’s poem says: “With keen ears and eyes, in a man’s body — what the Great Potter bestows is not poor. Only by exploring the Moon Cave does one know things; without treading on the Root of Heaven, how can one know man? When Qian meets Xun, behold the Moon Cave; where Earth meets Thunder, see the Root of Heaven. Between the Root of Heaven and the Moon Cave come and go at ease, and all thirty-six palaces are spring.” And again: “At the winter solstice, in the middle of zi, the heart of Heaven does not shift. Where one yang first stirs, when the ten thousand things are not yet born. The dark wine’s taste is just becoming bland; the great sound is just at its faintest. If you do not believe these words, go and ask Paoxi.” These two poems can of course serve as one proof of the Way of Heaven. Yet the dark of the moon, the new moon, the quarters and the full moon, filling and emptying, waxing and waning, are in fact all contained in the twelve hexagrams; if one can thoroughly grasp the hexagram diagram, there is nothing about the true nature of the great Way that one will not know. The Patriarch did not begrudge a pearl for every character, teaching people to seek the meaning and awaken to the Way — compassion at its utmost! And fearing that people would take this book lightly and profane it, he says that wherever it is kept he has specially sent thunder generals to guard it, to show how precious it is. Why should students not encourage one another in this!

(At this point the original book has the “Diagram of the Hexagrams”: in the center a Taiji diagram in the form of yin-yang fish, marked “dark of the moon, new moon, first day, geng” and “fifteenth, jia, Gou, withdrawing the tally”; around it the twelve sovereign hexagrams are arranged in order, as in the table below.)

Key Terms

  • Re-establishing nature and life, remaking Qian and Kun: the higher work after refining the self and reverting the elixir.
  • Playing the stringless zither, the lute of loving union: the one yin and the one yang, one singing and the other following, never apart — not a reference to man and woman.
  • Winding River, Kunlun: metaphors for below and above; the golden-bright moon rises from below to above.
  • Sun and moon joined like a jade disc, Dharma body, embryo of the Way: see the sun and moon joined like a jade disc, spontaneous Dharma Body and Holy Embryo.
  • Vermilion tangerine, quicksilver: the vermilion tangerine is a metaphor for the elixir; quicksilver is the mercury within cinnabar (the yin within Li). See Lead and Mercury.
  • Never use any implement: the true elixir method borrows nothing external.
  • “Mountain-peak battle-gathering”: side-door schools that take sexual intercourse between man and woman, “gathering and replenishing”, as the Way; Patriarch Lü condemns them.
  • Methods of deliberate action: all artificial, contrived methods.
  • Dead quietism: sitting like a withered log in blank emptiness; see realm of yin / realm of the aggregates.
  • The twelve sovereign hexagrams: Fu, Lin, Tai, Dazhuang, Guai and Qian are the rising of yang; Gou, Dun, Pi, Guan, Bo and Kun the descent of yin. See fire phasing.
  • Root of Heaven, Moon Cave: the places where one yang and one yin are first born; from Shao Yong’s poem.
  • Dark wine, great sound: the blandest taste and the faintest sound, metaphors for the state when one yang first stirs.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “All methods of deliberate action belong to people of hell” and “believe in the cause of dead quietism” — Patriarch Lü criticizes two extremes at once: on one side all kinds of artificial contrivance and side-door ways; on the other, clinging to lifeless stillness and sitting until the body decays. The right way lies between them: still but not dead, present but not contrived.
  2. “Never use any implement” — any “cultivation” that needs to rely on external things or other people is not the alchemical Way spoken of here.
  3. Shao Yong’s lines “Where one yang first stirs, when the ten thousand things are not yet born. The dark wine’s taste is just becoming bland; the great sound is just at its faintest” are the most beautiful description of that spark of life in stillness: extremely bland, extremely subtle — not something dramatic.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Mountain-peak battle-gathering”, “mouth to mouth”: side-door practices criticized in Patriarch Lü’s text; we translate them briefly according to the original meaning and do not elaborate.
  2. “Entering and approaching the furnace”: refers to people who cling only to terms such as “furnace” and “tripod”; some say it refers to expressions from the bedchamber arts. We translate according to the original.
  3. “Thin as a magpie, shaped like a crane”: describing extreme emaciation.
  4. “The moon hangs over Xichuan; rosy clouds come to the shores of southern Chu”: Xichuan stands for the west (geng, Dui) and southern Chu for the south (Li); geographical directions are used as metaphors for the yin and yang in the body.
  5. The Diagram of the Hexagrams is an illustration in the original book; this site describes its content in words and a table.

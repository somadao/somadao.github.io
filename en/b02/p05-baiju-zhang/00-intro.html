Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter, Explained in Plain Language · 篇首导读

Introduction to Part Five

What this book is

The Hundred-Line Chapter (Baiju zhang 百句章) is attributed to Lü Dongbin and included in the Complete Works of Patriarch Lü (Lüzu quanshu 吕祖全书). The whole text has a hundred lines and five hundred characters, five characters to a line; the Qing-dynasty author Fu Jinquan, known as Jiyizi, praised it as “bright pearls dazzling the eye, every character subtle and precise”. It covers the whole of alchemical practice in three sections — seeing the nature, laying the foundation, and gathering the true lead up to sending out the spirit and facing the wall — and then adds three more: one on the “wondrous method”, one criticizing “false ways”, and one using the hexagrams of the Book of Changes to explain fire phasing.

The commentary again comes from the Collection on Understanding the Dao through the Union of Schools compiled by Ran Daoyuan, and again its author is the Daoist Fool; this time he wrote in the vernacular of his day (hence “explained in plain language”), and he drew a “Diagram of Performing the Changes” with his own hand.

In the Daoist Fool’s Simple Explanation of the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine in the previous part, he says several times “for the explanation, see the third section of the Hundred-Line Chapter” — the two parts are meant to be read side by side.

The Hundred-Line Chapter written by Lü Dongbin is taken from the Complete Works of Patriarch Lü. The Qing-dynasty author Fu Jinquan, known as Jiyizi, combined this chapter with Patriarch Lü’s Rhapsody of the Yellow Crane, Song of the True Scripture, Song of the Tripod Vessel and Song of Gathering the Metal to make his Commentary on Five Pieces. Fu praised the Hundred-Line Chapter, saying: “This book has a hundred lines in all, five hundred characters, like bright pearls dazzling the eye; every character is subtle and precise.”

To make it easier for students to understand, I have specially selected and printed here the Daoist Fool’s Plain-Language Explanation of Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter from the Collection on Understanding the Dao through the Union of Schools compiled by Ran Daoyuan.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

Only without thoughts can one be still; in stillness the breath becomes even of itself; when the breath is even, the breathing comes to rest; when the breathing rests, it returns to the root; returning to the root, one sees one’s original nature — first one sees the nature; then one lays the foundation by opening the Conception and Governing vessels; then one forges the sword and refines the self, watches the phases of the moon to gather the true lead, and advances and withdraws the fire according to the new moon, full moon and quarters; then come ten months of gestation, sending out the spirit, and facing the wall. All “methods of deliberate action” and “dead quietism” are false ways.

Reading tips

  1. The six lines of the first section, “seeing the nature”, are the ones in the whole text most worth learning by heart: only without thoughts can one be still; in stillness the breath becomes even of itself; when the breath is even, the breathing comes to rest; when the breathing rests, it returns to the root; returning to the root, one sees one’s original nature; only when one sees the nature is it real. This is the whole sequence of quiet sitting.
  2. The “true lead” section and the “wondrous method” section are full of metaphors — phases of the moon, husband and wife, zither and lute — and the Daoist Fool explains every one of them as the yin and yang within a single body; the “false ways” section explicitly criticizes “methods of deliberate action” such as sexual “battle-gathering” between man and woman, as well as the “dead quietism” of sitting like a log. This is entirely in line with Qiu Ling’s own stance in choosing texts.
  3. The original book includes a “Diagram of Performing the Changes” drawn by the Daoist Fool and a “Diagram of the Hexagrams” (the twelve sovereign hexagrams); we describe their content in the text in words and tables.

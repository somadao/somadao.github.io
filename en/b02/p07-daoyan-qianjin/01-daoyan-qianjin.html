Plain and Simple Words on the Dao

Plain and Simple Words on the Dao — concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, regulate the breath and concentrate the spirit; a level mind and harmonious breath; finding stillness in the midst of noise

About This Chapter

The text has sixteen short passages, which fall roughly into four groups:

  1. Levels (§1–§2): the levels of the great Way are “after non-action comes action; after action, one returns to non-action”; the three lesser stages of the inner elixir are accumulating essence and building up breath, opening the passes and unfolding the openings, and laying the foundation and refining the self; to begin, first close the three outer treasures (ears, eyes, mouth) and nourish the three inner treasures (essence, breath, spirit).
  2. Concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath (§3–§5): the core of the whole text. Concentrating the spirit is “gathering the mind that has already become clear and bringing it inside” — when the mind is not yet clear, do not close the eyes at random; regulating the breath is “once the mind and spirit are still, let the breath be natural; I simply keep to the natural”; keeping to clarity, stillness and naturalness is called “not forgetting”, following clarity, stillness and naturalness is called “not helping”; the true spirit is “the single numinous awareness, alone and awake”.
  3. Cultivating the mind and guarding the center (§6–§11): the mind is the dwelling of the nature; if one values the Way more, one values affairs less; essence, breath and spirit do not chase after things, ears, eyes and mouth do not disturb the center; from “the center within the body” one seeks “the center not within the body”; a level mind — “when the mind raises no waves, that is called level”; the Mysterious Pass is the spirit entering the breath.
  4. Daily work (§12–§16): begin with inner breathing; let Posterior Heaven breathing regulate itself; the human mind has a true side and a deluded side; when the mind wanders, bring it back, once it is back, let it go again, and one can find stillness in the midst of noise; wandering about and sitting like a log is not the Way; in nourishing the breath, guarding against anger is most urgent.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

夫道者，其层次须知三候三关，大抵不外四言：无为之后，继以有为，有为之后，复返无为而已。

内丹功夫，亦有小三候：积精累气为初候；开关展窍为中候；筑基炼己为三候。下手于初候求之，大抵清心寡欲，先闭外三宝养其内三宝而已。

As for the Way, one must know that its levels are the three stages and the three passes; in general they amount to no more than four phrases: after non-action comes action; after action, one returns again to non-action — that is all.

The work of the inner elixir also has “three lesser stages”: accumulating essence and building up breath is the first stage; opening the passes and unfolding the openings is the middle stage; laying the foundation and refining the self is the third stage. To begin, seek it in the first stage; in general this is simply clearing the mind and reducing desires, first closing the three outer treasures to nourish the three inner treasures.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

外三宝为耳、目、口，内三宝为精、气、神。

The three outer treasures are the ears, eyes and mouth; the three inner treasures are essence, breath and spirit.

§2
Original (Chinese)

《系辞》云：“穷理尽性以至于命”，即是道家层次，一步赶步功夫。何谓穷理？读真函，访真诀，观造化，参《河洛》，趁清闲而保气，守精神以筑基。一面穷理，一面尽性，乃有不坏之形躯，以图长生之妙药。性者内也，命者外也。以内接外，合而为一，则大道成矣。以至于三字，明明有将性立命、后天返先天口诀在内，特无诚心人再求诀中诀以了之也。

The Appended Statements say: “Exhaust the principles, fulfill the nature, and so arrive at life (ming).” This is the order of levels in Daoism, work that follows step after step. What is “exhausting the principles”? Reading the true scriptures, seeking out the true formulas, observing creation, studying the River Chart and the Luo Writing, taking advantage of leisure to preserve the breath, guarding essence and spirit to lay the foundation. While exhausting the principles, one fulfills the nature; only then does one have an indestructible body with which to pursue the wondrous medicine of long life. Nature is within, life is without; joining the within to the without and uniting them into one, the great Way is accomplished. In the three characters “and so arrive at” are clearly contained the formulas of “establishing life by means of nature” and “returning from the Posterior Heaven to the Prior Heaven”; it is only that people without a sincere heart do not go on to seek this “formula within the formula” to complete it.

§3
Original (Chinese)

凝神调息，调息凝神。八个字就是下手工夫，须一片做去，分层次而不断，乃可。凝神者，收已清之心而入其内也。心未清时，眼勿乱闭。先要自劝自勉，劝得回来，清凉恬淡，始行收入气穴，乃曰凝神。凝起神了，然后如坐高山而视众山众水，如燃天灯而照九幽九昧，所谓凝神于虚者此也。调息不难，心神一静，随息自然，我只守其自然，加以神光下照，即调息也。调息者，调度阴跷之息与吾心中之气，相会于气穴中也。心止于脐下曰凝神，气归于脐下曰调息。神息相依，守其清静自然，曰勿忘。顺其清静自然，曰勿助。勿忘勿助，以默以柔，息活泼而心自在，即用钻字诀，以虚空为藏心之所，以昏默为息神之乡，三番两次，澄之又澄，忽然神息相忘，神气融合，不觉恍然阳生而人如醉矣。

Concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, regulate the breath and concentrate the spirit — these eight characters are the work of getting started; they must be done as one piece, divided into stages and yet without interruption. Concentrating the spirit means gathering the mind that has already become clear and bringing it inside. When the mind is not yet clear, do not close the eyes at random; first persuade and encourage yourself, bring the mind back, and when it is cool, calm and tranquil, then begin to gather it into the Cavity of Breath — only this is called concentrating the spirit. Once the spirit is concentrated, it is like sitting on a high mountain looking out over all the mountains and waters, like lighting a heavenly lamp to illuminate the darkest depths — this is what is called “concentrating the spirit in emptiness”. Regulating the breath is not difficult: once the mind and spirit are still, let the breath be natural; I simply keep to this naturalness, and add the light of the spirit shining downward — this is regulating the breath. Regulating the breath means directing the breath of the yinqiao and the breath in my heart so that they meet in the Cavity of Breath. The mind resting below the navel is called concentrating the spirit; the breath returning below the navel is called regulating the breath. Spirit and breath rely on each other: keeping to clarity, stillness and naturalness is called “not forgetting”; following clarity, stillness and naturalness is called “not helping”. Not forgetting and not helping, silent and soft, the breath lively and the mind at ease — then use the formula of “boring in”: take empty space as the place to store the mind, take dim silence as the home where the spirit rests; again and again, clarifying and clarifying again, suddenly spirit and breath forget each other, spirit and breath merge, and before one knows it, in a sudden daze, yang is born and one is as if drunk.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

此处气穴指下丹田，阴跷即会阴穴，脐下实为脐内。

The “Cavity of Breath” here refers to the lower elixir field; the yinqiao is the Huiyin point (the perineum); “below the navel” really means “inside the navel” (inward from the navel).

§4
Original (Chinese)

真消息，玄关发现时也。凡丹旨中有先天字、真字、元字，皆是阴阳鼎中生出来的，皆是杳冥昏默后产出来的。以后看丹经可类推矣。

This moment of being as if drunk is the true sign; it is when the Mysterious Pass appears. Whenever the purport of the alchemical books contains the words “Prior Heaven”, “true” or “original”, all of these are things born from the tripod of yin and yang, all produced after the dark and obscure, dim and silent. From now on, when reading alchemical scriptures, one can infer the rest from this.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

阴阳鼎指下丹田。

The “tripod of yin and yang” refers to the lower Cinnabar Field.

§5
Original (Chinese)

神要真神，方算先天。真神者，真念是他，真心是他，真意是他。如何辨得真？诀曰：玄关火发，杳冥冲醒，一灵独觉者是也。丹家云：一念从规中起，即真神即真念也。又云：微茫之中心光发现，即真神即真心也。又云：定中生慧，一意斡旋，即真神，即真意也。真神从不神中炼出，学者知之。

The spirit must be the true spirit; only then does it count as Prior Heaven. The true spirit — the true thought is it, the true mind is it, the True Intention is it too. How can one tell what is true? The formula says: when the fire of the Mysterious Pass flares and one is jolted awake out of the dark and obscure, the single numinous awareness, alone and awake — that is it. The alchemists say: a thought arising from the compass center is the true spirit, is the true thought. They also say: the light of the mind appearing in the faint and indistinct is the true spirit, is the true mind. They also say: wisdom arising in concentration, one intention turning the pivot, is the true spirit, is the true intention. The true spirit is refined out of the “non-spirit”; students should know this.

§6
Original (Chinese)

大道以修心炼性为首，性在心内，心包性外，是性为定理之主人，心为栖性之庐舍。修心者存心也。炼性者养性也。存心者坚固城廓，不使房屋倒坍，即筑基也。养性者浇培鄞鄂，务使内药成全，即炼己也。心朗朗，性安安，情欲不干，无思无虑，心与性内外坦然，不烦不恼，此修心炼性之效，即内丹也。

The great Way puts cultivating the mind and refining the nature first. The nature is within the mind, the mind wraps around the nature; so the nature is the master of settled principle, and the mind is the dwelling where the nature lodges. Cultivating the mind is preserving the mind; refining the nature is nourishing the nature. Preserving the mind is strengthening the city walls so that the house does not collapse — this is laying the foundation. Nourishing the nature is watering and tending the embryo and its outline, making sure that the inner medicine is completed — this is refining the self. The mind bright and clear, the nature calm and at peace, untroubled by feelings and desires, without thought or worry, mind and nature at ease within and without, neither vexed nor distressed — this is the effect of cultivating the mind and refining the nature, and it is the inner elixir.

§7
Original (Chinese)

世有学道数月而不见其寸进者，为无真心向道也。人若有心于道，自然无事于心。人若心重于道，自然心轻于事。人若心浓于道，自然心淡于事。守其性兮不散乱，存其神兮不昏沉，又安有渴睡杂念之扰哉！

There are people in the world who study the Way for months without seeing an inch of progress; it is because they have no true heart turned toward the Way. If one has a heart for the Way, naturally one has nothing on one’s mind. If one values the Way more, naturally one values affairs less. If one’s heart is thick toward the Way, naturally it is thin toward affairs. Guard the nature and it does not scatter; preserve the spirit and it does not sink into torpor — then how could there be any trouble from drowsiness or stray thoughts!

§8
Original (Chinese)

潜心于渊神不外游，心牵于事火动于中，火动于中必摇其精。心静则息自调，静久则心自定。死心以养气，息机以纯心。精气神为内三宝，耳目口为外三宝。常使内三宝不逐物而游，外三宝不透中而扰，呼吸绵绵深入丹田，使呼吸为夫妇，神气为子母，子母夫妇聚而不离，故心不外驰，意不外想，神不外游，精不妄动，常熏蒸于四肢，此金丹大道之正宗也。

Sink the mind into the abyss and the spirit does not wander outward; when the mind is pulled by affairs, fire stirs within; when fire stirs within, it is bound to shake the essence. When the mind is still, the breathing regulates itself; when stillness lasts, the mind settles of itself. Let the mind die down to nourish the breath; still all scheming to make the mind pure. Essence, breath and spirit are the three inner treasures; ears, eyes and mouth are the three outer treasures. Always keep the three inner treasures from chasing after things and wandering off, and the three outer treasures from piercing into the center and disturbing it; let the breathing go continuously and deeply into the elixir field, making exhalation and inhalation like husband and wife and spirit and breath like mother and child; mother and child, husband and wife gather and do not part. So the mind does not rush outward, the intention does not think outward, the spirit does not wander outward, the essence does not stir recklessly, and it constantly steams through the four limbs — this is the true lineage of the great Way of the Golden Elixir.

§9
Original (Chinese)

大道从中字入门。所谓中字者，一在身中，一不在身中。功夫须两层做：第一寻身中之中。朱子云：守中制外。夫守中者，须要回光返照，注意规中，于脐下一寸三分处，不即不离，此寻身中之中也。第二求不在身中之中。《中庸》云：喜怒哀乐之未发。此未发时，不见不闻，戒慎幽独，自然性定神清，神清气慧，到此方见本来面目，此求不在身中之中也。以在身中之中，求不在身中之中，然后人欲易净，天理复明，千古圣贤仙佛，皆以此为第一步功夫。

The great Way is entered through the single word “center”. Of what is called the “center”, one is within the body and one is not within the body. The work must be done on two levels. First, seek the center within the body. Master Zhu says: “Guard the center to govern the outside.” Guarding the center requires reverting the light to shine within, fixing the attention on the compass center, at the place one cun three fen below the navel, neither clinging nor leaving — this is seeking the center within the body. Second, seek the center not within the body. The Doctrine of the Mean speaks of “before joy, anger, sorrow and pleasure have arisen”; at this time before they have arisen, seeing nothing and hearing nothing, being watchful and cautious even when alone in the dark, the nature naturally settles and the spirit becomes clear, the spirit clear and the breath wise — only on reaching this point does one see one’s original face. This is seeking the center not within the body. By the center within the body, seek the center not within the body; then human desires are easily cleansed and the principle of Heaven shines again. The sages, worthies, immortals and buddhas of all ages took this as the first step of the work.

Qiu Ling's noteon §9
Original note (Chinese)

脐下一寸三分指脐内一寸三分。

“One cun three fen below the navel” means one cun three fen inward from the navel.

§10
Original (Chinese)

打坐之中，最要凝神调息，以暇以整，勿助勿忘，未有不逐日长功夫者。

凝神调息，要心平气和。心平则神凝，气和则息调。心平，平字最妙。心不起波之谓平，心执其中之谓平。平即在此中也。心在此中，乃不起波。此中即丹经之玄关一窍也。

In sitting, what matters most is concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath, at leisure and in good order, neither helping nor forgetting; there is no one who does this and does not grow in the work day by day.

Concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath requires a level mind and harmonious breath. When the mind is level, the spirit concentrates; when the breath is harmonious, the breathing is regulated. A level mind — the word “level” is the most wonderful. When the mind raises no waves, that is called level; when the mind holds to the center, that is called level. Levelness is right here in this center; when the mind is in this center, it raises no waves. This center is what the alchemical scriptures call the single opening of the Mysterious Pass.

§11
Original (Chinese)

修炼不知玄关，无论其他，只此便如入暗室一般，从何下手？玄关者，气穴也，气穴者，神入气中，如在深穴之中也。神气相恋，则玄关之体已立。

If those who cultivate do not know the Mysterious Pass, never mind anything else — this alone is like walking into a dark room; where would they begin? The Mysterious Pass is the Cavity of Breath; the Cavity of Breath is the spirit entering the breath, as if in a deep cavity. When spirit and breath cling to each other, the substance of the Mysterious Pass is already established.

Qiu Ling's noteon §11
Original note (Chinese)

此处气穴，是指行功中神气相恋后出现的一种玄妙现象。

The “Cavity of Breath” here refers to a mysterious phenomenon that appears in practice after spirit and breath cling to each other (not to a fixed acupoint).

§12
Original (Chinese)

古仙云：调息要调真息息，炼神须炼不神神。真息之息，息乎其息者也。不神之神，神乎其神者也。总要无人心有道心，将此道心返入虚无，昏昏默默，存于规中，乃能养真息之息，得不神之神。

初学必从内呼吸下手。此个呼吸，乃是离父母重立胞胎之地。人能从此处立功，便如母呼亦呼，母吸亦吸之时，好象重生之身一般。

An ancient immortal said: “In regulating the breath, regulate the breath of the true breath; in refining the spirit, refine the spirit of the non-spirit.” “The breath of the true breath” is resting the breathing within the breathing itself; “the spirit of the non-spirit” is a wonder beyond the wonderful. In short, one must have no human mind but the mind of the Way, return this mind of the Way to emptiness, dim and silent, and keep it in the compass center; only then can one nourish the breath of the true breath and obtain the spirit of the non-spirit.

Beginners must start with inner breathing. This breathing is the place where, having left father and mother, one establishes the womb anew. If one can work here, it is like the time in the mother’s womb when “the mother breathes out and one breathes out, the mother breathes in and one breathes in” — as if one were born again with a new body.

§13
Original (Chinese)

大凡打坐，须将神抱住气，意系住息，在丹田中宛转悠扬，聚而不散，则内藏之气，与外来之气交结于丹田，日充月盛，达乎四肢，流乎百脉，撞开夹脊双关而上游于泥丸，旋复降下绛宫而下丹田，神气相守，息息相依，河车之路通矣。功夫到此，筑基之效已得一半了。总是要勤虚炼耳。

调息须以后天呼吸，寻真人呼吸之处。古云：“后天呼吸起微风，引起真人呼吸功。”然调后天呼吸，须任他自调，方能调得起先天呼吸。我准致虚守静而已。真息一动，玄关即不远矣。照此进功，筑基可翘足而至，不必百日也。

Whenever one sits, the spirit must embrace the breath and the intention must bind the breathing; they circle gently and freely in the elixir field, gathered and not scattering. Then the breath stored within and the breath coming from outside join in the elixir field, growing fuller day by day and more abundant month by month, reaching the four limbs and flowing through the hundred vessels; it bursts open the twin passes of the Spine-Straddling Pass and travels up to the Muddy Pellet, then turns and descends through the Crimson Palace to the lower elixir field; spirit and breath guard each other, every breath relies on the other, and the road of the River Chariot is open. When the work reaches this point, half the effect of laying the foundation has been obtained. In short, one must diligently refine emptiness.

Regulating the breath must use Posterior Heaven breathing to find the place where the true person breathes. The ancients said: “Posterior Heaven breathing raises a gentle wind, which calls forth the work of the true person’s breathing.” But in regulating Posterior Heaven breathing, one must let it regulate itself; only then can one call forth Prior Heaven breathing. I simply bring emptiness to its utmost and guard stillness, and that is all. Once the True Breath stirs, the Mysterious Pass is not far. If one advances in the work like this, laying the foundation can be reached in no time — it need not take a hundred days.

§14
Original (Chinese)

《道德经》“致虚极，守静笃”二句，可浑讲，亦可折讲。浑言之，只是教人以入定之功耳。折言之，则虚是虚无，极是中极，静是安静，笃是专笃。犹言致吾神于虚无之间，而准其中极之地，守其神于安静之内，必尽其专笃之功。

人心者二：一真一妄。故觅真心者，不生妄念，即是真心。真心之性格，最宽大，最光明。真心之所居，最安然，最自在。以真心理事，千条一贯。以真心寻道，万殊一本。然人要用他应事，就要养得他壮大，就要守得他安闲，然后劳而不劳，静而能应。丹诀云：心走即收回，收回又放下，用后复求安，求安即生悟也。谁云闹中不可取静耶？

The two lines of the Daode jing, “Bring emptiness to its utmost, guard stillness steadfastly”, can be explained as a whole or taken apart. Taken as a whole, they simply teach the work of entering concentration. Taken apart, “emptiness” is emptiness and nothingness, “utmost” is the central pole, “stillness” is calm, and “steadfastly” is single-minded devotion — as if to say: bring my spirit into the midst of emptiness and nothingness and aim it at the place of the central pole; guard my spirit within calm, and fully carry out the work of single-minded devotion.

The human mind is twofold: one true, one deluded. So in seeking the true mind — not giving rise to deluded thoughts is the true mind. The character of the true mind is most generous and most bright; the place where the true mind dwells is most at peace and most at ease. Handling affairs with the true mind, a thousand matters run on one thread; seeking the Way with the true mind, ten thousand differences have one root. But if one is to use it to respond to affairs, one must nourish it until it is strong, and guard it until it is at leisure; then one can labor without laboring, be still and yet able to respond. The alchemical formula says: when the mind wanders, bring it back; once it is back, let it go again; after use, seek peace again, and seeking peace gives rise to insight. Who says one cannot find stillness in the midst of noise?

§15
Original (Chinese)

游方枯坐，固非道也。然不游行于城市云山，当以气游行于通身关窍内，乃可。不打坐于枯木寒堂，须以神打坐于此身妙窍中，乃可。

凡人养神养气之际，神即为收气主宰。收得一分气，便得一分宝。收得十分气，便得十分宝。气之贵重，世上凡金凡玉，虽百两不换一分。何必与世人争利息乎？利多生忿恚，忿恚属火，气亦火种。忿恚一生，气随之走，欲留而不能留。又其甚者，连母带子一齐飞散。故养气以戒忿恚为切。欲戒忿恚，仍以养心养神为切。

Wandering about and sitting like a withered log are certainly not the Way. But if one does not travel through cities and cloud-covered mountains, one should let the breath travel through the passes and openings of the whole body; if one does not sit in meditation in a cold hall like a withered tree, one must let the spirit sit in meditation in the wondrous opening of this body.

Whenever people nourish the spirit and the breath, the spirit is the master that gathers the breath. Gather one part of breath and you gain one part of treasure; gather ten parts of breath and you gain ten parts of treasure. Breath is so precious that the ordinary gold and jade of the world, even a hundred ounces of them, could not buy one part of it. Why compete with the people of the world for profit? Much profit gives rise to anger; anger belongs to fire, and breath too is a seed of fire. Once anger arises, the breath goes off with it; one would like to keep it but cannot. Worse still, mother and child scatter and fly off together. So in nourishing the breath, guarding against anger is most urgent; and to guard against anger, nourishing the mind and nourishing the spirit are still the most urgent.

§16
Original (Chinese)

学道之士须要清心、清意，方得真清之药物也。毋逞气质之性，毋运思虑之神，毋使呼吸之气，毋用交感之精。然真精动于何时？真神生于何地？真气运于何方？真性养于何所？是不可不得明辨而细言之也。

Those who study the Way must clear the mind and clear the intention; only then do they obtain truly clear medicinal ingredients. Do not indulge the nature of temperament; do not use the spirit of deliberation; do not drive the breath of respiration; do not use the essence of sexual intercourse. Then at what time does the true essence stir? In what place is the true spirit born? In what direction does the true breath move? In what place is the true nature nourished? These must not fail to be clearly distinguished and explained in detail.

Key Terms

  • Three stages and three passes: the levels of the great Way; the three lesser stages are accumulating essence and building up breath, opening the passes and unfolding the openings, and laying the foundation and refining the self. See the three transmutations.
  • “After non-action comes action; after action, one returns to non-action”: the four phrases on the levels of the great Way.
  • Three outer treasures, three inner treasures: ears, eyes and mouth, and essence, breath and spirit; see Three Lights.
  • “Exhaust the principles, fulfill the nature, and so arrive at life”: Book of Changes, “Discussion of the Trigrams”; see inner nature / life.
  • Establishing life by means of nature, returning from the Posterior Heaven to the Prior Heaven: the formulas within the three characters “and so arrive at”; see Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven.
  • Concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath: the eight-character work of getting started; see concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath and regulating the breath.
  • Concentrating the spirit in emptiness: like sitting on a high mountain looking out over all the mountains and waters.
  • Yinqiao: the perineum; see perineum.
  • Below the navel: Qiu Ling notes that it really means inside the navel; see lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Not forgetting and not helping: keeping to clarity, stillness and naturalness, and following clarity, stillness and naturalness.
  • The formula of “boring in”: take empty space as the place to store the mind, dim silence as the home where the spirit rests; see the dim and obscure.
  • True spirit, true thought, true mind, true intention: the single numinous awareness, alone and awake; see True Intention and Original Spirit.
  • Preserving the mind, nourishing the nature: preserving the mind is laying the foundation, nourishing the nature is refining the self.
  • Embryo and its outline: the foundation of the elixir; see spontaneous Dharma Body.
  • Guarding the center to govern the outside, the center within the body, the center not within the body: see Central Yellow.
  • A level mind: when the mind raises no waves, that is called level; when the mind holds to the center, that is called level.
  • Mysterious Pass, Cavity of Breath: the spirit entering the breath, spirit and breath clinging to each other; Qiu Ling notes that it is a mysterious phenomenon appearing in practice. See Mysterious Opening / Mysterious Pass.
  • The breath of the true breath, the spirit of the non-spirit: words of an ancient immortal.
  • Inner breathing, the true person’s breathing: see True Breath.
  • The road of the River Chariot: see Celestial Circuit.
  • “Bring emptiness to its utmost, guard stillness steadfastly”: Daode jing, chapter 16.
  • “When the mind wanders, bring it back; once it is back, let it go again”: an alchemical formula, the method of finding stillness in the midst of noise.
  • Anger: in nourishing the breath, guarding against anger is most urgent.
  • “Do not indulge the nature of temperament … do not use the essence of sexual intercourse”: the Posterior Heaven nature, spirit, breath and essence must not be used.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “When the mind is not yet clear, do not close the eyes at random” — before sitting, first bring the mind back; begin only when it is cool, calm and tranquil. This is the most practical sentence in this part, and in the whole book.
  2. “When the mind is still, the breathing regulates itself” — do not try to control the breathing; when the mind is still, the breathing becomes even by itself.
  3. “A level mind — the word ‘level’ is the most wonderful” — a mind that raises no waves is itself the work. You can use the word “level” as a reminder in daily life.
  4. “If one values the Way more, naturally one values affairs less” — slow progress is often not a matter of the wrong method, but of the mind still being elsewhere.
  5. “When the mind wanders, bring it back; once it is back, let it go again” — when your mind drifts, bring it back; once it is back, do not grip it tightly — let go again. One can find stillness even in the midst of noise; there is no need to wait for a quiet environment before practicing.
  6. “Why compete with the people of the world for profit?” — anger is what consumes the breath most. To nourish the breath, first guard against anger; to guard against anger, first nourish the mind.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Exhaust the principles, fulfill the nature, and so arrive at life”: from the “Discussion of the Trigrams” (Shuogua zhuan) of the Book of Changes; the original book says “the Appended Statements say”, and we follow the book.
  2. “The three characters ‘and so arrive at’”: refers to the three characters yi zhi yu 以至于 in “exhaust the principles, fulfill the nature, and so arrive at life”.
  3. “Once the spirit is concentrated” (ningqi shen le 凝起神了): the original has a colloquial tone here, which we keep as in the book.
  4. “Guard the center to govern the outside”: the idea comes from Song Neo-Confucianism (Cheng Yi’s “Four Admonitions”, “Admonition on Seeing”: “govern it from the outside in order to settle what is within”); the original book says “Master Zhu says”, and we follow the book.
  5. “Posterior Heaven breathing raises a gentle wind, which calls forth the work of the true person’s breathing”: an old saying often quoted in alchemical books.
  6. “In short, one must diligently refine emptiness” (zongshi yao qin xu lian er 总是要勤虚炼耳): meaning one must be diligent in “refining emptiness”.
  7. “Reached in no time” (qiaozu er zhi 翘足而至): literally “reached by lifting a foot”, meaning very quickly.
  8. Qiu Ling slightly abridged this text, and the original book does not mark where; we translate the text as printed in this book.
  9. The original consists of a number of natural paragraphs, which we have grouped by content into sixteen short passages; Qiu Ling’s editor’s notes, which are in brackets within the text in the original, have been moved after the corresponding passages.

Introduction to Part Seven

What this book is

Plain and Simple Words on the Dao (Daoyan qianjin shuo 道言浅近说) is a collection of sayings attributed to Zhang Sanfeng, included in the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng edited by Li Hanxu. The words “plain and simple” in the title are its defining feature: there are no long passages of hexagram imagery and coded language; almost every passage speaks directly about how to sit, how to concentrate the spirit, how to regulate the breath, and what to do when the mind goes wrong. Qiu Ling says that it “uses plain and simple language, combined with his rich experience and insight in practice, to explain the levels and the knacks of alchemical practice, and is therefore required reading for students entering the practice”.

We have already met its sentences several times in earlier parts — Chen Yingning’s notes in the appendix to the first book quote “Concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, regulate the breath and concentrate the spirit — these eight characters must be done as one piece”, and “the practice of gathering the mind for beginners” in the third part, the Secret Principles of the Three Carts, is almost the same passage. This part is where those words come from.

After the text come “five passages collected by Sanfeng”: Bai Ziqing’s (Bai Yuchan’s) Formula of the Mysterious Pass and Formula of the Furnace and Tripod, and three passages by Lu Qianxu on regulating the breath.

Qiu Ling slightly abridged the text and added a few short editor’s notes in places (mainly to clarify acupoints).

Qiu Ling's editorial note

此篇选自李涵虚编订之《三丰全集》，后附张三丰氏辑说五则。张氏以浅近之语言，结合其丰富的练功经验及体会，阐明丹功层次和诀窍，故为学者入门所必读。编者对本文略有删节。

This text is taken from the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng edited by Li Hanxu, followed by five passages collected by Mr. Zhang Sanfeng. Zhang uses plain and simple language, combined with his rich experience and insight in practice, to explain the levels and the knacks of alchemical practice; it is therefore required reading for students entering the practice. The editor has slightly abridged the text.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

Eight characters: concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, regulate the breath and concentrate the spirit — only when the mind is clear is it gathered into the cavity of breath, and when the mind is still, the breathing regulates itself; keeping to clarity, stillness and naturalness is “not forgetting”, following clarity, stillness and naturalness is “not helping”; from the “center” within the body, seek the “center” that is not within the body; when the mind wanders, bring it back, and once it is back, let go again — even in the midst of noise one can find stillness.

Reading tips

  1. Of the whole Essentials for Entering Alchemical Practice, this is the part best suited to reading every day. Almost every passage can be used directly in quiet sitting and in daily life.
  2. Some passages especially recommended: “A level mind — the word ‘level’ is the most wonderful. When the mind raises no waves, that is called level”; “If one values the Way more, naturally one values affairs less”; “When the mind wanders, bring it back; once it is back, let it go again; after use, seek peace again, and seeking peace gives rise to insight. Who says one cannot find stillness in the midst of noise?”; “Breath is so precious … why compete with the people of the world for profit?”
  3. Qiu Ling’s editor’s notes mainly correct the location of acupoints: where the text says “below the navel” or “one cun three fen below the navel”, Qiu Ling points out that it really means “inside the navel” (inward), not the skin surface below the navel.
  4. Lu Qianxu’s second and third appended passages use expressions such as “the man does not loosen his robe, the woman does not untie her sash” and “stopping and delighting, the man places himself below the woman”, and the text itself explains that “these are all only metaphors for concentrating the spirit and gathering the breath”; they use man and woman as a metaphor for the union of spirit and breath, and we translate accordingly.

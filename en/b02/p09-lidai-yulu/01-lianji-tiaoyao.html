Refining the Self and Regulating the Medicine

Sayings selection, section 1 · Refining the self and regulating the medicine — on refining the self and returning to emptiness, the method of regulating the medicine and gathering the faint yang at the start, on the order of fire phasing

Safety note (translator): “The method of regulating the medicine and gathering the faint yang at the start” in this section is the beginning work of the Wu-Liu school’s alchemy for men. It takes “the male organ rising vigorously” as the sign of the Living Zi Hour, and includes practices such as “welcoming it back to the furnace from the yinqiao, ten times or several dozen times, stopping when the outer form subsides”. We translate according to the original meaning. Please note that Liang Jingyang in the book says plainly: “If the time happens to come, one must not hastily apply the four-word formula; simply concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root.” Beginners should do only the work of concentrating the spirit, regulating the breath and being still; do not deliberately clench, hold the breath or “welcome” the breath with force. This applies especially to anyone with prostate or pelvic-floor conditions.

About This Chapter

The three short discussions in this section are about “before beginning” and “the moment of beginning”:

  • On refining the self and returning to emptiness (Wu Chongxu): “refining” is austerity, diligent effort, abstention and cutting off attachment; the “self” is the original spirit, the true intention, the body of the nature that is originally empty. Wealth and rank, fame, wife and children, treasures, and even every matter “already known, already done, already mastered” belong to craving and attachment — “if there is a single thing on your mind, you enter into that one thing and not into the Way”. The work of returning to emptiness “lies only in having no mind when facing circumstances”: the myriad phenomena are all empty, not a speck of dust clings, not a single thought arises, and the six faculties are in great concentration.
  • The method of regulating the medicine and gathering the faint yang at the start (Liu Huayang and others): there is no other formula for cultivating the great Way; it is only the Prior Heaven original spirit and original breath. In the dim and indistinct, knowing who is dim and indistinct — that is the original spirit; in the dim and indistinct, the true mechanism stirring of itself and the male organ rising vigorously — that is the original breath. Regulating the medicine has its time, its place and its method: the time is “when the outer kidney is about to rise” (not rising because of a thought); the place is the Cavity of Breath “behind the navel, in front of the kidneys and slightly below”; the method is “one must use the breath of respiration to keep it and bring it back to refine it”.
  • On the order of fire phasing (Liu Huayang): refining the self → regulating the medicine → the Celestial Circuit; the three are not one thing. When spirit and breath are about to unite but have not yet united, preserving the spirit and using the breath is the martial fire; once spirit and breath have united, forgetting the breath and forgetting the intention, “blowing without breathing, illuminating without preserving”, is the civil fire. “When the mind is present everywhere, that is called illumination; when desire is extinguished everywhere, that is called forgetting” — true forgetting and true illumination are one yet two, two yet one. The “stages” of fire phasing are: the breath stirring is one stage, driving the breath with the spirit is one stage; gathering it back to the furnace and sealing it are each one stage; rising, descending and ablution make four stages; together six stages.

Original Text and Translation

§1On refining the self and returning to emptiness
Original (Chinese)

伍冲虚曰：炼者苦行其当行之事曰炼，精进励志而求其必成曰炼，绝禁其不当为之事曰炼，割绝贪爱而不留余爱亦曰炼。凡富贵功名、妻子、珍财、田宅，及一切事之已知者、已行者、已能者，皆属贪爱，唯割舍尽绝，不留丝毫，方名万缘不挂。若有一件挂心，便入此一件不入于道，故必割而又割，绝而又绝。事与念割绝尽净，而后可称真炼。己者元神也，真意也，即我本来虚空性体也。静者为性，动者为意，妙用则为神也。四者（注：即己、性、意、神）未发之前，浑然如太虚。因机萌而言，故有己、意、性、神之分。还虚者，复还无极鸿濛未判之初。其功惟在对境无心而已。所谓万象皆空，一尘不染，一念不起，六根（注：即耳、目、鼻、舌、身、意）大定，此即本来之性体完全也。若不炼己还虚，则临时熟境难忘，神驰气散，安能夺得造化之机，还我神室，而为金丹发生之本耶？

Wu Chongxu says: Diligently doing what one ought to do is called “refining”; striving with determination and seeking to be sure of success is called “refining”; abstaining from what one ought not to do is called “refining”; cutting off craving and attachment without leaving the slightest remnant is also called “refining”. Wealth and rank, fame, wife and children, treasures and money, fields and houses, and every matter already known, already done, already mastered, all belong to craving and attachment; only when they are all cut off and given up, without leaving a trace, is it called “not hanging on to any of the ten thousand ties”. If there is a single thing on your mind, you enter into that one thing and not into the Way; so one must cut and cut again, sever and sever again. Only when affairs and thoughts are all cut off and cleansed can it be called true refining. The “self” is the original spirit, the true intention, the body of my nature that is originally empty. What is still is called the nature, what moves is called the intention, and its wondrous function is called the spirit. Before these four (editor’s note: the self, the nature, the intention and the spirit) arise, they are undifferentiated like the Great Void; only by speaking of the stirring of the mechanism are the self, intention, nature and spirit distinguished. “Returning to emptiness” is going back to the very beginning of the Limitless, before the primordial chaos was divided. Its work lies only in “having no mind when facing circumstances” — what is called “the myriad phenomena are all empty, not a speck of dust clings, not a single thought arises, the six faculties (editor’s note: ears, eyes, nose, tongue, body and mind) are in great concentration”; this is the original body of the nature made whole. If one does not refine the self and return to emptiness, then at the critical moment familiar scenes are hard to forget, the spirit rushes off and the breath scatters — how could one seize the mechanism of creation, restore one’s chamber of the spirit, and make it the root from which the Golden Elixir arises?

§2The method of regulating the medicine and gathering the faint yang at the start (1)
Original (Chinese)

柳华阳曰：欲修大道，理无别诀，无非先天元神元气而已。神乃心中之神，气即肾中之气。何以谓之先天？当虚寂恍惚时是也。何以谓之元神？以其从先天中出，既知恍惚是谁恍惚，此即先天之元神也。恍惚之时，不觉真机自动，阳物勃然而举，此即先天之元气也。吕祖曰：纯阴之下，须是用火煅炼，方得阳气发生，神明自来。华阳曰：中年人药少，不能采取，须是用火煅炼，方得有药可采，此机先一着以采微阳也。梁靖阳曰：虚耗者用筑基之功，平日既已炼己，入手当即调药。偶逢时至，未可遽行四字诀（注：即吸、舐、撮、闭），只是凝神气穴，息息归根而已。

Liu Huayang says: If you want to cultivate the great Way, in principle there is no other formula; it is nothing but the Prior Heaven original spirit and original breath. The spirit is the spirit in the heart; the breath is the breath in the kidneys. What is called Prior Heaven? It is the time of empty silence, dim and indistinct. What is called the original spirit? Because it comes out of the Prior Heaven — since you know the dim and indistinct, who is it that is dim and indistinct? This is the Prior Heaven original spirit. In the dim and indistinct, without your noticing, the true mechanism stirs of itself and the male organ rises vigorously — this is the Prior Heaven original breath. Patriarch Lü says: under pure yin, one must forge and refine with fire; only then is yang breath born and the spirit’s brightness comes of itself. Huayang says: middle-aged people have little medicine and cannot gather it; they must forge and refine with fire, and only then is there medicine to gather — this is the move before the moment, to gather the “faint yang”. Liang Jingyang says: those who are depleted should use the work of laying the foundation; those who have already refined the self in daily life should regulate the medicine as soon as they begin. If the time happens to come, one must not hastily apply the four-word formula (editor’s note: inhaling, pressing the tongue up, drawing in, closing); simply concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root.

§3The method of regulating the medicine and gathering the faint yang at the start (2)
Original (Chinese)

夫调药有时有地有法。时者何？觅元子曰：外肾欲举之时，即是身中活子时。华阳曰：外肾举者，非有念而举，乃自无而生，生而或速或缓，皆由活动之机。若有念而举，炼之即成幻丹。浑然问曰：若睡浓之时，不觉而自举，及偶然觉之，此时下手，亦成幻丹否？答曰：正睡浓时，自己身心俱已不觉，念从何有乎？尝闻吕祖云：动则施功静则眠。夏云峰曰：忙里偷闲调外药，无中生有采先天。俞玉吾曰：修炼日久，更无梦觉之异，虽当寝寐，神亦不寐；精生之时，不待唤醒，亦自觉悟，此即调药时也。

Regulating the medicine has its time, its place and its method. What is the time? Miyuanzi says: when the outer kidney is about to rise, that is the Living Zi Hour in the body. Huayang says: when the outer kidney rises, it is not rising because of a thought; it is born out of nothing, and when it is born, whether quickly or slowly, it is all due to the living mechanism. If it rises because of a thought, refining it produces an illusory elixir. Hunran asks: if during deep sleep it rises of itself without one’s noticing, and one happens to notice it, and begins work at this time, does that also produce an illusory elixir? Answer: right in deep sleep, one’s own body and mind are already unaware — where would a thought come from? I once heard Patriarch Lü say: “When there is movement, apply the work; when there is stillness, sleep.” Xia Yunfeng says: “Steal leisure amid busyness to regulate the outer medicine; from nothing produce something to gather the Prior Heaven.” Yu Yuwu says: when one has practiced for a long time, there is no longer any difference between dreaming and waking; even in sleep the spirit does not sleep; when the essence is born, one wakes of oneself without waiting to be woken — this is the time to regulate the medicine.

§4The method of regulating the medicine and gathering the faint yang at the start (3)
Original (Chinese)

地者何？华阳曰：其脉在脐后肾前稍下，前七后三，中空一穴，古曰内肾，又名气穴，正是调药炼精之所，即元气之融动处也。其真实在处，又在学者临机审察耳。法者何？《黄庭经》曰：呼吸元气以求仙。华阳曰：呼吸者后天之气也，元气者先天之气也。先后原有兼用之法，若不兼用，元气顺流而出，不能成丹矣。必假呼吸之气留归以炼之。当呼吸之机，我则从阴跷迎归炉，或十迎，或数十迎，外形（注：即阳物）倒则止矣，如冲虚所谓，以后天呼吸气留恋神气是也。栖云先生曰：人吃五谷，化为阴精。此物在里面作怪，只用丹田自然呼吸之气，吹动其中真火，水在上火在下，水得火自然化而为气，其气上腾熏蒸，传透一身之关窍，流通百脉。觅元子曰：阴精者，五谷饮食之精，苟非巽风坤火，猛烹极炼，此精必在身中引起思想淫欲，搅乱心君。务要凝神调息，使橐籥鼓风，而风吹火，烹炼阴精，化而为气。俞玉吾曰：内炼之道，至简至易，惟欲降心火入于丹田耳。此即微阳初动，凝神入气穴，息息归根，风火同用之法也。

What is the place? Huayang says: the vessel is behind the navel, in front of the kidneys and slightly below, seven parts to the front and three to the back, with an empty cavity in the middle, which the ancients called the “inner kidney” and also the “Cavity of Breath”; it is exactly the place for regulating the medicine and refining the essence, the place where the original breath melts and stirs. Where it really is, students must examine for themselves at the moment. What is the method? The Yellow Court Scripture says: “Breathe the original breath to seek immortality.” Huayang says: respiration is Posterior Heaven breath; the original breath is Prior Heaven breath. The Prior and Posterior Heaven have always had a method of being used together; if they are not used together, the original breath flows out along its ordinary course and no elixir can form. One must use the breath of respiration to keep it and bring it back to refine it. At the moment of breathing, I welcome it back to the furnace from the yinqiao, ten times or several dozen times, and stop when the outer form (editor’s note: the male organ) subsides — as Chongxu says, “using the Posterior Heaven breath of respiration to hold on to spirit and breath”. Master Qiyun says: people eat the five grains, which are transformed into yin essence. This thing makes trouble inside; simply use the natural breathing of the elixir field to blow on the true fire within it — water above, fire below; water, receiving fire, naturally turns into breath, and this breath rises and steams, penetrating the passes and openings of the whole body and flowing through the hundred vessels. Miyuanzi says: yin essence is the essence of the five grains, food and drink; if it is not fiercely cooked and thoroughly refined with the Xun wind and the Kun fire, this essence is bound to give rise in the body to lustful thoughts and desires and to disturb the sovereign of the heart. One must concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, let the bellows blow up a wind, and let the wind blow on the fire to cook and refine the yin essence and transform it into breath. Yu Yuwu says: the way of inner refining is utterly simple and utterly easy; it is only a matter of bringing the heart fire down into the elixir field. This is the method, when the faint yang first stirs, of concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root, wind and fire used together.

§5On the order of fire phasing
Original (Chinese)

华阳曰：夫道从炼己起手，次下手调药，既了手行周天。三事非一也。己熟或坐或卧，不觉忽然阳起，即回光返照，凝神入气穴，息息归根。此神气欲交未交之时，存神用息，绵绵若存，念兹在兹，此即谓之武火矣。神气既交，阳气已定，又当忘息忘意，用文火以养之，不息而嘘，不存而照，方得药产。但忘息，既不能以火熏之。但用息即是不忘，息无不泯之谓嘘，欲嘘不觉之谓忘。但用意即是不忘，但忘即不能以意照之。古云：心无不存之谓照，欲无不泯之谓忘。忘与照，一而二，二而一也。当忘之时，其心湛然，未尝不照。当照之时，纤毫不立，未尝不忘，是谓真忘真照也，此即谓之文火矣。文火既足，夜半忽然阳生，药产神知，光透帘帏。阳光一现，阳物勃然而举，则当采封运行。采运之时，存神用息，逆吹气穴，此即谓之武火也。封固沐浴归根，即用上文文火之法，照顾温养之。此即谓之文火矣。但不在交媾与周天之时，俱是用文火之法，以时刻温养之。而炼己之功，亦是用此法。不然，不能还虚。然候者亦非一说，不论阳生药产，但有气动者即为一候。以神驭气，又为一候。此乃神气会合之二候也。故曰：二候采牟尼者即此也。药气既产，往外采归炉为一候，而炉中封固，又为一候，亦谓之二候采牟尼。升降沐浴为四候，总谓之六候。此乃周天一时功法所用之六候也。

Huayang says: the Way starts with refining the self, next one begins regulating the medicine, and finally one completes the work by carrying out the Celestial Circuit; these three things are not one. When the self is well refined, whether sitting or lying, suddenly, without noticing, yang rises; then revert the light to shine within, concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root. This is the time when spirit and breath are about to unite but have not yet united; preserve the spirit and use the breath, continuous as if barely there, every thought here — this is called the martial fire. Once spirit and breath have united and the yang breath is settled, one should forget the breath and forget the intention, and nourish it with the civil fire: blowing without breathing, illuminating without preserving — only then is the medicine produced. If one merely forgets the breath, one cannot steam it with fire; if one merely uses the breath, that is not forgetting — when the breath is extinguished everywhere, that is called blowing; when one wishes to blow without being aware of it, that is called forgetting. If one merely uses the intention, that is not forgetting; if one merely forgets, one cannot illuminate it with the intention. The ancients said: “When the mind is present everywhere, that is called illumination; when desire is extinguished everywhere, that is called forgetting.” Forgetting and illumination are one yet two, two yet one. At the time of forgetting, the mind is limpid and never fails to illuminate; at the time of illuminating, not a hair is set up and one never fails to forget — this is called true forgetting and true illumination, and this is called the civil fire. When the civil fire is sufficient, at midnight yang is suddenly born, the medicine is produced and the spirit knows it, light shines through the curtains. Once the yang light appears and the male organ rises vigorously, one should gather, seal and circulate. At the time of gathering and circulating, preserve the spirit and use the breath, blowing in reverse into the Cavity of Breath — this is called the martial fire. For sealing and securing, ablution and returning to the root, use the civil-fire method above to look after and nourish it warmly — this too is called the civil fire. Except at the times of union and of the Celestial Circuit, one always uses the civil-fire method to nourish it warmly at every moment; and the work of refining the self also uses this method, otherwise one cannot return to emptiness. But “stage” does not have only one meaning. Whether yang is born or the medicine is produced, whenever the breath stirs, that is one stage; driving the breath with the spirit is another stage — these are the two stages of the meeting of spirit and breath; so “gathering the muni in two stages” refers to this. When the breath of the medicine has been produced, gathering it from outside back to the furnace is one stage, and sealing and securing it in the furnace is another stage — this too is called “gathering the muni in two stages”. Rising, descending and the two ablutions make four stages; altogether they are called the six stages. These are the six stages used in the method of one double hour of the Celestial Circuit.

Key Terms

  • Refining: austerity, diligent effort, abstention and cutting off attachment; see refining the self.
  • Not hanging on to any of the ten thousand ties: all craving and attachment cut off and given up.
  • Self, nature, intention, spirit: the self is the original spirit, the true intention; what is still is the nature, what moves is the intention, the wondrous function is the spirit. See Original Spirit and True Intention.
  • Returning to emptiness: going back to the very beginning of the Limitless, before the primordial chaos was divided; its work lies only in having no mind when facing circumstances.
  • Chamber of the spirit: the place where the original spirit dwells.
  • Prior Heaven original spirit and original breath: what knows the dim and indistinct in the dim and indistinct is the original spirit; the true mechanism stirring of itself in the dim and indistinct is the original breath.
  • Gathering the faint yang: middle-aged people have little medicine and must forge and refine with fire before there is medicine to gather.
  • Those who are depleted: people whose bodies are weakened.
  • When the outer kidney is about to rise, that is the Living Zi Hour: see outer kidney and Living Zi Hour.
  • Rising because of a thought produces an illusory elixir: see Clear source, turbid source.
  • “When there is movement, apply the work; when there is stillness, sleep”: words of Patriarch Lü.
  • Inner kidney, Cavity of Breath: behind the navel, in front of the kidneys and slightly below, seven parts to the front and three to the back; see lower Cinnabar Field and Cave of Emptiness.
  • “Breathe the original breath to seek immortality”: Yellow Court Scripture.
  • Welcoming it back to the furnace from the yinqiao: the Wu-Liu practice for gathering the faint yang (see the note at the head of the chapter).
  • Yin essence: the essence of the five grains, food and drink, which must be cooked and refined into breath.
  • Xun wind and Kun fire: the wind of breathing and the fire of the elixir field; see Wind of Xun.
  • Martial fire, civil fire: preserving the spirit and using the breath is the martial fire; forgetting the breath and the intention, blowing without breathing, illuminating without preserving is the civil fire. See fire phasing.
  • True forgetting and true illumination: when the mind is present everywhere, that is called illumination; when desire is extinguished everywhere, that is called forgetting.
  • Light shining through the curtains: the yang light appearing; see stopping the fire (the yang light appearing three times).
  • Six stages: the breath stirring and the spirit driving it (“gathering the muni in two stages”); gathering back to the furnace and sealing; rising, descending and ablution make four stages. See fire phasing and ablution.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “If there is a single thing on your mind, you enter into that one thing and not into the Way.” Before sitting, first put down whatever you are working on; do not let anything hang on your mind.
  2. “Its work lies only in having no mind when facing circumstances.” Refining the self is nowhere else; it is in the mind not being pulled away when facing outer circumstances.
  3. In the dim and indistinct, know who it is that is dim and indistinct — this point of clear awareness is the original spirit. Do not fall asleep in the dimness.
  4. Liang Jingyang’s reminder is the safest: when the time comes, “simply concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root”.
  5. “When the mind is present everywhere, that is called illumination; when desire is extinguished everywhere, that is called forgetting” — the right measure in quiet sitting lies between “illuminating” and “forgetting”: always aware, yet grasping nothing.

Translator's Notes

  1. The “(注：……)” in the original text are editor’s notes added by Qiu Ling, inserted into the text in the original book; we keep them in the original as in the book, and render them in the translation as “editor’s note”.
  2. “Hunran”: a disciple of Liu Huayang, who appears in the book in question-and-answer form.
  3. “Seven parts to the front and three to the back”: the Cavity of Breath lies toward the front — seven parts in front and three behind.
  4. “Gathering the muni in two stages”: a phrase from alchemical books such as the Xingming guizhi; muni is a metaphor for the elixir.
  5. In the original book the last sentence of the Discussion on Refining the Self and Returning to Emptiness runs across a page break; “how could one seize the mechanism of creation, restore one’s chamber of the spirit” is to be read continuously.

Details of the Lesser Celestial Circuit

Sayings selection, section 3 · Details of the Lesser Celestial Circuit — gathering and sealing, the yang fire and the yin tally, the counts of breathing, advancing the fire at zi, ablution at mao and you, withdrawing the tally at wu, returning to the root and the intercalary remainder, stopping the fire when the elixir is ripe

About This Chapter

The eight short discussions in this section go through the details step by step, in the order of the Lesser Celestial Circuit:

Some especially practical sayings: Hunranzi, “do not be alarmed, and do not raise too sharp an awareness”; Huayang, “stopping the breath is not holding the breath”; “the three hundred counts are not really three hundred breaths; they are all metaphors”; Ziyang, “once refined, you must still know when to stop”.

Original Text and Translation

§1On gathering and sealing
Original (Chinese)

混然子曰：时至气化，机动籁鸣，火从脐下发。华阳曰：时至者，乃药产之时也。籁鸣者，即玄关之机动也。火者气也。脐下者丹田也。古人曰时至神知者此也。学者知此时之机，不可惊怪亦不可起太明觉，虽分老嫩，老则气散不升，嫩则气微不升，务在静候动旺始采，是谓当令。以顺行之时候，即逆行之时候也。二气直论曰：此气虽动，不得神宰之而顺，不成精；不得神宰之而逆，亦不得返气。逆修者不令出阳关，即用后天之真呼吸，寻真人呼吸处，一意规中，以神宰气，收藏于内，是谓采取。药既归炉，又当封固停息以伏神气，此入中宫沐浴，即运用天子时之头也。封固者温养之义，停息者非闭息也，乃用文火将神气俱伏于气穴也。随后火逼金行，有行动之机，则周天武火自此运起矣。

Hunranzi says: when the time comes, the breath transforms; the mechanism moves and the pipes of nature sound; fire arises from below the navel. Huayang says: “when the time comes” is the time the medicine is produced; “the pipes of nature sound” is the stirring of the mechanism of the Mysterious Pass; “fire” is breath; “below the navel” is the elixir field. The ancients’ “when the time comes, the spirit knows” refers to this. When students know the mechanism of this moment, they must not be alarmed, nor raise too sharp an awareness. Although one distinguishes old and young — if old, the breath scatters and does not rise; if young, the breath is faint and does not rise — one must wait in stillness until the movement is strong before gathering; this is called being in season. The time of going along the ordinary course is the time of going in reverse. The Straight Discussion of the Two Breaths says: although this breath has stirred, if it is not directed by the spirit and goes along the ordinary course, it does not become essence; if it is not directed by the spirit and goes in reverse, it cannot return to breath either. Those who cultivate in reverse do not let it go out through the Yang Pass; they use the true breathing of the Posterior Heaven to find where the true person breathes, with single intention on the compass center, directing the breath with the spirit and storing it within — this is called gathering. Once the medicine has returned to the furnace, one must further seal it and stop the breath to lay spirit and breath to rest; this is entering the central palace for ablution, which is the beginning of using the “Heavenly zi hour”. Sealing and securing means warm nourishing; stopping the breath is not holding the breath, but using the civil fire to lay both spirit and breath to rest in the Cavity of Breath. Then “the fire drives the metal to move”, there is a mechanism of movement, and the martial fire of the Celestial Circuit starts to run from here.

§2On the yang fire and the yin tally
Original (Chinese)

伍冲虚曰：小周天用于化精时，其中奥妙，有子午二时之阳火阴符，卯酉二时之沐浴也。曹还阳云：凡进则曰进阳火，凡退则曰退阴符。亦以阳用者曰火，以阴用者曰符。华阳曰：运火之时，后天气进，则谓之阳火；后天气退，则谓之阴符。凡阳火阴符沐浴归根，皆是借后天呼吸之气，以为周天度数之法则。若无其呼吸，则无其阳火、阴符、沐浴、归根矣。《三皇玉诀》云：阴符者暗合也。暗合天地之机，采运长生之本，故曰阴符。即以此知阳用者曰火。阳符其火候，故曰阳火也。

Wu Chongxu says: the Lesser Celestial Circuit is used at the time of transforming the essence; its mystery includes the yang fire and the yin tally of the two hours zi and wu, and the ablutions of the two hours mao and you. Cao Huanyang says: every advance is called advancing the yang fire; every withdrawal is called withdrawing the yin tally. That is, what is used in the yang manner is called fire, what is used in the yin manner is called tally. Huayang says: when moving the fire, the Posterior Heaven breath advancing is called the yang fire; the Posterior Heaven breath withdrawing is called the yin tally. The yang fire, the yin tally, ablution and returning to the root all use the Posterior Heaven breath of respiration as the rule for the degrees of the Celestial Circuit; without breathing, there would be no yang fire, yin tally, ablution or returning to the root. The Jade Formula of the Three Sovereigns says: “The yin tally is a hidden match — secretly matching the mechanism of Heaven and Earth, gathering and moving the root of long life; that is why it is called the yin tally.” From this one knows that what is used in the yang manner is called fire; yang matches its fire phasing, so it is called the yang fire.

§3On the counts of breathing
Original (Chinese)

古云：圣人传药不传火。冲虚云：谁云火候不可传，随机默运入玄玄，达观往昔千千圣，呼吸分明了却仙。明明言火候是呼吸也。萧紫虚曰：乾坤橐籥鼓有数。华阳曰：数者乃阴阳升降之度数，假呼吸之息数而定卦爻之揲数也。若言不用息数，则万万不能成丹矣。且数者，每步四揲。升为阳，阳为乾，乾用九，四九三十六。乾策总六爻之四揲，共二百一十有六。降为阴，阴为坤，坤用六，四六二十四。坤策总六爻之四揲，共一百四十有四，合成三百六十数，成其法轮一转之途步限度。但其中有沐浴不用九六四揲，不满三百六十之义。然而阖辟无数，六十在其中矣，此乃恐其太过不及之弊。故必依周天以为行住起止多少之限法也，三百数者，实非三百息，皆譬喻言也。

The ancients said: “The sages transmit the medicine but not the fire.” Chongxu says: “Who says fire phasing cannot be transmitted? Following the moment, silently circulate into the mystery of mysteries; looking over the thousands upon thousands of sages of the past, those who made their breathing clear became immortals.” This says plainly that fire phasing is breathing. Xiao Zixu says: “The bellows of Qian and Kun blow by number.” Huayang says: the “number” is the degrees of the rising and falling of yin and yang; the count of breaths is used to fix the count of the hexagram lines. To say that the count of breaths is not used would make it utterly impossible to form the elixir.

The actual way of counting is this: each step (that is, one “compass” on the Dharma Wheel, a point on the route of circulation) uses “four counts” (that is, four breaths). The rising phase belongs to yang; yang is Qian, and Qian uses nine (in each breath one silently counts from one to nine); four breaths times nine is thirty-six. The Qian count sums the four counts of the six points on the rising route along the Governing vessel (named zi, chou, yin, mao, chen, si), thirty-six at each point, two hundred and sixteen in all. The descending phase belongs to yin; yin is Kun, and Kun uses six (in each breath one silently counts from one to six); four breaths times six is twenty-four. The Kun count sums the four counts of the six points on the descending route along the Conception vessel (named wu, wei, shen, you, xu, hai), twenty-four at each point, one hundred and forty-four in all. The two together make the number three hundred and sixty, which is the length and limit of one turn of the Dharma Wheel (the Lesser Celestial Circuit). But at the two points mao and you there is ablution, and they are not counted with the four counts of nine and six, so the actual count does not reach three hundred and sixty. Yet the opening and closing of the breath does not stop there; it is only not counted, and the missing sixty is contained within it. These numbers are fixed for fear of the fault of the fire phasing going too far or falling short, so one must take the Celestial Circuit as the limit for how much one moves, rests, starts and stops. The number “three hundred” does not really mean three hundred breaths; it is all a metaphorical way of speaking.

Jingxuzi's explanationsummary, on §3

Jingxuzi, in his Essentials of Practice in the Celestial-Immortal Lineage of Wu and Liu, takes these numbers apart word by word: in the third part, on the life of Liu Huayang, he adds interlinear notes phrase by phrase to the same passage of Huayang, “each step four counts … these are only metaphors”; and in the Huang Yuanji sayings recorded in the second part, he supplies the further point that “the two hours mao and you are not counted”. Taken together with Huayang’s “Diagram of the Six Stages of the Dharma Wheel” and Xixinzi’s explanation in the tenth part of this book, the whole passage becomes perfectly clear:

  1. A “step” is a point, not a clock hour. Xixinzi says: “A step is a compass on the diagram of the Dharma Wheel. The twelve compasses are the twelve steps.” Huayang’s diagram of the six stages of the Dharma Wheel draws the route of the Lesser Celestial Circuit as a circle: from “Earth” at the bottom (the zi position, the Cavity of Breath, starting at the Xu-Wei cavity) it rises along the Governing vessel, marked first to sixth compass, to “Heaven” at the top (the wu position, the Muddy Pellet); then it descends along the Conception vessel, again marked first to sixth compass, back to “Earth”. So “one step” is one point on this route: when the breath reaches this position, four breaths are used here, then it goes on to the next position.
  2. Why are they also called “hours”? The twelve points are labeled with the names of the twelve double hours: the six rising positions are called zi, chou, yin, mao, chen, si (“the six yang hours”), the six descending positions wu, wei, shen, you, xu, hai (“the six yin hours”). Jingxuzi’s note writes “each step (hour)”; this “hour” is the name of a position, not a time on the clock — as Xixinzi says clearly, “the twelve hours of turning are the living hours in the body, not the hours of the day”, and the Governing and Conception vessels “have six compasses each, which are six hours, the degrees and positions of the lines”. The original book gives only the starting point (the Cavity of Breath), the summit (the Muddy Pellet) and the passes to be crossed on the way (rising through the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Jade Pillow; descending through the Storied Tower and the Crimson Palace); it does not map the twelve compasses one by one to specific acupoints. This depends on a teacher’s transmission and one’s own experience; there is no need to search rigidly for acupoints according to the diagram.
  3. “Four counts” means four breaths. Each step (each point) uses four breaths.
  4. “Qian uses nine; four nines are thirty-six”: when rising, in each breath one silently counts from one to nine; four breaths at one point, 4 × 9 = 36.
  5. “The Qian count … two hundred and sixteen in all”: six rising points, 36 each, 6 × 36 = 216.
  6. “Kun uses six; four sixes are twenty-four”: when descending, in each breath one silently counts from one to six; four breaths at one point, 4 × 6 = 24.
  7. “The Kun count … one hundred and forty-four in all”: six descending points, 24 each, 6 × 24 = 144.
  8. “The two together make the number three hundred and sixty, the length and limit of one turn of the Dharma Wheel”: 216 + 144 = 360, the “rule of limits” for one turn of the Lesser Celestial Circuit.
  9. “Ablution is not counted with the four counts of nine and six, so it does not reach three hundred and sixty”: the two points mao and you are the places of ablution (on the diagram of the six stages of the Dharma Wheel, “ablu-” is in the middle of the rising route and “-tion” in the middle of the descending route); here one neither advances the fire nor withdraws the tally. Huang Yuanji says “except the two hours, which are not counted”. Mao is among the six rising positions, so one 36 is not counted; you is among the six descending positions, so one 24 is not counted; 60 in all, so what is actually counted is 360 − 60 = 300, which Huang Yuanji calls “the Celestial Circuit’s number of three hundred”.
  10. “The opening and closing are uncounted; the sixty is contained within them”: although mao and you are not counted, the opening and closing of the breath go on as usual (as Yu Yuwu says above, “the alchemical Way has not a single breath of interruption”, and Chongxu, “then to whom is the breathing entrusted?”); they are simply not counted, and the missing 60 is contained in this uncounted opening and closing. Such limits are set for fear of the fire phasing going too far or falling short.
  11. “The three hundred counts are not really three hundred breaths”: by this calculation each point has only four breaths, and a whole circuit of twelve points is only some forty-odd breaths; three hundred and sixty (or three hundred) is the “number” silently counted in the mind, not the number of breaths. That is why Huayang himself says “the number three hundred and sixty is not really three hundred and sixty; it is only a metaphor”, and Huang Yuanji also says: “How could the number of the Celestial Circuit be without measure? And how could one cling rigidly to the measure?” — there are degrees, but they must not be held to rigidly.

Jingxuzi also points out that this rule of limits — nine, six, thirty-six, twenty-four, two hundred and sixteen, one hundred and forty-four — was first transmitted in full by the Perfected Xu Jingyang of the Western Jin; alchemists through the ages mostly only hinted at it, and it was only in the works of Wu Chongxu and Liu Huayang that it was written out completely again.

§4On advancing the fire at *zi*
Original (Chinese)

守阳曰：起火炼药。混然子曰：火逼金行。当起火之初，受气宜柔。华阳曰：火者呼吸之气也，金者元气也。盖金不能自升，必假火以逼之，使朝于乾宫。然炉中药物未旋，不可即行武火，须以温柔之火逼之。金有动机，则火当长矣。若药未动，气伏而缓，先起武火，则内之气不顺，坠于蹊径，欲归正路，不亦难乎！故曰宜柔也。紫清翁曰：此时以灵觉为用，如线抽傀儡，机动气流，微微通过尾闾，又是一关。冲虚曰：尾闾关名下鹊桥，取心神朱雀之义。凭心神领气度过此处，故有鹊桥之名。上鹊桥亦然。

Shouyang (Wu Chongxu) says: raise the fire to refine the medicine. Hunranzi says: the fire drives the metal to move; when the fire is first raised, the breath should be received softly. Huayang says: the fire is the breath of respiration; the metal is the original breath. The metal cannot rise by itself; it must use the fire to drive it, so that it pays homage to the Qian palace. But if the medicine in the furnace has not yet begun to turn, one must not apply the martial fire at once; one must drive it with a warm, gentle fire, and only when the metal has a mechanism of movement should the fire grow. If the medicine has not stirred and the breath lies low and slow, and the martial fire is raised first, the breath within does not flow smoothly and falls into side paths — and then to get back on the right road, is that not hard! That is why it says “softly”. Old Master Ziqing says: at this time numinous awareness is used, like pulling a puppet by its strings; the mechanism moves and the breath flows, faintly passing through the Caudal Funnel — this is another pass. Chongxu says: the Caudal Funnel pass is called the lower Magpie Bridge, taking the sense of the heart-spirit as the Vermilion Bird; relying on the heart-spirit to lead the breath across this place, it has the name Magpie Bridge. The upper Magpie Bridge is the same.

§5On ablution at *mao* and *you*
Original (Chinese)

《悟真》注曰：子进阳光，息火谓之沐浴。午退阴符，停符谓之沐浴。华阳曰：息火停符者，停住有作而行自然之妙运，非是停住先天而不行，是停住后天之武火也。俞玉吾曰：天道无一息不运，丹道无一息间断。冲虚云：世称沐浴不行火，且道呼吸寄向谁。要将四正融抽补，才得金丹一粒归。子野云：卯酉不进火，但以真气熏蒸而为沐浴，万古不移。白紫清曰：卯酉之息，以柔字而已。陈希夷曰：卯时沐浴酉时同。

A commentary on the Wuzhen pian says: at zi, advancing the yang light, resting the fire is called ablution; at wu, withdrawing the yin tally, stopping the tally is called ablution. Huayang says: resting the fire and stopping the tally means stopping deliberate movement and carrying out the wondrous natural circulation; it is not stopping the Prior Heaven so that it does not move, but stopping the Posterior Heaven martial fire. Yu Yuwu says: the Way of Heaven has not a single breath without circulation; the alchemical Way has not a single breath of interruption. Chongxu says: “People say that during ablution the fire is not applied — then to whom is the breathing entrusted? One must merge, extract and add at the four cardinal points before a grain of Golden Elixir comes home.” Ziye says: at mao and you the fire is not advanced; only the true breath steams, and this is ablution — unchanged for ten thousand ages. Bai Ziqing says: the breathing at mao and you is nothing but the word “soft”. Chen Xiyi says: ablution at mao is the same as at you.

§6On withdrawing the tally at *wu*
Original (Chinese)

冲虚曰：印堂鼻窍，一实一虚，名上鹊桥，宜防危险。陆潜虚曰：上风府而直至泥丸，神与气交会于此，则其流畅融洽，不言可知。少焉降为新美之津，则自重楼而下，由绛宫入紫庭，欲归其所藏之处而休焉。如此循环贯注，久久纯熟，气满三田，上下交泰，所谓气冲关节透，自然精满谷神存。造化至此，内练之征见矣。

Chongxu says: the Hall of Impression and the nasal opening, one solid and one empty, are called the upper Magpie Bridge; one should guard against danger there. Lu Qianxu says: rising to the Wind Palace and straight up to the Muddy Pellet, spirit and breath meet here, and how smooth and harmonious its flow is goes without saying. After a while it descends as fresh, fine saliva, coming down from the Storied Tower and entering the Purple Court through the Crimson Palace, wishing to return to the place where it is stored and rest there. Circulating and pouring through like this, ever more thoroughly practiced, the breath fills the three fields and above and below meet in peace — what is called “the breath surges through and the joints open; naturally the essence is full and the valley spirit remains”. When creation reaches this point, the signs of inner refining appear.

§7On returning to the root and the intercalary remainder
Original (Chinese)

冲虚曰：归根还于下丹田之处为闰余，有温养沐浴之候。知有闰则知天之实周矣。静而一动则一炼而周，使机之动而复动者，则炼而复炼，周而复周。盖气之动，必须炼之，则周天之火容易止。若不炼，则火不能速止，而大药亦不能发生。古云：运罢河车君再睡，明朝依旧接天根。正此也。

Chongxu says: returning to the root, going back to the place of the lower elixir field, is called the “intercalary remainder”, and has a time of warm nourishing and ablution. Knowing there is an intercalary period, one knows the real cycle of Heaven. When there is one movement in stillness, refine once and complete one circuit; if the mechanism moves and moves again, refine and refine again, circle and circle again. For when the breath stirs, it must be refined; only then is the fire of the Celestial Circuit easy to stop. If it is not refined, the fire cannot be stopped quickly, and the Great Medicine cannot arise either. The ancients said: “When the River Chariot is done, go back to sleep; tomorrow morning, connect with the Root of Heaven again.” This is exactly it.

§8On stopping the fire when the elixir is ripe
Original (Chinese)

华阳曰：止者不行升降也。然虽不行升降，时刻不可须臾离火，常常温火熏蒸，离则亦自走矣。紫阳曰：未炼还丹须速炼，炼了还须知止足，若也持盈未已心，不免一朝遭殆辱。盖言必知止也。冲虚曰：止火有龟缩不举之景，有阳光二现之景，皆真候也。盖当炼精之时，两眉间号曰明堂，恍如掣电，即有阳光一现之景。斯时也，火候未全，淫根未缩，一遇阳生，即当采炼运一周天。务须采炼多番，圆满三百妙周限数，则精尽成气。无精可炼，阳关已闭，无窍可通，方得淫根如龟之缩。所积阳气，尽伏气根，方得阳光二现。光既二现，纵有动机，亦去其火更宜入定，以培养其真阳，静听阳光之三现可也。阳光三现，大药纯乾可采矣。若行火至于四现，则大药之可定者，必随火之不定者，溢出而化为有形之精也，可不戒哉！

Huayang says: stopping means no more rising and descending. But although there is no more rising and descending, one must not leave the fire for a single moment; always steam it with a warm fire — if one leaves it, it will run off by itself. Ziyang says: “If you have not yet refined the reverted elixir, refine it quickly; once refined, you must still know when to stop. If, holding it full, your mind does not stop, you cannot avoid one day meeting danger and disgrace.” This says one must know when to stop. Chongxu says: in stopping the fire, there is the scene of “retracting like a tortoise and not rising”, and the scene of “the yang light appearing twice”; both are true signs. When refining the essence, the space between the two eyebrows is called the Bright Hall; something like a flash of lightning there is the scene of the yang light appearing once. At this time the fire phasing is not yet complete and the root of lust has not yet retracted; whenever yang is born, one should gather, refine and run one Celestial Circuit. One must gather and refine many times, completing the limit of three hundred wondrous circuits; then the essence is all transformed into breath. When there is no essence left to refine, the Yang Pass is closed and there is no opening to pass through, only then does one obtain the retraction of the root of lust like a tortoise. When the yang breath accumulated has all settled in the root of breath, only then does one obtain the yang light appearing twice. Once the light has appeared twice, even if there is a mechanism of movement, remove the fire and enter concentration all the more, to cultivate the true yang, and quietly wait for the yang light to appear a third time. When the yang light appears three times, the Great Medicine is pure Qian and can be gathered. If one applies the fire until it appears a fourth time, then the Great Medicine, which could have been settled, is bound to follow the unsettled fire, overflow, and turn into essence with form — must one not take warning!

Key Terms

  • When the time comes, the breath transforms; the mechanism moves and the pipes of nature sound: the time the medicine is produced, the mechanism of the Mysterious Pass stirring; see Living Zi Hour.
  • Do not be alarmed, do not raise too sharp an awareness: the right state of mind when gathering the medicine.
  • Gathering: single intention on the compass center, directing the breath with the spirit and storing it within.
  • Sealing and securing, stopping the breath: sealing is warm nourishing; stopping the breath is not holding the breath, but using the civil fire to lay spirit and breath to rest in the Cavity of Breath.
  • Heavenly zi hour: the beginning of the true zi hour.
  • The fire drives the metal to move: the fire of breathing drives the original breath upward.
  • Yang fire, yin tally: the Posterior Heaven breath advancing is the yang fire, withdrawing is the yin tally; see fire phasing.
  • “The sages transmit the medicine but not the fire”: an old saying; Wu Chongxu: fire phasing is breathing.
  • Count (she 揲), tally of stalks (ce 策): numbers borrowed from the divination method of the Changes. She is counting in groups; here it means four breaths at each point (compass). The Qian count of two hundred and sixteen and the Kun count of one hundred and forty-four make three hundred and sixty. See Jingxuzi’s explanation under §3.
  • Ablution at mao and you not counted: the two hours mao and you are not counted, so the actual number is three hundred — “the sixty is contained within them”.
  • The breath should be received softly: at the first raising of the fire, do not hastily apply the martial fire.
  • Lower and upper Magpie Bridge: the Caudal Funnel, and the Hall of Impression and nasal opening; see Magpie Bridge.
  • Resting the fire and stopping the tally: stopping the Posterior Heaven martial fire, not stopping the Prior Heaven.
  • “The breathing at mao and you is nothing but the word ‘soft’”: words of Bai Ziqing; see ablution.
  • Purple Court: the central palace.
  • Intercalary remainder: the time of warm nourishing on returning to the root in the lower elixir field.
  • “When the River Chariot is done, go back to sleep; tomorrow morning, connect with the Root of Heaven again”: an old saying.
  • Stopping the fire: no more rising and descending, but never leaving the warm fire; see stopping the fire.
  • Retracting like a tortoise and not rising, the yang light appearing two, three or four times: see the horse-like retraction and stopping the fire.
  • Bright Hall: the space between the two eyebrows; see Celestial Eye.

Putting It into Practice

  1. When the mechanism of the breath arrives, “do not be alarmed, and do not raise too sharp an awareness” — neither startled nor delighted, and do not stare at it too deliberately.
  2. “Stopping the breath is not holding the breath” — the “stopping the breath” of the alchemical books is the mind becoming so still that the breathing naturally becomes fine; it is never holding the breath.
  3. “The breath should be received softly”, “the breathing at mao and you is nothing but the word ‘soft’” — the key to the breathing of the whole Lesser Celestial Circuit is the single word “soft”.
  4. The numbers are a rhythm, not a number of breaths: at each point (compass) the breath takes four breaths, counting silently from one to nine in each breath when rising and from one to six when descending, with mao and you not counted; three hundred and sixty is the number silently counted — “not really three hundred breaths; they are all metaphors”. There are degrees, but they must not be held to rigidly.
  5. “Once refined, you must still know when to stop” — when there are results, know when to stop; holding on when full does harm instead.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Hunranzi”: the Yuan-dynasty Daoist Wang Jie (style name Daoyuan), whose Daoist name was Hunranzi. The “Hunran” of the question-and-answer passages in the previous section is a disciple of Liu Huayang and not the same person as Hunranzi.
  2. “Straight Discussion of the Two Breaths”: the title of a book or chapter; its source has yet to be identified.
  3. “Shouyang”: Wu Chongxu’s Daoist name was “Shouyang”.
  4. “Xiao Zixu”: Xiao Tingzhi, a Daoist of the Song-Yuan period, whose Daoist name was Zixu.
  5. “Ziye”: the name of an alchemist; his life has yet to be identified.
  6. “Completing the limit of three hundred wondrous circuits”: “wondrous circuit” means the Celestial Circuit; three hundred is a round number.
  7. The original discussions in this section are short, and each forms one passage; §3, “On the Counts of Breathing”, has many numbers, so its translation is divided into two paragraphs.
  8. The numbers in §3 come from the Appended Statements of the Book of Changes, part one: “The stalks of Qian are two hundred and sixteen, the stalks of Kun one hundred and forty-four, in all three hundred and sixty, corresponding to the days of the year.” The ancients divined with yarrow stalks, counting them in groups of four (“count them by fours”); an old yang line yields thirty-six stalks (four times nine), an old yin line twenty-four stalks (four times six), and multiplying by six lines gives two hundred and sixteen and one hundred and forty-four. The alchemists borrowed these numbers to fix the rhythm of fire phasing in the Lesser Celestial Circuit.
  9. §3, “the Qian count sums the four counts of six lines”: Jingxuzi’s book quotes this as “sums the four counts of nine lines”, but his interlinear note clearly writes “six yang hours × thirty-six lines = two hundred and sixteen lines” (the “yang hours” here are the six rising points), which agrees with this book’s “six lines”.
  10. Jingxuzi’s explanation under §3 summarizes the meaning of Jingxuzi’s interlinear notes on the Liu Huayang entry in the third part (“Brief Accounts of the Immortal Patriarchs through the Ages”) of his Essentials of Practice in the Celestial-Immortal Lineage of Wu and Liu, and of the Huang Yuanji sayings recorded in its second part; it is not a verbatim quotation. A “step” is a “compass” (point) on the Dharma Wheel diagram; compare the diagram of the six stages of the Dharma Wheel attached to the second section of the ninth part of this book, and Xixinzi’s explanation in the tenth part.

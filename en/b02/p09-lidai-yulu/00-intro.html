Introduction to Part Nine

What this book is

This part is taken from the Collection on Understanding the Dao through the Union of Schools compiled by Ran Daoyuan. Its contents are drawn essentially from The Immortal Lineage of Wu and Liu (Wu Liu xianzong 伍柳仙宗) by Wu Chongxu and Liu Huayang; it is built mainly on the teachings of the Wu-Liu school, while also citing the words of other masters — Patriarch Lü, Zhang Ziyang, Bai Ziqing, Yu Yuwu (Yu Yan), Lu Qianxu, Qiu Changchun, Cao Huanyang and others. Qiu Ling says that it “gathers clear and concise discussions, includes no deep and abstruse texts, and is well ordered in its sections”.

The whole part consists of fifteen short discussions, going from refining the self and returning to emptiness and the first steps of regulating the medicine, all the way through every link of the Lesser Celestial Circuit: gathering, sealing and securing, advancing the fire, ablution, withdrawing the tally, returning to the root, and stopping the fire. The Essentials of Great Accomplishment in the first book is the Wu-Liu school’s “record of practice”; this part is the Wu-Liu school’s “lecture notes”, and the two can be read side by side.

This text is taken from the Collection on Understanding the Dao through the Union of Schools compiled by Ran Daoyuan, and is drawn essentially from The Immortal Lineage of Wu and Liu by Wu Chongxu and Liu Huayang. Although it is built mainly on the teachings of the Wu-Liu school, it also cites the words of other masters; it gathers clear and concise discussions, includes no deep and abstruse texts, and is well ordered in its sections, so I introduce it to readers.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

First refine the self — cut off all ties, have no mind when facing circumstances; then regulate the medicine — when yang is born in the dim and indistinct, concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root; then carry out the Lesser Celestial Circuit in order: nourish warmly with the civil fire, gather and move with the martial fire, never missing the moment in each movement and each stillness — “it starts here and it ends here”; all numbers are metaphors, all scenes are judged by “when the time comes, the spirit knows”, and when the fire is sufficient, it is stopped.

Reading tips

  1. The Discussion on Refining the Self and Returning to Emptiness in the first section is the most worth reading: Wu Chongxu says, “if there is a single thing on your mind, you enter into that one thing and not into the Way”; the work of returning to emptiness “lies only in having no mind when facing circumstances”.
  2. The Wu-Liu school teaches an alchemy for men, and the book often uses “the male organ rising vigorously”, the “outer kidney” and “the outer form subsiding” as markers of fire phasing; we translate according to the original meaning and have added notes at the relevant places.
  3. Some especially sober remarks: “Stopping the breath is not holding the breath”; “the three hundred counts are not really three hundred breaths; they are all metaphors”; “when the fire is first raised, the breath should be received softly … if the martial fire is raised first, the breath within does not flow smoothly”; “Bai Ziqing says: the breathing at mao and you is nothing but the word ‘soft’”.
  4. The original book includes a “Diagram of the Conception and Governing Vessels” and a “Diagram of the Six Stages of the Dharma Wheel”; in line with our established principle, the diagrams are not reproduced for now, and their content is explained in words in the text.

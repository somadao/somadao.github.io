Li Hanxu’s Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven, Annotated · 后天串述

Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven

Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven — the nine levels of entry: gathering the mind, seeking the breath, concentrating the spirit, unfolding the opening, opening the passes, laying the foundation, obtaining the medicine, forming the elixir, refining the self

Safety note (translator): This text speaks of “turning the spirit into intention, leading it out through the Caudal Funnel, breaking through the three passes in one rush and flying up to the Muddy Pellet”, and of “looking upward with the eyes”. These are things the ancients did to follow a movement of breath that had already become full and surged up on its own. Qiu Ling quotes Xixinzi, who says it plainly: many people “talk about the River Chariot while their stray thoughts are not yet cleared and their mind is not yet clear”, practicing in confusion — “how can they hope to succeed?” First do the honest work of gathering the mind, regulating the breath and settling into stillness. If there is no natural movement of breath, do not use the mind to push the breath through the passes. If you experience a swollen or aching head, palpitations or insomnia during practice, stop and relax, and see a doctor if necessary.

About This Chapter

Li Hanxu says that his commentaries on the Daode jing, the Yellow Court Scripture, the Scripture of the Great Cavern and the Rootless Tree all speak of the workings of the Prior Heaven and are not methods for beginners. To climb high one must start low, so he wrote this text specially as a path of entry. He arranges the introductory work in nine levels:

LevelWorkKey point
1Gathering the mindGather the mind inward; take the center as the summit and harmony as the rule
2Seeking the breathFind where the breath stirs at the yinqiao and join it with stillness
3Concentrating the spiritSpirit and breath meet, the true seed is born and lies hidden in the Cinnabar Field — this is called embryonic breathing
4Unfolding the openingSomething surges out of the inner tripod and rises through the Penetrating Vessel to the heart
5Opening the passesWhen the surge is strong, turn the spirit into intention and lead it through the three passes of the spine up to the Muddy Pellet
6Laying the foundationRestrain essence and spirit with the “ren lead” (the one breath produced in the Cave of Emptiness)
7Obtaining the medicineThe lead breath floats up to Kunlun; look upward with the eyes and hold it for a while
8Forming the elixirPress the tongue to the palate; sweet saliva descends; swallow it into the Yellow Court, where it forms the inner elixir
9Refining the selfAmid desires be without desire, in the dust go beyond the dust, forget feelings in the face of circumstances, accumulate merit and virtue

Qiu Ling’s notes add three things. First, order: when starting alchemical practice one must know the sequence; aiming too high is sure to be wasted effort. Second, constitution: a youth, a person in the prime of life and an old person each begin differently. Third, he quotes Xixinzi, who describes the work of beginning by gathering the mind in great detail: when you sit, first quiet the mind, close the mouth and regulate the breath; move the spirit to the yinqiao for a moment, then raise it into the “opening of emptiness”; keep the mind neither too tight nor too loose; and within the dim and obscure state keep a thread of awareness.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

予著《道德》、《黄庭》、《大洞》、《无根》诸注，皆言先天之用，而非初学法门也。夫行远自迩，登高自卑，若不明后天次序，譬诸世上功名，未举茂才孝廉，空想进士翰林也。因作《后天串述》一篇，为入门之路焉。

一收心　二寻气　三凝神

四展窍　五开关　六筑基

七得药　八结丹　九炼己

My commentaries on the Daode jing, the Yellow Court Scripture, the Scripture of the Great Cavern and the Rootless Tree all speak of the workings of the Prior Heaven; they are not methods for beginners. A long journey starts from what is near; a climb to the heights starts from below. Not to understand the order of the Posterior Heaven work is like someone seeking an official career who has not yet passed even the first and second examinations, yet dreams of becoming a metropolitan graduate and a member of the Hanlin Academy. So I have written this Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven as a path of entry.

One, gathering the mind; two, seeking the breath; three, concentrating the spirit; four, unfolding the opening; five, opening the passes; six, laying the foundation; seven, obtaining the medicine; eight, forming the elixir; nine, refining the self.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

丹功入门下手，便要知程序，如不知程序，胡乱去做，或好高骛远，轻视初步浅近功夫，必白费时间而无所成。古人云：“欲登高，必自下，欲陟遐，必自迩”。起首当从初步炼精化气做起。若舍近而图远，如李涵虚氏所谓“未举茂才孝廉，空想进士翰林”，则徒劳无功也。故入门之路，有此九层功法。

When starting alchemical practice, one must know the procedure. If you do not know it and act at random, or aim too high and look down on the simple first steps, you are sure to waste your time and achieve nothing. The ancients said: “To climb high, start from below; to go far, start from what is near.” One should begin with the first stage, refining essence into breath. If you abandon what is near and reach for what is far — like someone who, in Li Hanxu’s words, “has not yet passed the first examinations, yet dreams of becoming a metropolitan graduate and entering the Hanlin Academy” — your effort will be in vain. That is why the path of entry has these nine levels.

§2
Original (Chinese)

太上有言：“贵以贱为本，高以下为基。”后天资补，贱下之道也。贱也者，师所谓说着丑也。下也者，经所谓下而取也。培养丹基，纯以精气为宝。其行功法也，要先收心入内，以中为极，以和为则，以神为体，定也。以意为用，慧也。中是活活泼泼、不见不闻之处；和是专气致柔、抱神以静之功。定中生慧，坐照如如，媾元精而生元气，展窍开关不难也。

The Most High Lord Lao says: “The noble takes the lowly as its root; the high takes the low as its foundation.” Posterior Heaven nourishment and replenishment is this way of the “lowly” and the “low”. The “lowly” is what the teachers mean by “it sounds ugly when said”; the “low” is what the scriptures mean by “taking from below”. In cultivating the foundation of the elixir, essence and breath alone are the treasures. The method of practice is this: first gather the mind and bring it inward, taking the “center” as the highest standard and “harmony” as the rule; take the spirit as the substance — this is “concentration” (ding 定); take the intention as the function — this is “insight” (hui 慧). The “center” is that lively, active place that can be neither seen nor heard; “harmony” is the work of concentrating the breath until it becomes soft, and embracing the spirit in stillness. When insight is born within concentration and you sit illuminating, unmoving in suchness, the original essence unites and gives birth to the original breath, and unfolding the opening and opening the passes will not be difficult.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

后天资补虽是“贱下之道”，但为培养丹基所不可少。丹功略分为上中下三乘，如登楼阁；由下层炼精化气始，至中层炼气化神，到上层炼神还虚。逐层上迁，名为“三迁”。修炼功夫又有童、壮、老三种体质之分：童体精、气、神三宝全，易修易成，一直养性到底，但古今稀少；壮年破体必用筑补之功，方可还成童体，然后养性完功；老年精气枯，下手多费调药（真气）一段功夫，调到有药，采补还元，仍养性完功。

Although Posterior Heaven nourishment and replenishment is “the way of the lowly and the low”, it is indispensable for cultivating the foundation of the elixir. Alchemical practice is roughly divided into three vehicles — lower, middle and upper — like climbing a tower: one begins on the lower floor with refining essence into breath, goes up to the middle floor, refining breath into spirit, and then to the upper floor, refining spirit to return to emptiness. Moving up level by level is called “the three moves”. The practice also distinguishes three kinds of constitution: youth, prime of life and old age. A youth’s three treasures of essence, breath and spirit are complete; such a person cultivates easily and succeeds easily, and can go straight on nourishing the nature to the end — but such people have always been rare. A person in the prime of life whose body has already been “broken” must use the work of laying the foundation and replenishing to be restored to the state of a youth, and then complete the work by nourishing the nature. In old age, essence and breath are depleted; at the start one has to spend an extra stage on “regulating the medicine” (nurturing the true breath) until there is medicine, then gather it, replenish and restore the original breath, and then still complete the work by nourishing the nature.

§3
Original (Chinese)

寻气在阴跷为先。元精者，阴跷一脉逐日生人之元气也。学人采取元精，必寻气之活动处，而以静合之，此之谓神气交。神气交则男女媾精，真种化生。真种者，后天鼎之真气也。后天鼎者，即元神元气交合之所也，一名灵父灵母。此气从鼎中炼出，即宜凝其神，柔其意，以柔制刚，自然入我内鼎。调之和之，煅之炼之，潜伏于丹田之中，呼吸乎虚无之内，是名命蒂，又号胎息。

Seeking the breath begins at the yinqiao. The original essence is the original breath that the yinqiao channel produces each day to nourish us. To gather the original essence, the student must find the place where the breath stirs and join it with stillness; this is called “the meeting of spirit and breath”. When spirit and breath meet, this is what the alchemical books call “man and woman join their essences”, and the true seed is born. The true seed is the true breath in the “Posterior Heaven tripod”. The Posterior Heaven tripod is the place where the original spirit and the original breath unite; it is also called “the spiritual father and spiritual mother”. Once this breath has been refined out of the tripod, you should concentrate the spirit and soften the intention, overcoming the hard with the soft, and it will naturally enter my “inner tripod”. Harmonise it and temper it, forge it and refine it, let it lie hidden in the Cinnabar Field and breathe within emptiness: this is called the “stalk of life”, and also “embryonic breathing”.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

30年代出版的洗心子著《明道语录》一书，对李涵虚氏著《三车秘旨》所载收心法下手功夫加以解释，可作此篇注脚。洗心子云：“行功难入恍惚杳冥者，查系着相，妄念未净所致，道之节序亦未明了。论初下手之功，简言之，一念不生，静入恍惚杳冥，以至钻杳冥，即为初还虚也；否则非正法”。又云：“每入坐，先静心，次缄口，次调息。息如何调？心静则气平，不调之调也。鼻息若是和平，不急不缓不粗，则闭目内观，将神移至肾根之下阴跷一脉（即阴囊后、谷道前之间）。片时，即将心神提上虚无窍内。窍在何处？即脐之后、腰之前、心之下、肾之上，中间一带，不可拘执，即是言其大概情形。观照时，在此一带停神安息，当以自然为主。心不可太严，太严则炎，即是用力着相，务必顺其自然之象。心不可太散，太散则冷，即是昏迷落空，务必守其自然之景。始终的妙用，内息匀称，勿助勿忘，心如太虚太空，即恍惚杳冥也。但恍惚杳冥中有个知觉，知其中有息，相依相随，则不虚空（此即是真空不空，自此以下，功夫又进一步）。如常在不虚不空之间，则加静之又静，清而又清，一切放下，全体皆忘，心神默默，气息绵绵，皆入于杳冥之中，此之谓钻杳冥。钻杳冥即虚空藏心，昏默息神，即是初还虚之功已到。此杳冥中有气，一神独觉，即真息也。真息发现，有凭证，即熏心酥痒，极其愉快。此时心神勿贪着其乐，须要安定在腔子里虚无窍内。如此积累久之，则命蒂生，而阳气长，乃可展窍开关，始行第一河车之功也。每查同志行功，多系妄念未净，心亦未清，便言河车，不依程序，不遵规矩，混乱行来，焉望成就？”

此段功夫主要是凝神调息，先神注阴跷（即会阴）片刻，寻气于此，再移神下丹田，顺其自然，守其自然，才能恍惚杳冥。此乃道门西派李氏功法之特点。张紫阳《八脉经》云：阴跷“上通泥丸，下透涌泉，倘能知此，使真气聚散，皆从此关窍，则天门常开，地户永闭，尻脉周流于一身，贯通上下，和气自然上朝，阳长阴消，水中火发，雪里花开，所谓’天根月窟闲来往，三十六宫都是春’。得之者身体轻健，容衰返壮，昏昏默默，如醉如痴，此其验也”。

The book Recorded Sayings on Understanding the Dao (Mingdao yulu 明道语录) by Xixinzi, published in the 1930s, explains the “method of beginning by gathering the mind” set out in Li Hanxu’s Secret Principles of the Three Carts, and can serve as a footnote to this text. Xixinzi says: “Those who find it hard to enter the dim and obscure state in their practice have, on examination, all become attached to forms, and their stray thoughts are not yet cleared; nor have they understood the stages of the Way. As for the first work at the outset, put simply: let not a single thought arise and quietly enter the dim and obscure, until you ‘bore into the obscure’ — this is the first return to emptiness. Anything else is not the right method.”

He also says: “Each time you sit, first quiet the mind, then close the mouth, then regulate the breath. How is the breath regulated? When the mind is quiet, the breath is even of itself — this is regulating without regulating. When the breath through the nose is even — neither hurried nor slow nor coarse — close the eyes and look inward, and move the spirit to the yinqiao channel below the root of the kidneys (that is, the point between the scrotum and the anus). After a moment, raise the mind and spirit into the ‘opening of emptiness’. Where is this opening? Behind the navel, in front of the waist, below the heart and above the kidneys — the area in between. Do not cling to a precise spot; this only describes it roughly. While contemplating, rest the spirit and settle the breath in this area, taking naturalness as the main thing. The mind must not be too tight: too tight and it becomes heated — that is, using force and clinging to form; you must follow its natural appearance. The mind must not be too loose: too loose and it becomes cold — that is, falling into a stupor and emptiness; you must keep to its natural scene. The wonderful function from beginning to end is this: the inner breath is even, neither helped along nor forgotten, and the mind is like the great void and the great emptiness — that is the dim and obscure state. But within the dim and obscure there must be a thread of awareness, knowing that there is breath in it, mind and breath relying on and following each other; then it is not empty (this is ‘true emptiness that is not empty’; from here on the work goes a step further). If you remain constantly between not-void and not-empty, add stillness upon stillness, clarity upon clarity; let everything go and forget the whole body; mind and spirit silent, breath continuous and fine, all enter the obscure — this is called ‘boring into the obscure’. Boring into the obscure means hiding the mind in emptiness and stilling the spirit in silent darkness; this means the first return to emptiness has been reached. In this obscurity there is breath, and a single spirit alone is aware of it: that is the true breath. When the true breath appears, there is a sign: the heart feels as if suffused with warmth, tingling and itching, extremely pleasant. At this point the mind and spirit must not cling to the pleasure; they must stay settled in the opening of emptiness within the body. When this has accumulated for a long time, the stalk of life is born and the yang breath grows; only then can one unfold the opening and open the passes, and begin the work of the first River Chariot. Every time I look at how my fellow practitioners work, most of them talk about the River Chariot while their stray thoughts are not yet cleared and their mind is not yet clear; they do not follow the procedure or keep to the rules, but practice in confusion — how can they hope to succeed?”

The work in this passage is mainly concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath: first fix the spirit on the yinqiao (that is, the perineum) for a moment, seeking the breath there; then move the spirit to the lower Cinnabar Field, follow what is natural and keep to what is natural, and only then can one enter the dim and obscure state. This is a distinctive feature of the method of Li of the Western school. Zhang Ziyang’s Scripture of the Eight Vessels (Bamai jing 八脉经) says of the yinqiao: “Above it connects with the Muddy Pellet; below it reaches the Bubbling Spring. If you understand this and let the true breath gather and disperse entirely through this pass, the Gate of Heaven will always be open and the Door of Earth forever closed; the sacral vessel will circulate through the whole body, connecting top and bottom, the harmonious breath will rise of itself to pay homage above, yang will grow and yin will wane, fire will spring from water and flowers bloom in the snow — as the poem says, ‘Coming and going at leisure between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon, all thirty-six palaces are spring.' Those who obtain it become light and strong in body, their declining looks become vigorous again, and they are dim and silent, as if drunk or dazed: this is its proof.”

§4
Original (Chinese)

忽然而内鼎之间，冲出一物，跳跳跃跃，嘘嘘喷喷，直由冲脉上至心府，即展窍时也。候其冲突有力时，乃变神为意，引出尾闾，一撞三关，飞上泥丸，即开关也。

Suddenly something surges out of the inner tripod, leaping and bounding, hissing and spurting, and rises straight up the Penetrating Vessel to the heart: this is the time of “unfolding the opening”. Wait until its thrust is strong, then turn the spirit into intention, lead it out through the Caudal Funnel, break through the three passes in one rush and fly up to the Muddy Pellet: this is “opening the passes”.

§5
Original (Chinese)

关窍既开，乃行养己之功，而谈筑基之道。筑基者，采彼气血，补我精神。精神虽壮，又恐动摇，于是以壬铅制之。壬铅者，二气媾而生者也。原夫坎宫之气，地气也。离宫之气，天气也。天地交合之时，混混沌沌，氤氤氲氲，结为虚无窟子。虚无窟中，旋产一气，即以此气为壬铅，此得药时也。

Once the passes and openings are open, one does the work of “nourishing the self” and speaks of the way of laying the foundation. Laying the foundation means “gathering that one’s breath and blood to replenish my spirit”. Although the spirit becomes strong, one fears it may waver, so one restrains it with the “ren lead”. The ren lead is born from the union of two breaths. The breath of the Kan palace is the breath of Earth; the breath of the Li palace is the breath of Heaven. When Heaven and Earth unite, all is chaotic and undifferentiated, dense and misty, and they form a “Cave of Emptiness”. In the Cave of Emptiness a breath is produced at once; this breath is taken as the ren lead. This is the time of “obtaining the medicine”.

§6
Original (Chinese)

铅之体有气无质，以故清而上浮，至昆仑时，要以目光上视，神气相息于顶中。凝住一时，阳极阴生，始以舌倒抵上腭，鼻息要匀。抵腭久之，乃有美津降下。寒泉滴滴，虽不甚多，然一吞下重楼，以意送回黄庭。却又奇怪，发生如澎湃一般。始知大士甘露，原不可多得也。降入黄庭，结为内丹。

The substance of the lead is breath without physical form, so it is clear and floats upward. When it reaches Kunlun (the crown of the head), look upward with the eyes so that spirit and breath come to rest together in the crown. Hold it for a while; when yang reaches its peak, yin is born. Only then turn the tongue back against the upper palate, keeping the breath through the nose even. After the tongue has pressed against the palate for a long time, sweet saliva comes down, drop by drop like a cold spring. Though it is not much, once it is swallowed down the Twelve-Storied Tower and sent back to the Yellow Court with the intention, strangely it surges up like a rushing tide. Only then do you realise that the Sweet Dew of the Bodhisattva is truly not something one obtains often. (The dew) descends into the Yellow Court and forms the inner elixir.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4–§6
Original note (Chinese)

下田有气上冲心府，即展窍时也，乃可引气过尾闾（在脊椎骨最下段），破夹脊（在背中，与心相对）、玉枕（在后头部两侧风池穴之间）三关而上泥丸（在头顶百会穴内），亦即小周天。此时须目光上视昆仑（即头顶），舌舐上腭，神气在顶中凝住一时，气化为液，吞下重楼（喉管），降落黄庭（此处指虚无窟子、内鼎，下丹田之上），名为玉液丹头，宝之裕之，是为筑基。筑基既久，积累益深，乃得药结丹。以后功夫，须要绵绵不绝，尽养丹之妙。

When breath rises from the lower field and surges up to the heart, it is the time of unfolding the opening. Only then may one lead the breath through the Caudal Funnel (at the lowest part of the spine), break through the three passes of the Spine-Straddling Pass (in the middle of the back, opposite the heart) and the Jade Pillow (at the back of the head, between the two fengchi points), and rise to the Muddy Pellet (inside the baihui point at the crown) — which is also the Lesser Celestial Circuit. At this time one must look upward with the eyes towards Kunlun (the crown of the head) and lick the upper palate with the tongue; spirit and breath are held together in the crown for a while, the breath turns into liquid, which is swallowed down the Twelve-Storied Tower (the throat) and descends into the Yellow Court (here meaning the Cave of Emptiness, the inner tripod, above the lower Cinnabar Field). It is called the “jade-liquid elixir head”; treasure it and let it grow abundant — this is laying the foundation. When the foundation has been laid for a long time and the accumulation grows deeper, one obtains the medicine and forms the elixir. The work after that must be continuous and unbroken, making the most of the wonderful function of nourishing the elixir.

§7
Original (Chinese)

以后则在欲无欲，在尘出尘，对境忘情，炼铅伏汞，赶退三尸五贼，消磨六欲七情，骨气俱是金精，肌肤皆成玉质。则内发天机，外合人事。毋意毋必，毋固毋我，积功累德，炼气养神，物来事至，心境自放光明。即景会心，慧剑劈开尘障。此时抱璞守贞，防微虑险，天人合发，万物定基，精神永固，一气还虚。此炼己之功完备矣。

From then on, one must be amid desires yet without desire, in the dust of the world yet beyond it, forgetting feelings in the face of circumstances; refine the lead and subdue the mercury, drive out the Three Corpses and the Five Thieves, wear away the six desires and seven emotions, until bones and breath have all become golden essence and flesh and skin have all become jade. Then within, the workings of Heaven are set in motion, and without, one is in accord with human affairs. Without speculation, without dogmatism, without obstinacy, without self, one accumulates merit and virtue, refines the breath and nourishes the spirit; when things come and events arise, the mind naturally radiates light. Grasping with the heart whatever is before one’s eyes, one cleaves the obstacles of the world with the sword of wisdom. At this time one must preserve one’s original simplicity and guard one’s integrity, guard against the smallest dangers; Heaven and humanity act together, all things are firmly founded, the spirit is forever secure, and the one breath returns to emptiness. With this, the work of refining the self is complete.

§8
Original (Chinese)

入门者，必先收心、寻气、凝神、展窍、开关、筑基、得药、结丹、炼己。此九层功夫，乃为入道之门。既已入门，阴阳归一，无往不复，穷理尽性，以至于命，自有为而入无为，由勉强而抵自然，未有金丹不成者矣。

Those who enter the gate must first gather the mind, seek the breath, concentrate the spirit, unfold the opening, open the passes, lay the foundation, obtain the medicine, form the elixir and refine the self. These nine levels of work are the gate into the Way. Once one has entered the gate, yin and yang return to oneness and there is nothing that does not return to its origin; one exhausts the principles of things and fully realises one’s nature, until one reaches one’s destiny; from action one enters non-action, from effort one arrives at naturalness — and there has never been anyone in whom the Golden Elixir was not then accomplished.

Key Terms

  • Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven: an account that strings the Posterior Heaven work of entry into an ordered sequence. The Classic of Ending (Wenzhong jing 文终经) is one of the Thirteen Scriptures of the Most High.
  • The nine levels of work: gathering the mind, seeking the breath, concentrating the spirit, unfolding the opening, opening the passes, laying the foundation, obtaining the medicine, forming the elixir, refining the self.
  • The “lowly” and the “low”: the way of Posterior Heaven nourishment and replenishment — “it sounds ugly when said”, “taking from below”.
  • Centre and harmony: the center is the lively place that can be neither seen nor heard; harmony is the work of concentrating the breath until it becomes soft and embracing the spirit in stillness.
  • Concentration and insight: taking the spirit as substance is concentration; taking the intention as function is insight. See cessation and contemplation.
  • Yinqiao: the place where the Western school seeks the breath; Qiu Ling identifies it with the perineum (between the scrotum and the anus). See Perineum.
  • The meeting of spirit and breath: finding where the breath stirs and joining it with stillness.
  • Posterior Heaven tripod, spiritual father and spiritual mother: the place where the original spirit and the original breath unite.
  • Inner tripod, Cave of Emptiness, opening of emptiness: key places in the Western school — behind the navel, in front of the waist, below the heart and above the kidneys; not to be fixed to a precise spot.
  • Stalk of life, embryonic breathing: the true breath lying hidden in the Cinnabar Field and breathing within emptiness. See True Breath.
  • Unfolding the opening: the breath surging up the Penetrating Vessel to the heart. Opening the passes: leading the breath through the three passes up to the Muddy Pellet. See opening the passes and Three Passes.
  • Penetrating Vessel: one of the eight extraordinary vessels; it starts in the lower abdomen and runs up the front of the trunk.
  • Ren lead: the one breath produced in the Cave of Emptiness when the two breaths of Heaven and Earth unite. See One Breath of Prior Heaven.
  • Breath of the Kan palace, breath of the Li palace: the breath of Earth and the breath of Heaven. See conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Kunlun: the crown of the head.
  • Jade-liquid elixir head: breath turned into liquid that descends into the Yellow Court; treasuring it and letting it grow abundant is laying the foundation.
  • Three Corpses and Five Thieves, six desires and seven emotions: the yin pathogenic forces and the passions within the body.
  • “Without speculation, without dogmatism, without obstinacy, without self”: the “four things Confucius was free of” (Analects, “Zihan”).
  • The three moves: moving up level by level from refining essence into breath, to refining breath into spirit, to refining spirit to return to emptiness. See the three transmutations.
  • Three constitutions — youth, prime of life, old age: a youth can go straight on nourishing the nature; a person in the prime of life must first lay the foundation and replenish; an old person must spend extra work on regulating the medicine.
  • Boring into the obscure, the first return to emptiness: Xixinzi’s terms. See the dim and obscure.
  • True emptiness that is not empty: within the dim and obscure there is a thread of awareness, knowing that there is breath in it.
  • The mind neither too tight nor too loose: too tight and it becomes heated (using force, clinging to form); too loose and it becomes cold (falling into stupor and emptiness). See torpor and distraction.
  • Root of Heaven and Cave of the Moon: words from a poem by Shao Yong, a metaphor for the coming and going of yin and yang.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Know the order first. None of the nine levels may be skipped. Most people need — and should — work only on the first two or three: gathering the mind, seeking the breath (in practice, becoming aware of the body in stillness) and concentrating the spirit.
  2. Xixinzi’s three steps for sitting down: first quiet the mind, then close the mouth, then regulate the breath. “When the mind is quiet, the breath is even of itself — this is regulating without regulating.” You do not need to control the breath; once the mind is quiet, the breath becomes calm on its own.
  3. “Not too tight, not too loose” is the whole of fire control. Too much effort and you become heated (clinging to form); too relaxed and you become cold (falling into stupor and emptiness). If you feel tense, ease off a little; if you feel dull, gather your attention a little.
  4. Within the dim state there must be awareness. Even when you are so still that everything feels vague, still know clearly that the breath is there — “true emptiness that is not empty”. This bit of clarity is what separates entering stillness from dozing off.
  5. When pleasant sensations come, do not crave them. “At this point the mind and spirit must not cling to the pleasure” — when good feelings arise, rest in them; do not chase them and do not try to keep them.
  6. Refining the self happens in daily life: “forgetting feelings in the face of circumstances”, “when things come and events arise, the mind naturally radiates light”. Beyond sitting practice, keeping the mind calm while dealing with people and doing your work is the real practice.

Translator's Notes

  1. In the original, Li Hanxu compares the stages to the imperial examinations: the maocai (another name for the xiucai, the first degree, renamed under the Eastern Han to avoid the personal name of Emperor Guangwu, Liu Xiu) and the xiaolian (in the Ming and Qing, the provincial graduate, juren), before the metropolitan graduate (jinshi) and the Hanlin Academy.
  2. “The noble takes the lowly as its root; the high takes the low as its foundation”: Daode jing, chapter 39.
  3. “Man and woman join their essences”, “gathering that one’s breath and blood to replenish my spirit”: metaphors commonly used in alchemical books. Qiu Ling states clearly in his preface that wherever the texts in this book originally speak of “grafting”, he explains them according to the “pure and quiet” method — “a single person’s body also has its own yin and yang”. In the pure-and-quiet reading, “man and woman join their essences” is a metaphor for the meeting of spirit and breath (Li and Kan) within the body; “that one” means the Posterior Heaven essence and breath of the Kan palace within the body, and “I” means the original spirit of the Li palace. The translation follows this principle, keeping the original metaphors in quotation marks.
  4. “The Sweet Dew of the Bodhisattva”: the Bodhisattva is Guanyin (Avalokiteśvara), who is often depicted holding a vase of sweet dew.
  5. “Strangely it surges up like a rushing tide”: describes the strong reaction inside the body after the saliva is swallowed.
  6. Zhang Ziyang’s Scripture of the Eight Vessels: attributed to Zhang Boduan and quoted by Li Shizhen in his Study of the Eight Extraordinary Vessels (Qijing bamai kao 奇经八脉考). In the quotation, the “sacral vessel” refers to the region of the tailbone.
  7. “Coming and going at leisure between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon, all thirty-six palaces are spring”: a line from Shao Yong’s poem “Chanting on Observing Things” (Guanwu yin 观物吟).
  8. Qiu Ling printed the original text as one long passage; we have divided it into eight short sections according to the nine levels of work. Qiu Ling’s notes originally came at the end of the text; we have placed them after the sections they explain.
  9. Xixinzi’s Recorded Sayings on Understanding the Dao was published in the 1930s; little is known about the author. The passage here is quoted in Qiu Ling’s notes.

Li Hanxu’s Secret Principles of the Three Carts, Annotated · 第二件河车

The Second River Chariot

The Second River Chariot — the work of moving the essence: the jade-liquid River Chariot; fixing the spirit on the heels

Safety note (translator): The original text of this section speaks of “cooking fiercely and refining urgently”, and, when taking the jade liquid, of “holding the breath in the nose, clenching the jaws, pressing both hands back against the seat, the head and face turned up towards the roof beam … holding the breath through the nose without letting it out”. This is a forceful method of the ancients; please do not imitate the breath-holding or the forceful raising of the head. Holding the breath can cause dizziness, chest tightness and a sudden rise in blood pressure, and is especially dangerous for people with high blood pressure, heart disease, glaucoma or problems of the neck. When there is plenty of saliva in the mouth, simply swallow it naturally and easily.

About This Chapter

Once the first River Chariot has obtained the lesser medicine and formed the elixir head, one moves on to the second — the jade-liquid River Chariot that moves the “essence”. Its purpose is “to draw the lead of Kan, control the mercury of Li and refine one’s own nature”.

The process is as follows:

  1. Guarding the Cinnabar Field firmly: from then on the inner breath is continuous, natural and at ease, and one guards the Cinnabar Field firmly. Every morning at the chen hour, sitting or lying down, the elixir rises to the heart like a soft ball of cotton; one must still draw it back into emptiness, until it is “dim and without trace”, so that it is not lost — “when the spirit returns into the body, the breath returns of itself”.
  2. Fixing the spirit on the heels: after a long time the Cinnabar Field is like spring water first rising, flowing and rippling; keep to the natural inner breath and cook it, and the water turns into warm breath that flows down the inner thighs to the Bubbling Springs. At this point fix the spirit on the two heels and let the true breath follow — what the Zhuangzi calls “the breath of the true man comes from his heels”.
  3. The Yellow River rolling back: when the Bubbling Springs are settled and still, bring the mind back to the Caudal Funnel and wait silently. Suddenly you feel something arrive at the Caudal Funnel — like a ball of cotton, like a bun, like a lump of breath — heavy and hard to move; then adjust the inner breath and refine it fiercely with single-minded attention, and a stream of hot liquid passes out of the Caudal Funnel, through the lumbar spine and up to the Muddy Pellet.
  4. Taking the jade liquid: in the Muddy Pellet there is a roaring sound of water, which after a long time subsides, and the spirit rests within it. Then lick the upper palate with the tongue; the golden liquid fills the tongue; swallow it down the Twelve-Storied Tower, it pours into the Flowery Pool (between the two breasts), falls into the Yellow Court, and the fire of the heart becomes calm and settled.

Qiu Ling’s notes point out the difference between this cart and the first: the first begins at the perineum and goes on to unfolding the opening and opening the passes; this one fixes the spirit on the heels and then returns to the Caudal Funnel. He also specially quotes Chen Yingning’s remark that in the three River Chariots “there are inevitably some exaggerated descriptions and inappropriate metaphors; readers should please not cling to the literal wording” — which probably refers to sentences such as “a sound like wind and thunder” and “a roaring sound of water in the palace of the Muddy Pellet”.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

运精功夫，所以抽坎铅，制离汞，炼己性也。前此运气日久，得了小药，结了丹头，以后绵绵内息，天然自在，固守丹田。每早辰间，清坐清卧，其丹如一团软绵，升于心府，仍要收回虚中，杳然无影，方不走失。诀曰：“神返身中气自还。”正此时也。

The work of moving the essence is for drawing the lead of Kan, controlling the mercury of Li and refining one’s own nature. Earlier, after moving the breath for a long time, one obtained the lesser medicine and formed the elixir head; from then on the inner breath is continuous, natural and at ease, and one guards the Cinnabar Field firmly. Every morning at the chen hour, sitting or lying quietly, the elixir rises to the heart like a soft ball of cotton; one must still draw it back into emptiness, until it is dim and without trace, so that it is not lost. The formula says: “When the spirit returns into the body, the breath returns of itself.” This is exactly the time.

§2
Original (Chinese)

怀抱日深，忽然间丹田如春水初生，溶溶漾漾，即守自然之内息，烹之炼之，其水忽化为热气，由两胯内边流至涌泉，须要神注两踵，真息随之，此所谓真人之息以踵也。如此片时，涌泉定静，即将心返尾闾，默默守候，忽觉有物来尾闾间，似绵陀，似馒头，似气块，沉滞难行，就要调停内息，专心一志，猛烹急炼。乃有一股热汤，透出尾闾，徐徐过腰脊，滔滔上泥丸，方谓之黄河倒卷，漕溪逆运。

As one embraces it more deeply day by day, suddenly the Cinnabar Field is like spring water first rising, flowing and rippling. Then keep to the natural inner breath, cook it and refine it, and the water suddenly turns into warm breath, flowing down the inner sides of the thighs to the Bubbling Springs. At this point the spirit must be fixed on the two heels, and the true breath follows it — this is what is called “the breath of the true man comes from his heels”. After a while like this, when the Bubbling Springs are settled and still, bring the mind back to the Caudal Funnel and wait silently. Suddenly you feel something arrive at the Caudal Funnel — like a ball of cotton, like a bun, like a lump of breath — heavy and hard to move. Then adjust the inner breath, with single-minded attention, cook fiercely and refine urgently. A stream of hot liquid then passes out of the Caudal Funnel, slowly through the lumbar spine, and surges up to the Muddy Pellet. Only this is called “the Yellow River rolling back, the Caoxi stream flowing against the current”.

§3
Original (Chinese)

此等河车，《大洞经》所云：“勒精卫泥丸”。吕祖所云：“搬精入上宫，不与运气同”也。泥丸宫中，水声震响，久之而水声止息，神即休于其中，持守片时，乃以舌倒舐上腭，鼻中忍气，牙关紧闭，两手反抵坐榻，头面仰对空梁，候他金液满舌，其鼻息忍而不播，伊乃咽了一声，流入气管，降下重楼十二阶梯，神水灌注华池矣。

This kind of River Chariot is what the Scripture of the Great Cavern calls “reining in the essence to guard the Muddy Pellet”, and what Patriarch Lü calls “carrying the essence into the upper palace, not the same as moving the breath”. In the palace of the Muddy Pellet there is a roaring sound of water; after a long time the sound subsides, and the spirit rests within it. Hold this for a while, then turn the tongue back to lick the upper palate, hold the breath in the nose, clench the jaws, press both hands back against the seat, turn the head and face up towards the roof beam, and wait until the golden liquid fills the tongue; with the breath through the nose held and not let out, it goes down with a gulp, descends the twelve steps of the Twelve-Storied Tower, and the divine water pours into the Flowery Pool.

§4
Original (Chinese)

这个华池，人多不知，或言舌胎下，或言下丹田，皆非也。此华池在人两乳中间，名曰上气海，与玄膺隔一层耳。白玉蟾云：“华池正在气海内”是也。水满华池，走而不守，至于绛宫，心地清凉，落于黄庭，心火泰定，此之谓抽铅制汞，牵虎降龙。既未两卦，周流不息，即玉液炼己之事也。

Few people know this Flowery Pool. Some say it is under the root of the tongue, some say it is the lower Cinnabar Field; both are wrong. This Flowery Pool is between a person’s two breasts; it is called the “upper Sea of Breath”, separated from the Mysterious Breast by only one layer. Bai Yuchan’s “the Flowery Pool is right within the Sea of Breath” refers to this. When the water fills the Flowery Pool, it flows on without stopping; reaching the Crimson Palace, the ground of the mind becomes clear and cool; falling into the Yellow Court, the fire of the heart becomes calm and settled. This is called “drawing the lead to control the mercury, leading the tiger to subdue the dragon”. The two hexagrams Already Across and Not Yet Across circulate without ceasing — this is the business of refining the self with the jade liquid.

§5
Original (Chinese)

但此玉液，不能日日常有，须加前头运气功夫，运之数次，乃有一次。若做到玉液长来之时，则黄中通理，皮肤润泽，心君闲逸，性体光明，对境忘情，在欲出欲，随缘度日，在尘离尘，真意坚牢，剑锋犀利，圆陀陀，光灼灼，赤洒洒，亮铮铮，此炼己纯熟时也。于是第三车功夫，又听下文分解。

But this jade liquid cannot be there every day; one must add the earlier work of moving the breath, and only after moving it several times does it come once. If one reaches the point where the jade liquid comes constantly, then “the yellow within penetrates to the principle”, the skin becomes moist and smooth, the lord of the heart is at leisure, the substance of the nature is bright; one forgets feelings in the face of circumstances, is amid desires yet beyond desire, passes one’s days according to conditions, is in the dust yet apart from the dust; the true intention is firm and solid, the sword’s edge keen and sharp — perfectly round, blazing with light, utterly bare, gleaming bright. This is the time when refining the self has become thoroughly practiced. As for the work of the third cart, please listen to the explanation below.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

第二件河车，即玉液河车，运精功夫。前此第一件河车下手功夫，以闭目内视阴跷（会阴）片刻，始移神脐后下田而至展窍开关为其特色，则此第二河车以随机守内而神注两踵片时，继应机而返尾闾，猛烹急炼，随之过腰脊而上泥丸为其特色。此均西派功夫特征，异于他功。

第一河车已结丹头，第二河车则有丹感。丹者，不过一团凝结之真气而已。第一河车有物从下田升于心府，第二河车则有物来到尾闾，似气块或馒头。此皆行功内景，标志两个河车相异之处。

《庄子》云：“众人之息以喉，真人之息以踵”。此其息深深之义。第二河车运用此义，而神注两踵，真息随之，直到涌泉静定。盖神息相依，息恋神而住，神依息而留，神既注两踵，遂得息之深深也。《道窍谈》曰：“踵也者，相接不断，绵绵若存也。气彻涌泉，往来不绝。吾师云，以内息踵外息，以外息踵内息，以息息踵息息，此即以踵之妙也”。

玉液河车为更进上一层功夫，服食玉液之秘法，文中详矣！但玉液不能日日常有，运之数次乃有一次；若做到玉液长来，则功夫又进一层。此为经验之谈。

陈樱宁氏于此书卷首“读者须知”中指出：“三件河车文中，未免有形容太过及譬喻不当之处，读者幸勿拘泥于字面而曲为之说”。陈氏所指似是第一件河车一节中“忽于丹田中突出一物，有声如风雷之响”及第二件河车节中“泥丸宫中水声震响”等句。

The second River Chariot is the jade-liquid River Chariot, the work of moving the essence. The starting work of the earlier first River Chariot is characterised by closing the eyes and looking inward at the yinqiao (the perineum) for a moment, then moving the spirit to the lower field behind the navel, until the opening unfolds and the passes open. This second River Chariot, then, is characterised by guarding within as the occasion arises and fixing the spirit on the two heels for a moment, then, as the moment requires, returning to the Caudal Funnel, cooking fiercely and refining urgently, and following it through the lumbar spine up to the Muddy Pellet. These are all features of the work of the Western school, different from other methods.

The first River Chariot has already formed the elixir head; in the second River Chariot there is a “sensation of the elixir”. The elixir is nothing but a ball of congealed true breath. In the first River Chariot something rises from the lower field to the heart; in the second River Chariot something arrives at the Caudal Funnel, like a lump of breath or a bun. These are the inner scenes of the practice, marking the difference between the two River Chariots.

The Zhuangzi says: “The breath of ordinary people comes from their throats; the breath of the true man comes from his heels.” This is the meaning of the breath being deep and deep. The second River Chariot uses this meaning: the spirit is fixed on the two heels and the true breath follows, until the Bubbling Springs are settled and still. For spirit and breath rely on each other: the breath lingers with the spirit and stays, the spirit depends on the breath and remains; once the spirit is fixed on the two heels, the breath becomes deep and deep. Talks on the Openings of the Dao says: “‘Heels’ (zhong 踵) means continuing without break, barely there and yet always present. The breath penetrates to the Bubbling Springs, coming and going without ceasing. My teacher said: let the inner breath follow on the heels of the outer breath, let the outer breath follow on the heels of the inner breath, let each breath follow on the heels of each breath — this is the wonder of ‘from the heels’.”

The jade-liquid River Chariot is work of a higher level still; the secret method of taking the jade liquid is described in detail in the text! But the jade liquid cannot be there every day; only after moving it several times does it come once. If one reaches the point where the jade liquid comes constantly, the work has advanced another level. These are words from experience.

In the “Notes for readers” at the head of this book, Mr Chen Yingning points out: “In the text on the three River Chariots there are inevitably some exaggerated descriptions and inappropriate metaphors; readers should please not cling to the literal wording and twist it into explanations.” What Mr Chen refers to seems to be sentences such as “suddenly something bursts out of the Cinnabar Field, with a sound like wind and thunder” in the section on the first River Chariot and “a roaring sound of water in the palace of the Muddy Pellet” in the section on the second River Chariot.

Key Terms

  • Moving the essence, jade-liquid River Chariot: the second River Chariot. See Celestial Circuit and Sweet Dew.
  • Drawing the lead of Kan, controlling the mercury of Li: taking the true yang within Kan to control the yin within Li. See Lead and Mercury and conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Refining one’s own nature: see refining the self.
  • “When the spirit returns into the body, the breath returns of itself”: words of the Celestial Master Zhang Xujing (see the note to the fifth poem in the appendix of the first book).
  • “The breath of the true man comes from his heels”: Zhuangzi, “The Great Ancestral Teacher”; Qiu Ling glosses it as “the breath deep and deep”. See True Breath.
  • The two heels, the Bubbling Springs: the heels and the soles of the feet.
  • “The Yellow River rolling back, the Caoxi stream flowing against the current”: metaphors for the breath going against the current from the Caudal Funnel up to the Muddy Pellet.
  • “Reining in the essence to guard the Muddy Pellet”: words from the Scripture of the Great Cavern.
  • “Carrying the essence into the upper palace”: words of Patriarch Lü.
  • Flowery Pool: in this section Li Hanxu places it between the two breasts and calls it the “upper Sea of Breath”, unlike the usual Flowery Pool in the mouth. See Breast Stream.
  • The two hexagrams Already Across and Not Yet Across: metaphors for the back-and-forth of water and fire.
  • “The yellow within penetrates to the principle”: from the “Wenyan” commentary on the hexagram Kun in the Book of Changes; a metaphor for inner openness.
  • “Perfectly round, blazing with light”: describing the original nature as complete and bright.
  • Sensation of the elixir: the feeling brought by a ball of congealed true breath.
  • The wonder of “from the heels”: letting the inner breath follow on the heels of the outer breath, and the outer breath on the heels of the inner breath.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “The elixir rises to the heart like a soft ball of cotton; one must still draw it back into emptiness” — when a sensation comes, draw it back; do not run after it. This attitude runs through the whole text.
  2. “The breath of the true man comes from his heels” is worth borrowing: breathe deeply and slowly, relaxed; you can occasionally let the attention sink to the soles of the feet, so that the whole body settles. Many people can feel this way of relaxing.
  3. The jade liquid “comes once only after moving it several times” — there is no need to expect sweet dew in the mouth every time you sit. If it comes, swallow it; if not, that is fine.
  4. Chen Yingning’s caution applies to all the vivid descriptions in this text: “a sound like wind and thunder”, “a roaring sound of water” and so on are descriptions, not standards. Do not use them to check whether you have them or not.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Holding the breath in the nose … with the breath through the nose held and not let out”: “let out” (bo 播) means to disperse or exhale. This is a method the ancients used when taking the jade liquid; as explained in the safety note at the head of the chapter, we do not recommend imitating it.
  2. “Flowery Pool”: alchemical books usually mean the area under the tongue in the mouth (see the glossary). Here Li Hanxu gives a different account, placing it between the two breasts as the “upper Sea of Breath”, that is, around the danzhong point. Both views exist; we translate the original as it stands.
  3. “The two hexagrams Already Across and Not Yet Across”: Already Across (water above fire) and Not Yet Across (fire above water), which alchemical books use as metaphors for the exchange of water and fire (kidneys and heart).
  4. “Chen Yingning”: the original book misprints his name with the character 樱 instead of 撄.
  5. The original text of this section is one passage; we have divided it into five short sections.

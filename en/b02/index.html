Essentials for Entering Alchemical Practice

丹功入门指要 · Edited and annotated by Qiu Ling · Guangdong Science and Technology Press · 1991

The sister volume to Selected Ancient Chinese Qigong Texts, Annotated, compiled by Qiu Ling in Hong Kong in 1988. It is built mainly on the three introductory works of Li Hanxu of the Western school (the Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven, the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind and the Secret Principles of the Three Carts) and on Zhang Sanfeng’s Plain and Simple Words on the Dao and Straight Talk on the Mysterious Pivot, together with famous short classics such as Master Cui’s Mirror for Compounding the Medicine, Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter and Hundred-Character Stele, sayings of alchemists through the ages, Xixinzi’s answers to students, Xiao Tianshi’s brief account of the schools of alchemy, and Xu Zhenyi’s Quiet Sitting for Health as an appendix. Its subject is "entering": the order in which to begin, the right measure in gathering the mind and regulating the breath, and the difficulties commonly met in practice.

How to read

  1. Every chapter follows the same order: About This Chapter → Original Text and Translation → Key Terms → Putting It into Practice → Translator's Notes. The classical Chinese original is folded away; you can read the translation alone and follow everything.
  2. Qiu Ling's notes come right after the passage they explain. The Chinese of each note can be unfolded; the full translation is shown below it.
  3. Wherever the texts chosen for this book originally speak of "grafting" (dual cultivation between a man and a woman), Qiu Ling explains them according to the "pure and quiet" method — a single person's body has its own yin and yang — and the translation follows the same principle.
  4. Passages marked "Safety note" record vigorous methods or very advanced stages described by the ancients. Read them for understanding only; do not try them on your own.

I · Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven1 chapter

Li Hanxu’s Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven, Annotated

There are nine levels of entry, and none may be skipped: first gather the mind inward and keep to the center and to harmony; find where the breath stirs at the yinqiao and join it with stillness; when spirit and breath meet, the true seed is born of itself; when it surges up to the heart (unfolding the opening), lead it through the three passes up to the Muddy Pellet (opening the passes); then lay the foundation, obtain the medicine and form the elixir; and finally refine the self in daily life, "forgetting feelings in the face of circumstances" — moving from effort to naturalness.

II · Nine Levels of Refining the Mind1 chapter

Li Hanxu’s Record of Following the Path through the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind

Refining the elixir is refining the mind: from stopping stray thoughts and making mind and breath one, to keeping the mind unmoved when the Mysterious Pass returns and following the movement, to nourishing the nature with the jade liquid, uniting with life through the golden liquid, nourishing warmly and sending out the spirit, and finally dissolving even the "numinous mind" back into emptiness — each level remedies the mind left behind by the level before.

III · Secret Principles of the Three Carts4 chapters

Li Hanxu’s Secret Principles of the Three Carts, Annotated

The same River Chariot route, travelled three times, each time deeper: first wait in the obscure for the return of one yang, follow it to move the breath through the passes and turn breath into liquid to form the elixir head; then move the jade liquid and refine one's own nature; finally meet life with nature, through the seven returns and nine reversions, and refine the spirit to return to emptiness — and the starting point of it all is the section on "gathering the mind": quieting the mind, closing the mouth, regulating the breath and boring into the obscure.

IV · Master Cui’s Mirror for Compounding the Medicine3 chapters

Master Cui’s Mirror for Compounding the Medicine, with Commentary

When the Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven breaths unite and one is as if drunk or dazed, that is the Mysterious Pass; with wu and ji (the true intention) as the director and geng and jia (fire phasing) determining advance and withdrawal, one uses the wind of the breath to move the spirit-fire across the upper and lower Magpie Bridges into the Yellow Chamber; the fire must be such that "water fears drying up, fire fears turning cold", and missing by a hair means failure — and after that, refining the self with the jade liquid, taking the golden liquid, forming and shedding the embryo, everything lies in "only utmost sincerity, following what is natural".

V · Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter2 chapters

Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter, Explained in Plain Language

Only without thoughts can one be still; in stillness the breath becomes even of itself; when the breath is even, the breathing comes to rest; when the breathing rests, it returns to the root; returning to the root, one sees one's original nature — first one sees the nature; then one lays the foundation by opening the Conception and Governing vessels; then one forges the sword and refines the self, watches the phases of the moon to gather the true lead, and advances and withdraws the fire according to the new moon, full moon and quarters; then come ten months of gestation, sending out the spirit, and facing the wall. All "methods of deliberate action" and "dead quietism" are false ways.

VI · Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Stele2 chapters

Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Stele, with Two Commentaries

Nourishing the breath relies on "forgetting words and keeping guard" — not speaking, the spirit guarding the center; subduing the mind relies on "acting without acting" — calming the deluded mind without contriving; when one can be still and can respond, responding to things without being deluded, the nature abides of itself, the breath returns of itself and the elixir forms of itself; at the extreme of stillness comes a clap of thunder, white clouds pay homage at the summit, sweet dew sprinkles down, one drinks the wine of long life by oneself and listens quietly to the stringless tune — these twenty lines are the ladder up to Heaven.

VII · Plain and Simple Words on the Dao2 chapters

Zhang Sanfeng’s Plain and Simple Words on the Dao

Eight characters: concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, regulate the breath and concentrate the spirit — only when the mind is clear is it gathered into the cavity of breath, and when the mind is still, the breathing regulates itself; keeping to clarity, stillness and naturalness is "not forgetting", following clarity, stillness and naturalness is "not helping"; from the "center" within the body, seek the "center" that is not within the body; when the mind wanders, bring it back, and once it is back, let go again — even in the midst of noise one can find stillness.

VIII · Straight Talk on the Mysterious Pivot3 chapters

The First Two Pieces of Zhang Sanfeng’s Straight Talk on the Mysterious Pivot

Do not cling to action, do not get attached to non-action; first sweep away stray thoughts, be still for a double hour, then lower the curtains and illuminate the space below the heart and above the kidneys; when the breath comes but the spirit is not whole, pay no attention to it; within a quarter of an hour there are all three stages of the work — those with few stray thoughts obtain the elixir early, those with many stray thoughts obtain it late.

IX · Sayings of Alchemists through the Ages3 chapters

Sayings of Alchemists through the Ages on Alchemical Practice, a Selection

First refine the self — cut off all ties, have no mind when facing circumstances; then regulate the medicine — when yang is born in the dim and indistinct, concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root; then carry out the Lesser Celestial Circuit in order: nourish warmly with the civil fire, gather and move with the martial fire, never missing the moment in each movement and each stillness — "it starts here and it ends here"; all numbers are metaphors, all scenes are judged by "when the time comes, the spirit knows", and when the fire is sufficient, it is stopped.

X · Xixinzi’s Answers to Students4 chapters

Xixinzi’s Answers to Students’ Questions

Put down all ties, sit upright with legs crossed, rest the mind in the elixir field below the navel, let the breathing follow its own nature, neither forgetting nor helping, spirit and breath relying on each other; when stillness reaches the dim and obscure and both self and others are forgotten, that is the first return to emptiness, the first seeing of the nature; from here, when stillness reaches its extreme and yang is born, one can speak of gathering the medicine, regulating the medicine, laying the foundation and the Celestial Circuit — do not crave or cling to any scene, do not hold rigidly to any number, and without a true transmission do not act at random.

XI · The Schools of the Way of the Elixir1 chapter

Xiao Tianshi’s Brief Account of the Essentials of the Schools of the Way of the Elixir

The alchemical Way has a thousand gates and a hundred schools, whose common aim is the dual cultivation and dual completion of nature and life, beginning with exhausting the principles; within the human-elixir method, the Wenshi school is the highest and the Shaoyang school the largest, and the Shaoyang school is best known for its Southern and Northern lineages, to which the Eastern, Western, Central, Sanfeng, Wu-Liu, Qingcheng and other schools can all be assigned; but whatever metaphors are used, without understanding true lead and true mercury, the advance and withdrawal of fire phasing, the true moment of gathering, and the aim of warm nourishing, it is all useless — "Ten thousand scrolls of alchemical scriptures — what matters is transmission from heart to heart; the oral formulas of Heaven's mechanism must always be taught by a teacher."

Appendix · Quiet Sitting for Health2 chapters

Appendix: Quiet Sitting for Health

The root of human life is at the navel, and quiet sitting means gathering the mind and intention on a point below the navel, going from effort to naturalness, so that body and mind become settled; the method has only two great requirements — a correct posture without force, and breathing that follows its own nature and gradually becomes fine; the mind passes from "with desire, observing the opening" (guarding the elixir field, watching the breath, counting the breath) to "without desire, observing the wonder" (following its own nature in silent illumination); after long sitting the elixir field grows warm and trembles, and everything is left to take its natural course.

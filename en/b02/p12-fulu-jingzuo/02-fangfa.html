Appendix: Quiet Sitting for Health · 方法

Method

Appendix, Quiet Sitting for Health, section 2 · Method — posture, hands, face and breathing, the state of mind, time, breathing, trembling in the belly

About This Chapter

Xu Zhenyi says the method of quiet sitting “has two great requirements: first, posture; second, regulating the breath”. This section covers every detail of a sitting from beginning to end, which can be arranged as a checklist:

ItemKey points
SettingA quiet room or the bedroom, windows open and doors shut, undisturbed; a soft, thick cushion
Before sittingLoosen clothes and belt; straighten the body and sit upright
LegsFull lotus, half lotus or lower crossing (crossed legs) all work equally well; raise the buttocks by four or five fen
Chest, buttocks, bellyChest slightly forward; buttocks slightly back, so the spine is not bent; lower belly settled — gathering the mind and intention below the navel, not using force
HandsLightly clasped, resting in front of the lower belly, fingertips without force
Head and faceHead and neck upright; ears as if not hearing; eyes lightly closed (those who tend to doze may keep them slightly open — “lowering the curtain”); mouth closed, tongue against the palate; swallow the saliva; breathe through the nose
State of mindFirst “with desire, observing the opening”: the intention rests in the lower elixir field, aware that breathing out is here and breathing in is here; once the mind is unified, “without desire, observing the wonder”: silent illumination, following what is natural. If this is hard to begin with, first watch the breath; counting the breath can be combined with either
TimeOnce in the morning and once in the evening is best, at least once a day; from thirty minutes, forty minutes to an hour is better; if only once, in the morning
BreathingVery still and fine, so quiet that you cannot hear it yourself — but beginners must not force it
After sittingOpen the eyes slowly, massage the belly, stretch the hands and feet; if sweating, wait until the heat subsides before going into the wind
TremblingAfter long practice the elixir field grows warm, surges and trembles; do not be alarmed, do not seek it, do not suppress it — let it take its natural course

Original Text and Translation

§1The two great requirements; points to note before and after quiet sitting
Original (Chinese)

静坐之方法，有两大要件：一为姿势；二为调息。此即入门之关键。今依次说明于下：

一、备静室一间，或就卧室，开窗阖户，不使他人来扰。

二、制软厚之褥垫，备久坐之用，或用被毯折叠代替亦可。

三、入坐前宽衣解带，使筋肉不受拘束。

四、平直其身，端正就坐。

五、静坐毕，宜慢慢张眼，抚摩上下腹及舒放手足，切勿匆遽。如身热有汗，必俟热退后，始可见风。

The method of quiet sitting has two great requirements: first, posture; second, regulating the breath. These are the key to entering. They are explained below in order:

  1. Prepare a quiet room, or use the bedroom, with the windows open and the door shut, so that no one comes to disturb you.
  2. Make a soft, thick cushion for long sitting; folded quilts or blankets can also be used instead.
  3. Before sitting, loosen your clothes and belt, so that the muscles are not constrained.
  4. Straighten the body and sit upright.
  5. When the sitting is over, open the eyes slowly, massage the upper and lower belly and stretch the hands and feet; never be hasty. If the body is hot and sweating, wait until the heat subsides before going out into the wind.
§2The legs in quiet sitting
Original (Chinese)

一、盘足而坐。既以左脚加于右股之上，复以右脚互加于左股之上，称为双盘膝。初学者不易仿效，年龄较长学之更难，不必勉强。

二、盘时或以左脚加于右股之上，或以右脚加于左股之上，均可随意。右式称为单盘膝。初学者不能双盘，自以单盘膝为宜。惟须注意姿势端直，身不倾斜，其功效相同。

三、以右脚置于左股下，复以左脚置于右股下，两小腿向后成交叉形。右式称为下盘膝。如因年老，筋骨较硬，单盘亦不易办到，可用下盘膝，其功效亦同。

四、无论双盘单盘或下盘，三种坐姿，均须于臀部处，用软褥垫高四五分，使比腿部略高，以免腰部弯曲。

五、初习盘足时，必觉麻木，可忍耐之，久则渐至自然。

六、麻木不能忍者，可上下交换其足，如再不能忍，则暂弛之，待麻木既去，再返坐。

七、如能十分忍耐，任其极端麻木，则麻木之后，自然能恢复原状。若经过此阶段者，盘坐时即永不再麻木矣。

  1. Sit with the legs crossed. Place the left foot on the right thigh, and then the right foot on the left thigh; this is called the full lotus. Beginners do not find it easy to imitate, and older people find it even harder; there is no need to force it.
  2. When crossing, either place the left foot on the right thigh or the right foot on the left thigh, as you like. This form is called the half lotus. Beginners who cannot do the full lotus should naturally use the half lotus. Only take care that the posture is upright and the body does not lean; its effect is the same.
  3. Place the right foot under the left thigh and the left foot under the right thigh, the two lower legs crossing behind. This form is called the lower crossing. If, because of age and stiff sinews and bones, even the half lotus is hard to do, the lower crossing can be used; its effect is the same.
  4. Whether full lotus, half lotus or lower crossing, in all three postures the buttocks must be raised four or five fen on a soft cushion, so that they are slightly higher than the legs, to keep the lower back from bending.
  5. When you first practice crossing the legs, you are bound to feel numbness; you can bear it, and in time it gradually becomes natural.
  6. If the numbness is unbearable, you can switch the feet above and below; if it is still unbearable, loosen them for a while, and when the numbness has gone, sit again.
  7. If you can endure it fully and let the numbness reach its extreme, then after the numbness the legs will naturally return to normal. Once you have passed through this stage, you will never feel numb again when sitting cross-legged.
§3The chest, buttocks and belly in quiet sitting
Original (Chinese)

一、胸部宜顺其自然，微微向前，使气易下降，惟不可使腰弯曲，形如新月，反致阻碍气机。

二、臀部宜向后稍稍凸出，使脊骨不曲。脊骨之形，本三折如弓，在臀部处，略向外弯，故坐时臀部宜凸出，然不可有意用力外凸，循其自然之姿势可也。

三、腹之下部宜镇定。镇定下腹，即所以安定身心，然非有意运力入腹，乃集中心意于下腹部，即所谓脐下丹田，使心平息和，而身心自易安定矣。

  1. The chest should follow what is natural, leaning slightly forward so that the breath descends easily; but the lower back must not be allowed to bend into the shape of a crescent moon, which would instead obstruct the mechanism of the breath.
  2. The buttocks should protrude slightly backward, so that the spine is not bent. The spine is by nature curved in three bends like a bow, curving slightly outward at the buttocks; so when sitting the buttocks should protrude — but do not deliberately push them out with force; simply follow the natural posture.
  3. The lower part of the belly should be settled. Settling the lower belly is the way to settle body and mind; but it is not deliberately pushing force into the belly — it is gathering the mind and intention in the lower belly, that is, the so-called elixir field below the navel, so that the mind is level and the breath harmonious, and body and mind easily become settled of themselves.
§4The hands in quiet sitting
Original (Chinese)

一、两手轻轻交握，贴于小腹之前，垂置小腿上。

二、交握之法，以一手轻握他手四指，两拇指结成交叉之形。

三、或以左手握右手，右手握左手，均各随意。

四、两手交握垂下处所，各随人之肢体所宜，或在腹下，或在股上，不必一定。

五、两手下垂及交握之指尖，当悉任自然，不宜些须着力。

  1. Clasp the two hands lightly, resting against the front of the lower belly and hanging down onto the lower legs.
  2. The way to clasp: one hand lightly holds the four fingers of the other, and the two thumbs cross.
  3. Either the left hand holds the right or the right hand holds the left, as you like.
  4. Where the clasped hands rest depends on what suits each person’s limbs — below the belly or on the thighs; it need not be fixed.
  5. The hanging hands and the fingertips of the clasp should be left entirely natural, without the slightest force.
§5The face, ears, eyes, mouth, saliva and breathing in quiet sitting
Original (Chinese)

一、头颈正直，面宜向前。

二、两耳宜如不闻。

三、眼宜轻闭。

亦有主张两眼微开者，此名垂帘。大抵坐时易于昏睡者，宜用此法。若不昏睡，以闭为宜，盖闭则心静也。

四、口宜噤，舌抵上腭。

五、口生津液，宜咽下。

六、呼吸宜用鼻，不可开口。

  1. The head and neck are upright, the face looking forward.
  2. The two ears should be as if not hearing.
  3. The eyes should be lightly closed.Some advocate keeping the eyes slightly open; this is called “lowering the curtain”. In general, those who easily become drowsy while sitting should use this method. If you do not become drowsy, it is better to close them, for when they are closed the mind is still.
  4. The mouth should be closed, the tongue against the upper palate.
  5. When saliva forms in the mouth, it should be swallowed.
  6. Breathe through the nose; do not open the mouth.
§6The state of mind in quiet sitting (1): turning the light back on the elixir field — from "with desire, observing the opening" to "without desire, observing the wonder"
Original (Chinese)

一、宜一切放下，勿起妄念。

不起妄念之法，即将诚意存于下丹田，极力遏止杂念，用目内观，默默返照于下丹田一点（道经所谓有欲观窍）。丹田在脐下（注：即脐内）一寸三分之地位。但返照时，只须注意脐下一带。不必拘于尺寸。使意念照定此处不移，呼觉在此，吸觉在此，息息不离此点，使心与息相依，于是注意之点愈明显，则其他杂念愈伏藏。反复练习，妄念自渐渐消除，心境自能专一。心既专一，觉神不外驰时，则不必如前时之着意，只须顺其自然而寂照之（道经所谓无欲观妙）。前者因杂念纷起，所以要集中意志于一处，制伏杂念。现在杂念渐少，神不外驰，已由勉强臻于自然。此时则当顺其自然，使脑中的注意力愈轻松愈好。如有一分注意力，即有一分不自然。不自然，就不能到“无思无虑，心意寂然，如皓月悬空，浩净无滓”之妙境，但亦不可意散，如意散，中无一点诚意，则又落空也。由此轻松之寂照，自渐至无人无我，清静空寂之妙境。而此时身体，已于不知不觉中，逐渐转强，容光焕发，诸病全消矣。

  1. Put everything down and do not give rise to deluded thoughts.The way not to give rise to deluded thoughts is to keep a sincere intention in the lower elixir field, holding back stray thoughts with all your strength, using the eyes to look within and silently turning the light back on a point in the lower elixir field (what the Daoist scriptures call “with desire, observing the opening”). The elixir field is one cun three fen below the navel (editor’s note: that is, inside the navel). But in turning the light back, you need only attend to the general area below the navel, without being bound by exact measurements. Let the intention illuminate this place steadily without moving, aware that breathing out is here and breathing in is here, every breath never leaving this point, so that mind and breath rely on each other; then the more distinct the point of attention becomes, the more the other stray thoughts hide away. With repeated practice, deluded thoughts will gradually disappear of themselves, and the mind will be able to become unified. Once the mind is unified and you feel the spirit no longer rushing outward, you no longer need to apply the intention as before; you need only follow what is natural and illuminate it in silence (what the Daoist scriptures call “without desire, observing the wonder”). Before, because stray thoughts arose in confusion, you had to concentrate your will in one place to subdue them. Now that stray thoughts are fewer and the spirit does not rush outward, you have gone from effort to naturalness. At this time you should follow what is natural, and the more relaxed the attention in the head, the better. If there is one part of attention, there is one part of unnaturalness. Without naturalness, you cannot reach the wondrous state of “without thought or worry, the mind and intention silent, like a bright moon hanging in the sky, pure and without sediment”. But the intention must not scatter either; if it scatters, with not a trace of sincere intention within, one falls into emptiness again. From this relaxed silent illumination, one gradually reaches the wondrous state of no others and no self, clear, still, empty and silent. And by this time, without one’s noticing, the body has gradually grown stronger, the face glows, and all illnesses have disappeared.
§7The state of mind in quiet sitting (2): the method of watching the breath
Original (Chinese)

二、初学静坐，往往因年龄愈长，妄念愈难消除。前返照丹田之法，如不易办到，宜改用观息法。

法以观随念至（即闭目以心意观想着息头，在鼻前去来），念在于息，观在于息。念随息之出入，念念依息，息息必观，观定不移，念头即住。念头能住，即不可过于着意，只要顺其自然，有意无意的，或知而不观即可。久久练习纯熟，然后再移守脐下丹田，自较为易矣。

  1. When first learning quiet sitting, the older one is, the harder it often is to get rid of deluded thoughts. If the method above of turning the light back on the elixir field is not easy to carry out, it is better to change to the method of watching the breath.In this method, the watching follows the thought (that is, closing the eyes and using the mind and intention to visualize the tip of the breath going and coming in front of the nose); the thought is on the breath, the watching is on the breath. The thought follows the breath in and out; every thought relies on the breath, every breath is watched; when the watching is steady and does not move, the thoughts come to rest. Once the thoughts can rest, do not apply the intention too much; simply follow what is natural, half intending and half not, or knowing without watching. When, after long practice, it has become thoroughly familiar, then shift to guarding the elixir field below the navel, and it will naturally be easier.
§8The state of mind in quiet sitting (3): the method of counting the breath
Original (Chinese)

三、返照丹田，或用观息法，均可兼用数息法。

一呼一吸为一息，自一息至五息，或至十息，自存一信念，在此五息或十息中，不得起其他杂念。如被他念来扰，则另从头数。务要一念到底，数完一遍，再接数多遍。待至纯熟后，可由十息增至二十息，逐渐增至百息皆可。直至心平气和，息微不可数，则可停数。至此念头即住矣。

  1. Whether turning the light back on the elixir field or using the method of watching the breath, either can be combined with the method of counting the breath.One exhalation and one inhalation make one breath. From one breath to five, or to ten, keep a single resolve that during these five or ten breaths no other stray thought may arise. If another thought comes to disturb you, start counting again from the beginning. Be sure to keep one thought through to the end; when you have counted one round, go on counting more rounds. When it has become thoroughly familiar, you can increase from ten breaths to twenty, and gradually even to a hundred. When at last the mind is level and the breath harmonious, and the breath is too faint to count, you can stop counting. At this point the thoughts have come to rest.
§9The state of mind in quiet sitting (4): other key points
Original (Chinese)

四、静坐本可以消除疾病，增进健康，然此等要求愈病及健康之观念，亦宜屏弃勿思。

五、静坐者宜如宗教家，具有信仰心，必坚定不移，继续行持，久乃大效。有效与否，全视信心。

六、在习坐初期，尚未生效前，应尽量减少房事，否则难期成效。至要至要！

七、关于阳生采取，以及其他细微等法，可参看本书其他各篇。

  1. Quiet sitting can of course get rid of illness and improve health; but even such thoughts of seeking to cure illness and gain health should be set aside and not dwelt on.
  2. The one who sits quietly should be like a person of religion, with a heart of faith, firm and unwavering, continuing to practice; only after a long time is there great effect. Whether there is an effect or not depends entirely on faith.
  3. In the early period of learning to sit, before any effect has appeared, sexual activity should be reduced as much as possible; otherwise results can hardly be expected. This is most important!
  4. As for gathering when yang is born, and other finer methods, see the other parts of this book.
§10The time for quiet sitting
Original (Chinese)

一、静坐之功候，到极深时，应终日行、住、坐、卧，念兹在兹方可。然初习时，不可不规定时间，以早晨起床及晚间就寝前各坐一次为宜。否则每日至少必有一次静坐。

二、每日静坐之时间，固应愈长愈妙，然不必有意求长，当听其自然，能坐至三十分钟，日久继续不断，则其收效已不少矣。

三、事繁之人，每次静坐，以四十分钟为宜，能延长至一小时更妙。

四、时间不论早昼晚皆宜。若每日只能一次者，以早晨起床时为佳。

五、每晚就寝前，能做十五分钟或二十分钟之短时间静坐，亦颇有效。总之，以起床后之静坐为主，就寝前之静坐副之可也。

  1. When the work of quiet sitting reaches great depth, one should keep it in mind constantly all day, walking, standing, sitting and lying down. But when first practicing, one must set fixed times; sitting once on rising in the morning and once at night before going to bed is best. Otherwise, there must be at least one quiet sitting a day.
  2. The longer the daily sitting, of course, the better; but one need not deliberately seek length; let it be natural. If you can sit for thirty minutes and keep it up day after day without interruption, the results will already be considerable.
  3. For busy people, forty minutes each sitting is suitable; extending it to an hour is even better.
  4. Morning, noon and evening are all suitable times. If you can sit only once a day, the morning on rising is best.
  5. A short sitting of fifteen or twenty minutes every night before going to bed is also quite effective. In short, the sitting after rising is the main one, and the sitting before going to bed is secondary.
§11Breathing in quiet sitting
Original (Chinese)

一、一呼一吸谓之一息，宜极静细，以静坐时自己亦不闻其声为合度，但初学者不可勉强，仍宜顺其自然呼吸。久久习之，呼吸自渐渐入细，出入极微，自己不觉不知，宛如无呼吸之状态。

二、能达无呼吸之状态，则无呼息无吸息。虽有呼吸器，似无所用之，而气息仿佛从全身毛孔出入，至此乃达调息之极功，然初学者不可有意求之，须听其自然，至要。

  1. One exhalation and one inhalation are called one breath; it should be extremely still and fine — the right measure is that during quiet sitting you yourself cannot hear its sound. But beginners must not force it; it is better to let the breathing follow its own nature. With long practice, the breathing gradually becomes fine of itself, going in and out extremely faintly, without your noticing or knowing it, as if in a state of no breathing.
  2. When one can reach the state of no breathing, there is neither exhalation nor inhalation. Although there are breathing organs, they seem to have no use, and the breath seems to go in and out through the pores of the whole body — this is the highest achievement of regulating the breath. But beginners must not deliberately seek it; they must leave it to take its natural course. This is most important.
§12Trembling in the belly during quiet sitting
Original (Chinese)

一、静坐日久，脐下丹田发现一种震动之现象，即为气足之证。

二、震动之前，必先觉脐下有一股热力，往来动荡。

三、热力动荡既久，忽然发生一种震动，能使全身皆震。斯时不可惊骇，当一任其自然。

四、震动之速度，及震动之久暂，人各不同，皆起于自然，不可强求，亦不可遏抑。

五、震动时宜以意（不可用力），引此动力自尾闾（臀后脊骨下端尽处，名尾闾）循背脊上行，而达于顶。复透过顶，自颜面徐徐下降胸部，而达于脐下（自尾闾上行至下降胸部，非一时之事，或距震动后数月，或经年不定，阅者勿误会）。久之则此动力，自能上下升降，并可以意运之于全身，洋溢四达，虽指甲毛发之尖，亦能感之。斯时全体皆热，愉快异常，是即回复胎息之始基也。功夫至此，烦恼不生，悦怿无量，延年却病，更其余事耳。惟在学者之力行何如耳。

  1. After long days of quiet sitting, a phenomenon of trembling appears in the elixir field below the navel; this is evidence that the breath is sufficient.
  2. Before the trembling, one is bound first to feel a current of warmth below the navel, surging back and forth.
  3. When the warmth has surged for a long time, suddenly a trembling arises that can make the whole body tremble. At this time do not be alarmed; let it take its natural course entirely.
  4. The speed of the trembling and how long it lasts differ from person to person; all arise naturally and must neither be forced nor suppressed.
  5. During the trembling, use the intention (not force) to lead this moving power from the Caudal Funnel (the lowest end of the spine behind the buttocks is called the Caudal Funnel) up along the spine to the crown. Then, passing through the crown, it descends slowly from the face to the chest, and reaches below the navel (going up from the Caudal Funnel and down to the chest is not a matter of a single occasion; it may be months after the trembling began, or a year or more — readers should not misunderstand). In time this moving power can rise and fall by itself, and can be moved by the intention throughout the whole body, overflowing in every direction, so that even the tips of the nails and hair can feel it. At this time the whole body is warm and unusually pleasant; this is the very beginning of the return to embryonic breathing. When the work has reached this point, vexations do not arise and joy is boundless; prolonging life and warding off illness are minor matters besides. It all depends on how vigorously the student practices.

Key Terms

  • Full lotus, half lotus, lower crossing: three ways of sitting cross-legged, with the same effect; the lower crossing is what is commonly called the “easy” crossed-leg posture.
  • Lowering the curtain: keeping the eyes slightly open, used by those who easily become drowsy; see lowering the curtains.
  • Tongue against the palate, swallowing the saliva: see Flowery Pool and Sweet Dew.
  • With desire, observing the opening; without desire, observing the wonder: from Laozi, chapter 1, “Ever without desire, one observes its wonder; ever with desire, one observes its boundary”; Xu takes guarding the elixir field as “observing the opening” and silent illumination that follows what is natural as “observing the wonder”. See Reverting the Light.
  • The method of watching the breath: closing the eyes and visualizing with the mind the tip of the breath going and coming in front of the nose, every thought relying on the breath.
  • The method of counting the breath: counting from one to five or ten, starting again whenever a stray thought arises; increasing once familiar, and stopping when the breath is too faint to count.
  • The intention scattering, falling into emptiness: in silent illumination, if there is no sincere intention at all, one falls into emptiness; see torpor and distraction.
  • The state of no breathing: the breath seeming to go in and out through the pores of the whole body, the highest achievement of regulating the breath; see True Breath.
  • Evidence that the breath is sufficient: warmth surging in the elixir field, then trembling.
  • Caudal Funnel: the lowest end of the spine behind the buttocks; see Caudal Funnel.
  • Up from the Caudal Funnel, through the crown, down to the chest and below the navel: the route of the Lesser Celestial Circuit; see Celestial Circuit.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Sit upright and use no force anywhere in the body. Raise the buttocks a little and the lower back straightens naturally; let the chest lean slightly forward, the hands clasp lightly, the fingertips relaxed.
  2. First observe the opening, then observe the wonder. When there are many stray thoughts, rest the mind and intention in the area below the navel, aware that breathing out is here and breathing in is here; once the mind is still, lighten it — “if there is one part of attention, there is one part of unnaturalness” — but do not let it scatter into emptiness either.
  3. If guarding the elixir field is hard, begin by watching or counting the breath.
  4. Do not seek length, do not seek results. Thirty minutes a day, kept up without interruption, will bring gains; let go even of the thought of “curing illness”.
  5. Let the breathing simply follow its own nature. “The right measure is that you yourself cannot hear its sound”, but beginners must not force it.
  6. Let warmth and trembling in the elixir field take their natural course — do not be alarmed, do not seek it, do not suppress it. Leading the breath upward “is not a matter of a single occasion”; do not hurry.

Translator's Notes

  1. §6, “one cun three fen below the navel (editor’s note: that is, inside the navel)”: the “note” in brackets is in the original book; the translation renders it as “editor’s note”.
  2. §6, “pure and without sediment”: here the original reads 浩净 (“vast and clean”), unlike 洁净 (“pure”) in the passage quoted in section one; the translation uses “pure” in both places.
  3. §7, “the watching follows the thought” (guan sui nian zhi 观随念至): so in the original; the explanation in brackets is “using the mind and intention to visualize the tip of the breath going and coming in front of the nose”, and we translate accordingly.
  4. §2, safety note: the original says that beginners “can bear” the numbness of crossed legs, and even “let the numbness reach its extreme”. Modern readers should note that enduring leg numbness for a long time may compress the nerves; when numbness is marked, simply switch the feet or loosen them for a while, as in item 6 of the original — there is no need to push through. Those with knee or hip injuries or conditions can sit on a chair, keeping the back naturally upright.
  5. §12, safety note: warmth and trembling in the elixir field are commonly reported by sitters past and present, and the original stresses that they “must neither be forced nor suppressed”. If violent, uncontrollable shaking occurs, or it is accompanied by headaches, palpitations, insomnia or other discomfort, stop practicing and consult a reliable teacher, and see a doctor if necessary. Do not use force in order to produce trembling.
  6. “Xu Zhenyi”: the original book calls him only “Mr. Xu Zhenyi”; his life has yet to be identified.

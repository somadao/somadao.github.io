Introduction to the Appendix

What this book is

This is the appendix of the whole book, a method for beginners written by Xu Zhenyi. Qiu Ling says that although the book has already gathered a number of fairly plain alchemical scriptures and questions and answers on practice, “the methods for getting started are still rather sketchy”, and beginners who want to learn to sit may still feel at a loss — so he appended this piece. It is written in modern plain language, without coded terms, and goes from “why sit quietly” to “how to cross the legs, where to put the hands, whether the eyes are open or closed, where to put the mind, how long to sit”, and finally to warmth in the elixir field, trembling, and the movement of the breath up the back and down the front. It can be called a complete beginner’s manual for quiet sitting.

Qiu Ling's editorial note

本书虽已收集多种较显浅之丹经及练功问答，作为学者修炼入门之用，然入手方法尚嫌简略，初学之士欲习打坐，恐仍不免茫然。故选取徐真一氏所著入手方法一种，作为附录。此功较为详明，可为初学楷则。学者以此印证丹经，自可入门。

Although this book has already gathered a number of fairly plain alchemical scriptures and questions and answers on practice for students entering cultivation, the methods for getting started are still rather sketchy, and beginners who want to learn to sit will probably still feel at a loss. So I have chosen a method for getting started written by Mr. Xu Zhenyi as an appendix. This practice is fairly detailed and clear and can serve as a model for beginners. If students use it to check against the alchemical scriptures, they will naturally find the way in.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

The root of human life is at the navel, and quiet sitting means gathering the mind and intention on a point below the navel, going from effort to naturalness, so that body and mind become settled; the method has only two great requirements — a correct posture without force, and breathing that follows its own nature and gradually becomes fine; the mind passes from “with desire, observing the opening” (guarding the elixir field, watching the breath, counting the breath) to “without desire, observing the wonder” (following its own nature in silent illumination); after long sitting the elixir field grows warm and trembles, and everything is left to take its natural course.

Reading tips

  1. This is the easiest part of the whole book to put into practice. If you are just beginning quiet sitting, you can read the “Method” of this part first, then go back to Xixinzi’s questions and answers in part ten.
  2. The two most important points of measure: “If there is one part of attention, there is one part of unnaturalness”, yet “the intention must not scatter either … with not a trace of sincere intention within, one falls into emptiness again”; as for breathing, “beginners must not force it; it is better to let it follow its own nature”.
  3. The original was written in modern times and uses expressions such as “cells”, “blood”, “toxins” and “hypnotism”, which are the scientific language of its day; we translate them according to the original meaning.
  4. On numbness from crossing the legs and on trembling in the belly, we have added safety notes for modern readers in the translator’s notes.

Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Stele, with Two Commentaries · 后十句：一声雷，上天梯

The Last Ten Lines: A Clap of Thunder, a Ladder up to Heaven

Hundred-Character Stele, last ten lines · Yin and yang give birth to each other, back and forth; a single clap of thunder transforms everything — white clouds pay homage at the summit, sweet dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru, twenty lines in all

Note (translator): This section speaks of “the True Breath stops of itself, the hundred vessels stop of themselves, sun and moon halt their light, the Jade Armil no longer turns”, “the two kidneys as if boiled in hot water, the bladder as if roasted by fire”, “the ears hear the sound of immortal music” and so on. These are the ancients’ descriptions of the scenes of deep concentration and of the stirring of the breath. They are not goals of practice; there is no need to pursue them, and still less should they be taken as standards for testing oneself.

About This Chapter

The last ten lines cover the way from the birth of the medicine to the completion of the elixir:

  • Yin and yang give birth to each other, back and forth; a single clap of thunder transforms everything: when emptiness is brought to its utmost and stillness is guarded steadfastly, the Supreme Ultimate in stillness gives rise to movement; in the belly (Kun, the Winding River) suddenly there is a point of numinous light like a grain of millet, the two kidneys are as if boiled in hot water, and inside the belly it is as if thunder were shaking — this is the Fu hexagram, the Root of Heaven appearing; helping it with the spirit, the breath passes the Caudal Funnel and rises to the Muddy Pellet (the Gou hexagram), and from the Moon Cave to the Hall of Impression it turns into sweet dew and falls into the Yellow Court. Lu’s commentary says these ten characters are “the great purport of making the elixir” and “the secret formula of making the elixir”, and quotes Shao Yong: “Suddenly at midnight, a clap of thunder; ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open one after another.”
  • White clouds pay homage at the summit; sweet dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru: once the medicine is obtained, the breath rises to the Muddy Pellet like white clouds gathering at the summit; it turns into jade nectar, fragrant, sweet and delicious, and descends the Storied Tower — “when sweet dew fills the mouth, see it off with the eyes, welcome it with the intention, and send it down to the elixir cauldron”.
  • Drink the wine of long life by yourself — free and easy, who could know? The divine water circulates above and below and is swallowed again and again; this is called the wine of long life, and this joy is known only to oneself.
  • Quietly listen to the stringless tune; clearly penetrate the mechanism of creation: with the elixir in the body, supreme harmony overflows — “the eyes have divine light, the ears numinous sounds, the mouth sweet saliva, the nose a strange fragrance” — and Lu’s commentary says “this follows necessarily from the principle; there is nothing strange about it”.
  • Twenty lines in all — truly a ladder up to Heaven: Zhang Sanfeng says these twenty lines contain “not a shred of falsehood”; Lu Qianxu says he “presumed to write a tentative commentary”, hoping only not to fail his teacher’s teaching.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

阴阳生反复，普化一声雷。

Yin and yang give birth to each other, back and forth, transforming everything — a single clap of thunder.

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §1
Original note (Chinese)

功夫到此，神不外驰，气不外泄，神归气穴，坎离已交，愈加猛烈精进。致虚之极，守静之笃，身静于杳冥之中，心澄于无何有之乡，则真息自住，百脉自停，日月停景，璇玑不行。太极静而生动，阳产于西南之坤。坤即腹也，又名曲江，忽然一点灵光如黍米之大，即药生消息也。赫然光透，两肾如汤煎，膀胱如火炙，腹中如烈风之吼，腹内如震雷之声，即复卦天根现也。天根现，即固心王。以神助之，则其气如火逼金上行，穿过尾闾，轻轻运，默默举，一团和气，如雷之震，上升泥丸，周身踊跃，即天风姤卦也。由月窟至印堂，眉中漏出元光，即太极动而生阴，化成神水甘露，内有黍米之珠，落在黄庭之中，点我离中灵汞，结成圣相之体。行周天火候一度，烹之炼之，丹自结矣。

When the work reaches this point, the spirit does not rush outward, the breath does not leak outward, the spirit returns to the cavity of breath, Kan and Li have already united, and one advances all the more vigorously and diligently. When emptiness is brought to its utmost and stillness is guarded steadfastly, the body is still in the dark and obscure, the mind is clear in the land of nothing whatever; then the True Breath stops of itself, the hundred vessels stop of themselves, sun and moon halt their light, and the Jade Armil no longer turns. The Supreme Ultimate in stillness gives rise to movement, and yang is produced in Kun in the southwest. Kun is the belly, also called the Winding River; suddenly there is a point of numinous light as large as a grain of millet — this is the sign of the medicine being born. The light shines through brilliantly, the two kidneys are as if boiled in hot water, the bladder as if roasted by fire, in the belly it is like the howling of a fierce wind, inside the belly like the sound of shaking thunder — this is the Fu hexagram, the Root of Heaven appearing. When the Root of Heaven appears, one must make the king of the mind firm. Help it with the spirit, and the breath, like metal driven by fire, moves upward, passes through the Caudal Funnel — move it lightly, raise it silently — a mass of harmonious breath, shaking like thunder, rises to the Muddy Pellet, and the whole body leaps — this is the Gou hexagram, Heaven over Wind. From the Moon Cave to the Hall of Impression, the original light leaks out between the eyebrows — this is the Supreme Ultimate moving and giving rise to yin; it turns into divine water and sweet dew, containing a pearl like a grain of millet, and falls into the Yellow Court, touching and transforming the numinous mercury within my Li and forming the body of the holy image. Carry out one round of the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit, cook it and refine it, and the elixir forms of itself.

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §1
Original note (Chinese)

此十字者妙不可言。盖阴阳反复，乃作丹之大旨。普化雷声，乃作丹之秘诀。所谓天机秘密，正在于此。夫神仙丹法，皆以阴阳反复而成，故以药材而言，则阴中用阳，阳中用阴，此阴阳之反复一也。以交媾而言，则女居日位，男配蟾宫，此阴阳之反复二也。以合丹而言，则举水以灭火，以金而伐木，此阴阳之反复三也。如此颠倒异常，大类可见。至其天机玄妙，则在普化句中。邵子之诗有云：“忽然夜半一声雷，万户千门次递开。识得无中含有意，许君亲见伏羲来”。盖地中有雷，于卦为复。一阳来复，所谓爻动之时，身中冬至，正好寻铅。得诀修之，则大地山河皆成七宝。故云普化言一声者，重始气也。此中别有单符单诀，贵在师传，学人更当洁己虚心，以期际遇可也。

These ten characters are wonderful beyond words. “Yin and yang back and forth” is the great purport of making the elixir; “the clap of thunder that transforms everything” is the secret formula of making the elixir. What is called the secret of Heaven’s mechanism lies exactly here. The elixir methods of the immortals are all accomplished through yin and yang back and forth. In terms of the ingredients, one uses yang within yin and yin within yang — this is the first reversal of yin and yang. In terms of intercourse, “the woman dwells in the sun’s place, the man is matched with the toad palace” — this is the second reversal of yin and yang. In terms of compounding the elixir, one raises water to extinguish fire and uses metal to cut wood — this is the third reversal of yin and yang. From such extraordinary inversions, the general idea can be seen. As for the mysterious subtlety of Heaven’s mechanism, it lies in the line about “transforming everything”. Master Shao’s poem says: “Suddenly at midnight, a clap of thunder; ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open one after another. If you recognize that within nothingness there is meaning, you are allowed to see Fuxi come in person.” Thunder within the earth is, among the hexagrams, Fu. One yang comes back — this is what is called “the moment the line moves”, the winter solstice within the body, just the time to seek the lead. If one obtains the formula and cultivates it, the great earth, mountains and rivers all become the seven treasures. So it says “transforming everything” and also “a single clap”, giving weight to the very first breath. Within this there is also a separately transmitted talisman and formula, which must be passed on by a teacher; students should all the more purify themselves and empty their minds, and hope for the right encounter.

§2
Original (Chinese)

白云朝顶上，甘露洒须弥。

White clouds pay homage at the summit; sweet dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru.

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §2
Original note (Chinese)

到此地位，药即得矣。二气结刀圭，关窍开通，火降水升，一气周流，从太极中动天根，过玄谷关，升二十四椎骨节，至天谷关，月窟阴生香甜美味，降下重楼，无休无息，名曰甘露洒须弥。诀曰：甘露满口，以目送之，以意迎之，送下丹釜，凝结元气以养之。

At this stage the medicine is obtained. The two breaths form the knife-point, the passes and openings open, fire descends and water rises, and the one breath circulates: from within the Supreme Ultimate it stirs the Root of Heaven, passes the Mysterious Valley Pass, rises through the twenty-four vertebrae and reaches the Heavenly Valley Pass; in the Moon Cave yin is born, with a fragrant, sweet and delicious taste, and descends the Storied Tower without rest or pause — this is called “sweet dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru”. The formula says: when sweet dew fills the mouth, see it off with the eyes, welcome it with the intention, send it down to the elixir cauldron, and congeal the original breath to nourish it.

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §2
Original note (Chinese)

此十字言气回之征验。盖先天之气，生于爻动之期。此时运剑追来，度鹊桥，贯尾闾，循督脉而上，通于泥丸。但觉油然滃然，如白云之朝于顶上者。顷之，化为玉浆，味如甘露，洒于须弥，降于重楼，入于中宫。所谓气回丹结，其象如此。须弥山名，佛语须弥，此云妙高，即顶上之义。《紫庭经》云：“采之服之未片晌，一道白脉冲泥丸，化为五浆流入口，香甜清爽遍舌端”。意盖本此。

These ten characters speak of the signs that the breath has returned. The Prior Heaven breath is born at the time when the line moves. At this moment one wields the sword to chase it back, crosses the Magpie Bridge, passes through the Caudal Funnel, rises along the Governing vessel and reaches the Muddy Pellet. One only feels it welling up and billowing, like white clouds gathering at the summit. After a while it turns into jade nectar, tasting like sweet dew; it sprinkles Mount Sumeru, descends the Storied Tower and enters the central palace. What is called “the breath returns and the elixir forms” looks like this. Sumeru is the name of a mountain; in Buddhist language “Sumeru” is translated here as “wondrously high”, meaning the top of the head. The Scripture of the Purple Court says: “Gather it and take it, and in less than a moment, a white vessel surges up to the Muddy Pellet, turns into jade nectar and flows into the mouth, fragrant, sweet, clear and fresh all over the tip of the tongue.” The idea probably comes from this.

§3
Original (Chinese)

自饮长生酒，逍遥谁得知？

Drink the wine of long life by yourself — free and easy, who could know?

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §3
Original note (Chinese)

养气到此，骨节已开，神水不住，上下周流，往来不息，时时吞咽，谓之长生酒。诀曰：流珠灌养灵根性，修行之人知不知？

When nourishing the breath reaches this point, the joints have opened and the divine water does not stop; it circulates above and below, coming and going without cease, and is swallowed again and again — this is called the wine of long life. The formula says: the flowing pearl irrigates and nourishes the nature of the spiritual root — do practitioners know it or not?

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §3
Original note (Chinese)

气化为水，甘美莫加，故玉液琼浆，随宜立号。《悟真篇》云：“长男乍饮西方酒”，此长生酒也；“雪山一味好醍醐”，此长生酒也；“壶内旋斟延命酒，鼎中收取还魂浆”，此长生酒也。是皆己所独得，无人与共，故曰自饮，逍遥快乐自得之义。夫此酒既不能与人共，此乐又能与人知耶？

The breath turns into water, sweet beyond compare; so “jade liquid” and “nectar of jasper” are names given as the situation suits. The Wuzhen pian says “the eldest son suddenly drinks the wine of the west” — this is the wine of long life; “on the snow mountain, one flavor, fine ghee” — this is the wine of long life; “in the gourd, pour out at once the wine that prolongs life; in the tripod, collect the nectar that brings back the soul” — this too is the wine of long life. All this is obtained by oneself alone, shared with no one, so it says “drink by yourself”, meaning free and easy, happy and content within oneself. Since this wine cannot be shared with others, how could this joy be made known to others?

§4
Original (Chinese)

静听无弦曲，明通造化机。

Quietly listen to the stringless tune; clearly penetrate the mechanism of creation.

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §4
Original note (Chinese)

功夫到此，耳听仙乐之音，又有钟鼓之韵，五气朝元，三花聚顶，如晚鸦来栖之状。心田开朗，智慧自生，已得六通之妙。此乃实有也。非与师遇，此事难知。

When the work reaches this point, the ears hear the sound of immortal music, and also the resonance of bells and drums; the five breaths return to the origin and the three flowers gather at the summit, like evening crows coming home to roost. The field of the mind opens bright, wisdom arises of itself, and one has already obtained the wonder of the six penetrations. This truly exists. Without meeting a teacher, this matter is hard to know.

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §4
Original note (Chinese)

《太上日用经》云：“无弦之曲，不言而自声，不鼓而自鸣。”盖丹在身中，太和充溢，是以目有神光，耳有灵响，口有甘津，鼻有异香，理所必至，无足异者。吾师意在简文，聊举其一，即其余可推也。

The Most High’s Scripture for Daily Use says: “The stringless tune sounds of itself without speaking, rings of itself without being struck.” With the elixir in the body, supreme harmony overflows; so the eyes have divine light, the ears numinous sounds, the mouth sweet saliva, the nose a strange fragrance — this follows necessarily from the principle; there is nothing strange about it. My teacher intended the text to be concise, so he mentioned only one of these; the rest can be inferred.

§5
Original (Chinese)

都来二十句，端的上天梯。

Twenty lines in all — truly a ladder up to Heaven.

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §5
Original note (Chinese)

自养气忘言至此二十句，皆是吕祖真正口诀功夫，无半点虚伪，乃修行之阶梯。得悟此诀与注者，可急行之勿妄漏泄，勿示匪人，珍重奉行。

From “to nourish the breath, forget words” to here, these twenty lines are all Patriarch Lü’s true oral formulas and work, without a shred of falsehood; they are the ladder of cultivation. Those who awaken to this formula and its commentary may put it into practice at once; do not leak it carelessly, do not show it to the wrong people; treasure it and carry it out with reverence.

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §5
Original note (Chinese)

吾师百字灵文，乃千圣登真之梯筏，学人谁不知诵，求其融会贯通，以得夫立言之意者，盖亦少矣。星谫劣不文，蒙师提挈有年，金丹大道，尝窃与闻，考之此篇，若合符节，乃敢僭为测疏，作济度之津梁，开时人之眼目。极知狂诞，无所逃罪，然使好道之伦，玩索而有得焉，庶几不负吾师之教乎。

My teacher’s hundred-character numinous text is the ladder and raft by which a thousand sages ascended to perfection. What student does not know how to recite it? But those who grasp it thoroughly and attain the intention behind its words are probably few. I, Xing, shallow and without literary talent, have been guided by my teacher for many years and have privately heard something of the great Way of the Golden Elixir; examining this piece against it, I found that they match like the two halves of a tally, and so I have dared to presume to write a tentative commentary, as a bridge for crossing over and to open the eyes of people today. I know full well that this is wild and presumptuous and that I cannot escape blame; but if lovers of the Way can ponder it and gain something, perhaps I shall not have failed my teacher’s teaching.

Key Terms

  • Yin and yang give birth to each other, back and forth: Lu’s commentary: the great purport of making the elixir; three reversals — in the ingredients, in intercourse and in compounding the elixir.
  • A single clap of thunder transforms everything: the moment one yang comes back; see Living Zi Hour.
  • Kun, Winding River: the belly; see lower Cinnabar Field.
  • The Fu hexagram and the Root of Heaven, the Gou hexagram and the Moon Cave: the birth of one yang and of one yin; see fire phasing.
  • Making the king of the mind firm: steadying the mind and spirit.
  • Hall of Impression, original light: the light between the eyebrows; see Celestial Eye.
  • “The woman dwells in the sun’s place, the man is matched with the toad palace”: a metaphor for Kan and Li each hiding the other (the yin within Li, the yang within Kan); see conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Raising water to extinguish fire, using metal to cut wood: the inversion in compounding the elixir; see Lead and Mercury.
  • Separately transmitted talisman and formula: oral formulas transmitted singly.
  • Mysterious Valley Pass, Heavenly Valley Pass: the area of the Caudal Funnel and the Muddy Pellet; see Muddy Pellet.
  • Twenty-four vertebrae: the spine.
  • “When sweet dew fills the mouth, see it off with the eyes, welcome it with the intention”: Zhang Sanfeng’s formula; see Sweet Dew.
  • Elixir cauldron: the lower elixir field, the earthen cauldron.
  • Sumeru: in Buddhist language “wondrously high”, i.e. the top of the head.
  • Wine of long life, jade liquid and nectar of jasper, ghee: fine names for saliva.
  • Flowing pearl: another name for mercury.
  • Stringless tune: sounding of itself without speaking, ringing of itself without being struck.
  • The five breaths return to the origin, the three flowers gather at the summit: see the five breaths returning to the origin.
  • Six penetrations: the six supernormal powers of Buddhism.
  • “The eyes have divine light, the ears numinous sounds, the mouth sweet saliva, the nose a strange fragrance”: Lu’s commentary: supreme harmony overflowing, which follows necessarily from the principle.
  • Tentative commentary: Lu Qianxu’s own name for his commentary.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “When sweet dew fills the mouth, see it off with the eyes, welcome it with the intention” — when saliva increases during quiet sitting, there is no need to do anything deliberate; simply send it down lightly with the gaze and the intention. This is the simplest way to use this formula.
  2. Lu Qianxu: “this follows necessarily from the principle; there is nothing strange about it” — the various pleasant sensations that appear in the body are all the natural result of stillness and ease; there is no need to be amazed by them or to cling to them.
  3. Everything comes from stillness: only when “emptiness is brought to its utmost and stillness is guarded steadfastly” is there “a single clap of thunder”. Without stillness, nothing that follows can happen.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text in this book reads “quietly listen to the stringless tune”, while the common edition reads “sitting, listen to the stringless tune”; we follow this book.
  2. Lu’s commentary quotes “ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open 次递”; the common text of Shao Yong’s poem reads “次第” (“one after another”). The translation follows the common text.
  3. Lu’s commentary quotes the Scripture of the Purple Court as “turns into 五浆 (five nectars)”; “five” (五) should be “jade” (玉). We translate it as “jade nectar”; the original is kept as in the book.
  4. “I, Xing”: “Xing” is Lu Xixing referring to himself by his given name.
  5. “The woman dwells in the sun’s place, the man is matched with the toad palace”: within the sun (Li) there is yin, and within the moon (Kan) there is yang; the alchemists use “woman” and “man” as metaphors for them. This is a metaphor from the hexagram images and has nothing to do with relations between men and women.

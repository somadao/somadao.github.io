Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Stele, with Two Commentaries · 篇首导读

Introduction to Part Six

What this book is

The Hundred-Character Stele (Baizi bei 百字碑) is attributed to Lü Dongbin: twenty lines, a hundred characters, known as the “Hundred-Character Numinous Text”, and one of the most widely circulated short works of the alchemical Way. Its first two lines, “To nourish the breath, forget words and keep guard; to subdue the mind, act without acting”, have always been regarded as the master formula for entering inner alchemy.

Qiu Ling chose two commentaries:

  • Zhang Sanfeng’s commentary: by Zhang Sanfeng, the Ming-dynasty patriarch of the Wudang school, included in the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng (Sanfeng quanji 三丰全集). The commentary is “precise and concise, grasping the spirit and marrow”, and almost every line comes with a formula. When Li Hanxu edited the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng, he commented: “Patriarch Lü, holding the great vow of saving people, passed on this Hundred-Character Stele; Patriarch Zhang, holding the great vow of saving people, annotated this Hundred-Character Stele. Patriarch Zhang’s heart is Patriarch Lü’s heart.”
  • Lu Qianxu’s commentary: the Tentative Commentary on Lord Chunyang Lü’s Hundred-Character Stele (Chunyang Lü gong baizi bei ceshu 纯阳吕公百字碑测疏) by Lu Xixing (styled Qianxu), the Ming-dynasty patriarch of the Eastern school of Daoism, in his Outer History of Fanghu (Fanghu waishi 方壶外史). He calls this hundred-character numinous text “the ladder and raft by which a thousand sages ascended to perfection”; his commentary is more detailed and often cites the Daode jing, the Cantong qi and the Wuzhen pian as mutual proof.

Qiu Ling says it was “hard to give up either” of the two commentaries, so he appended both after the original text. Both authors lived in the Ming dynasty and their works are in the public domain; we give the original and the translation in full.

In the previous part, Nine Levels of Refining the Mind, Ni Qinghe’s explanation of level five compares the Hundred-Character Stele with the nine levels of the work (“white clouds pay homage at the summit” is level three, “sweet dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru” is level four); the two can be read side by side.

Qiu Ling's editorial note

《百字碑》为吕洞宾所作，世称“百字灵文”，历代多有注家。明代武当派祖师张三丰亦有注，收入《三丰全集》中。所注精而简，得其神髓。李涵虚编订《三丰全集》，评张氏注云：“吕祖抱度人洪愿而传此《百字碑》，张祖抱度人大愿而注此《百字碑》，张祖之心即吕祖之心也，故曰纯阳三丰，乃神仙中耳目”。明代道门东派祖师陆潜虚在其所著《方壶外史》中亦有《纯阳吕公百字碑测疏》，称此百字灵文“乃千圣登真之梯筏”，所注若合符节，亦较为详明。

现将此两注合并在《百字碑》原文之后，实因难以有所割舍也。

The Hundred-Character Stele was written by Lü Dongbin and is known as the “Hundred-Character Numinous Text”; many commentators through the ages have annotated it. Zhang Sanfeng, the Ming-dynasty patriarch of the Wudang school, also wrote a commentary, included in the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng; it is precise and concise and grasps the spirit and marrow. When Li Hanxu edited the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng, he commented on Zhang’s commentary: “Patriarch Lü, holding the great vow of saving people, passed on this Hundred-Character Stele; Patriarch Zhang, holding the great vow of saving people, annotated this Hundred-Character Stele. Patriarch Zhang’s heart is Patriarch Lü’s heart; that is why it is said that Chunyang (Patriarch Lü) and Sanfeng are the ears and eyes among the immortals.” Lu Qianxu, the Ming-dynasty patriarch of the Eastern school of Daoism, also wrote a Tentative Commentary on Lord Chunyang Lü’s Hundred-Character Stele in his Outer History of Fanghu, calling this hundred-character numinous text “the ladder and raft by which a thousand sages ascended to perfection”; his commentary matches the original meaning like the two halves of a tally, and is also rather detailed and clear.

I now append these two commentaries together after the original text of the Hundred-Character Stele, truly because it was hard to give up either of them.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

Nourishing the breath relies on “forgetting words and keeping guard” — not speaking, the spirit guarding the center; subduing the mind relies on “acting without acting” — calming the deluded mind without contriving; when one can be still and can respond, responding to things without being deluded, the nature abides of itself, the breath returns of itself and the elixir forms of itself; at the extreme of stillness comes a clap of thunder, white clouds pay homage at the summit, sweet dew sprinkles down, one drinks the wine of long life by oneself and listens quietly to the stringless tune — these twenty lines are the ladder up to Heaven.

Original text

养气忘言守，降心为不为。动静知宗祖，无事更寻谁？真常须应物，应物要不迷。不迷性自住，性住气自回。气回丹自结，壶中配坎离。阴阳生反复，普化一声雷。白云朝顶上，甘露洒须弥。自饮长生酒，逍遥谁得知？坐听无弦曲，明通造化机。都来二十句，端的上天梯。

To nourish the breath, forget words and keep guard; to subdue the mind, act without acting. In movement and stillness, know the ancestor; when there is nothing to do, whom else would you seek? The true constant must respond to things; in responding to things, one must not be deluded. Not deluded, the nature abides of itself; when the nature abides, the breath returns of itself. When the breath returns, the elixir forms of itself; in the gourd, pair Kan and Li. Yin and yang give birth to each other, back and forth; a single clap of thunder transforms everything. White clouds pay homage at the summit; sweet dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru. Drink the wine of long life by yourself — free and easy, who could know? Sitting, listen to the stringless tune; clearly penetrate the mechanism of creation. Twenty lines in all — truly a ladder up to Heaven.

(The above is the full text of the common edition, for reading aloud; a few characters in the version printed in this book differ slightly, such as “the true constant needs to respond to things” and “quietly listen to the stringless tune”; see the chapters.)

Reading tips

  1. The first four lines are the most worth reading again and again. Zhang Sanfeng’s comment “the ordinary person’s mind is ceaselessly agitated; when a practitioner wants the mind to enter stillness, what matters is to subdue the two eyes”, and his formula “let the eyes look at the nose, the nose at the navel; above and below attend to each other, mind and breath rely on each other”, can be used by anyone.
  2. Lu Qianxu’s explanation of “forgetting words” is important: forgetting words is not clamping the mouth shut and refusing to speak, but the spirit guarding within and both feelings and circumstances forgotten, so that naturally there is nothing to say — “if one deliberately keeps silent, shutting tight and hiding deep, it becomes a sickness of the mind instead”.
  3. Zhang Sanfeng’s formula on the third couplet, “fix the intention and everything goes wrong; do nothing and you fall into emptiness”, says everything about the right measure of effort in a single line.
  4. Lu Qianxu’s commentary on the sixth couplet uses “the woman dwells in the sun’s place, the man is matched with the toad palace” to explain “yin and yang back and forth”. This is a hexagram metaphor of the alchemical books (Kan and Li each hiding the other); we translate it as the yin and yang within a single body.

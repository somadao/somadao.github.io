Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Stele, with Two Commentaries · 前十句：养气降心，丹自结

The First Ten Lines: Nourishing the Breath, Subduing the Mind, the Elixir Forms of Itself

Hundred-Character Stele, first ten lines · To nourish the breath, forget words and keep guard; to subdue the mind, act without acting — when the nature abides, the breath returns of itself; when the breath returns, the elixir forms of itself

About This Chapter

The first ten lines (five couplets) cover the way from the first steps to the forming of the elixir:

The main points of Lu Qianxu’s commentary: forgetting words is “having no intention to speak, so that words do not come out of themselves”, not “deliberately keeping silent”; what is subdued is the deluded mind, not the true mind; within movement and stillness, the ancestor is nature and the lineage is life — “knowing the ancestor of nature, one cultivates concentration in the Li palace; knowing the lineage of life, one seeks the mystery in the watery storehouse”; the “true constant” is neither identical with things nor separate from them — “above, do not fall into annihilation; below, do not cling to forms”; and what is called abiding of itself, returning of itself, forming of itself is “in every case the wondrous function of the natural, what is called non-action within action. As soon as there is any arranging or setting up, one strays into the selfishness of the deviant and false”.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

养气忘言守，降心为不为。

To nourish the breath, forget words and keep guard; to subdue the mind, act without acting.

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §1
Original note (Chinese)

凡修行者，先须养气。养气之法，在乎忘言守一。忘言则气不散，守一则神不出。诀曰：缄舌静，抱神定。

凡人之心动荡不已，修行人心欲入静，贵乎制伏两眼。眼者心之门户，须垂帘塞兑，一切事体，以心为剑。想世事无益于我，火烈顿除，莫去贪着。诀云：以眼视鼻，以鼻视脐，上下相顾，心息相依，着意玄关，便可降伏思虑。

All who cultivate must first nourish the breath. The method of nourishing the breath lies in forgetting words and guarding the One: when words are forgotten, the breath does not scatter; when the One is guarded, the spirit does not go out. The formula says: hold the tongue still; embrace the spirit and settle.

The ordinary person’s mind is ceaselessly agitated. When a practitioner wants the mind to enter stillness, what matters is to subdue the two eyes. The eyes are the door of the mind; one must lower the curtains and close the mouth, and in all matters take the mind as one’s sword. Think that worldly affairs are of no benefit to you, and the raging fire in the heart is at once put out; do not crave or cling. The formula says: let the eyes look at the nose, the nose at the navel; above and below attend to each other, mind and breath relying on each other; fix the intention on the Mysterious Pass, and you can subdue thinking.

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §1
Original note (Chinese)

夫学道修真之子，进步入门，先须理会性命二字。性有性源，命有命蒂。性源要清静，命蒂要坚固。命蒂固则元气充，气充而精自盈矣。性源清则元神定，神定而气自灵矣。何谓命蒂？真息是也。何谓性源？心地是也。我师教人有法，开口便说养气降心。而养气降心，自有真诀，故曰：养气忘言守。忘言守，养气之真诀也。五字之中，忘字守字，要有下落。盖忘言者，非缄闭其口，而使之不言也。涵固精神，沉潜内守，情境两忘，无心于言，而言自不出也。若存心缄默，固闭深藏，反成心病。守之云者，守此气也。守之者谁？神守之也。守于何处？《道德经》云：“多言数穷，不如守中”。中者，神气归复之处，人之大中极也。《参同契》云：“闭塞其兑，筑固灵株”。闭兑者，即忘言之义。灵株者，即神气之根。盖能常守于此，则心息相依，子母相见，神气混融，打成一片，绵绵迤迤，久之而成大定。少焉，静极生动，真火熏蒸，金精吐华，冲关透顶，灌注上下，气得其养。其妙用有如此者。

《道德经》云：“人之生也，负阴而抱阳，冲气以为和”。要知人身中之气，即天地之冲气也。尔其升降阖辟，常与天地之气相为流通。医书谓此气周流人身，随呼吸以往来，昼夜八百一十丈，一呼一吸为一息，昼夜一万三千五百息，而息息各归于其根。庄子云：“真人之息以踵，众人之息以喉”。以踵者，心息相依归乎其根也。古仙有云：“昔日逢师传口诀，只要凝神入气穴”。忘言守中，非凝神入气穴而何？此之谓归根，此之谓复命。深根固蒂，长生久视之道，端在于此。

夫养气之诀，既已直露于前，故此下复说降心之诀。盖降心者，降伏妄心，非真心也。夫人之一心本来无二，但以迷觉而分真妄。人生而静，天之性也；感于物而动，性之欲也。既有欲矣，则情随境转，真以妄迷，纷然而起欲作之心。故《道德经》云：“化而欲作，吾将镇之以无名之朴”。今夫众人皆欲为，而我独镇之，以不为则妄念息，而此心将自降矣。妄本无体，皆因真心迷惑而然，今而不为，则必有以真见。夫一切有为之法，皆如梦幻泡影，虚妄不常，是以忘机绝虑，将此希求贪着之心，裂教粉碎，是谓以真销妄。妄尽真存，正觉现前，方名见性。如此则言不期忘而自忘守，不期固而自固，是知了命之宗。关于性地，我师十字之中，千古内炼之丹诀，无出于此，直至采药行火，抱元守一，彻始彻终无过此诀。妙哉！妙哉！

Those who study the Way and cultivate perfection, when they take their first steps through the gate, must first understand the two words “nature” and “life”. Nature has its source, life has its stalk. The source of nature must be clear and still; the stalk of life must be firm. When the stalk of life is firm, the original breath is full; when the breath is full, the essence fills of itself. When the source of nature is clear, the original spirit is settled; when the spirit is settled, the breath becomes numinous of itself. What is the stalk of life? It is the True Breath. What is the source of nature? It is the ground of the mind. My teacher (Patriarch Lü) had a method for teaching people: as soon as he opened his mouth he spoke of nourishing the breath and subduing the mind; and nourishing the breath and subduing the mind have their own true formula, so he says “to nourish the breath, forget words and keep guard” — “forget words and keep guard” is the true formula for nourishing the breath. Of these five characters, “forget” and “guard” must be given their proper footing. Forgetting words is not shutting the mouth tight and forbidding it to speak; it is cherishing and holding the essence and spirit, sinking down and guarding within, forgetting both feelings and circumstances, having no intention to speak, so that words do not come out of themselves. If one deliberately keeps silent, shutting tight and hiding deep, it becomes a sickness of the mind instead. “Guarding” means guarding this breath. Who guards it? The spirit guards it. Where is it guarded? The Daode jing says: “Much speech soon comes to an end; better to guard the center.” The “center” is where spirit and breath return and are restored, the great central pole of the human body. The Cantong qi says: “Shut the openings, build up and strengthen the numinous root.” “Shutting the openings” is the meaning of forgetting words; the “numinous root” is the root of spirit and breath. If one can always guard here, mind and breath rely on each other, mother and child meet, spirit and breath merge into one piece, continuous and unbroken, and in time this becomes great concentration. Before long, stillness reaches its extreme and gives rise to movement; the true fire steams, the golden essence puts forth its flower, breaks through the passes and pierces the crown, and pours through above and below — and the breath is nourished. Such is its wondrous function.

The Daode jing says: “When people are born, they carry yin on their backs and embrace yang, and the blending breath makes their harmony.” One must know that the breath in the human body is the blending breath of Heaven and Earth; its rising and falling, opening and closing, constantly flow together with the breath of Heaven and Earth. Medical books say this breath circulates through the body, coming and going with breathing, travelling eight hundred and ten zhang in a day and night; one exhalation and one inhalation make one breath, thirteen thousand five hundred breaths in a day and night, and every breath returns to its own root. Zhuangzi says: “The true person breathes from the heels; ordinary people breathe from the throat.” “From the heels” means mind and breath relying on each other and returning to their root. An ancient immortal said: “Long ago I met a teacher who passed on the oral formula: only concentrate the spirit in the cavity of breath.” Forgetting words and guarding the center — what is that if not concentrating the spirit in the cavity of breath? This is called returning to the root; this is called restoring life. The way of deep roots and a firm stalk, of long life and lasting vision, lies wholly here.

Since the formula for nourishing the breath has been revealed directly above, what follows speaks of the formula for subduing the mind. Subduing the mind means subduing the deluded mind, not the true mind. A person’s one mind is originally not two; only because of delusion and awakening is it divided into true and deluded. “A person is born still; that is the nature from Heaven. Moved by things, the nature has desires.” Once there are desires, feelings turn with circumstances, the true is confused by the deluded, and a mind that wants to act arises in confusion. That is why the Daode jing says: “If, as they are transformed, desire to act arises, I will calm it with the nameless uncarved block.” Everyone wants to act, and I alone calm it: by “not acting”, deluded thoughts cease, and this mind will subdue itself. The deluded mind originally has no substance; it all comes from the true mind being confused. If now one does not act, one is bound to see the true. All conditioned methods are like dreams, illusions, bubbles and shadows, empty, false and impermanent; so one forgets scheming and cuts off deliberation, and smashes this mind of seeking and clinging to pieces — this is called “dissolving the deluded with the true”. When the deluded is gone and the true remains, and right awakening is present, only then is it called seeing the nature. In this way words are forgotten of themselves without expecting to forget them, and guarding becomes firm of itself without expecting it to be firm; thus one knows that the principle of completing life depends on the ground of the nature. Within these ten characters of my teacher, all the alchemical formulas of inner refining through the ages are contained; right up to gathering the medicine and applying the fire, embracing the origin and guarding the One, from beginning to end nothing surpasses this formula. Wonderful! Wonderful!

§2
Original (Chinese)

动静知宗祖，无事更寻谁？

In movement and stillness, know the ancestor; apart from this, whom else would you seek?

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §2
Original note (Chinese)

动静者，一阴一阳也。宗祖者，生身之处也。修行人当知父母未生之前，即玄牝也。一身上下、乾坤、八卦、五行、四象聚会之处，乃天地未判之先一点灵光而成，即太极也。心之下，肾之上，仿佛之内，念头无息，所起之处，即是宗祖。所谓动静者，调和真气，安理真元也。盖呼接天根，吸接地根，即阖户之谓坤，辟户之谓乾。呼则龙吟云起，吸则虎啸风生。一阖一辟，一动一静，贵乎心意不动，任其真息往来，绵绵若存，调息至无息之息，打成一片，斯神可凝，丹可就矣。

若能养气忘言守，降伏身心，神归气穴，意注规中，混融一气，如鸡抱卵，如龙养珠，念兹在兹，须臾不离，日久功深，自然现出黍米之珠，光耀如日。默化元神，灵明莫测，即此是也。

Movement and stillness are the one yin and the one yang. The ancestor is the place from which the body is born. A practitioner should know that what was before father and mother gave birth is the Mysterious Female. The place in the body where Qian and Kun above and below, the eight trigrams, the five phases and the four images gather was formed from one point of numinous light before Heaven and Earth were divided — it is the Supreme Ultimate. Below the heart and above the kidneys, somewhere in between, where thoughts never cease, the place where they arise — that is the ancestor. What is called movement and stillness is harmonizing the true breath and settling the true origin. Exhaling connects with the root of Heaven, inhaling connects with the root of Earth — that is, “closing the door is called Kun, opening the door is called Qian”. When exhaling, the dragon sings and clouds rise; when inhaling, the tiger roars and wind arises. In each closing and opening, each movement and stillness, what matters is that the mind and intention do not move; let the True Breath come and go, continuous as if barely there, and regulate the breath until it is “the breath of no breathing”, merged into one piece — then the spirit can be concentrated and the elixir can be achieved.

If one can nourish the breath, forget words and keep guard, subdue body and mind, let the spirit return to the cavity of breath and the intention be fixed on the compass center, merging into one breath, like a hen brooding her egg, like a dragon nourishing its pearl, every thought here, never apart for an instant, then as the days lengthen and the work deepens, a pearl like a grain of millet will naturally appear, shining like the sun. Silently transforming the original spirit, numinous and bright beyond measure — this is it.

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §2
Original note (Chinese)

上言养气降心，静守内炼，乃无为之道。复恐世人不知，此外复有有作之基，乃高真上仙以术延命之事，故吃紧提出动静二字，要人知宗认祖。盖金丹之道，无为为体，有为为用，动中采，静中炼，二者不可偏废。故知动而不知静，则基址不立，而无积精累气之功。知静而不知动，则天机不合，而失临炉采药之旨。要之动其宗也，静其祖也。祖者性祖，静则得之。宗者命宗，非动不立也。知性祖，故修定于离宫。知命宗，故求玄于水府。如是双修，方为究竟。然方其无事之时，忘言默守，屏事息机，无思也，无为也，寂然不动，泰然大定，斯已矣，更俟寻谁？何以学道之人寻铅觅地，结侣求财，种种外求，席不暇暖，此中正好参详，方见良功心苦。我师说到此地，已将肝胆照人，分明指出修行门径。奈何世人不能领悟，直将容易读过，良可惜哉！

Above, nourishing the breath and subduing the mind, guarding in stillness and refining within, were spoken of; that is the way of non-action. But fearing that people of the world would not know that beyond this there is also a basis of deliberate work — the means by which high perfected ones and supreme immortals prolong life by method — he immediately brings up the two words “movement and stillness”, wanting people to know the lineage and recognize the ancestor. For the Way of the Golden Elixir takes non-action as its substance and action as its function; one gathers in movement and refines in stillness, and neither may be neglected. So if one knows movement but not stillness, the foundation is not laid and there is no work of accumulating essence and building up breath; if one knows stillness but not movement, one does not accord with the mechanism of Heaven and misses the point of approaching the furnace and gathering the medicine. In short, movement is its lineage and stillness its ancestor. The ancestor is the ancestor of nature, obtained through stillness; the lineage is the lineage of life, which cannot be established without movement. Knowing the ancestor of nature, one “cultivates concentration in the Li palace”; knowing the lineage of life, one “seeks the mystery in the watery storehouse”. Only such dual cultivation is complete. Yet when there is nothing to do, forgetting words and guarding silently, setting affairs aside and stilling scheming, without thought and without action, silent and unmoving, serene in great concentration — that is enough; whom else would you wait to seek? Why do students of the Way go searching for lead and looking for places, forming companionships and seeking wealth, all kinds of searching outside, without even time to warm their seats? This is just the place to examine carefully, and then one sees the Patriarch’s earnest care. When my teacher reached this point, he had already laid bare his heart and plainly pointed out the gateway of cultivation. Alas, people of the world cannot grasp it and simply read it through lightly — truly a pity!

§3
Original (Chinese)

真常要应物，应物要不迷。

The true constant needs to respond to things; in responding to things, one must not be deluded.

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §3
Original note (Chinese)

此道乃真常之道，以应事易于昏迷。故接物不可迷于尘事。若不应接，则空寂虚无。须要来则应之，事去不留，光明正大，乃是不迷。真性清静，元神凝结。诀曰：着意头头错，无为又落空。

This Way is the Way of the true constant; because one is easily confused in responding to affairs, in dealing with things one must not be lost in worldly matters. But if one does not respond at all, one falls into empty silence and void. One must respond when things come and not hold on when they are gone, open and upright — that is not being deluded. The true nature is clear and still, the original spirit congeals. The formula says: fix the intention and everything goes wrong; do nothing and you fall into emptiness. (As soon as you fix the intention, everything goes wrong; with no action at all, you fall into emptiness again.)

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §3
Original note (Chinese)

何谓真常？性祖是也。何以明之？凡所有相，皆是虚妄，会有变灭，而不能久。故佛经云：万法无性，惟此一真法界，方为实相，故曰真常。然所谓真常者非与物即，非与物离，要在能静能应常，应常静而常不迷。能不迷则应物无迹，而真性见矣，是谓炼己纯熟，而有为之道始可行也。二要字，上不要断灭，下不要着相，皆吃紧醒人之词。

What is the true constant? It is the ancestor of nature. How can this be shown? All that has form is empty and false, bound to change and perish, and cannot last. That is why the Buddhist scriptures say: the ten thousand dharmas have no self-nature; only this one true dharma-realm is the real form, and so it is called “the true constant”. Yet what is called the true constant is neither identical with things nor separate from them; the key is to be able to be still and able to respond: always responding, always still, and always undeluded. When one can be undeluded, one responds to things without leaving a trace, and the true nature appears; this is called refining the self to maturity, and only then can the way of action begin. As for the two words “needs” and “must”: the first means do not fall into annihilation, the second means do not cling to forms — both are urgent words to wake people up.

§4
Original (Chinese)

不迷性自住，性住气自回。

Not deluded, the nature abides of itself; when the nature abides, the breath returns of itself.

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §4
Original note (Chinese)

凡人性烈如火，喜怒哀乐，爱恶欲憎，变态无常，但有触动，便生妄想，难以静性。必要有真惩忿，则火降；真窒欲，则水升。身不动名曰炼精。炼精则虎啸，元神凝固。心不动名曰炼气。炼气则龙吟，元气存守。意不动名曰炼神。炼神则二气交，三元混，元气自回矣。三元者，精气神也。二气者，阴阳也。修行人应物不迷，则元神自归，本性自住矣。性住则身中先天之气自回，复命归根有何难哉？诀曰：回光返照，一心中存，内想不出，外想不入。

The ordinary person’s temper is as fierce as fire: joy, anger, sorrow and pleasure, love, hate, desire and loathing change without constancy; at the slightest touch, deluded thoughts arise, and it is hard to still the nature. One must truly restrain anger, and the fire descends; truly stop up desire, and the water rises. The body unmoving is called refining the essence; refining the essence, the tiger roars and the original spirit congeals. The heart unmoving is called refining the breath; refining the breath, the dragon sings and the original breath is preserved. The intention unmoving is called refining the spirit; refining the spirit, the two breaths of yin and yang unite, the three origins of essence, breath and spirit merge into one, and the original breath returns of itself. The three origins are essence, breath and spirit; the two breaths are yin and yang. When a practitioner responds to things without being deluded, the original spirit returns of itself and the original nature abides of itself. When the nature abides, the Prior Heaven breath in the body returns of itself — then what is difficult about restoring life and returning to the root? The formula says: revert the light to shine within, keep the one mind in the center; thoughts inside do not go out, thoughts outside do not come in.

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §4
Original note (Chinese)

对境忘情，方云大定。故曰不迷性自住。性住，则己汞住矣。己汞既住，方可求铅。故曰性住气自回。回者，来归之义。《契》云：“金来归性初，乃得称还丹”。曰归曰还，回之义备矣。

Only forgetting feelings when facing circumstances can be called great concentration; so it says “not deluded, the nature abides of itself”. When the nature abides, one’s own mercury abides; only when one’s own mercury abides can one seek the lead; so it says “when the nature abides, the breath returns of itself”. “Returns” means comes back. The Cantong qi says: “Only when the metal comes back to its original nature can it be called the reverted elixir.” With “comes back” and “reverts”, the meaning of “returns” is complete.

§5
Original (Chinese)

气回丹自结，壶中配坎离。

When the breath returns, the elixir forms of itself; in the gourd, pair Kan and Li.

Zhang Sanfeng's commentaryon §5
Original note (Chinese)

修行人性不迷尘事，则气自回。将见二气升降于中宫，阴阳配合于丹鼎，忽觉肾中一缕热气上冲心府，情来归性，如夫妇配合，如痴如醉，二气氤氲结成丹质，而气穴中水火相交循环不已，则神驭气，气留形，不必杂术自长生。诀曰：耳目口三宝，闭塞勿发通，真人潜深渊，浮游守规中，直至丹田气满，结成刀圭也。

When a practitioner’s nature is not lost in worldly matters, the breath returns of itself. One will see the two breaths of yin and yang rising and falling in the central palace and pairing in the elixir tripod; suddenly one feels a thread of warm breath in the kidneys surging up to the heart, feeling comes back to nature, like husband and wife pairing, as if dazed or drunk; the two breaths mingle densely and form the substance of the elixir, while in the cavity of breath water and fire unite and circulate without end. Then the spirit drives the breath and the breath keeps the form; without any miscellaneous arts, one naturally lives long. The formula says: the three treasures of ears, eyes and mouth — shut them, do not let them open outward; the true person sinks into the deep abyss, floating about, guarding the compass center. (Ears, eyes and mouth, these three treasures, must be shut and not open outward; the true person, hidden in the deep abyss, floats about and guards the compass center.) Until the elixir field is full of breath and the knife-point is formed.

Lu Qianxu's commentaryon §5
Original note (Chinese)

气自外回，丹从中结。壶中者，大丹凝结之处也。坎离者，阴阳互藏之卦象，铅汞水火之异名。丹法以乌兔为药材，必须取坎填离，以铅投汞，二者匀平配合，混入中宫，然后龙吟虎啸，而产玄珠于正位。其言自住自回自结者，要皆自然之妙用，所谓有为中之无为。一有安排布置，则涉于邪伪之私，而去道远矣！

The breath returns from outside; the elixir forms from within. “In the gourd” is the place where the great elixir congeals. “Kan and Li” are the hexagram images of yin and yang each hiding the other, other names for lead and mercury, water and fire. The elixir method takes the crow and the hare (sun and moon) as its ingredients; one must take from Kan to fill Li, and cast lead into mercury, pairing the two evenly and merging them into the central palace; then the dragon sings and the tiger roars, and the mysterious pearl is produced in the proper position. What is called abiding of itself, returning of itself and forming of itself is in every case the wondrous function of the natural, what is called non-action within action. As soon as there is any arranging or setting up, one strays into the selfishness of the deviant and false, and is far from the Way!

Key Terms

  • Forgetting words and guarding the One: Zhang Sanfeng’s commentary; Lu’s commentary: forgetting words is not shutting the mouth but having no intention to speak.
  • “Hold the tongue still, embrace the spirit and settle”: Zhang Sanfeng’s formula.
  • Subduing the two eyes: the eyes are the door of the mind; see lowering the curtains.
  • “Let the eyes look at the nose, the nose at the navel”: Zhang Sanfeng’s formula; see looking at the tip of the nose.
  • Source of nature, stalk of life: the source of nature is the ground of the mind, the stalk of life is the True Breath; see inner nature / life and True Breath.
  • Guarding the center: Daode jing, “Much speech soon comes to an end; better to guard the center”; see Central Yellow.
  • “Shut the openings, build up and strengthen the numinous root”: Cantong qi; shutting the openings is forgetting words, the numinous root is the root of spirit and breath.
  • Blending breath: the harmonizing breath of Heaven and Earth.
  • Subduing the mind: subduing the deluded mind, not the true mind.
  • “Calm it with the nameless uncarved block”: Daode jing, chapter 37.
  • Dissolving the deluded with the true: when the deluded is gone and the true remains and right awakening is present, only then is it called seeing the nature.
  • Ancestor and lineage: the place from which the body is born, below the heart and above the kidneys; Lu’s commentary: the ancestor is the ancestor of nature (stillness), the lineage the lineage of life (movement). See Mysterious Opening / Mysterious Pass.
  • “Closing the door is called Kun, opening the door is called Qian”: Appended Statements, a metaphor for the opening and closing of breathing.
  • The breath of no breathing: breath regulation at its utmost; see regulating the breath.
  • Cultivating concentration in the Li palace, seeking the mystery in the watery storehouse: first cultivate concentration of the mind, then seek the work on life; see conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • True constant: the ancestor of nature; neither identical with things nor separate from them, able to be still and able to respond.
  • “Fix the intention and everything goes wrong; do nothing and you fall into emptiness”: Zhang Sanfeng’s formula.
  • Restraining anger and stopping up desire: from the Image commentary on the Sun (Decrease) hexagram of the Book of Changes.
  • “The body unmoving refines the essence, the heart unmoving refines the breath, the intention unmoving refines the spirit”: Zhang Sanfeng’s commentary.
  • Three origins: essence, breath and spirit.
  • “Thoughts inside do not go out, thoughts outside do not come in”: the formula for reverting the light to shine within.
  • “Only when the metal comes back to its original nature can it be called the reverted elixir”: Cantong qi.
  • In the gourd: the place where the great elixir congeals. Knife-point: another name for the elixir; see Golden Flower.
  • The three treasures of ears, eyes and mouth: see Three Lights.
  • Non-action within action: abiding of itself, returning of itself, forming of itself.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Zhang Sanfeng’s formula for beginners: first take care of the eyes. “The eyes are the door of the mind”: lower the eyelids, let the gaze rest lightly on the tip of the nose, then follow the breathing down to feel the lower belly — above and below attend to each other, mind and breath rely on each other.
  2. Lu Qianxu’s reminder: forgetting words is not forcing yourself to keep silent. “If one deliberately keeps silent, shutting tight and hiding deep, it becomes a sickness of the mind instead.” When the mind is still, you naturally do not want to speak — that is forgetting words.
  3. “Fix the intention and everything goes wrong; do nothing and you fall into emptiness.” Using force is wrong; letting go completely is empty — between the two lies fire phasing.
  4. The body unmoving, the heart unmoving, the intention unmoving are three levels of stillness: first let the body settle, then let the mind stop grasping, and finally let the intention also come to rest.
  5. “Respond when things come and do not hold on when they are gone” — this is the best way to carry quiet sitting into daily life.
  6. “As soon as there is any arranging or setting up, one strays into the selfishness of the deviant and false” — the elixir forms by itself, the breath returns by itself; all we do is stay undeluded and guard the center.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text in this book reads “the true constant needs (yao 要) to respond to things”, while the common edition reads “must (xu 须) respond to things”; we follow this book.
  2. Lu’s commentary, “如此则言不期忘而自忘守，不期固而自固”: the punctuation should be “言不期忘而自忘，守不期固而自固” (words are forgotten of themselves without expecting to forget them; guarding becomes firm of itself without expecting it to be firm); we translate accordingly.
  3. Lu’s commentary, “是知了命之宗。关于性地”: these should be read together as “是知了命之宗，关于性地” (thus one knows that the principle of completing life depends on the ground of the nature); we translate accordingly.
  4. “My teacher”: in his commentary Lu Qianxu calls Patriarch Lü “my teacher”; he claimed to have received direct transmission from Patriarch Lü.
  5. “Medical books say this breath … eight hundred and ten zhang in a day and night … thirteen thousand five hundred breaths”: an ancient medical view from the “Fifty Circuits” chapter of the Lingshu, translated as in the original.
  6. “Searching for lead and looking for places, forming companionships and seeking wealth”: all kinds of searching outside (for external medicines, places to practice, Daoist companions, funds); Lu Qianxu considers none of them as good as seeking within.

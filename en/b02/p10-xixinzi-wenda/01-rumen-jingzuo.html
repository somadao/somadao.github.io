Xixinzi’s Answers to Students’ Questions · 入门与静坐

Entering the Way and Quiet Sitting

Xixinzi’s Answers to Students’ Questions, section 1 · Entering the Way and quiet sitting — dual cultivation of nature and life, first methods, stopping thoughts below the navel, spirit and breath relying on each other, seeing the nature

About This Chapter

This section is the introductory part of the whole, thirteen exchanges going from “what is dual cultivation of nature and life” to “how to sit”, “where to put the mind” and “what is seeing the nature”. Xixinzi’s teaching can be summed up as one line:

StepXixinzi’s words
PreparationPut down all ties, find a quiet small room, spread a thick bed mat, loosen your clothes and belt
PostureSit upright, legs crossed, mouth closed, eyes closed, hands clasped and resting on the legs; the whole body lively, never using force
Where to put the mindWhen there are many thoughts, rest it in the elixir field below the navel (the Cavity of Breath); when the mind is still, rest it in stillness
BreathingLet the breath through the nose follow its own nature, even, neither slow nor hurried
The right measureKeeping to clarity, stillness and naturalness is called not forgetting; following clarity, stillness and naturalness is called not helping
Which stageNo thoughts and regulated breath is spirit and breath relying on each other; further stillness to the dim and obscure, self and others both forgotten, is seeing the nature, the first return to emptiness

The most important sentence is: “If you have not practiced it yourself, however clear the theory may be, it is in the end only the moon in the water and a flower in the mirror.”

Original Text and Translation

§1What is dual cultivation of nature and life
Original (Chinese)

问：何谓性命双修？

答：何谓道？《易》曰：“一阴一阳”是也。修道者，修此阴阳也。吕祖云：“丹经部部说阴阳，二字名为万法王”。此个总名，为天地人物，生生化化之根源，横遍竖穷，莫非此也。且人类无论男女，皆各全阴阳二气，《易》曰“各正性命”是也，不假外求，不过一落后天，性在天边，命沉海底，性命名宿耳，故当养其二气，均则生，偏则病，尽则死矣。修道者将各宿之二气，益之累之，以清静功夫，和合凝集以复其初，即是以神宰气，以性制命，二者相需而行，故为性命双修。

Question: What is dual cultivation of nature and life?

Answer: What is the Way? The Book of Changes says: “One yin and one yang — that is the Way.” Cultivating the Way is cultivating this yin and yang. Patriarch Lü says: “Every alchemical scripture speaks of yin and yang; these two words may be called the king of all methods.” This general name is the source of all the generation and transformation of Heaven, Earth, people and things; across all space and through all time, there is nothing that is not this. Moreover, all human beings, men and women alike, each possess the two breaths of yin and yang complete — the Book of Changes says “each makes its nature and life correct” — and there is no need to seek them outside. It is only that once one falls into the Posterior Heaven, the nature floats at the edge of the sky and life sinks to the bottom of the sea; nature and life each dwell in a place of their own. So one should nourish these two breaths: when they are balanced, one lives; when one-sided, one falls ill; when exhausted, one dies. Those who cultivate the Way increase and accumulate the two breaths that dwell apart, and with the work of clarity and stillness bring them together and concentrate them to restore their original state — this is directing the breath with the spirit, governing life with the nature; the two need each other and proceed together, and so it is called dual cultivation of nature and life.

§2First methods
Original (Chinese)

问：请示初步功法。

答：夫人之所以生存者，阴阳二气也，道亦无非养此二气而已。培养之法，以清静为主，日间诸事不令入心，万缘放下，寻僻静小室独处，厚铺床褥，盘足端坐，闭目存神，于内观照，如坐高山，如燃天灯，凝起神了，鼻息顺其自然，勿忘勿助，神息相依，则守其清静自然，如静极欲睡，则熟睡一次，醒来仍旧静坐。如坐久不舒，则缓步园庭一时，仍入坐，饭后亦宜缓步，有病恐不能久坐，即照希夷老祖蛰龙法睡功甚妙，法则活而诀则一。初入坐念多，当止于脐下，久之心静，则止于静。照此恒勤行去，自有好音。此静以生阴，阴极生阳之理也。凡智者多知见，杂念难除，须下斩钉截铁之功，由勉而安，心死神活。

凡入坐时，内外皆以活泼为要，但又不离乎规矩，即身中衣带，亦须空松，以免缚束气机。初入坐，杂念纷扰，当毅力凝神于内，如念静时，则当放松，然又非任其放浪，必维系乎规矩之中，往来乎方圆之内（规圆象天，矩方象地，规矩方圆四字，亦要参悟）。丹书所谓当随当顺当守等法，有时有地，皆与人以规矩，非着意之谓，其巧处在行功时体察，适当为要。所谓“观照”，观则慧眼，照如日月。二句是何意义？慧眼无处不观，实未观，真是观。日月无处不照，本未照，真是照。明乎此义，始可以言“观照”。所以古人丹书，一字不苟，并有言外之旨，岂粗心人能猜测者哉。经曰：“真参实悟。”如何为真参？从力行中去参，始得其真。如何为实悟？在笃行内去悟，始得其实。换言之，硬要有恒，努力去做，方能实悉。所以如自未实行，任随理论如何清楚，终属水月镜花，难明真相。《浅说》云：“求诀中诀，要诚心人。”诚心人即实行者，实行达到那个景况，自家知道那个法诀，便是诀中诀也。

Question: Please show me the first methods.

Answer: What keeps people alive is the two breaths of yin and yang; the Way too is nothing but nourishing these two breaths. The method of cultivating them takes clarity and stillness as its main thing: do not let the affairs of the day enter the mind; put down all ties; find a quiet small room to be alone in, spread a thick bed mat, sit upright with legs crossed, close the eyes and preserve the spirit, observing and illuminating within — like sitting on a high mountain, like lighting a heavenly lamp. Once the spirit is concentrated, let the breath through the nose follow its own nature, neither forgetting nor helping, spirit and breath relying on each other, and then keep to this clarity, stillness and naturalness. If stillness reaches its extreme and you want to sleep, sleep soundly once, and when you wake, go back to quiet sitting. If you have sat a long time and are uncomfortable, walk slowly in the garden for a while, then sit again; walking slowly after meals is also good. Those who are ill and fear they cannot sit long can follow the sleeping practice of the “Hibernating Dragon Method” of the old Patriarch Xiyi (Chen Tuan), which is excellent — the method is alive, the formula is one. When you first sit and have many thoughts, rest the mind below the navel; after a while, when the mind is still, rest it in stillness. If you keep at it like this constantly and diligently, good news will naturally come. This is the principle of stillness giving birth to yin and yin at its extreme giving birth to yang. Clever people have many views and opinions, and their stray thoughts are hard to remove; they must put in work as decisive as cutting nails and chopping iron, going from effort to ease — the mind dead and the spirit alive.

Whenever you sit, liveliness inside and out is the key, yet without departing from the rules; even your clothes and belt must be loose, so as not to bind the mechanism of the breath. When you first sit and stray thoughts swarm, use your will to concentrate the spirit within; when the thoughts become still, you should relax — but not let them run loose; you must keep them within the rules, letting them come and go within the square and the circle (the compass is round and stands for Heaven, the square is square and stands for Earth; the four words “compass, square, circle, square” must also be contemplated). The methods the alchemical books describe — “should follow”, “should go along with”, “should guard” — each have their time and place; all give people rules, and do not mean applying the intention deliberately. Their knack lies in sensing them during practice; what is appropriate is the key. What is called “observing and illuminating”: observing is the eye of wisdom, illuminating is like the sun and moon. What do these two phrases mean? The eye of wisdom observes everywhere, yet in fact does not observe — that is true observing. The sun and moon illuminate everywhere, yet originally do not illuminate — that is true illuminating. Only when you understand this meaning can you speak of “observing and illuminating”. So the alchemical books of the ancients are exact in every word and have meanings beyond the words — how could careless people guess them? The scripture says: “True inquiry, real awakening.” What is true inquiry? Inquire through vigorous practice, and you will get the truth. What is real awakening? Awaken within steadfast practice, and you will get the reality. In other words, you must have perseverance and work hard; only then can you really understand. So if you have not practiced it yourself, however clear the theory may be, it is in the end only the moon in the water and a flower in the mirror, and the truth is hard to see. The Plain Words says: “To seek the formula within the formula requires a sincere person.” A sincere person is one who practices; when practice reaches that state, you yourself know that formula — and that is the formula within the formula.

§3Stopping thoughts below the navel
Original (Chinese)

问：初入坐杂念多，当止心于脐下，未审如何止法，方为合式。

答：入坐时念多，则止于脐下丹田。丹田分上中下，此是脐下丹田，要参悟。看《循途录》、《初层炼心》，便知的确。心止于是，犹之系马有桩，如系稳到神息相依时，则守其清静自然，名勿忘，顺其清静自然，名勿助。勿忘勿助，便是合式。

脐下丹田，又名气穴，此穴为古今修炼者一定不移之处。修道初乘，行炼精化气之功，自起首以至大丹，从中采取烹炼运行，种种行功，皆在此间。此间即中也。

Question: When I first sit, I have many stray thoughts, and I should rest the mind below the navel. I do not know how to rest it so that it is right.

Answer: When you sit and have many thoughts, rest them in the elixir field below the navel. The elixir field is divided into upper, middle and lower; this is the lower Cinnabar Field below the navel — contemplate this. Read the Record of Following the Path and The First Level of Refining the Mind, and you will know exactly where it is. Resting the mind here is like tying a horse to a post; once it is tied firmly, when spirit and breath come to rely on each other, keep to this clarity, stillness and naturalness — this is called “not forgetting”; follow this clarity, stillness and naturalness — this is called “not helping”. Neither forgetting nor helping — that is right.

The elixir field below the navel is also called the Cavity of Breath; this cavity is the fixed, unchanging place for practitioners old and new. In the first vehicle of cultivating the Way, doing the work of refining essence into breath, from the very beginning up to the great elixir, all the gathering, cooking, refining and circulating, every kind of work, takes place here. This place is the “center”.

§4Spirit and breath relying on each other
Original (Chinese)

问：神息相依时，则守其清静自然，须坐至如何地步，方为神息相依之时，神息相依之景如何？请分别示知。

答：神息是两项，即凝神调息。心止于脐下曰凝神，气归于脐下曰调息。脐下二字要参悟，以铜人仰卧图测之，则与前七后三之说符，以本人中指中节量之，则其穴之上下大小，恰与相合。坐到无念，则神凝矣。鼻息细微，则息调矣。无念息调，即神息相依时也。

Question: “When spirit and breath rely on each other, keep to clarity, stillness and naturalness” — to what point must one sit for it to be the time when spirit and breath rely on each other? What is the scene of spirit and breath relying on each other? Please tell me each in turn.

Answer: Spirit and breath are two items, namely concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath. The mind resting below the navel is called concentrating the spirit; the breath returning below the navel is called regulating the breath. Contemplate the words “below the navel”: measured on the bronze figure lying on its back, it matches the saying “seven in front and three behind”; measured with the middle joint of your own middle finger, the size and position of the cavity fit exactly. When you sit until there are no thoughts, the spirit is concentrated; when the breath through the nose is fine and faint, the breath is regulated. No thoughts and regulated breath — that is the time when spirit and breath rely on each other.

§5Observing and illuminating
Original (Chinese)

问：以神观照，“观照”二字，于丹经往往见之，但未知究竟作何解释，其法若何？

答：观照者，返观内照也。入坐时，返回驰外之念，观照于内之脐下也。脐下二字，请认的确。

Question: “Observing and illuminating with the spirit” — the words “observing and illuminating” are often seen in the alchemical scriptures, but I do not know what they really mean. What is the method?

Answer: Observing and illuminating is turning the gaze back and illuminating within. When you sit, bring back the thoughts that rush outward, and observe and illuminate below the navel within. Please identify the words “below the navel” precisely.

§6Original spirit and cognitive spirit
Original (Chinese)

问：存神神息，及以神之神，是否均指后天思虑之识神，抑系指先天之元神？乞详示。

答：神固要用元，须知元从何来。初入坐，杂念纷纷，本是识神，由识勉强做到念静不动时，岂非元乎。其实还是一个神。以有念无念分之，转识即成元矣。

Question: The “spirit” in “preserving the spirit”, “spirit and breath” and “with the spirit” — does it always refer to the Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit of deliberation, or to the Prior Heaven Original Spirit? Please explain in detail.

Answer: The spirit should of course be the original spirit, but you must know where the original comes from. When you first sit and stray thoughts swarm, that is the cognitive spirit; when, from the cognitive, you work your way to the point where thoughts are still and unmoving, is that not the original? In fact it is still the same spirit, distinguished only by having thoughts or having none — turn the cognitive around and it becomes the original.

§7What books to read
Original (Chinese)

问：初练丹功，应读何书？

答：古来千经万典，皆是一道。古经词奥喻深，初学难明，唯伍冲虚、柳华阳、李涵虚三真人之书，最显，最精详，《伍柳仙宗》、《道窍谈》、《三车秘旨》、《九层炼心循途录》等是。其他如《吕祖百句章》、张三丰《道言浅近说》、《玄机直讲》等，亦宜细读。

Question: When first practicing alchemy, what books should one read?

Answer: The thousands of scriptures and ten thousand classics of the past all speak of the one Way. The ancient scriptures are obscure in wording and deep in metaphor, hard for beginners to understand; only the books of the three Perfected Ones Wu Chongxu, Liu Huayang and Li Hanxu are the clearest and most precise and detailed — The Immortal Lineage of Wu and Liu, Talks on the Openings of the Dao, the Secret Principles of the Three Carts, the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind with the Record of Following the Path, and so on. Others, such as Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter and Zhang Sanfeng’s Plain and Simple Words on the Dao and Straight Talk on the Mysterious Pivot, should also be read carefully.

§8The three vehicles of the elixir method
Original (Chinese)

问：西派祖师李涵虚将道分为三乘，请问如何分法？

答：涵虚祖师以中乘名上乘，上乘名大乘，其言曰：“夫丹有二品，而分之则有三乘”。三乘丹法，皆采铅华，皆称还丹，但有大小先后之不同耳。一曰初乘，名为结丹，又名玉液还丹，后天中返先天，去癸取壬，而以玉液培之，圆成内丹，此尽性之学，人仙也。一曰上乘，即号还丹，又曰七返，以后天所返之先天，炼出先天，立为丹母，此立命之学，地仙也。一曰大乘，名为九转大还丹，其药以十五夜月圆为喻，先天中先天，火到即行，化为白液，吞归腹内，凝为至坚，是为金液还丹，至灵至妙，成圣成真，此性命归了之学，天仙也。

Question: Li Hanxu, the patriarch of the Western school, divided the Way into three vehicles. How did he divide them?

Answer: Patriarch Hanxu called what is usually called the “middle vehicle” the “upper vehicle”, and what is usually called the “upper vehicle” the “great vehicle”. He said: “The elixir has two grades, and divided, there are three vehicles.” All three vehicles of the elixir method gather the flower of lead, and all are called the reverted elixir; they differ only in size and order.

The first is the first vehicle, called forming the elixir, also called the jade-liquid reverted elixir: within the Posterior Heaven one returns to the Prior Heaven, removing the gui water and taking the ren water, then nourishing it with jade liquid to perfect the inner elixir. This is the learning of fulfilling the nature, which makes a human immortal.

The second is the upper vehicle, simply called the reverted elixir, also called “the seven returns”: from the Prior Heaven returned from the Posterior Heaven, one refines out the Prior Heaven and establishes it as the mother of the elixir. This is the learning of establishing life, which makes an earthly immortal.

The third is the great vehicle, called the great reverted elixir of the nine cycles: its medicine is likened to the full moon on the fifteenth night; it is the Prior Heaven within the Prior Heaven; when the fire arrives one acts, it turns into white liquid, is swallowed back into the belly and congeals into the hardest thing — this is the golden-liquid reverted elixir, utterly numinous and utterly wondrous, making one a sage and a perfected one. This is the learning of completing both nature and life, which makes a celestial immortal.

§9The real meaning of the One
Original (Chinese)

问：请问一之实义。

答：一非虚名也，即太极也，玄关也，先天一气也。功夫到无人无我无物，以至空无所空，寂无所寂，无无亦无，则先天一气，从虚无中来，来时，气发则成窍，机息则渺茫，此即一之实义也。借一之形，炼一之气，即得一矣。盗天地，夺造化，无限仙阶，从此拾级而登，故曰得一而万事毕。

Question: Please tell me the real meaning of the One.

Answer: The One is not an empty name; it is the Supreme Ultimate, the Mysterious Pass, the One Breath of Prior Heaven. When the work reaches no others, no self, no things, until emptiness has nothing left to empty and silence has nothing left to silence, and even nothingness is not, then the One Breath of Prior Heaven comes out of emptiness. When it comes, the breath stirs and becomes an opening; when the mechanism rests, it is vague and boundless — this is the real meaning of the One. Borrowing the form of the One to refine the breath of the One is obtaining the One. Robbing Heaven and Earth, seizing creation — the endless steps of immortality are all climbed one by one from here; so it is said, “obtain the One and the ten thousand affairs are complete”.

§10Three levels of work within a quarter of an hour
Original (Chinese)

问：何谓静功在一刻之中亦有三层功夫在内？

答：张三丰祖师云：静功在一刻，一刻之中，亦有炼精化气、炼气化神、炼神还虚三层功夫在内。即一时一日一月一年皆然，并不是不分次序也。例如坐下闭目存神，使心静息调，即是炼精化气之功；回光返照，凝神丹穴，使真息来往，内中静极而动，动极而静，无限天机，即是炼气化神之功；如此真气朝元，阴阳反复交媾一番，自然风恬浪静，我于此时，正念止于丹田，即是封固火候，亦即是炼神还虚之功也。年月日时，久久行此三部工夫，以至真空真静，十年百年，打破虚空，与太虚同体，乃无次序之可言。

Question: What does it mean that “within a quarter of an hour of quiet practice there are also three levels of work”?

Answer: Patriarch Zhang Sanfeng says: even if quiet practice lasts only a quarter of an hour, within that quarter of an hour there are also the three levels of work of refining essence into breath, refining breath into spirit and refining spirit to return to emptiness; and so it is with a double hour, a day, a month and a year — it does not mean that there is no order. For example: sitting down, closing the eyes and preserving the spirit, making the mind still and the breath regulated, is the work of refining essence into breath; reverting the light to shine within, concentrating the spirit in the elixir cavity, letting the True Breath come and go, within stillness reaching its extreme and turning to movement, movement reaching its extreme and turning to stillness, the mechanism of Heaven without limit, is the work of refining breath into spirit; when in this way the true breath returns to the origin and yin and yang unite back and forth for a while, naturally the wind dies down and the waves calm; at this time I rest right thought in the elixir field — this is the fire phasing of sealing and securing, and also the work of refining spirit to return to emptiness. Year, month, day and double hour, practice these three stages of work for a long time, until true emptiness and true stillness; in ten years or a hundred years, one breaks through emptiness and becomes one body with the Great Void — and then there is no order to speak of.

§11What is seeing the nature
Original (Chinese)

问：何谓见性？

答：《百句章》已明明指出，力行由无念而静，静而气平，气平息住，归还生身受气的根本地，自己见到本来面目，方是真见性，又即丹经所谓玄关发露，又即《易》之太极，又即我佛如来。如来者何？如我生来之时也。试思如生来之初，是否气伏息住，混沌杳冥，浑然一团，中含一点，名曰太极。虽云一点，实空虚无形；虽云空虚，其中有灵觉之神气在内，此便是真空。以其中空而有灵觉，静而生阴，动而生阳，即性根命蒂也，丹经又名真土，又名真意。动而生出一阳，从神妙中生出有来，此便是妙有。从此生生不已，则起朽生枯，七返九还，功成皆基于此。

Question: What is seeing the nature?

Answer: The Hundred-Line Chapter has already pointed it out plainly: practice vigorously, going from no thoughts to stillness, from stillness to the breath becoming even, from the breath becoming even to the breathing coming to rest, returning to the fundamental place where one was born and received breath, and seeing one’s own original face — only that is truly seeing the nature. It is also what the alchemical scriptures call “the Mysterious Pass revealing itself”, the Supreme Ultimate of the Book of Changes, and the “Tathāgata” of the Buddhists. What is the “Tathāgata” (rulai, “thus come”)? It is “as I was when I came into life”. Consider: at the very beginning, when you were born, was not the breath hidden and the breathing at rest, chaotic, dark and obscure, an undifferentiated whole containing one point within? This is called the Supreme Ultimate. Although it is called a point, it is in reality empty and formless; although it is called empty, within it there is the numinous, aware breath of the spirit — this is “true emptiness”. Because it is empty within yet has numinous awareness, in stillness it gives rise to yin and in movement to yang; it is the root of the nature and the stalk of life; the alchemical scriptures also call it the true earth and also the True Intention. Moving, it gives rise to one yang, bringing “being” out of the spirit’s wonder — this is “wondrous being”. From here it generates without end, raising the rotten and reviving the withered; the achievement of the seven returns and nine reversions is all built on this.

§12Noise and sleep
Original (Chinese)

问：静坐如何对待喧闹与睡眠？

答：人既立定修身之志，日常刻刻检点放心，于未坐之先，有杂妄则扫除净尽，然后端坐，盘足闭口，握手仰置腿上，瞑目回光，返照脐下丹田穴，先将呼吸调匀，不缓不急，气息绵绵，深入丹田。如心静息微，则守着清静自然的景况，又当勿助勿忘，久之恍恍惚惚，到忘人忘我时，则神气融和矣，以后便有阳生之现相。如日夜静坐中，有喧闹之声入耳，注意定心调息，勿着喧闹，习而久之，自然闹中亦静矣。静坐极易睡眠，其初要力敌睡魔。如坐至静极，睡去亦无妨害，醒来如活子时发现，急起端坐，行采炼固养各法，风平浪息为要。

Question: In quiet sitting, how should one deal with noise and with sleep?

Answer: Once a person has made up his mind to cultivate himself, in daily life he must check his strayed mind at every moment. Before sitting, sweep away all stray and deluded thoughts, then sit upright, legs crossed, mouth closed, hands clasped and resting palms up on the legs, close the eyes and revert the light, illuminating the elixir field cavity below the navel. First make the breathing even, neither slow nor hurried, the breath continuous, deep into the elixir field. When the mind is still and the breath faint, keep to this state of clarity, stillness and naturalness, again neither helping nor forgetting; after a while, dim and indistinct, when you forget others and forget yourself, spirit and breath merge, and afterward there will be the phenomenon of yang being born. If during quiet sitting by day or night noises enter your ears, attend to settling the mind and regulating the breath, and do not cling to the noise; with practice, naturally even in noise you will be still. Quiet sitting very easily leads to sleep; at first you must fight hard against the demon of sleep. If you sit until stillness reaches its extreme and fall asleep, there is no harm; if on waking the Living Zi Hour appears, get up quickly, sit upright, and carry out the methods of gathering, refining, securing and nourishing, with the wind calm and the waves still as the key.

§13Posture, reverting the light, and the Living Zi Hour
Original (Chinese)

问：静坐之姿式如何？何谓回光返照与活子时？

答：静坐在外面之形式有五：“端坐”，“盘足”，“闭口”，“合眼”，“握手置腿上”。以上形式，不可着力，全身骨肉，须令活泼，即衣带亦要空松无拘。在内一方面，合眼后，将目光内观下丹田，即回光返照也。两耳亦内听，与目光同住，鼻息不要达到丹田，内息调至似有似无，心便静了，须听其自然，勿助勿忘。此精微处，要在身中体察适当。如何为适当，即是心里念头一不动时，便守其清静自然，而入恍惚杳冥之景象也。每坐皆能达到此景象，则智慧渐增矣。所问活子时者，即是身中阳气发动时，为活动之子时，非日夜钟表不移之子时也。若身中阳气发动时，急端坐静定，收藏阳气为要。

Question: What is the posture for quiet sitting? What are reverting the light to shine within and the Living Zi Hour?

Answer: The outward form of quiet sitting has five items: “sitting upright”, “crossing the legs”, “closing the mouth”, “closing the eyes”, and the firm grip (“clasping the hands and resting them on the legs”). None of these forms may use force; the bones and flesh of the whole body must be lively, and even clothes and belt must be loose and unconstrained. On the inner side: after closing the eyes, turn the gaze inward to observe the lower elixir field — that is reverting the light to shine within. The two ears also listen inward, resting together with the gaze. Do not (deliberately) send the breath through the nose to the elixir field; regulate the inner breath until it seems present and absent, and the mind becomes still; then let it follow its own nature, neither helping nor forgetting. This subtle point must be sensed appropriately within the body. What is appropriate? It is that as soon as the thoughts in the mind stop moving, you keep to clarity, stillness and naturalness and enter the scene of the dim and obscure. If you can reach this scene every time you sit, wisdom will gradually increase. The Living Zi Hour you ask about is the time when the yang breath in the body stirs — the “living zi hour”, not the fixed zi hour of day and night on the clock. When the yang breath in the body stirs, quickly sit upright, still and settled; storing the yang breath is the key.

Key Terms

  • Dual cultivation of nature and life: directing the breath with the spirit, governing life with the nature, the two needing each other and proceeding together; see inner nature / life.
  • The nature at the edge of the sky, life at the bottom of the sea: in the Posterior Heaven person, nature and life dwell apart and cannot unite.
  • Hibernating Dragon Method: the sleeping practice of Chen Tuan (Xiyi).
  • Neither forgetting nor helping: keeping to clarity, stillness and naturalness is called not forgetting; following clarity, stillness and naturalness is called not helping.
  • Observing and illuminating: turning the gaze back and illuminating within; see Reverting the Light; “the eye of wisdom observes everywhere, yet in fact does not observe — that is true observing”.
  • Formula within the formula: the formula you yourself know when practice reaches that state.
  • The elixir field below the navel, the Cavity of Breath: the place of all the work of the first vehicle; see lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Seven in front and three behind: the Cavity of Breath lies between the navel and the waist, seven from the navel behind and three from the waist in front; see section 2, §18.
  • Spirit and breath relying on each other: concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath united; no thoughts and regulated breath is spirit and breath relying on each other. See regulating the breath.
  • Turning the cognitive into the original: the cognitive spirit, still to the point of no thoughts, is the original spirit; see Original Spirit and cognitive spirit.
  • Three vehicles: the first vehicle (jade-liquid reverted elixir, human immortal), the upper vehicle (reverted elixir of the seven returns, earthly immortal), the great vehicle (great golden-liquid reverted elixir of the nine cycles, celestial immortal); see the three transmutations.
  • Removing the gui water and taking the ren water: removing the Posterior Heaven gui water that has form and taking the Prior Heaven ren water that is formless.
  • The One: the Supreme Ultimate, the Mysterious Pass, the One Breath of Prior Heaven; see One Breath of Prior Heaven and Mysterious Opening / Mysterious Pass.
  • True emptiness, wondrous being: empty within yet with numinous awareness is true emptiness; moving and giving rise to one yang is wondrous being.
  • Root of the nature and stalk of life, true earth: see True Intention.
  • Living Zi Hour: the time when the yang breath in the body stirs, not the zi hour on the clock; see Living Zi Hour.
  • The dim and obscure: see the dim and obscure.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Before sitting, sweep the mind; while sitting, loosen your clothes and belt and use no force anywhere in the body. The five outward forms (sitting upright, crossing the legs, closing the mouth, closing the eyes, clasping the hands) all “may not use force”.
  2. When there are many thoughts, rest them below the navel; when the mind is still, rest it in stillness. Resting the mind in the elixir field is like a hitching post for a horse; once it is tied firmly, there is no need to stare at it — guarding without guarding rigidly, letting go without letting loose, is neither forgetting nor helping.
  3. Do not push the breath down to the elixir field. Just make the breathing even and let it follow its nature until it seems present and absent, and the mind becomes still of itself.
  4. If you want to sleep, sleep; when you wake, sit again; if you have sat long and are uncomfortable, take a walk. Quiet sitting is not grim endurance.
  5. Clever people have many stray thoughts and must put in decisive work. But this force is applied to “letting go”, not to the body.
  6. However clearly the theory is explained, if you do not practice it yourself, it is only the moon in the water and a flower in the mirror.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original is a series of questions and answers without headings; the subheadings of each passage are ours, based on the content of the questions.
  2. “Nature and life each dwell in a place of their own” (xingming ming su er 性命名宿耳): meaning that nature and life each have their own dwelling (su in the sense of “dwell”), one above and one below, and cannot unite.
  3. “The Plain Words”: Zhang Sanfeng’s Plain and Simple Words on the Dao; see the seventh part of this book.
  4. “The Record of Following the Path”, “The First Level of Refining the Mind”: Li Hanxu’s Nine Levels of Refining the Mind and the Record of Following the Path appended to it; see the second part of this book.
  5. “The bronze figure lying on its back”: the acupoint chart of the ancient bronze acupuncture figure, lying face up.
  6. “Patriarch Hanxu called what is usually called the ‘middle vehicle’ the ‘upper vehicle’, and the ‘upper vehicle’ the ‘great vehicle’”: alchemical books usually divide the methods into lower, middle and upper vehicles; Li Hanxu renamed them the first, upper and great vehicles.
  7. “The Hundred-Line Chapter”: Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Line Chapter; see the fifth part of this book.
  8. “What is the ‘Tathāgata’? It is ‘as I was when I came into life’”: this is Xixinzi’s explanation playing on the literal meaning of the characters (ru “as”, lai “come”), not a Buddhist philological gloss.

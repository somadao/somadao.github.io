Master Cui’s Mirror for Compounding the Medicine, with Commentary · 大药服食，结胎脱胎

Taking the Great Medicine, Forming and Shedding the Embryo

Mirror for Compounding the Medicine, section 3 · Taking the Great Medicine, forming and shedding the embryo — drinking the knife-point, knowing what floats and sinks, guarding against danger in gathering the medicine, looking at one’s natal star and at the four cardinal points

Note (translator): This section is about taking the Great Medicine, forming the embryo and shedding the embryo — very advanced stages. It is for understanding only.

About This Chapter

The last passage has four groups:

  • Drink the knife-point, glimpse the skill of Heaven, distinguish the new moon and the full moon, know dusk and dawn: the “knife-point” is a metaphor for the great elixir of golden liquid, and “drinking” means taking it; one must distinguish the new and full moon (filling and emptying, waxing and waning) and know dusk and dawn (advancing, withdrawing and warm nourishing).
  • Know what floats and what sinks, understand host and guest; they must meet and gather, let nothing come between: metal sinks and wood floats — that is the nature of things; guest first and host after — that is ordinary courtesy. But in reverting the elixir it is the other way round: what floats does not float, what sinks does not sink, and the guest becomes host — this is “reversal”.
  • When gathering the medicine, adjust the work of the fire; if the breath is received too fast, guard against disaster; when the fire is sufficient, do not harm the elixir; Heaven and Earth are numinous, creation is sparing: at the time of gathering the Great Medicine over seven days, one must consider dangers and guard against them; gathering the medicine has its proper work, and the sufficiency of fire has its signs for stopping the fire.
  • When the embryo first forms, look at one’s natal star; when at last the embryo is shed, look at the four cardinal points; practice closely, and every line will be confirmed: the key to “looking” lies in the eyes. When the embryo first forms, one looks at the empty Mysterious Opening, like a hen sitting on her egg or a dragon nurturing its pearl; when the embryo is shed, one must not stay confined for long to the narrow crown of the head, but look at the four cardinal points, “transforming the whole body into a true realm of empty space”.

At the end the Daoist Fool says that the last two lines are Master Cui’s encouragement: if one truly advances in both knowing and doing, every line will be confirmed.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

饮刀圭，窥天巧。辨朔望，知昏晓。

Drink the knife-point, glimpse the skill of Heaven; distinguish the new moon and the full moon, know dusk and dawn.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §1
Original note (Chinese)

刀圭喻金液大丹。饮，服食也。饮刀圭，即《百字碑》自饮长生酒也。窥天巧，即《百句章》观天道，执运行，亦即下文朔望昏晓也。朔望有出庚圆甲，盈虚消长之宜辨。昏晓有朝屯暮蒙，进退温养之当知。皆服金液大丹，当务之急。解见《百句章》第三段，并八层炼心，亦可参悟。

The “knife-point” is a metaphor for the great elixir of golden liquid; “drink” means take. Drinking the knife-point is what the Hundred-Character Stele calls “drinking by oneself the wine of long life”. Glimpsing the skill of Heaven is what the Hundred-Line Chapter calls “observing the Way of Heaven and holding to its course”, which is also the new moon and full moon, dusk and dawn of the following lines. The new and full moon involve “emerging at geng and rounding at jia”, the filling and emptying, waxing and waning that must be distinguished; dusk and dawn involve “Zhun in the morning, Meng in the evening”, the advancing, withdrawing and warm nourishing that must be known. All these are urgent matters in taking the great elixir of golden liquid. For an explanation see the third section of the Hundred-Line Chapter, and the eighth level of the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind may also be consulted.

§2
Original (Chinese)

识浮沉，明主客。要聚会，莫间隔。

Know what floats and what sinks, understand host and guest; they must meet and gather, let nothing come between.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §2
Original note (Chinese)

此四句，亦为上文饮刀圭之要义。金沉木浮，物理本性。客先主后，人情常礼。乃还丹事理之喻则不然，务要浮者不浮，沉者不沉，反客为主，反主作宾。所以《百句章》云，颠倒法乾坤也。但非识透他轻浮沉重之性，明白这反主作宾之理，则东西间隔，聚会最难。今则识之明之，依法将刀圭入腹，名之曰铅投汞，金并水，后天反先天，婴儿会姹女，自无间隔之虞，即收聚会之果也。法详九层炼心，故不赘述。

These four lines are also the key to “drinking the knife-point” above. Metal sinks and wood floats — that is the nature of things; guest first and host after — that is ordinary courtesy. But in the metaphor for the matters and principles of reverting the elixir it is not so: what floats must not float, what sinks must not sink; the guest becomes host and the host becomes guest. That is why the Hundred-Line Chapter says, “reverse them, following Qian and Kun”. But if one does not see through their light, floating nature and heavy, sinking nature, and does not understand this principle of making the host the guest, east and west will be kept apart and their meeting will be most difficult. Now that one knows and understands this, and following the method takes the knife-point into the belly, this is called “lead cast into mercury”, “metal joined with water”, “the Posterior Heaven returning to the Prior Heaven”, “the infant meeting the lovely maiden”; naturally there is no fear of anything coming between, and one reaps the fruit of their meeting. The method is described in detail in the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind, so it is not repeated here.

§3
Original (Chinese)

采药时，调火功。受气急，防成凶。火候足，莫伤丹。天地灵，造化悭。

When gathering the medicine, adjust the work of the fire; if the breath is received too fast, guard against disaster. When the fire is sufficient, do not harm the elixir; Heaven and Earth are numinous, creation is sparing.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §3
Original note (Chinese)

七日采大药之时，天机深远，务要虑险防危，调停火候。盖采药有采药之正功，火足有止火之凭证。丹士谨慎，焉有伤丹之凶？否则，炉残鼎败在所不免。嗟乎金丹大药，天地灵宝，造化所悭，万劫难遇，绝不轻以予人者，乌可伤乎？崔公故提醒以勉之。

At the time of gathering the Great Medicine over seven days, the workings of Heaven are deep and far-reaching; one must consider dangers, guard against them and adjust the fire. For gathering the medicine has its proper work, and the sufficiency of fire has its signs for stopping the fire. If the alchemist is careful, how could there be the disaster of harming the elixir? Otherwise, a broken furnace and a ruined tripod can hardly be avoided. Alas, the Great Medicine of the Golden Elixir is the numinous treasure of Heaven and Earth, which creation spares and which is hard to meet in ten thousand kalpas; it is never given lightly — how could one harm it? That is why Master Cui reminds and encourages us.

§4
Original (Chinese)

初结胎，看本命。终脱胎，看四正。密密行，句句应。

When the embryo first forms, look at one’s natal star; when at last the embryo is shed, look at the four cardinal points. Practise closely, and every line will be confirmed.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §4
Original note (Chinese)

本命，虚无玄窍也。看者，机在目也。胎初结时，看在虚无玄窍，有终日绵绵，如鸡抱卵如龙养珠之情。终至脱胎，虽是移神内院，却又不可久拘狭隘之顶，所以要看四正，即子北、午南、卯东、酉西之四体。将全体化作一虚空真境，方无阻滞神灵之弊。以上大药服食脱胎。结尾两句，是崔公勉人之意。果能知行并进，则句句皆应，决然不爽。

The “natal star” is the empty Mysterious Opening. In “looking”, the key lies in the eyes. When the embryo first forms, one looks at the empty Mysterious Opening, continuously all day long, with the devotion of a hen sitting on her egg or a dragon nurturing its pearl. When at last the embryo is shed, although the spirit is moved into the inner court, one must not stay confined for long to the narrow crown of the head; so one must “look at the four cardinal points”, that is, the four directions — zi north, wu south, mao east and you west — transforming the whole body into a true realm of empty space; only then is there no fault of obstructing the spirit. The above is taking the Great Medicine and shedding the embryo. The last two lines express Master Cui’s encouragement: if one truly advances in both knowing and doing, every line will be confirmed, without the slightest error.

Key Terms

  • Knife-point: a metaphor for the great elixir of golden liquid. See Golden Flower.
  • “Drinking by oneself the wine of long life”: words from the Hundred-Character Stele; see the separate part later in this book.
  • New moon and full moon, “emerging at geng and rounding at jia”: the phases of the moon, metaphors for the waxing and waning of the fire. See fire phasing.
  • Dusk and dawn, “Zhun in the morning, Meng in the evening”: using the hexagram Zhun in the morning and Meng in the evening, metaphors for advancing, withdrawing and warm nourishing.
  • Floating and sinking, host and guest: metal sinks and wood floats, guest first and host after; in reverting the elixir these are reversed. See Lead and Mercury and reversal.
  • Lead cast into mercury, metal joined with water: see Lead and Mercury.
  • Gathering the Great Medicine over seven days: see the Great Medicine passing the gates.
  • Signs that the fire is sufficient and should be stopped: see stopping the fire.
  • Natal star: the empty Mysterious Opening. See Cave of Emptiness.
  • Looking: the key lies in the eyes. See Reverting the Light.
  • Like a hen sitting on her egg, like a dragon nurturing its pearl: the manner of warm nourishing and forming the embryo. See Holy Embryo.
  • The four cardinal points: the four directions zi north, wu south, mao east and you west; transforming the whole body into a true realm of empty space. See sending out the spirit.

Putting It into Practice

  1. This section is for understanding only. What can be borrowed is “like a hen sitting on her egg”: warm nourishing means being continuous, gentle and never leaving, without using force.
  2. “Transforming the whole body into a true realm of empty space” — do not lock the attention rigidly on one point in the body, but let the whole of body and mind open out into spaciousness. This agrees with Chen Yingning’s warning, in the appendix to the first book, that “one must not cling rigidly to one spot”.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Zhun in the morning, Meng in the evening”: alchemical books match the hexagrams of the Book of Changes to the fire phasing of a day, using Zhun in the morning and Meng in the evening; this comes from the Cantong qi.
  2. “Metal joined with water”: the common text reads “metal joined with wood”, and the original text of the third River Chariot in Part Three, the Secret Principles of the Three Carts, also reads “metal joined with wood”. We keep the reading of the original book and translate it as it stands.
  3. “Inner court”: the central palace. “The narrow crown of the head”: the Muddy Pellet at the crown — meaning that one must not stay confined to one place for long.
  4. The original text of this section has four groups, each with one passage of explanation.

The Opening and Obtaining the Medicine

Mirror for Compounding the Medicine, section 1 · The opening and obtaining the medicine and forming the elixir — Prior Heaven breath and Posterior Heaven breath, the upper and lower Magpie Bridges, water fears drying up and fire fears turning cold

About This Chapter

This section contains the eight-line “opening” of the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine and the passage on “obtaining the medicine and forming the elixir” — six groups in all:

  • The opening: “Prior Heaven breath, Posterior Heaven breath; those who obtain them are always as if drunk” — when the two breaths of the Prior Heaven and the Posterior Heaven unite, one is as if dazed or drunk, and this is the moment the Mysterious Pass appears; “the sun has its conjunctions, the moon has its conjunctions; fathom wu and ji, fix geng and jia” — wu and ji (the true intention) are the director of the central palace, geng and jia the fire phasing of advance and withdrawal.
  • Obtaining the medicine and forming the elixir: the upper and lower Magpie Bridges; raising the Xun wind and moving the Kun fire; entering the Yellow Chamber; water fears drying up, fire fears turning cold; the lead dragon rises, the mercury tiger descends; produced in Kun, planted in Qian; robbing Heaven and Earth, seizing creation; when water and fire meet, one never grows old.

The Daoist Fool’s Simple Explanation is very clear in several places: being “drunk” is the image of the dim and obscure state; the upper Magpie Bridge “need not be the curled tongue as the fixed bridge” — the “magpie” of the Magpie Bridge takes the heart-spirit as the Vermilion Bird leading the breath through the passes; and “only utmost sincerity, following what is natural” means “following the natural rising and falling of Heaven, Earth, sun and moon, without the slightest forcing”.

Original Text and Translation

§1The opening
Original (Chinese)

先天气，后天气，得之者，常似醉。

Prior Heaven breath, Posterior Heaven breath — those who obtain them are always as if drunk.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §1
Original note (Chinese)

先后二气，即内外呼吸也。醉，恍惚渺冥之象。言静坐，先后天二气一合，得到恍惚渺冥，如痴如醉之境。此乃真消息，玄关发现时也。似醉而曰常者，又即《清静经》所云，真常应物，真常得性，常应常静之义。仙师云，若得明师指示一身内外阴阳消息，下手速修，势如破竹矣。故此特举为修道之首务。

The two breaths of the Prior Heaven and the Posterior Heaven are the inner and outer breathing. “Drunk” is the image of the dim and obscure. It means that when one sits in stillness and the two breaths of the Prior Heaven and the Posterior Heaven unite, one enters the dim and obscure state, as if dazed or drunk. This is the true sign, the moment when the Mysterious Pass appears. It says “as if drunk” and also “always”, which is the meaning of the Scripture of Clarity and Stillness: “the true constant responds to things, the true constant obtains the nature; always responding, always still”. An immortal teacher said: if you obtain an enlightened teacher’s instruction on the waxing and waning of yin and yang inside and outside the body, and begin cultivating quickly, it will be like splitting bamboo. That is why it is singled out here as the first task of cultivating the Way.

§2The opening
Original (Chinese)

日有合，月有合。穷戊己，定庚甲。

The sun has its conjunctions, the moon has its conjunctions. Fathom wu and ji; fix geng and jia.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §2
Original note (Chinese)

《易》云：悬象著明，莫大乎日月。日为太阳。月为太阴。天地亦阴阳也。天地之阴阳不可见，以日月显之。吕祖云：“丹经部部言阴阳，二字名为万法王”。崔公举此，为修丹者阴阳升降，盈虚消长之实证。合日者，合其一日一周也。合月者，合其一月一周也。又无论合日合月，穷其源，必以戊己为中宫主宰而运行。定其时，必以庚甲为盈虚进退之火候。然而戊己又可统乎身中真日月。盖戊为阳土，己为阴土。丹士用功，静极而动，是为戊土。炼戊土者，得坎月之铅。动极而静，是为己土。炼己土者，得离日之汞。所以穷尽此中事理，令日月交合而不失庚甲，方道金丹自结，再说日月，贯彻丹道始终。始则法运行之度，继则借照耀之力，终则成化形之功。亦如中宫戊己，主输运于庚甲，媒娉婴姹团圆，直至食脉俱绝，无声无臭，乃归勿用。故均首提示人以注意。（以上总冒）

The Book of Changes says: “Of the images hung in the sky, none is more brilliant than the sun and moon.” The sun is the great yang, the moon the great yin. Heaven and Earth are yin and yang too; the yin and yang of Heaven and Earth cannot be seen, so they are shown through the sun and moon. Patriarch Lü says: “Every alchemical scripture speaks of yin and yang; these two words may be called the king of all methods.” Master Cui cites this as concrete proof of the rising and falling, the filling and emptying, the waxing and waning of yin and yang for those who cultivate the elixir. “Conjunction of the sun” means matching its daily cycle; “conjunction of the moon” means matching its monthly cycle. Whether matching the sun or the moon, if one traces it to its source, it must run with wu and ji as the director of the central palace; to fix its time, one must take geng and jia as the fire phasing of filling and emptying, advancing and withdrawing. Yet wu and ji can also command the true sun and moon within the body: wu is yang earth, ji is yin earth. When the alchemist works, stillness reaching its extreme and turning to movement is wu earth; whoever refines wu earth obtains the lead of the Kan moon. Movement reaching its extreme and turning to stillness is ji earth; whoever refines ji earth obtains the mercury of the Li sun. So only when one thoroughly understands the matters and principles here, and lets sun and moon unite without missing the moment of geng and jia, can one say that the Golden Elixir forms of itself. And the sun and moon run through the whole of the alchemical Way from beginning to end: at the beginning one follows the measure of their movement, next one borrows the power of their light, and in the end one accomplishes the work of transforming form. It is also like wu and ji in the central palace, presiding over the transport between geng and jia, acting as matchmaker to unite the infant and the lovely maiden, until eating and pulse both cease, without sound or smell, and one returns to “not acting”. So all of this is put first, to draw people’s attention. (The above is the opening.)

§3Obtaining the medicine and forming the elixir
Original (Chinese)

上鹊桥，下鹊桥。天应星，地应潮。起巽风，运坤火。入黄房，成至宝。

Upper Magpie Bridge, lower Magpie Bridge; Heaven answers with the stars, Earth answers with the tides. Raise the Xun wind, move the Kun fire; enter the Yellow Chamber, and the supreme treasure is formed.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §3
Original note (Chinese)

马跳潭溪，因无桥也。人身任督二脉，上交脑顶，有气食二管隔之；下交会阴，有前后二阴间之。修士欲通此脉，非桥则不能迳度。然桥以鹊名者何哉？盖取心神为朱雀，领气过关之义，非必以卷舌为一定法桥也。且上桥用舌，下桥又用何物乎？此理易明，不必深究。天应星者，即二十八宿，一年一周。地应潮者，是海水潮汐，一月一周。应星，则有过宫度数。应潮，则有晦朔弦望。皆喻任督交通之事理也。起巽风、运坤火者，是又升降之法则。巽风，即先后天呼吸之气。火借风而愈焰，故运火必起巽风。仙经云：鼓动巽风，煽开炉焰是也。黄房，中宫也。宝即身中丹药升降炼成。宝之为贵，无论牟尼黍米，皆以黄房为宝藏。故必入藏，乃成至宝也。

A horse leaps across a deep stream because there is no bridge. The Conception and Governing vessels of the body meet above at the crown of the head, separated by the windpipe and the gullet, and meet below at the perineum, separated by the front and back openings. A practitioner who wants to open up these vessels cannot cross directly without a bridge. Why then is the bridge named after the magpie? It takes the meaning of the heart-spirit as the Vermilion Bird leading the breath through the passes; it is not necessary to take the curled tongue as the fixed bridge. Besides, if the upper bridge uses the tongue, what does the lower bridge use? The principle is easy to understand; there is no need to go into it deeply. “Heaven answers with the stars” refers to the twenty-eight lunar mansions, which make one revolution a year; “Earth answers with the tides” refers to the ebb and flow of the sea, which make one cycle a month. Answering the stars, there are degrees of passing through the palaces; answering the tides, there are the dark moon, the new moon, the quarters and the full moon. Both are metaphors for the communication of the Conception and Governing vessels. “Raise the Xun wind, move the Kun fire” is again a rule of rising and falling: the Xun wind is the breath of Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven breathing; fire blazes more strongly with the help of wind, so to move the fire one must raise the Xun wind. The immortal scriptures say, “Stir up the Xun wind, fan open the flames of the furnace.” The Yellow Chamber is the central palace. The treasure is what is refined in the body through the rising and falling of the elixir medicine. What makes the treasure precious — whether it is called the mani pearl or the grain of millet — is that the Yellow Chamber is its treasury; so it must be stored there to become the supreme treasure.

§4Obtaining the medicine and forming the elixir
Original (Chinese)

水怕干，火怕寒。差毫发，不成丹。

Water fears drying up, fire fears turning cold. Miss by a hair, and no elixir forms.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §4
Original note (Chinese)

此言温养至宝，功归内息。水干则火炎，火寒则水冷，均不匀称。务必勿助勿忘，绵绵不绝，固蒂深根，乃尽养丹之妙。否则差之毫厘，丹不成矣。

This says that in warmly nourishing the supreme treasure, the work rests with the inner breath. If the water dries up, the fire blazes; if the fire turns cold, the water freezes — neither is even. One must neither help nor forget, continuous and unbroken, making the stalk firm and the root deep; only then can one realise fully the wonder of nourishing the elixir. Otherwise, miss by a hair, and the elixir will not form.

§5Obtaining the medicine and forming the elixir
Original (Chinese)

铅龙升，汞虎降。驱二物，勿纵放。产在坤，种在乾。但至诚，法自然。

The lead dragon rises, the mercury tiger descends. Drive the two things; do not let them run loose. Produced in Kun, planted in Qian. Only utmost sincerity; follow what is natural.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §5
Original note (Chinese)

汞铅龙虎，皆玉液之比喻。铅易沉，汞易飞。龙虎猛烈难驯，岂能任意而升降？然功夫一到，肾气上升，心液下降，自有龙虎归伏之时，即得铅汞相制之性，二物升降，似有驱之者然。夫驱则有为渊驱鱼，为丛驱雀，自然就范之意。如其纵放，则尽性之事难了，故以勿字警之。此即六层炼心，坎离交媾，金水初生也。

承上而言，金水生于丹田，产在坤也。运上泥丸，种在乾也。其中调停真息鼓舞之功，务要专心一志，法天地日月自然之升降，毫不容于勉强。古仙所谓自然升降自抽添者此也。

Mercury and lead, dragon and tiger, are all metaphors for the jade liquid. Lead sinks easily and mercury flies off easily; dragon and tiger are fierce and hard to tame — how can they be made to rise and fall at will? Yet when the work arrives, the breath of the kidneys rises and the liquid of the heart descends, and naturally there comes a time when the dragon and tiger submit; then one obtains the nature by which lead and mercury restrain each other, and the two things rise and fall as if something were driving them. “Drive” has the sense of “driving fish into the deep, driving sparrows into the thicket” — making them fall into line naturally. If one lets them run loose, the business of fully realising the nature will hardly be completed; hence the warning word “do not”. This is the conjunction of Kan and Li and the first birth of metal-water at the sixth level of the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind.

Continuing from the above: the metal-water born in the Cinnabar Field is “produced in Kun”; moved up to the Muddy Pellet, it is “planted in Qian”. In the work of adjusting the true breath and drumming it up within this, one must be single-minded and follow the natural rising and falling of Heaven, Earth, sun and moon, without the slightest forcing. This is what the immortals of old meant by “rising and falling naturally, adding and subtracting by themselves”.

§6Obtaining the medicine and forming the elixir
Original (Chinese)

盗天地，夺造化。攒五行，会八卦。水真水，火真火。水火交，永不老。

Rob Heaven and Earth, seize creation; gather the Five Phases, bring together the Eight Trigrams. The water is true water, the fire is true fire; when water and fire meet, one never grows old.

The Daoist Fool's explanationon §6
Original note (Chinese)

盗夺攒会，即他丹经所谓五行四相会中宫也。暗盗天地之精华，明夺造化之功能，五行生克，八卦摩荡，攒会黄房，纽结一团，至此无所用其生克摩荡矣。此所以为盗为夺为攒会也。其言真水火交而不老者，以玉液为坎水离火交合而成，坎得乾之一阳，离得坤之一阴，乾坤为先天真阴阳，得此即得内丹，所以能收不老之效果。然而天地为一阴一阳之道之化身，能盗天地，则造化五行八卦，都总括其中。何也？由阴阳而生水火，由水火而成五行，由五行生克而有造有化，因有造化而分布八卦，但能握要以图，总不出天地之一阴一阳而已。《阴符》云：其盗机也，天下能见莫能知。《系辞》亦以作《易》者为知盗。故盗机逆用，仙家以为丹道始终。是此一盗之功能，而夺攒会胥在其中矣。再想方诸取水于月，阳燧取火于日，其中之妙，日月欤？水火欤？方诸、阳燧欤？盖亦形色中自有个盗夺攒会者在也。知此，则真水火交而永不老之义愈明矣。（以上得药结丹）

Robbing, seizing, gathering and bringing together is what other alchemical scriptures call “the Five Phases and four images meet in the central palace”. Secretly one robs the essence of Heaven and Earth, openly one seizes the powers of creation; the Five Phases generating and overcoming each other, the Eight Trigrams rubbing against and stirring each other, are all gathered into the Yellow Chamber and knotted into a single ball, and from then on there is no further use for generating, overcoming, rubbing and stirring. That is why it is called robbing, seizing, gathering and bringing together. It says that when true water and fire meet one does not grow old, because the jade liquid is formed by the union of the water of Kan and the fire of Li: Kan has obtained the one yang of Qian, Li has obtained the one yin of Kun; Qian and Kun are the true yin and yang of the Prior Heaven, and obtaining them is obtaining the inner elixir — hence the effect of not growing old. Yet Heaven and Earth are the embodiment of “one yin and one yang is called the Way”; if one can rob Heaven and Earth, then creation, the Five Phases and the Eight Trigrams are all included. Why? From yin and yang come water and fire; from water and fire come the Five Phases; from the generating and overcoming of the Five Phases come creation and transformation; and because there is creation and transformation, the Eight Trigrams are laid out. But if one grasps the essential and pursues it, it never goes beyond the one yin and one yang of Heaven and Earth. The Scripture of the Hidden Accord says: “Its robbing mechanism — no one in the world can see it, no one can know it.” The Great Commentary also holds that those who made the Changes understood “robbing”. So by using the robbing mechanism in reverse, the immortals make it the beginning and end of the alchemical Way. In this one power of “robbing”, seizing, gathering and bringing together are all included. Think again of the fangzhu mirror drawing water from the moon and the yangsui mirror drawing fire from the sun: where does the wonder lie — in the sun and moon? in water and fire? or in the fangzhu and yangsui? Probably within form and colour there is something that robs, seizes, gathers and brings together. Understanding this, the meaning of “true water and fire meet, and one never grows old” becomes clearer still. (The above is obtaining the medicine and forming the elixir.)

Key Terms

  • Prior Heaven breath, Posterior Heaven breath: the Daoist Fool explains them as inner and outer breathing. See Prior Heaven / Posterior Heaven.
  • “Always as if drunk”: the image of the dim and obscure, the moment the Mysterious Pass appears. See the dim and obscure and Mysterious Pass.
  • Wu and ji: the director of the central palace; stillness reaching its extreme and turning to movement is wu earth, movement reaching its extreme and turning to stillness is ji earth. See True Intention.
  • Geng and jia: the fire phasing of filling and emptying, advancing and withdrawing. See fire phasing.
  • Upper and lower Magpie Bridges: the joining of the Conception and Governing vessels at the crown and at the perineum; the Daoist Fool: “it is not necessary to take the curled tongue as the fixed bridge”. See Magpie Bridge and Conception and Governing vessels.
  • Heaven answers with the stars, Earth answers with the tides: metaphors for the cycles of communication of the Conception and Governing vessels.
  • Xun wind: the breath of Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven breathing. Kun fire: the spirit-fire rising from the lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Yellow Chamber: the central palace. See Yellow Court.
  • Supreme treasure, mani pearl, grain of millet: other names for the elixir. See Golden Flower.
  • Water fears drying up, fire fears turning cold: the right measure in warm nourishing.
  • Lead dragon, mercury tiger: in this text both are metaphors for the jade liquid. See Lead and Mercury.
  • Produced in Kun, planted in Qian: metal-water is born in the Cinnabar Field (Kun) and moved up to the Muddy Pellet (Qian).
  • Only utmost sincerity, follow what is natural: not the slightest forcing.
  • Rob Heaven and Earth, seize creation, gather the Five Phases, bring together the Eight Trigrams: see Five Breaths return to the Origin (gathering the Five Phases).
  • Fangzhu and yangsui: ancient instruments for drawing water from the moon and fire from the sun.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “Water fears drying up, fire fears turning cold; miss by a hair, and no elixir forms.” Use it as a self-check while sitting: if you are too tight or heated, that is “the water drying up and the fire blazing” — ease off a little; if you are too loose or dull, that is “the fire turning cold and the water freezing” — gather your spirit a little.
  2. “Only utmost sincerity; follow what is natural.” Utmost sincerity is single-minded attention; following what is natural is not forcing — you need both.
  3. There is no need to cling to “curling the tongue” for the Magpie Bridge. Resting the tongue lightly on the palate is a good habit, but as the Daoist Fool says plainly, what matters is the heart-spirit leading the breath, not the position of the tongue.
  4. “Always as if drunk” is a very relaxed state, but the explanation also quotes the Scripture of Clarity and Stillness: “always responding, always still” — drunk but not stupefied, responsive within stillness.

Translator's Notes

  1. Qiu Ling’s editorial note originally reads “Cui Xifan of the Han dynasty wrote the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine” and “since the Han”. Cui Xifan is generally thought to have lived at the end of the Tang and the Five Dynasties (with the title “Perfected of Utmost Unity”), as we noted in the first book; in the translation we have left out “Han dynasty” to avoid misleading readers.
  2. “Fathom wu and ji, fix geng and jia”: the common text has the same wording; we follow the original book.
  3. “Acting as matchmaker to unite the infant and the lovely maiden”: 娉 is read as 聘, “to arrange a marriage”; a metaphor for the true intention bringing together the infant (lead) and the lovely maiden (mercury).
  4. “Its robbing mechanism — no one in the world can see it, no one can know it”: the original book’s wording differs slightly from the common text of the Scripture of the Hidden Accord; the translation follows the common text.
  5. “Driving fish into the deep, driving sparrows into the thicket”: from Mencius, “Li Lou I”; used here in the sense of “falling into line naturally”.
  6. The Daoist Fool’s Simple Explanation comes from the Collection on Understanding the Dao through the Union of Schools compiled by Ran Daoyuan and published during the War of Resistance against Japan; the author signed with a Daoist name, and nothing is known of his life.

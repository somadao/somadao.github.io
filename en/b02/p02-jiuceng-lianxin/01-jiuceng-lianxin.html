Li Hanxu’s Record of Following the Path through the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind · 九层炼心

The Nine Levels of Refining the Mind

The Nine Levels of Refining the Mind — from stopping stray thoughts to returning to emptiness: which mind is refined at each level

Safety note (translator): At level three, “treading on the fire cloud, walking to the Caudal Funnel … beating the iron drum and passing the three passes”; in Ni Qinghe’s explanation of level four, “the attitude of reverently holding the breath, swallowing again and again”; and at level six, leading the saliva with the mind through all four limbs — these are all methods with some force. Please note: if no warm current arises naturally, do not use the mind to push the breath through the passes; do not deliberately hold your breath. In his explanation of level one, Ni Qinghe mentions keeping the attention on the region of the reproductive organs, and Qiu Ling adds that “one may also keep it on the perineum”. Any such focus should be gentle; if strong sexual arousal, restlessness or local discomfort appears, return the attention to the lower abdomen or stop. Levels eight and nine (sending out the spirit, refining emptiness) are for understanding only.

About This Chapter

Li Hanxu divides the refining of the mind into nine levels, each corresponding to a “work”:

LevelWhich mind is refinedKey methodName of the work
1The mind not yet pure (stray thoughts, wandering fancies)Cessation and contemplation: the mind stops at the Cave of Emptiness, looks inward, keeps company with the breathRefining the mind and nourishing the breath
2The mind entering concentration (carried off by the cognitive spirit)When mind and breath rely on each other, let awareness dissolve and become one pieceRefining the mind and uniting with the breath
3The mind of “return” (moved when a beautiful scene appears)When the Mysterious Pass first appears, tread the fire cloud through the three passes and rest at KunlunRefining the mind and advancing the breath
4The mind of withdrawal (stirred by the cognitive spirit in the Muddy Pellet)Still the mind on the summit of Kunlun; the breath turns to liquid and descends to the Yellow Court and the Original SeaRefining the mind and obtaining the breath
5The mind laying the foundation (slackness, desire)Practise by the zi and wu hours, adding and subtracting day by day, taking from Kan to fill LiRefining the mind and accumulating breath
6The mind completing the nature (attention not single-minded)When metal-water is first born, send it down to the Bubbling Springs and up against the current to the Valley of Heaven — the jade-liquid reverted elixirRefining the mind and illuminating the nature
7The nature already illuminated (mercury tends to fly off)Unite nature with life — the great elixir of golden liquid, “seven returns and nine reversions”Refining the mind and preserving the spirit
8The mind already subdued (concentrated but not numinous)Ten months of warm nourishing: numinous, then moving, then changing, then transformingRefining the mind and completing the spirit
9The mind already numinous (numinous but not empty)Refining emptiness: the heart vast and boundless, all things become nothingThe whole of refining the mind

The explanation by Ni Qinghe that Qiu Ling appended breaks each level down into “phenomena / connection / method / verification / comment”, with many very practical pointers — for example, at level one, “this method does not require waiting for the Living Zi Hour; one can practice quietly at any time”; at level two, “the mind should not take ‘fasting of the mind and sitting in forgetfulness’ as the ultimate; there should be a living atmosphere, not a dead one”; and at level eight, “at the very last move, go back and do the very first move again”. We summarise these in our own words after each level.

Original Text and Translation

§1Level one
Original (Chinese)

初层炼心者，是炼未纯之心也。未纯之心多妄想，多游思。妄想生于贪欲。游思起于不觉。学人打坐之际，非不欲摒去尘情，无如妄想才除，游思忽起。法在止观，乃可渐渐消熔。止则止于脐堂之后，命门之前，其中稍下有个虚无窟子。吾心止于是而内观之，心照空中，与气相守，维系乎规矩之间，来往乎方圆之内。息息归根，合自然之造化。巍巍不动，立清静之元基。从此一线心光，与一缕真气相接，浑浑灏灏，安安闲闲。此炼心养气之功也。

The first level of refining the mind refines the mind that is not yet pure. The impure mind is full of stray thoughts and wandering fancies. Stray thoughts are born of greed and desire; wandering fancies arise from lack of awareness. When students sit in meditation, it is not that they do not want to put aside worldly feelings; but no sooner are stray thoughts removed than wandering fancies suddenly arise. The method lies in “cessation and contemplation”; only then can they gradually be dissolved. “Cessation” means stopping behind the navel and in front of the Gate of Life, a little lower down between them, where there is a “Cave of Emptiness”. My mind stops here and looks inward at it; the mind shines into the emptiness, keeps company with the breath, held within the compass and square, coming and going within the round and the square. Each breath returns to the root, in accord with the creative transformations of nature; lofty and unmoving, one establishes the original foundation of clarity and stillness. From then on, a thread of the mind’s light joins a wisp of true breath, vast and boundless, peaceful and at ease. This is the work of “refining the mind and nourishing the breath”.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §1
Original note (Chinese)

（邱陵）编者按：亦可守会阴。

Ni Qinghe sums up the phenomena of this level as follows: when one first sits, the breath of the body is not yet pure and the thoughts and intentions scatter outward, so stray thoughts and wandering fancies keep coming — remove one and another pops up.

As for the method, he explains that the ancients used “Gate of Life” for the area to the left and right of the two kidneys, and that the “Cave of Emptiness” was also called the lower Cinnabar Field or the Jade Furnace; one rests one’s thoughts and intention here, looks inward, shines the mind into it and keeps company with the breath. The verification is that the “mind’s light” (which he explains as the gathering of thought and intention) joins the “true breath” (which he explains as the gathering of the body’s energy), and one feels undivided and at ease.

Two points in his comments are worth noting. First, this level does not require waiting for the “Living Zi Hour”; one can practice quietly at any time. First gather in as much as possible the life energy at the gates of one’s six senses and the true yang breath of Heaven and Earth, and let the two join — this is the beginning of laying the foundation. Before practice, mind and breath are two things; with practice they become one. Second, he holds that the actual place to begin can be lower still: for men the testicles, for women the ovaries (both of which he calls the yinqiao), in order to rekindle the life force.

Here Qiu Ling adds a short editorial note: one may also keep the attention on the perineum.

§2Level two
Original (Chinese)

二层炼心者，是炼入定之心也。前此一线心光，与一缕真气相接，若能直造窈冥，自当透出玄窍。奈何定心不固，每为识神所迁，心与气离，仍不能见本来面目。法在心息相依之时，即把知觉泯去，心在气中而不知，气包心外而不晓，氤氤氲氲，打成一片。此炼心合气之功也。

The second level of refining the mind refines the mind that is entering concentration. Earlier, a thread of the mind’s light joined a wisp of true breath; if one could go straight on into the obscure depths, the Mysterious Opening would naturally appear. But the concentrated mind is not yet firm and is often carried off by the cognitive spirit; mind and breath separate, and one still cannot see one’s original face. The method lies in this: when mind and breath rely on each other, let “awareness” itself dissolve — the mind is within the breath without knowing it, the breath envelops the mind without its noticing, dense and misty, all become one piece. This is the work of “refining the mind and uniting with the breath”.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §2

Ni Qinghe explains “mind” as the workings of intention and awareness, and “breath” as respiration. He draws a careful distinction between the two levels: at level one the mind “clings” to the Cinnabar Field; at this level the mind first “is tied” to the breathing, and then the mind is dissolved — what is called “returning the spirit to follow the breath”; if the mind rises again, one again “dissolves the mind to follow the breath”.

The standard is this: the mind is within the breath, yet does not know that the breath envelops it, nor notice the mingling of the two breaths — they have already become one piece.

He comments that this is a state of complete concentration, which the ancients called the “state of concentration”; it is not a separate practice. This level gives weight to the joining of the two breaths; the mind has no other object, but it does not take “fasting of the mind and sitting in forgetfulness” as the ultimate — in the practice there should be a “living atmosphere”, not a “dead atmosphere”; one should nurture the life force and extinguish the forces of death.

§3Level three
Original (Chinese)

三层炼心者，是炼来复之心也。前此氤氤氲氲，打成一片，重阴之下，一阳来复，是名天地之心，即是玄关一窍。此刻精气神都在先天，鸿濛初判，并不分真精真气真神，即此是真精真气真神。若能一心不动，便可当下采取运行。无奈见所未见，闻所未闻，美景现前，忙无措手，心一动而落在后天，遂分为精气神矣。法在玄关初现之时，即刻踏住火云，走到尾闾，坚其心，柔其息，敲铁鼓而过三关，休息于昆仑焉。此炼心进气之功也。

The third level of refining the mind refines the mind of “return”. Earlier, all became dense and misty and one piece; beneath layer upon layer of yin, one yang returns. This is called “the heart of Heaven and Earth”, which is the single opening of the Mysterious Pass. At this moment essence, breath and spirit are all in the Prior Heaven, as when primordial chaos first divides; there is no separation into true essence, true breath and true spirit — and precisely this is true essence, true breath and true spirit. If one can keep the mind unmoved, one can gather it and set it in motion right then. But one sees what one has never seen and hears what one has never heard; a beautiful scene appears before one’s eyes and one is flustered and does not know what to do; the mind moves and falls back into the Posterior Heaven, and so it divides again into essence, breath and spirit. The method lies in this: the moment the Mysterious Pass first appears, at once “tread on the fire cloud” and walk to the Caudal Funnel; make the mind firm and the breath soft, “beat the iron drum” and pass the three passes, and rest at Kunlun. This is the work of “refining the mind and advancing the breath”.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §3

Ni Qinghe explains that “layer upon layer of yin” refers to the original breath and “one yang” to the return of the original essence; he says frankly that this sometimes shows itself as sexual arousal, which one should not cling to — it is simply a “phenomenon of the life force”, that is, the single opening of the Mysterious Pass. At this moment essence, breath and spirit are mixed into one and can be gathered and set in motion right then; if the mind stirs, the breath rushes off and the essence scatters, one falls back into the Posterior Heaven and they divide into three.

As for the method, he explains the “fire cloud” as a warm current and “beating the iron drum” as a metaphor for a strong and resolute spirit: at the moment the life force first stirs, follow the warm current to the Caudal Funnel, pass the three passes of the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Jade Pillow, and rest at the Muddy Pellet (the crown of the head). He says that what the ancients called “refining the mind and advancing the breath” is really “refining the mind and moving the breath”.

He also makes a distinction: the “mind and breath relying on each other” of the previous level is really “following the breath, not the mind”; this level is “leaving the breathing and following the qi”. Here qi is a bodily activity with form and appearance, like a warm current, rising along the Governing Vessel. It is the same as the method of “returning the essence to replenish the brain”, except that one need not wait passively for the Living Zi Hour — there can be an “artificial Living Zi”.

§4Level four
Original (Chinese)

四层炼心者，是炼退藏之心也。前此踏火云，过三关，心与气随，固已入于泥丸矣。然在泥丸宫内，或有识神引动，则气寒而凝，必不能化为真水，洒濯三宫，前功尽弃矣。法在昆仑顶上，息心主静，与气交融，气乃化为美液，从上腭落下。卷舌承露，吞而送之，注心于绛宫，注心于黄庭，注心于元海。一路响声，直送到底，又待玄关之现焉。此炼心得气之功也。

The fourth level of refining the mind refines the mind of withdrawal. Earlier, one trod on the fire cloud and passed the three passes, mind and breath following each other, and so has certainly entered the Muddy Pellet. But if inside the palace of the Muddy Pellet the cognitive spirit stirs things up, the breath will “turn cold and congeal” and can certainly not change into true water to wash the three palaces; all the previous work will be lost. The method lies in this: on the summit of Kunlun, still the mind and keep to stillness, merging with the breath; the breath then changes into a fine liquid that falls from the upper palate. Curl the tongue to receive this “dew”, swallow it and send it down: fix the mind on the Crimson Palace, fix the mind on the Yellow Court, fix the mind on the Original Sea. All the way down there is a sound; send it straight to the bottom, and then wait again for the Mysterious Pass to appear. This is the work of “refining the mind and obtaining the breath”.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §4

Ni Qinghe holds that when the cognitive spirit (the activity of thought) stirs in the brain, the phrase that the breath “turns cold and congeals” is a slip in the ancients’ choice of words; in fact the breath “slackens and scatters”. He explains the “three palaces” as the Twelve-Storied Tower, the Crimson Palace (1.3 cun inward from the point between the two nipples) and the Yellow Court (above the lower Cinnabar Field), finally descending into the Cinnabar Field.

On the phrase “then wait again for the Mysterious Pass to appear” he notes that one can practice as usual; there is no need to wait.

When a student asked how breath is turned into water, his answer was roughly this: use the single word “congeal” — congeal it at the crown of the head, take the attitude of facing the divine and reverently holding the breath, swallow again and again, and the breath will naturally turn into water.

He also points out that in the turning of the River Chariot the front and back routes differ: going up the back (the Governing Vessel) what moves is “breath”; coming down the front (the Conception Vessel) what moves is “water”. The sound all the way down when swallowing saliva is still “breath transformed and descending”; people today call it a “gas phenomenon” (gas in the stomach and intestines), and it is one link in the cycle of transformations of cultivating the breath.

§5Level five
Original (Chinese)

五层炼心者，是炼筑基之心也。前此入泥丸而归气穴，已有河车路径。从此一心做去，日夜不休，基成何待百日乎？然或有懈心，有欲心，作辍相仍，丹基难固。夫筑基所以聚精会神也。功夫不勤，精神仍然散乱，何以延年奉道？法在行凭子午，逐日抽添，取坎填离，积金实腹。此炼心累气之功也。

The fifth level of refining the mind refines the mind that is laying the foundation. Earlier, one went from the Muddy Pellet back to the Cavity of Breath, and the path of the River Chariot is already there. If from now on one keeps at it single-mindedly, day and night without rest, why should the foundation take a hundred days? But if there is a slack mind or a desiring mind, practicing and stopping by turns, the foundation of the elixir will hardly become firm. Laying the foundation is for gathering essence and concentrating spirit. If the work is not diligent, essence and spirit remain scattered — how then can one prolong one’s years and serve the Way? The method lies in practicing by the zi and wu hours, adding and subtracting day by day, taking from Kan to fill Li, accumulating metal to fill the belly. This is the work of “refining the mind and accumulating breath”.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §5

Ni Qinghe says that “laying the foundation” is the collective name for the first four levels of work and their continued practice; as for its length, besides “a hundred days” there is also talk of “a year”. With slackness, impulses, and practicing and stopping by turns, the foundation of the elixir will hardly become firm; if the work is not diligent, the breath of the body cannot return to unity, it remains scattered and one easily falls ill — how then can one speak of prolonging one’s years?

He explains the four phrases of the method as follows: “practicing by the zi and wu hours” — practicing at set times; “adding and subtracting day by day” — working day by day; “taking from Kan to fill Li” — strengthening the spirit through the body; “moving the essence to fill the belly” — strengthening the body through the body.

His comment is very frank: the ancients played word games, using “nine levels” to hint at the “nine heavens”; this level in fact has no work of its own but is a synthesis of the first four. One can work on the first four separately; once they are well practiced, work on them together; and after a long time alternate between working on them separately and together. As the work advances, the body’s breath becomes vigorous and spirit and breath full — this is the true meaning of “laying the foundation”.

He also compares it with Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Stele: the first line, “To nourish the breath, forget words and keep guard”, is the key to the whole text, with the emphasis on the word “keep guard”. In the stele, “the ancestor”, “who”, “the true constant”, “nature”, “breath” and “elixir” all speak of the same thing; “Kan and Li” and “yin and yang” are two images used as metaphors. “White clouds pay homage at the summit” corresponds to level three, “sweet dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru” to level four, “a single clap of thunder transforms all” is the sign of level four, and “sitting and listening to the stringless tune, one clearly understands the workings of creation” echoes the guarding of the breath and listening to the breath in the first line. (The full text of the Hundred-Character Stele is given in a later part of this book.)

§6Level six
Original (Chinese)

六层炼心者，是炼了性之心也。前此河车转动，聚精会神，则灵根充实矣。从此心液下降，肾气上升，是为坎离交。杳冥中有信，浩浩如潮。一半水气，濛濛如雾，一半云气，是名金水初生。方修玉液还丹，倘用心不专，则尽性之事难了。法在于金水初生之日，由丹田分下涌泉，霎时而合到尾闾。调停真息，鼓之舞之，乃能滔滔逆上，至于天谷。涓涓咽下，落于黄庭。如此则朝朝灌溉，心地清凉，血化为膏，意凝为土，土中生汞，汞性圆明，遇物不迁，灵剑在手。孟子谓尽其心者知其性也。仙家名为阴丹、内丹。此炼心明性之功也。

The sixth level of refining the mind refines the mind that completes the nature. Earlier, the River Chariot turned, essence was gathered and spirit concentrated, and so the spiritual root has become full. From now on the liquid of the heart descends and the breath of the kidneys rises: this is the conjunction of Kan and Li. Within the obscure there is a sign, vast like a tide — half of it water vapour, hazy like mist, half of it cloud vapour; this is called “metal-water first born”. Only now does one cultivate the jade-liquid reverted elixir; if one’s attention is not single-minded, the business of fully realising the nature will hardly be completed. The method lies in this: on the day metal-water is first born, divide it from the Cinnabar Field down to the Bubbling Springs, and in an instant bring it together at the Caudal Funnel; adjust the true breath, drum it up and set it dancing, and only then can it surge up against the current to the Valley of Heaven; then swallow it down in a fine trickle to fall into the Yellow Court. If one irrigates like this every day, the ground of the mind becomes clear and cool, the blood turns into ointment, the intention congeals into earth, mercury is born in the earth, the nature of mercury is round and bright, it does not shift when it meets things, and one holds the spiritual sword in one’s hand. Mencius says: “Those who fully realise their minds know their nature.” The immortals call it the “yin elixir” or the “inner elixir”. This is the work of “refining the mind and illuminating the nature”.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §6

Ni Qinghe explains that the “liquid of the heart” is the breath of the heart turned into liquid, and the “breath of the kidneys” is breath transformed from essence; one fully realises the nature in order to establish life, and this life is not ordinary life but the life of longevity. Among the signs, “the blood turns into ointment” is a metaphor for slowing the metabolism, that is, slowing ageing; “the intention congeals into earth” is a metaphor for the mind and intention constantly remaining in concentration; “mercury” refers to the true nature or true spirit. He says that although this is called “refining the mind and illuminating the nature”, it also has effects on the body.

He holds that this method is the ancient “method of refining the body with jade liquid”, systematised and placed here, and that it can also be practiced on its own. Its range of movement is larger than that of laying the foundation: in laying the foundation the path of the true intention ideally runs only along the midline of the front and back of the trunk, whereas this method reaches all four limbs. For this method the posture should be changed to ordinary upright sitting rather than cross-legged sitting, or a standing posture may be even more suitable.

The method he describes in detail runs roughly as follows. First shine the attention on the “Mysterious Breast” opening under the tongue; in a moment the mouth fills with saliva; rinse gently a few times and slowly lead it down with the intention along the Conception Vessel on the front of the body, through the Twelve-Storied Tower, the “sword tip” (the lower end of the breastbone), the zhongwan and the qique to the Sea of Breath (four finger-widths below the navel); from the Sea of Breath divide it into two paths down the thighs, knees, zusanli points, the backs of the feet and the big toes, turning to the Bubbling Springs; then from the heels and the backs of the knees up the back of the thighs to meet at the Caudal Funnel; past the kidney area and the Spine-Straddling Pass, divide it to the two shoulders and arms down to the backs of the hands, turn at the middle fingers to the palms, past the wrists, up along the sides of the chest and the cheeks, pour it over the crown from the back of the head, then down through the Hall of Light to the upper palate, and receive it with the tongue back at the Mysterious Breast. That counts as one round; pause a little and do it again. He says the effect is that places of stagnation gradually open and the seven openings communicate with each other.

He also lists a few ancient sayings as background, among them Shi Jianwu’s “Breath is the medicine that adds years; the mind is the spirit that directs the breath; if spirit and breath can be made to stay, one is an immortal”, and “Where the eyes go, the mind is there too; where the mind goes, the breath goes too” — in moving the breath, one should use the eyes.

§7Level seven
Original (Chinese)

七层炼心者，是炼已明之性也。前此金水河车，仙师名为内炼，到此还有外炼功夫。以外合内，真心乃聚而不散。盖内体虽明，好飞者汞性。内修虽具，易坏者阴丹。设或保养不纯，则心性复灭矣。法在以虚明之心，妙有之性，和砂拌土，种在彼家。彼家虚而由我实之，彼家无而自我有之。以有投无，以实入虚，死心不动，霎时间先天一气从虚无中来。一候为一阳，有如震。二候为二阳，有如兑。时值二候，正宜合丹。那边吐出一弦真气，其喻为虎向水中生。这边落下一点玄光，其喻为龙从火里出。两边龙虎会合，性情交感，一场大战，宛如天地晦冥，身心两静矣。俄而三阳发动，有如乾卦。如潮如火，如雾如烟，如雷如电，如雪如花，身中阳铅恍耀，我即持剑掌印，踏罡步斗，鼓动元和，猛烹极炼，透三关而上泥丸，一身毛窍皆开，比前玉液河车更不同也。吞而服之，以先天制后天，性命合而为一，即大还丹也。性属火，其数七。命属金，其数九。返本还元，故名七返九还金液大丹。从此铅来制汞，其心长明，永不动摇矣。此炼心存神之功也。

The seventh level of refining the mind refines the nature that is already illuminated. The earlier River Chariot of metal-water is what the immortal teachers call “inner refining”; here there is still the work of “outer refining” — uniting the outer with the inner, so that the true mind gathers and does not scatter. For although the inner substance is illuminated, the nature of mercury is to fly off; although the inner cultivation is complete, the yin elixir is easily spoiled. If one’s care is not pure, the mind and nature will be extinguished again. The method lies in this: with the empty and bright mind and the wondrously existing nature, “mix the cinnabar with soil and plant it in that one’s house” — where that one’s house is empty, I fill it; where that one’s house has nothing, I give it something. Casting the existent into the non-existent, putting the full into the empty, the mind dead and unmoving, in an instant the One Breath of Prior Heaven comes out of emptiness. The first stage is one yang, like the trigram Zhen; the second stage is two yang, like the trigram Dui. At the second stage is the right time to unite the elixir. Over there a crescent of true breath is breathed out — the metaphor is “the tiger is born in the water”; over here a point of mysterious light descends — the metaphor is “the dragon comes out of the fire”. When dragon and tiger on the two sides meet and nature and emotion respond to each other, there is a great battle, as if Heaven and Earth were plunged into darkness, and body and mind both become still. Soon three yang stir, like the trigram Qian — like a tide, like fire, like mist, like smoke, like thunder, like lightning, like snow, like flowers; the yang lead in the body flashes and shines. I then hold the sword and form the seal, tread the stars of the Dipper, stir up the original harmonious breath, cook it fiercely and refine it to the utmost, pass through the three passes and go up to the Muddy Pellet; every pore of the body opens — quite different again from the earlier jade-liquid River Chariot. Swallow it and take it, controlling the Posterior Heaven with the Prior Heaven; nature and life unite into one — this is the great reverted elixir. Nature belongs to fire, whose number is seven; life belongs to metal, whose number is nine. Returning to the root and reverting to the origin, it is therefore called “the great elixir of golden liquid of the seven returns and nine reversions”. From then on the lead controls the mercury, the mind is always bright and never wavers again. This is the work of “refining the mind and preserving the spirit”.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §7

Ni Qinghe begins his explanation of this level with the “concepts”: the work distinguishes host and guest, and the practice distinguishes “that one” and “I” — “nature” is the subject and “life” the object; nature is the Prior Heaven and life the Posterior Heaven. So “mix the cinnabar with soil and plant it in that one’s house” is a metaphor for the method: casting the empty and bright mind and the principle of wondrous existence into the side of “life”, casting the existent into the non-existent and the full into the empty.

He sums up the key as “controlling the Posterior Heaven with the Prior Heaven; nature and life unite into one”, technically called “controlling the mercury with the lead, the mind always bright”. The verification is the stirring of three yang in the image of Qian, the various scenes like a tide or like fire, passing through the three passes up to the Muddy Pellet, and every pore of the body opening.

He comments that this method is the true method of the “Golden Elixir”: it has already left behind the shallow phenomena of body and mind and deals only with the expressions of a body and mind that have already been trained, integrating them with each other.

§8Level eight
Original (Chinese)

八层炼心者，是炼已伏之心而使之通神也。前此七返九还，以铅制汞，心已定矣。但要温之养之，务使身中之气，尽化为神。身中之神，能游于外。于是取一年十二月气候，除卯酉二月为沐浴，余十月为进退，故名十月温养。非言要十个月功夫也，否则心虽定而不灵。锻之炼之，灵心日见，灵则动，动则变，变则化，故有出神之事，而不为物情所迷。此炼心成神之功也。

The eighth level of refining the mind refines the mind that has already been subdued and makes it communicate with the spirit. Earlier, with the seven returns and nine reversions and the lead controlling the mercury, the mind has become settled. But it still has to be warmed and nourished, so that all the breath in the body is transformed into spirit, and the spirit in the body is able to roam outside it. So one takes the climate of the twelve months of a year: the two months of mao and you are for ablution, and the remaining ten months for advancing and withdrawing — hence the name “ten months of warm nourishing”. This does not mean that ten months of work are required. Otherwise the mind, though settled, is not numinous. Forge it and refine it, and the numinous mind appears more each day; being numinous, it moves; moving, it changes; changing, it transforms. Hence there is sending out the spirit, without being deluded by things and feelings. This is the work of “refining the mind and completing the spirit”.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §8

Ni Qinghe says that the aim of this level is “sending out the spirit”: higher spiritual capacities have already been acquired, but they still have to be warmed and nourished, so that the life potential in the body is transformed into a “spirit” that can be gathered in and let out. The method is to practice “warm nourishing”: take non-action as the work and relaxation as the principle, yet still sit and cultivate the obscure. Without warm nourishing, the potential, though gathered, is not numinous; relaxing it, letting it bend back in order to stretch, makes it numinous, moving, changing and transforming. Sending out the spirit has its appearances; one must on no account be deluded by things and feelings.

Two sentences in his comments are very important. First, “laying the foundation → refining the body with jade liquid → the Golden Elixir → sending out the spirit → returning to emptiness and reverting to the truth” is the standard sequence of steps, an ideal order. Second, during warm nourishing the range of the spirit stretches very wide, and one must take great care for one’s future. The principle of the earlier generations was “at the very last move, go back and do the very first move again”, so that cultivation and one’s whole life end together; one follows nature while remaining in command, taking “plant the right cause and obtain the right fruit” as the standard, and must never act wildly or rashly.

§9Level nine
Original (Chinese)

九层炼心者，是炼已灵之心而使之归空也。前此温养功深，神已出而不惑，随心所欲，无往不宜，高踏云霄，遍游海岛，致足乐矣。但灵心不虚，则不能包涵万有。此所以有炼虚一著也。炼虚者，心胸浩荡，众有皆无，清空一气，盘旋天地间，是我非我，是空不空。世界有毁，惟空不毁。乾坤有碍，惟空无碍。此所以神满虚空法周沙界也。此炼心之始末也，无以加矣。

The ninth level of refining the mind refines the mind that has already become numinous and makes it return to emptiness. Earlier, the warm nourishing went deep and the spirit can now go out without being deluded; following the mind’s wishes, nothing is amiss wherever one goes; one treads high on the clouds and roams all the islands of the sea — the height of joy. But if the numinous mind is not empty, it cannot embrace the myriad things. That is why there is the move of “refining emptiness”. Refining emptiness means the heart becomes vast and boundless, all that exists becomes nothing, a single breath of clear emptiness circles between Heaven and Earth — it is I and not I, it is empty and not empty. The world may be destroyed; only emptiness is not destroyed. Heaven and Earth may be obstructed; only emptiness is unobstructed. This is why “the spirit fills empty space and the Dharma pervades worlds as numerous as the sands”. This is the beginning and end of refining the mind; nothing can be added.

Ni Qinghe's explanationsummary, on §9

Ni Qinghe says that the aim of this level is “refining the spirit to return to the Great Void”; the Great Void is also called the Great Ultimate, the source of all that exists. The method is what the original text says: the heart vast and boundless, all things transformed. He comments that when the work has reached level eight, the path of the body has come to an end; the spirit can still go further beyond. If all one’s cultivation and refinement is for preserving a “personalised self”, one is still held back by it; only when this boundary is dissolved, so that oneself is the universe and the universe is oneself, is one united with the Way in truth.

Key Terms

  • Refining the mind: the essential path of alchemical practice from beginning to end; “refining the mind is half the work of success”.
  • Cessation and contemplation: stopping at the Cave of Emptiness and looking inward at it.
  • Cave of Emptiness: behind the navel and in front of the Gate of Life, a little lower down between them.
  • Gate of Life (mingmen 命门): in this text, the area between the two kidneys (Ni Qinghe: the ancients called the area to the left and right of the kidneys the Gate of Life).
  • Compass and square, round and square: metaphors for the range within which one keeps to the center.
  • A thread of the mind’s light, a wisp of true breath: the joining of attention with the sensation of breath in the body.
  • The obscure depths, the original face: the state of deep concentration and one’s original nature. See the dim and obscure.
  • Cognitive spirit: the mental activity that separates the mind from the breath.
  • The heart of Heaven and Earth: one yang returning beneath layer upon layer of yin; that is, the single opening of the Mysterious Pass and the Living Zi Hour.
  • When primordial chaos first divides: the moment before essence, breath and spirit are separated.
  • Treading on the fire cloud: Ni Qinghe explains it as following a warm current. Beating the iron drum: Ni Qinghe explains it as a strong and resolute spirit.
  • Kunlun: the Muddy Pellet at the crown of the head.
  • Three palaces: Ni Qinghe explains them as the Twelve-Storied Tower, the Crimson Palace and the Yellow Court. Original Sea: the lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Curling the tongue to receive the dew: curling the tongue to catch the saliva descending from the upper palate. See Sweet Dew.
  • Practicing by the zi and wu hours, adding and subtracting day by day: working at set times and every day. See fire phasing.
  • Taking from Kan to fill Li, accumulating metal to fill the belly: see conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Metal-water first born: the image of the breath within the obscure rising like a tide, half water vapour and half cloud vapour.
  • Jade-liquid reverted elixir, jade-liquid River Chariot, refining the body with jade liquid: the work of leading the saliva to circulate through the whole body.
  • Mysterious Breast: an opening under the tongue, the source of saliva.
  • Yin elixir, inner elixir: what is obtained by refining the mind and illuminating the nature.
  • “Mix the cinnabar with soil and plant it in that one’s house”: veiled alchemical language; Ni Qinghe explains it as “nature is host, life is guest”.
  • One yang like Zhen, two yang like Dui, three yang like Qian: trigram images for the yang breath growing from faint to full.
  • “The tiger is born in the water, the dragon comes out of the fire”: lead (life) and mercury (nature). See Lead and Mercury.
  • Holding the sword and forming the seal, treading the stars of the Dipper: terms from Daoist ritual, here metaphors for mastery and use.
  • The great elixir of golden liquid of the seven returns and nine reversions: the number of nature is seven and that of life is nine. See Golden Flower.
  • Ten months of warm nourishing: the climate of a year used as a metaphor; ten actual months are not required. See the three transmutations.
  • Sending out the spirit, refining emptiness: see the three transmutations.
  • “The spirit fills empty space and the Dharma pervades worlds as numerous as the sands”: a Buddhist expression — the spirit fills all of space, and the Dharma pervades worlds as numerous as the sands of the Ganges.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Level one contains all the essentials of everyday sitting: rest the attention lightly deep in the lower abdomen, look inward and keep company with the breath; do not chase stray thoughts and do not fight them either — let them gradually dissolve. As Ni Qinghe puts it well, there is no need to wait for any special moment; you can practice quietly at any time.
  2. Level two is the key step in “entering concentration”: when mind and breath have relied on each other for a while, let go even of the awareness that “I am relying on the breath”, and mind and breath become one piece. Letting go is not dozing off — there should be a “living atmosphere”, not a “dead atmosphere”.
  3. The lesson of level three: the moment a good experience comes, if the mind stirs, it is lost. “If one can keep the mind unmoved, one can gather it right then” — when an experience comes, staying unmoved is the best way of “gathering” it.
  4. The slack mind, the desiring mind and practicing and stopping by turns at level five are the real reasons most people’s practice does not succeed. Perseverance matters more than technique.
  5. Ni Qinghe’s words “at the very last move, go back and do the very first move again” are worth remembering: whatever level you have reached, returning to the first steps of gathering the mind and stopping thoughts is always right.

Translator's Notes

  1. In the original book, Ni Qinghe’s explanation from The Immortals’ Art of Long Life is appended after Li Hanxu’s text, level by level. This site gives only a summary of it.
  2. In Ni Qinghe’s explanation of level eight, mao and you are glossed as “the second and tenth lunar months”. By the earthly branches used for months, mao is the second month and you the eighth; “tenth” in the original book must be a misprint or a slip of the pen.
  3. At level seven the original text says “the work of refining the mind and preserving the spirit”, while Ni Qinghe’s outline has “refining the mind and completing the spirit”, the same as level eight. We follow the original book.
  4. “Navel hall” (qitang 脐堂) means the navel.
  5. The “gas phenomenon” is Ni Qinghe’s modern explanation of the sounds in the belly when swallowing saliva: the movement of gas in the stomach and intestines.

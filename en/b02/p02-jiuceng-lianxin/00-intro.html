Li Hanxu’s Record of Following the Path through the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind · 篇首导读

Introduction to Part Two

What this book is

The Nine Levels of Refining the Mind is also by Li Hanxu, and it forms a pair with the previous text, the Serial Account of the Posterior Heaven. The Serial Account tells you “what to do” — the order of the nine levels of work; the Nine Levels of Refining the Mind tells you “how to refine the mind” — at each level of work, what goes wrong with the mind and how to remedy it. Every level follows the same pattern: first it says what kind of mind is being refined at this level, then what problem the previous level has left behind, then “the method lies in …”, ending with “this is the work of refining the mind and …”.

The nine levels run from “refining the mind that is not yet pure” (stray thoughts and wandering fancies) all the way to “refining the mind that has become numinous and making it return to emptiness” (refining emptiness). The Lesser Celestial Circuit, the jade-liquid reverted elixir, the great elixir of golden liquid, the ten months of warm nourishing, sending out the spirit and returning to emptiness — the whole course of alchemical practice is here. That is why Qiu Ling says that “from these nine levels one can glimpse the whole course of Daoist alchemical practice”.

After the original text Qiu Ling appended the level-by-level explanation of this work from The Immortals’ Art of Long Life (Xianjia changsheng shu 仙家长生术) by Ni Qinghe, the Taiwanese “Daoist of True Unity”. This site gives a summary of his explanation in our own words after each level.

Qiu Ling's editorial note

此篇亦为清代长乙山人李涵虚所作，选自《太上十三经注解》一书。文中尽言小大周天始末。从此九层功法，可窥见道家丹功全程。

炼丹先要炼心，炼心者乃丹功彻始彻终之要道。古人云：“炼丹而不先炼心，犹鞭马使奔而羁其足也”。炼心为功成一半功夫。

本篇一向较少注释者，现选台湾出版的真一道人倪清和所著《仙家长生术》一书有关本篇解释附后，作为参考。

This text is also by Li Hanxu, the Changyi hermit of the Qing dynasty, and is taken from his Annotations on the Thirteen Scriptures of the Most High. It tells the whole story of the Lesser and Greater Celestial Circuits from beginning to end. From these nine levels of practice one can glimpse the whole course of Daoist alchemical practice.

To refine the elixir one must first refine the mind; refining the mind is the essential path of alchemical practice from beginning to end. The ancients said: “To refine the elixir without first refining the mind is like whipping a horse to make it run while hobbling its feet.” Refining the mind is half the work of success.

Few people have ever annotated this text. I have therefore chosen the explanation of it in The Immortals’ Art of Long Life by Ni Qinghe, the “Daoist of True Unity”, published in Taiwan, and appended it for reference.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

Refining the elixir is refining the mind: from stopping stray thoughts and making mind and breath one, to keeping the mind unmoved when the Mysterious Pass returns and following the movement, to nourishing the nature with the jade liquid, uniting with life through the golden liquid, nourishing warmly and sending out the spirit, and finally dissolving even the “numinous mind” back into emptiness — each level remedies the mind left behind by the level before.

Reading tips

  1. The first two levels are the most practical. Level one — “stop at the Cave of Emptiness and look inward at it … a thread of the mind’s light joins a wisp of true breath” — and level two — “when mind and breath rely on each other, let awareness itself dissolve” — can be experienced by anyone who sits in meditation.
  2. The warning at level three is excellent: when the Mysterious Pass returns, “you see what you have never seen, hear what you have never heard; a beautiful scene appears before you and you are flustered and do not know what to do; the mind moves and falls back into the Posterior Heaven” — the moment a good experience appears, if the mind stirs, it is lost.
  3. “Mix the cinnabar with soil and plant it in that one’s house” at level seven is veiled alchemical language. Following the pure-and-quiet principle laid down in Qiu Ling’s preface, Ni Qinghe too understands “that one” and “I” as life and nature, guest and host. We translate accordingly.
  4. From level three on, “passing the three passes”, “curling the tongue to receive the dew”, the “jade-liquid River Chariot”, “sending out the spirit” and so on belong to progressively deeper stages. Safety notes have been added wherever the methods are forceful; levels eight and nine are for understanding only.

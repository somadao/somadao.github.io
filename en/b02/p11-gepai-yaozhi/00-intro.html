Introduction to Part Eleven

What this book is

This part is taken from Xiao Tianshi’s Outline of Daoist Health Cultivation (Daojia yangsheng xue gaiyao 道家养生学概要), published in Taiwan. Xiao Tianshi was a well-known Taiwanese scholar of Daoism who edited the Essence of the Daoist Canon (Daozang jinghua 道藏精华), more than five hundred titles. This part does not teach methods; it gives a “map of the schools” of the alchemical Way: where each school came from, to whom it was transmitted, whether it advocated solitary pure cultivation or dual cultivation, and what its representative works are. Qiu Ling placed it at the end of the whole book so that those who practice alchemy would know the origins of the schools.

Qiu Ling's editorial note

此篇选自台湾出版的肖天石著《道家养生学概要》一书。肖氏为台湾著名道学研究家，曾主编《道藏精华》五百余种。学者若修习道门丹功，应知道门气功各派渊源，肖氏观点可供参考。

This text is taken from the Outline of Daoist Health Cultivation by Xiao Tianshi, published in Taiwan. Mr. Xiao is a well-known Taiwanese researcher of Daoism and once edited the Essence of the Daoist Canon, more than five hundred titles. If students cultivate Daoist alchemical practice, they should know the origins of the various schools of Daoist qigong; Mr. Xiao’s views may serve as a reference.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

The alchemical Way has a thousand gates and a hundred schools, whose common aim is the dual cultivation and dual completion of nature and life, beginning with exhausting the principles; within the human-elixir method, the Wenshi school is the highest and the Shaoyang school the largest, and the Shaoyang school is best known for its Southern and Northern lineages, to which the Eastern, Western, Central, Sanfeng, Wu-Liu, Qingcheng and other schools can all be assigned; but whatever metaphors are used, without understanding true lead and true mercury, the advance and withdrawal of fire phasing, the true moment of gathering, and the aim of warm nourishing, it is all useless — “Ten thousand scrolls of alchemical scriptures — what matters is transmission from heart to heart; the oral formulas of Heaven’s mechanism must always be taught by a teacher.”

Reading tips

  1. This is an overview of schools, not a method. In introducing the schools, Xiao Tianshi says plainly which ones advocated “dual cultivation of yin and yang” or “dual cultivation of man and woman”; we translate according to the original meaning, for understanding the history of the tradition only. All the texts in this book are understood by Qiu Ling according to the pure and quiet method (a single person’s body has its own yin and yang), and readers who practice should go by the earlier parts.
  2. The judgments are Mr. Xiao’s own opinions. For example, he says the Wu-Liu school, “though not the highest alchemical method”, has a method that “is moreover extremely cumbersome”, that the Eastern school “is truly a supreme great method”, and that the Three Peaks (Sanfeng 三峰) school is “a heterodox side door”. Qiu Ling says “Mr. Xiao’s views may serve as a reference”; we translate them without passing judgment.
  3. Relation to the earlier parts of this book: the Western school of Li Hanxu (parts one to three), the Sanfeng school of Zhang Sanfeng (parts seven and eight) and the Wu-Liu school (part nine) can all be placed here in the history of the alchemical Way.
  4. The last sentence matters most: “Ten thousand scrolls of alchemical scriptures — what matters is transmission from heart to heart; the oral formulas of Heaven’s mechanism must always be taught by a teacher.”

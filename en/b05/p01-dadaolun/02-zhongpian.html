Treatise on the Great Way, Middle Essay

Middle Essay of the Treatise on the Great Way — the Golden Elixir is inherent in our very birth; to seek the Reverted Elixir one must first sever desire and wield the killing edge, for going forward makes mortals while reversal makes immortals

About This Chapter

Shorter than the Upper Essay, the Middle Essay speaks in one sustained sweep about the Golden Elixir:

  1. The three things that matter most in life: Between heaven and earth, the most luminous and precious is the human being; the busiest and swiftest is time; and what can grow truly great and endure is the Golden Elixir. Tragically, people drown in fame, fortune, and tangled passions until death arrives and regret comes too late.
  2. The Golden Elixir is not far away: It is "neither absent from the world nor unattainable in the heavens," but "inherent in our very birth"—simple, easy, and close at hand. Recalling a passage from his earlier Rhapsody on the Golden Elixir, Zhang Sanfeng strings together classic alchemical images—Qian and Kun, Kan and Li, the two eights, Lead Flower, the western quarter of Geng, the Knife-Tip Measure, Dragon and Tiger, Metal Duke and Jade Maiden—to sketch the entire arc of gathering the medicine and refining the elixir.
  3. Starting out means severing desire: Quoting the Folio on Awakening to Reality—"Only when you master the killing impulse and grasp cyclic return do you see that grace is born out of harm"—Zhang urges keeping a spiritual sword constantly in hand to cut off external cravings the instant they stir. Once steady habit leaves the mind unmoved before sensory objects, one can walk the Way of returning to the root.
  4. Forward makes mortals; reversal makes immortals: The Way of the Golden Elixir follows the very same principle as conceiving a human body, differing only in forward flow versus reversal. Zhang Sanfeng stresses once more that this is no crooked side-path.

The original commentary at the close notes that this essay focuses on the "outer medicine of the Golden Elixir" while weaving in the "inner medicine of the Golden Liquid," and that every line of the recalled rhapsody is an oral formula.

Reading Note: The passage from the Rhapsody on the Golden Elixir speaks entirely in metaphor. Our translation renders its literal imagery alongside standard alchemical sense without offering step-by-step practice instructions. Expressions such as "of the same kind" and "the other as host, oneself as guest" are interpreted through the pure-stillness method; see the Translator's Notes to the Upper Essay.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

天地之间，至灵至贵者——人也。最忙最速者——时也。可大可久者——金丹也。惜人多溺于功名富贵场中，爱欲恩情之内，狼贪不已，蛾扑何休，一朝大限临身，斯时悔之何及，惟其甘分待终，就死而已，谁知有长生不老之方，谁悟有金丹灵药之妙，诚可惜哉！

Between heaven and earth, the most luminous and precious being is humankind; the busiest and swiftest thing is time; and what can be great and enduring is the Golden Elixir. Alas, most people sink into arenas of rank, wealth, and status, and into webs of craving and attachment—ravenous as wolves without end, throwing themselves like moths into a flame without pause. One morning the final limit arrives, and how can regret reach back in time? Nothing remains but to accept one's lot and wait for death. Who knows that a method for ageless longevity exists? Who awakens to the wonder of the wondrous Medicine of the Golden Elixir? Truly, what a pity!

§2
Original (Chinese)

此金丹灵药，非世间之所无，非天上之不可得者，只在于同类中求之，乃生身固有之物也。简而且易，至近非遥，余尝有金丹赋记之，词极朗畅，今追忆其中段云「夫造金丹者，始则借乾坤为玄牝，学造化于阴阳，识二八之相当，知坎离之互藏，候金炁之满足，听潮水之汪洋；继则看铅花于癸后，玩月夕于庚方，制刀圭于片晌，罢龙虎之战场，唤金公而归舍，配玉女而入房。」果能此道矣。虽愚必明，虽柔必强，先儒曰「圣人不言易，以滋人之惑；亦未尝言难，以阻人之进。」若人用意追求，殷勤修炼，自必入圣超凡，长登寿域，永享无穷之乐也。岂小补哉！

This wondrous Medicine of the Golden Elixir is neither missing from the world nor locked away beyond reach in the heavens. Seek it only within its own kind—it is something inherent in our body from birth, simple and straightforward, close at hand rather than remote.

I once wrote a Rhapsody on the Golden Elixir describing it in bright, flowing verse. Recalling its middle section now: "To forge the Golden Elixir, one first borrows Qian and Kun as the Mysterious Female (see Mysterious Opening / Mysterious Pass) and learns creation from yin and yang; recognizes the exact balance of the two eights, and knows how Kan and Li conceal one another; awaits the fullness of Metal Breath, and listens to the swelling ocean tide. Next, one watches the Lead Flower after the Gui phase, and admires the evening crescent in the western quarter of Geng; compounds the Knife-Tip Measure (see True Intention) in a single instant, and stills the battlefield of Dragon and Tiger; calls the Metal Duke home to his dwelling, and weds him to the Jade Maiden in the chamber." (Every line here speaks in alchemical code: Qian and Kun signify the cauldron and vessel; Kan, Li, and the two eights signify the balanced pairing of yin and yang medicines; full Metal Breath and the swelling tide describe the arising of the medicine; Lead Flower after Gui and the crescent in Geng point to the timing of gathering; and the Knife-Tip Measure, Dragon and Tiger, Metal Duke and Jade Maiden signify harmonizing Lead and Mercury through True Intention to congeal the elixir.)

Truly walk this Way, and though dull you will grow clear, though weak you will grow strong. Earlier Confucians remarked: "The sages do not call it easy, lest they breed confusion; nor do they call it hard, lest they block progress." If a person seeks it with steady intent and cultivates it diligently, one will naturally transcend the ordinary, enter sagehood, ascend the realm of longevity, and enjoy boundless joy forever—hardly a minor benefit!

§3
Original (Chinese)

且人为功名富贵，亦有备极穷苦而后可成者，若以劳苦之心，易而为修炼之心，将见九还到手万劫存神，以比功名富贵，孰短孰长耶？仲尼曰「不义而富且贵，于我如浮云。」又曰「其为仁矣。不使不仁者加乎其身。」不仁不义，莫甚于狂贪妄想，胡氏曰「志于道，则外物不足以累其身。」悟真云「若会杀机明反复，始知害里却生恩。」

Moreover, for the sake of rank, wealth, and honor, people willingly endure every hardship before succeeding. If you simply turn that hardworking resolve into a resolve for cultivation, you will see the Ninefold Reverted Elixir come into your hands and preserve your spirit for ten thousand kalpas. Set beside fame and fortune, which is fleeting and which endures? Confucius said: "Wealth and honor gained without righteousness are to me like floating clouds." He also said: "One who truly practices humaneness lets nothing inhumane cling to his person." Nothing is more inhumane or unrighteous than wild greed and deluded cravings. Master Hu Anguo said: "Set your heart on the Way, and external things can no longer burden your person." And the Folio on Awakening to Reality declares: "Once you grasp the killing edge and understand cyclic return, you first realize that grace is born right out of harm."

§4
Original (Chinese)

是知欲求还丹，必先绝欲，勤于杀机者，刻刻有灵剑在手，外欲乍乘，急须就起杀机，勿容纵意，久久纯熟，对境无心，即可行返本归根之道，易翼云「终日乾乾，反复道也。」反复之道，得长生之果证也。人胡不勉而行之，万物如草木之汇，犹能归根返本以历岁时，人为万物之灵，动至死地，是反不如草木也。能不愧乎？

From this we know that whoever seeks the Reverted Elixir must first sever desire. One who diligently applies the "killing edge" holds a luminous sword of wisdom in hand at every moment: the instant an outside desire slips in, one immediately raises that edge to cut it off, allowing no indulgence of fancy. Over long practice this matures into ease; facing external circumstances with an unruffled mind, one is ready to walk the Way of returning to the origin and reverting to the root. As the Ten Wings of the Book of Changes say: "Diligent all day long—this is returning again and again to the Way." That Way of cyclic return yields the realized fruit of long life. Why do people not exert themselves to practice it? Even grasses and trees return to their roots each season to endure through the years; yet human beings, the most luminous of all creatures, rush headlong into death—falling short even of plants and trees. Should that not move us to shame?

§5
Original (Chinese)

夫此返本归根之道，又非邪径旁门之说也。世人以德行为先，阴功为本，察阴阳造化之机，求玄牝乾坤之妙，辨二八坎离之物，定金花水月之时，施降龙伏虎之威，明立命生身之处，其间致虚守静，他主我宾，日月交光，戊己为用，则丹成于反掌矣。易云「男女媾精，万物化生。」盖人有此身，亦因父母而得，倘无父母，身何有乎？故作金丹之道与生身事同，但顺则成人，逆则成仙，顺逆之间，天地悬隔，祗要逆用阴阳，自然成就，并非邪径旁门也。

Now this Way of returning to the root is by no means the teaching of crooked paths or heterodox sects. Putting virtuous conduct first and hidden merit as the foundation, one observes the pivot of yin and yang in creation, seeks the mystery of the Mysterious Female and Qian and Kun, discerns the medicines of the two eights and Kan and Li, determines the exact moment of the Golden Flower and water-reflected moon, wields the power that tames the Dragon and subdues the Tiger, and understands the very place where life is established and the body generated. Throughout it all, one attains utmost emptiness and guards deep stillness, letting "the other be host and oneself the guest" (letting Prior Heaven Breath lead rather than forcing with the conscious mind), uniting sun and moon in radiance (see union of sun and moon), and taking Wu and Ji (True Intention) as the active pivot—then the Elixir forms as effortlessly as turning over one's hand.

The Book of Changes states: "Male and female mingle their essences, and the myriad things are transformed and born." Indeed, we possess this body only through our father and mother; without parents, where would the body come from? Hence the Way of forming the Golden Elixir shares the exact same principle as generating a human body: moving forward produces a mortal, while moving in reversal produces an immortal. Between forward flow and reversal lies a gulf as wide as heaven and earth. Simply employ yin and yang in reversal and completion follows naturally—this is no crooked byway at all.

§6
Original (Chinese)

兹余所论，大泄真机，皆列圣口传心授之旨，人能照此下手行持，自能夺天地玄妙之功，穷鬼神不测之奥，诚金丹之口诀也。除此之外，再无别传，先贤曰「圣人未生，道在天地；圣人已往，道著六经。」予之末论，虽不敢与圣经相比，亦可为问道之正途，如拨云雾而见青天，似剪荆棘而寻大路，坦然无碍，豁然有门，学者若能专心研究，自然默契仙缘，幸勿轻易视之也。万金难换，百宝难求，勿示非人，尚其重之。

What I have set out here discloses the true mechanism openly—every word reflects the oral and heart-to-heart transmission of the sages of old. Whoever takes hold of this and puts it into practice will naturally seize the subtle work of heaven and earth and fathom mysteries beyond gods and spirits. Truly these are the oral formulas of the Golden Elixir; outside of this, there is no other secret transmission. Earlier worthies said: "Before the sages were born, the Way resided in heaven and earth; after the sages departed, the Way was inscribed in the Six Classics." Though my humble treatise dares not compare itself to the classics of the sages, it can still serve as a straight road for seekers of the Way—like parting clouds and mist to behold clear sky, or clearing brambles to uncover the highway, open and unobstructed, suddenly revealing the gate. If students study it with single-minded care, they will quietly align with an immortal affinity. Do not regard it lightly: ten thousand pieces of gold could not buy it, nor a hundred treasures procure it. Show it not to the unworthy, and cherish it deeply.

Original Commentary
Original (Chinese)

悟真篇云「学仙须是学天仙，惟有金丹最的端。」此篇专讲金丹外药，而金液内药亦次其中矣。反反复复，总劝人及时省悟炼己求铅，觉世婆心传神简上，旧赋一段句句口诀，有志者，再求口诀中之口诀，则还丹不难矣。

Original commentary: The Folio on Awakening to Reality says: "To study immortality, study Celestial Immortality; only the Golden Elixir is truly certain and direct." This essay speaks specifically of the outer medicine of the Golden Elixir while weaving in the inner medicine of the Golden Liquid alongside it. Over and again it urges readers to wake up in time, refine the self, and seek True Lead—an earnest compassion for awakening the world shines vividly off the page. Every line of the recalled rhapsody is an oral formula; if an earnest seeker goes on to seek the formula within the formula, attaining the Reverted Elixir will not be difficult.

Key Terms

  • Golden Elixir (jindan): called here "something inherent in our body from birth," to be sought only "within its own kind."
  • Rhapsody on the Golden Elixir (Jindan fu): an earlier composition by Zhang Sanfeng, now lost except for the middle passage recalled here, which outlines gathering the medicine and refining the elixir in symbolic verse.
  • Two eights (erba): half a catty and eight ounces—an image for yin and yang medicines balanced in equal measure.
  • Lead Flower after Gui and evening moon in Geng: in alchemical imagery, the "arising of Gui" marks the instant desire stirs and Posterior Heaven turbid essence is about to form, before which Prior Heaven Breath must be gathered; the crescent moon appearing in the western quarter of Geng on the third evening symbolizes the first stirring of yang.
  • Knife-Tip Measure (daogui): the characters Wu and Ji (the two Earth stems) combine graphically into gui, symbolizing the harmonizing role of True Intention.
  • Metal Duke and Jade Maiden: Metal Duke stands for Lead (Prior Heaven Breath) and Jade Maiden for Mercury (Original Spirit), picturing the union of Lead and Mercury.
  • Killing edge and sword of wisdom: clear awareness that immediately severs stray cravings; see refining the self.
  • Returning to the origin and reverting to the root: turning back from Posterior Heaven to Prior Heaven; see concentrating Spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath.
  • The other as host, oneself as guest: letting Prior Heaven lead while Posterior Heaven steps back, without forcing through willful intent.
  • reversal: "Forward makes mortals; reversal makes immortals."

Putting It into Practice

  1. "Keep a luminous sword in hand at every moment." The instant a craving arises, notice it and let it go—that is what Zhang Sanfeng calls the "killing edge," and it is the entry point of all cultivation.
  2. Staying unmoved before circumstances comes through steady repetition. Desire is not severed once and for all in a single stroke; it is cut off again and again until quiet clarity becomes second nature.
  3. Redirect part of the drive spent chasing status into cultivating yourself. Zhang Sanfeng's comparison is plain and practical: people endure endless strain for career and wealth; turning even a portion of that grit inward is enough.
  4. "Attain utmost emptiness, guard deep stillness; let the other be host and oneself the guest." During quiet sitting, do not strain to "make" anything happen—grow still and let the body's natural transformations take the lead.
  5. Treat coded metaphors as overview only. The passage from the Rhapsody on the Golden Elixir is a poetic summary, never a literal manual; this site does not offer practice routines derived from it.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Like moths into a flame without pause": a vivid metaphor for insatiable craving.
  2. "I once wrote a Rhapsody on the Golden Elixir": the full text is no longer extant; only this middle passage survives here.
  3. "Watches the Lead Flower after the Gui phase": alchemical texts typically say "gather swiftly when Gui stirs" or "when Lead meets Gui, gather at once," meaning to collect Prior Heaven Breath just before Posterior Heaven desire (Gui water) forms; the original text literally reads "after Gui" (gui hou) and is preserved as printed.
  4. "Earlier Confucians remarked: 'The sages do not call it easy...'": a saying of Song Neo-Confucians, credited simply to "earlier Confucians" in the original.
  5. "Master Hu": identified by the modern editor of the base text as the Song scholar Hu Anguo.
  6. "Once you grasp the killing edge and understand cyclic return, you first realize that grace is born right out of harm": a quatrain line from the Folio on Awakening to Reality (Wuzhen pian).
  7. "Diligent all day long—this is returning again and again to the Way": from the Commentary on the Images for Hexagram Qian in the Book of Changes, cited here as the Ten Wings (Yi yi).
  8. "Male and female mingle their essences, and the myriad things are transformed and born": from the Appended Remarks of the Book of Changes. Here it illustrates the contrast between forward generation and alchemical reversal rather than sexual activity; consistent with this site's pure-stillness reading, see Note 16 of the Upper Essay.
  9. "Before the sages were born, the Way resided in heaven and earth; after the sages departed, the Way was inscribed in the Six Classics": a Song Confucian maxim, attributed simply to "earlier worthies" in the original.

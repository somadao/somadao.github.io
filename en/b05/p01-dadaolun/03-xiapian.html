Treatise on the Great Way, Lower Essay

Lower Essay of the Treatise on the Great Way — one yin and one yang are one nature and one life; distinguishing intentional and non-intentional work, inner and outer medicine, why refining the self is hardest, and the full arc from laying the foundation to facing the wall

About This Chapter

The Lower Essay is the most hands-on, practice-oriented part of the Treatise on the Great Way—the original commentary praises it as "tracing every root and branch as clearly as a commentary and sub-commentary." It unfolds in five parts:

  1. Inner nature and life: "One yin and one yang is called the Way"—and one yin and one yang are simply inner nature (xing) and life (ming). Nature is principle; fulfilling nature completes the inner elixir. Life is Heaven's "mandate"; tending that mandate diligently and applying it rightly—refining Breath to become Spirit and refining Spirit to return to Heaven—completes the outer elixir.
  2. Recognizing the "first stirring of yang" at the outset: When True Lead first arises, it flashes "as swiftly as lightning" and in a blink turns into Posterior Heaven Gui water, no longer usable. Atop Mount Kunlun stands a mysterious gate through which only formless True Breath can pass, never anything with coarse physical substance—which is why sexual practices cannot work.
  3. Intentional work (youwei) and non-intentional work (wuwei): Intentional work is refining the self and laying the foundation before obtaining the Elixir—"a Way of quiet emptiness, holding to the feminine rather than the assertive," never sexual combat or forceful breath-drawing. Non-intentional work is gentle nurturing, ablution, shedding the embryo, and spiritual transformation after obtaining the Elixir, not mere defensive guarding of the mind.
  4. Inner medicine and outer medicine: Outer medicine arises in the Cave of Creation; it is Breath and establishes life. Inner medicine is produced within one's own body; it is Essence and nourishes inner nature. Dual cultivation of nature and life alone accords with the Way of the immortals.
  5. Refining the self is the hardest part: "Attaining the Reverted Elixir is easy; refining the self is hardest." If self-refinement is incomplete, when one sits down to practice, "outer fire may stir while the inner tally fails to respond," and stray impulses wreak havoc. Only when self-refinement matures—mind dead yet Spirit alive, body empty so Breath flows freely—can one speak of the first yang, the two phases, inverting water and fire into harmonious completion, and onward to ten months of shedding the embryo and nine years facing the wall.

Safety Note: The second half of this essay describes advanced stages such as gathering the medicine, drawing it into the center, shedding the embryo, and sending out the spirit. Please read those passages for understanding only. What you can genuinely put to use lies in the first half: "rectifying the mind and making intention sincere" and "refining the self"—keeping the mind uncluttered, quiet, and sparing in desire.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

一阴一阳之谓道，修道者，修此阴阳之道也。一阴一阳——一性一命而已矣。中庸云修道之谓教，三教圣人，皆本此道以立其教也。此道原于性、本于命，命犹令也。天以命而赐人以令也。性即理也。人以性而由天之理也。

"One yin and one yang is called the Way" (from the Appended Remarks of the Book of Changes). Cultivating the Way means cultivating this Way of yin and yang. And what are one yin and one yang? Simply one inner nature and one life (see inner nature (xing) / existence, life (ming)). The Doctrine of the Mean says, "Cultivating the Way is called teaching"—the sages of all three teachings founded their teachings upon this very Way. This Way originates in inner nature and is rooted in life. "Life" (ming) is like a "mandate" (ling): through life, Heaven confers its decree upon humanity. "Inner nature" (xing) is "principle" (li): through inner nature, human beings walk in Heaven's principle.

§2
Original (Chinese)

夫欲由其理，则外尽伦常者其理，内尽慎独者其理，忠孝友恭衷乎内也，然著其光辉则在外也。喜怒哀乐见于外也，然守其未发则在内也。明朗朗天，活泼泼地，尽其性而内丹成矣。夫欲全其令，则殷勤顾之者此令，依法用之者此令，存心养性——此顾命之勤劳也。集义生气——此用令之法度也。炼炁化神，炼神还天，复其性兼复其命，而外丹就矣。吾愿后之人修此正道，故直言之。

To walk in this principle: fulfilling human relationships outwardly is this principle, and keeping watch over oneself in solitude inwardly is also this principle. Loyalty, filial piety, brotherly care, and respect spring from within, yet their radiance shines outward; joy, anger, sorrow, and delight appear outwardly, yet guarding their state before they stir happens within. Bright and clear as the sky, lively and free as the earth—fulfill your inner nature, and the inner elixir is complete.

To keep Heaven's mandate whole: tending it attentively is this mandate, and applying it rightly is also this mandate. "Preserving the mind and nourishing inner nature"—that is the diligent care of tending the mandate. "Accumulating righteousness to generate breath" (as Mencius teaches of the flood-like breath)—that is the proper measure for applying the mandate. Refining Breath to become Spirit, and refining Spirit to return to Heaven (i.e., to emptiness; see the three transmutations), restoring inner nature and life together—then the outer elixir is accomplished. Because I hope later generations will cultivate this upright Way, I state it plainly.

§3
Original (Chinese)

修道以修身为大，然修身必先正心诚意，意诚心正则物欲皆除，然后讲立基之本，气为使焉。神为主焉。学者下手之初，必须知一阳初动之候，真铅始生之时，其炁迅速如电，而不能久居于先天，霎时而生癸水，顷刻而变经流，迨至生形化质，已属后天而不可用矣。

In cultivating the Way, cultivating one's own person is paramount; yet cultivating the person must begin with rectifying the mind and making intention sincere. Once intention is sincere and the mind is upright, material cravings drop away, and only then can one speak of the foundation (laying the foundation): Breath serves as the envoy, while Spirit sits as the master.

At the very outset of practice, the student must recognize the moment of the "first stirring of yang" (see Living Zi Hour)—the instant True Lead is first born. That Breath flashes as swiftly as lightning and cannot linger long in Prior Heaven: in a blink it turns into Gui water (Posterior Heaven turbid essence), and in a moment it shifts into downward-flowing discharge. Once it takes physical form and coarse substance, it already belongs to Posterior Heaven and can no longer be used.

§4
Original (Chinese)

昆仑之上有玄门，其窍甚小，阴阳会合时不许动摇，待其情性相感，自然彼我相通，凡有形质者不能升入窍内。夫惟真炁橐籥，乃能进于窍内也。故圣人直指以先天一炁冲开此窍，又曰修行之径路，可以续命延年，修真而全真，无来无去，不生不灭。

Atop Mount Kunlun (the crown of the head; see Muddy Pellet (niwan)) lies a Mysterious Gate whose opening is exceedingly small. When yin and yang meet, no stirring or agitation is permitted; wait until disposition and inner nature resonate with one another, and self and other naturally communicate. Nothing that possesses coarse physical substance can ascend into this opening; only the bellows-like opening and closing of True Breath can enter it. Thus the sages point straight to using the One Breath of Prior Heaven to break open this aperture, calling it the true pathway of cultivation: through it one extends life and lengthens years, refines the genuine to complete authenticity—neither coming nor going, neither born nor perishing.

§5
Original (Chinese)

今之愚人，闻说有用生阳之道者，却行御女巧诈之术，正如披麻救火、飞蛾扑灯，贪其美色，胡肆纵横，日则逞力多劳，夜则恣情纵欲，致使神昏炁败，髓竭精枯，犹不醒悟，甘分待终，古之贤人不然，忠孝两全，仁义博施，暗行方便，默积阴功，但以死生为念，不以名利关心，日则少虑无思，夜则清心寡欲，以此神全炁壮，髓满精盈，每叹凡躯如石中之火，似水上之沤，未闻道者急求师，已闻道者急求药，又能广参博采，信受奉行，求先天之大药，寻出世之丹方，忙忙下手速修，惟恐时不待人。

Foolish people today hear talk of "employing the Way of generating yang" and turn instead to crafty schemes of exploiting women—just like wearing hemp to fight a fire or a moth flinging itself at a lamp. Craving beauty and running wild without restraint, they strain their strength by day and indulge their appetites by night, until Spirit grows dim, Breath collapses, marrow runs dry, and essence withers. Still they refuse to wake up, passively awaiting their end.

The worthies of antiquity were altogether different. Fulfilling both loyalty and filial piety, extending humaneness and righteousness widely, quietly doing good turns and silently building hidden merit, they kept only life and death in mind and cared nothing for fame or gain. By day they kept anxious thoughts to a minimum; by night they kept the heart clear and desires sparse. Thus their Spirit stayed whole, Breath stayed vigorous, marrow stayed full, and essence stayed abundant. Ever sighing that the mortal frame is like a spark struck from flint or a bubble upon water, those who had not yet heard the Way hurried to seek a teacher, and those who had heard it hurried to seek the medicine. Consulting widely, gathering wisdom carefully, and taking it to heart in faithful practice, they sought the Great Medicine of Prior Heaven and the transcendent formula of the Elixir—setting to work at once lest time leave them behind.

§6
Original (Chinese)

夫道者岂是旁门小技，乃至人口传心授，金液还丹之妙道也。非定息二乘之法，乃最上一乘之道，以有为入无为，以外药修内药，以己而求彼，以阴而配阳，以铅而投汞，以炁而合神，无为者，非防危守城之方，乃得丹之后，温养沐浴之事，脱胎神化之功也。有为者，非采战提吸之术，九一动摇之法，乃安静虚无之道，守雌不雄，寂然不动感而遂通，此即未得丹之前，炼己筑基之事也。有为无为——体用之始终已见于此，内药外药——出处之法相又详于彼。

How could this Way be some trivial trick of a side-path? It is the wondrous Way of the Golden Liquid and Reverted Elixir, passed mouth to ear and heart to heart by Perfected Masters. It is not the lesser vehicle of fixed breath-holding, but the supreme single vehicle: entering non-intentional work through intentional work, cultivating the inner medicine through the outer medicine, seeking the other through oneself, pairing yin with yang, casting Lead into Mercury, and uniting Breath with Spirit.

What is called "non-intentional work" (wuwei) is not merely the defensive tactic of "guarding against peril and holding the city wall"; it is the work after obtaining the Elixir—gentle nurturing and ablution (muyu), shedding the embryo and spiritual transformation. What is called "intentional work" (youwei) is not sexual combat, forceful inhalation, or the agitations of "nine shallow and one deep"; it is a Way of quiet stillness and emptiness, holding to the feminine without forcing the assertive, utterly still yet resonating the instant it is stirred—in short, the work of refining the self and laying the foundation before the Elixir is obtained. Here the whole arc of substance and function in intentional and non-intentional work is clear; next let us examine the origins and characteristics of inner and outer medicine.

§7
Original (Chinese)

外药者，在造化窟中而生，内药者，在自己身中而产，内药是精，外药是炁，内药养性，外药立命，性命双修方合神仙之道。

Outer medicine arises in the Cave of Creation (see the Cave of Emptiness); inner medicine is produced within one's own body. Inner medicine is Essence; outer medicine is Breath. Inner medicine nourishes inner nature; outer medicine establishes life (see Medicine (yao)). Only dual cultivation of nature and life accords with the Way of the immortals.

§8
Original (Chinese)

大修行人，欲求先天外药，必炼己以待阳生，用神炁炼成慧剑，采金水匀配柔刚，古人采药进火，全凭此物除七情之患、去五贼之害。盖还丹容易，炼己最难，若无炼己以去贼之患害，则不能常应常静，魂魄焉能受制，情欲岂不相干？若要入室施功，临炉下手，则外火虽动而内符不应，只因刚柔未配，以此慧剑无锋，群魔为害，心神不宁，欲念杂起，故乃逐境飘流，致使汞火飞扬，圣胎不结，如使炼己纯熟，则心无杂念，体若太虚，一尘不染，万虑皆空，心死则神活，体虚则气运，方许求一阳之道，二候之功，凭慧剑剖破鸿蒙，舒匠手凿开混沌，却用阴阳颠倒之法，水火既济之道，乃行地天交泰，使阳居下，火必照上，令阴在上，水能润下，只要苦行忍辱，身心不动，己之性若往，彼之炁自回，人能如此，便得守雌不雄，寂然不动，感而遂通之效也。

A great practitioner who wishes to obtain the Prior Heaven outer medicine must first refine the self while awaiting the birth of yang. Forging Spirit and Breath into the Sword of Wisdom, one gathers Metal and Water to blend the yielding and the firm in even measure. When the ancients gathered the medicine and advanced the fire, they relied entirely on this sword to banish the troubles of the seven emotions and remove the harm of the five thieves (the cravings of the five senses).

Attaining the Reverted Elixir is easy; refining the self is hardest. Without refining the self to clear away these inner thieves, one cannot remain still amid constant response—how could the ethereal and corporeal souls be brought under restraint, and how could emotional cravings fail to interfere? If one enters the chamber to practice and approaches the furnace in that state, the outer fire may stir yet the inner tally fails to answer. Simply because firm and yielding are not balanced, the Sword of Wisdom lacks an edge; swarms of inner demons cause havoc, mind and spirit grow unsettled, and stray desires erupt. Swept along by sensory impressions, the fire of Mercury scatters upward and the Holy Embryo cannot congeal.

Yet if self-refinement is thoroughly mature, the mind holds not a single stray thought, the body feels like Great Emptiness, unstained by a speck of dust, with ten thousand cares emptied away. When the ordinary mind dies, Spirit comes alive; when the body grows empty, Breath circulates freely. Only then may one pursue the Way of the first yang and the work of the two phases: parting the primeval haze with the Sword of Wisdom, carving open primordial chaos with a master artisan's hand, and applying the method of inverting yin and yang and harmonizing water and fire (see conjunction of Kan and Li). Practicing the Peace of Earth above Heaven, one places yang below so fire naturally shines upward, and yin above so water naturally moistens downward. Simply persevere through discipline and forbearance, keeping body and mind unmoved: when one's own inner nature reaches gently outward, the Breath of the other returns of itself. Whoever reaches this enjoys the true fruit of "holding to the feminine without forcing the assertive, utterly still yet resonating at the slightest touch."

§9
Original (Chinese)

太极将判之间，静已极而未至于动，阳初复而未离乎阴，候此先天真炁降，以法追摄，送入黄庭之中，日运己汞包固周密，汞气渐多，铅气渐散，合丹于鼎，又须调停真息，周流六虚，至声寂而意合，乃气匀而脉住，丹始凝结，只待圣胎气足，十月功圆，脱胎神化，降生婴儿，调之纯熟，出入纵横，往来无碍，不被群魔引诱，只待九转功成，面壁之时，炼精则化炁，炼神则化虚，形神俱妙与道合真，此大丈夫功成名遂之时也。

At the threshold where the Supreme Ultimate is about to divide—stillness having reached its utmost yet not yet tipping into movement, yang just returning yet not yet parting from yin—one awaits the descent of this Prior Heaven True Breath, draws it in according to method, and conveys it into the Yellow Court (huangting). Day by day one circulates one's own Mercury to wrap and seal it tightly; gradually the Mercury breath increases while the Lead breath disperses, merging into the Elixir within the tripod. One must also regulate True Breath so it circulates through all six quarters, until sound falls silent and intention unites, breath grows even and the pulse seems to pause—only then does the Elixir begin to congeal.

Then one simply waits until the Holy Embryo is filled with breath and ten months of work reach fullness, shedding the embryo in spiritual transformation to give birth to the Infant. Nurturing it until thoroughly mature, it goes out and returns freely in every direction without hindrance, unswayed by any illusion. Waiting until the nine revolutions are complete and the time of facing the wall arrives—refining Essence to become Breath, refining Spirit to become Emptiness, body and spirit alike perfected and united in truth with the Way (see the three transmutations)—this is the hour when a person of great resolve achieves full completion.

§10
Original (Chinese)

是道古人不传于世。盖缘愚人信之不笃，行之不勤，而且反生诽谤，是以秘而不传，予自得遇至人以来，述此修身秘要，以警觉后学，同志者各加黾勉，共陟仙都。

The ancients did not transmit this Way openly to the world, because foolish people neither trust it deeply nor practice it diligently, and even turn around to slander it; hence it was kept secret. Since I was fortunate to meet a Perfected Master, I have set down these secret essentials of self-cultivation to awaken later students. Let all fellow seekers exert themselves, and ascend together to the capital of the immortals!

Original Commentary
Original (Chinese)

此篇穷性命之真，发圣贤仙佛之理，本本原原如疏如注，有为无为之序，内药外药之分，养胎脱胎之妙，尽著简编，观止已，蔑以加矣。

Original commentary: This essay fathoms the truth of inner nature and life and expounds the principles shared by sages, worthies, immortals, and Buddhas, tracing every root and branch as clearly as a commentary and sub-commentary. The sequence of intentional and non-intentional work, the distinction between inner and outer medicine, and the wonders of nurturing and shedding the embryo are all laid out in full on these pages. One beholds perfection here—nothing more could be added.

Key Terms

  • One yin and one yang are one nature and one life: the essay equates yin and yang directly with inner nature and life; see inner nature (xing) / existence, life (ming).
  • Life is like a mandate; inner nature is principle: Heaven bestows its mandate through life, while human beings follow Heaven's principle through inner nature.
  • Fulfilling nature completes the inner elixir; restoring life completes the outer elixir: here "inner elixir" leans toward nature cultivation and "outer elixir" toward life cultivation—quite distinct from external laboratory alchemy with minerals.
  • First stirring of yang: the instant True Lead is first born; see Living Zi Hour.
  • Gui water and downward flow: when the first yang breath is not gathered, it turns into Posterior Heaven turbid essence flowing outward.
  • Mysterious Gate of Kunlun: located at the crown of the head; see Muddy Pellet (niwan).
  • Intentional work (youwei) and non-intentional work (wuwei): intentional work is refining the self and laying the foundation before obtaining the Elixir; non-intentional work is gentle nurturing, ablution, shedding the embryo, and spiritual transformation afterward.
  • Inner medicine and outer medicine: outer medicine arises in the Cave of Creation, is Breath, and establishes life; inner medicine is produced within one's own body, is Essence, and nourishes inner nature; see Medicine (yao).
  • Sword of Wisdom: forged from Spirit and Breath to cut away the seven emotions and five thieves; see refining the self.
  • The two phases (erhou): the phase of gathering the medicine and the phase of returning it to the furnace; see Golden Flower (jinhua).
  • Peace of Earth above Heaven (ditian jiaotai): placing yang below and yin above so fire shines upward and water moistens downward—that is, the conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Nine revolutions and facing the wall: see the three transmutations.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Begin by rectifying the mind and making intention sincere. Zhang Sanfeng writes that cultivating the person is paramount, and it must start with an upright mind and sincere intention—the step before every step.
  2. "Attaining the Reverted Elixir is easy; refining the self is hardest." The work anyone can actually practice is refining the self: fewer anxious thoughts by day, a clear heart and modest desires by night, and an uncluttered mind.
  3. Intentional work does not mean straining. What Zhang Sanfeng calls "intentional work" is quiet emptiness and holding to the feminine, never forceful breath-pulling; anything involving forcible drawing, breath-holding, or sexual combat falls completely outside this path.
  4. When outer fire stirs but the inner tally fails to answer, self-refinement is not yet ripe. If bodily sensations arise during quiet sitting and immediately pull the mind into agitation, the heart is not yet settled—do not rush ahead.
  5. Read advanced stages for understanding only. Gathering the medicine, ten months of shedding the embryo, and nine years facing the wall are traditional accounts; this site does not provide operational techniques for them.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Bright and clear as the sky, lively and free as the earth": a favorite Song–Ming Confucian expression for a luminous, unencumbered mind.
  2. "Refining Breath to become Spirit, and refining Spirit to return to Heaven": standard formulations usually say "return to Emptiness" (huanxu); the original text here reads "return to Heaven" (huantian) and is preserved as printed.
  3. "In a blink it turns into Gui water, and in a moment shifts into downward-flowing discharge": alchemists label the stirring of Prior Heaven Breath "Ren" and its shift into Posterior Heaven essence "Gui"; "downward flow" (jingliu) refers to ordinary discharged essence.
  4. "Calling it the true pathway of cultivation": the original text reads "also says" (you yue), which connects somewhat loosely to the preceding clause; preserved as printed.
  5. "Not sexual combat, forceful inhalation, or the agitations of 'nine shallow and one deep'": these are technical labels of heterodox bedchamber arts, all explicitly repudiated here by Zhang Sanfeng.
  6. "Seeking the other through oneself" and "when one's own inner nature reaches gently outward, the Breath of the other returns of itself": read here in the pure-stillness framework as Spirit seeking Breath; see Note 16 of the Upper Essay.
  7. "Parting the primeval haze with the Sword of Wisdom, carving open primordial chaos with a master artisan's hand": a metaphor for using clear awareness to open the primordial aperture of Prior Heaven.
  8. "Until sound falls silent and intention unites, breath grows even and the pulse seems to pause": traditional signs of Embryonic Breathing, when respiration becomes exceedingly fine and the pulse seems stilled.
  9. "Ascend together to the capital of the immortals": the verb zhi (陟) means to climb or ascend.

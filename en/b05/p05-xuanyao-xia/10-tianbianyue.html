V · Essentials of the Mystery (Part Two)

Nine Daoist Songs on the Moon at Heaven’s Edge

Nine Daoist Songs on the Moon at Heaven’s Edge — the moon at heaven’s edge, its shadow bright; when there is no thought, what need is there for effort? Where ideation ceases, the original substance is just-so

About This Chapter

All nine lyrics open with “Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow [bright / secluded / cold…].” The original title note explains that it draws on the meaning of “the moon at heaven’s edge emerging in the geng quarter”—the crescent new moon appearing in the western geng position on the third day of the lunar month, which inner alchemists use as a metaphor for the first stirring of yang.

The nine songs progress from exhortation to realization of the Way:

  • Nos. 1 & 2: The human realm is like an overturned basin, caught in clinging to family and boasting of wealth; “only through countless kalpas of cultivation is a human body gained,” so better to leap out of the red dust, seek an enlightened master, and cultivate.
  • No. 3: Becoming an immortal is not hard, yet first one must sever the Three Corpses: “with great spiritual power plant a lotus in the fire; with a master craftsman’s hand pole a boat against the current.”
  • Nos. 4 to 8: The Magpie Bridge, the Yellow Court (huangting), the Cowherd and Weaver Girl, the Dragon Maiden offering her pearl, a jade flute opening the heaven-penetrating aperture, awakening to true mystery beneath an ancient pine—all depict inner scenes along the alchemical path.
  • No. 9: Finally everything returns to “nothingness”: “when there is no thought, what need is there for effort? Where ideation ceases, the original substance is just-so (ruru), and that very suchness is the original road from the womb.”

Original Text and Translation

§1Nine Daoist Songs on the Moon at Heaven’s Edge
Original (Chinese)

（原题注：取天边月出庚之义。）

(Original title note: Drawn from the meaning of “the moon at heaven’s edge emerging in the geng quarter.”)

§2Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 1
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影明，照见人间似覆盆，覆盆多少寃家阵；累劫修才得人身，失却了万劫难寻，难寻八宝真如性；贪妻子羊伴虎群，夸富贵倚势欺贫，欺贫还有天报应；倒不如跳出红尘，拜明师早去修行，那时自有仙家分。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow bright, shining down upon the human world like an overturned basin—how many armies of karmic foes throng beneath that overturned basin! Only after cultivating across countless kalpas do you gain a human body; once lost, ten thousand kalpas will not easily recover it—hard to recover is the eight-jeweled nature of True Suchness. Clinging to wife and children is like a sheep keeping company with a pack of tigers; boasting of wealth and rank, leaning on power to bully the poor—bullying the poor still invites Heaven’s retribution. Far better to leap free of the red dust, bow to an enlightened master, and cultivate early; then your share in the immortal household will naturally arrive.

§3Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 2
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影幽，贪恋荣华谁肯休，不休那得仙缘凑；叹世人，系孤舟，好似一水上浮沤，浮沤散却难依就；倒不如早早回头，拜明师倒跨青牛，积功累行丹成后；守志时四海云游，得志时步上瀛洲，那时节，姓名才上金阶奏。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow secluded: who among those craving splendor and glory is willing to stop? Without stopping, how can immortal affinity ever come together? Alas for mortals moored to a solitary skiff, just like a bubble floating on water—once the bubble bursts, nothing remains to lean upon. Far better to turn back early, bow to an enlightened master, and ride the green ox backward; accumulating merit and virtuous deeds until the Elixir is complete, while keeping your resolve you roam like a cloud across the four seas, and when fulfillment arrives you step onto the Isle of Yingzhou—at that hour your name is presented at the Golden Steps.

§4Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 3
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影寒，要做神仙也不难，不难要把三尸斩；降龙钵口口相传，伏虎盂妙诀难言，难言莫与匪人显；大神通火里栽莲，高匠手逆水撑船，逆水撑船休要乱；主人翁掌定风帆，浪滚中采取真铅，那时方赴灵霄殿。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow cold: becoming a spirit-immortal is not hard—not hard, yet you must sever the Three Corpses. The dragon-subduing alms-bowl is passed from mouth to mouth; the wondrous formula of the tiger-taming bowl is hard to put in words—hard to speak of, never display it to unworthy people. Great spiritual power plants a lotus right in the fire; a master craftsman’s hand poles a boat upstream—when poling upstream, never lose your composure! Let the true Master hold the sail steady, and amid rolling waves gather True Lead; only then will you ascend to the Hall of Numinous Empyrean.

§5Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 4
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影高，有个神仙驾鹊桥，鹊桥路险人难到；黄庭宫采取药苗，银河内长有水潮，水潮方显玄中妙；有牛郎降下天曹，那织女忙把手招，牛郎织女同欢笑；愿上天赐个黄婆，好姻缘恍惚相交，功完行满天书诏。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow high: an immortal spans the Magpie Bridge—the road across the Magpie Bridge is steep and few can reach it. Gather the sprout of Medicine (yao) in the Yellow Court (huangting) Palace; within the Silver River tides forever rise, and those rising tides reveal the wonder within the mystery. The Cowherd descends from the heavenly bureau while the Weaver Girl hurries to wave him near, and Cowherd and Weaver Girl laugh together in joy. May Heaven bestow a Yellow Dame so that this fine union joins in the dim and obscure; when merit is complete and conduct fulfilled, Heaven’s decree summons you.

§6Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 5
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影孤，修行人，大丈夫；丈夫才入修行路，张仙姑曾拜吕祖；有龙女献上宝珠，宝珠拥出嫦娥护；泥丸宫降下琼酥，如朱橘酿酒醍醐，醍醐到口如甘露；进阳火要退阴符，入丹田牢牢封固，那时自有通天路。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow lone: a true cultivator is a person of great courage! Only the resolute enter the path of cultivation—Immortal Maiden Zhang herself once bowed to Patriarch Lü. The Dragon Maiden offers up a precious pearl, escorted forth under Chang’e’s protection. From the Muddy Pellet (niwan) Palace descends jade cream, like fine clarified nectar brewed from vermilion tangerines; reaching the mouth, that nectar tastes like Sweet Dew. Advance the yang fire and withdraw the yin tally, sealing it fast inside the Cinnabar Field—then a road straight through to Heaven opens of itself.

§7Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 6
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影飘，有个神仙品玉箫，玉箫品出通天窍；地天泰不动不摇，颠倒用手段要高，手高方显玄中妙；白云飞飞上九霄，黄芽长长就灵苗，先天奥妙谁知晓；滚盘珠难画难描，牟尼殿坚固坚牢，那时方把师恩报。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow drifting: an immortal plays a jade flute, blowing open the aperture that reaches Heaven. Earth and Heaven join in Tai (Peace), utterly still and unshaken; to apply the inverted method requires lofty skill, and lofty skill reveals the wonder within the mystery. White clouds soar up to the ninth empyrean, the Yellow Sprout grows into a numinous shoot—who truly knows the subtle secrets of Prior Heaven? A pearl rolling on a platter is impossible to paint or sketch; the Maṇi Hall stands solid and impregnable—only then do you truly repay your master’s grace.

§8Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 7
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影低，十八弟子戏牟尼，牟尼竟入长生地；西来意如醉如痴，观自在清净无为，无为妙用有为起；铅投汞寿与天齐，水火交永保无虞，三尸缩首魔王避；延寿酒，不用杯，甜如蜜自饮刀圭，醺醺去赴蟠桃会。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow low: eighteen disciples sport with the Maṇi jewel, and the Maṇi enters the ground of long life. The Patriarch’s meaning from the West feels like being tipsy or entranced; Sovereign Contemplation is pure, still, and non-acting, yet the wondrous function of non-action rises from intentional work. When Lead enters Mercury, lifespan equals Heaven’s; when water and fire unite, safety lasts forever, the Three Corpses shrink back, and the Demon King flees. The longevity wine needs no cup—sweet as honey, you drink the knife-tip measure by yourself, and tipsy with joy set off for the Banquet of Immortal Peaches.

§9Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 8
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影圆，古松树下悟真玄，真玄只许自家见；鸾凤飞腾在高山，鹧鸪唱守在泥丸，泥丸得见婴儿面；啣月华不后不前，抱日眠无地无天，无天反做玲珑殿；愿上天赐阵清烟，抱黍米升上云端，那时方赴灵霄殿。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow full: beneath an ancient pine awaken to the true mystery—a true mystery permitted only for oneself to see. Phoenixes soar aloft upon the high mountain while partridges sing guarding the Muddy Pellet (niwan), and inside the Muddy Pellet one beholds the Infant’s face. Holding the moon’s luminescence in the mouth, neither behind nor ahead; embracing the sun in sleep, with neither Earth nor Heaven—that very “no-Heaven” becomes an exquisite palace. May Heaven bestow a wisp of clear mist, carrying the millet-grain pearl up beyond the clouds—only then do you attend the Hall of Numinous Empyrean.

§10Moon at Heaven’s Edge · No. 9
Original (Chinese)

天边月，月影无，无相无形难画图，画图难入修行谱；无名姓却听招呼，无方体谁认亲疏，亲疏妙用全不露；无念时何用工夫，无想处本体如如，如如才是娘生路；无色界有条真路，无罣碍有个仙都，那时才把彩云步。

Moon at heaven’s edge, moon-shadow gone: formless and signless, impossible to paint—a painted chart could never enter the register of true cultivation. Having neither surname nor given name, it still answers when called; having neither direction nor bodily shape, who can mark near or far—yet its wondrous working of near and far stays wholly concealed. When there is no thought, what need is there for effort? Where ideation ceases, the original substance is just-so (ruru)—and that very suchness is the original road from your mother’s womb. In the Formless Realm lies a true road; where all hindrance vanishes stands an immortal capital—only then do you step upon the multicolored clouds.

Key Terms

  • Moon emerging in the geng quarter: On the third day of the lunar month, the crescent moon appears in the western geng position, symbolizing the first arising of yang; see Two Poems in the Ancient Style in this book.
  • Overturned basin (fupen): An upside-down bowl, symbolizing the darkness of the human world shut off from sunlight.
  • Eight-jeweled nature of True Suchness: A metaphor for the priceless innate nature.
  • Dragon-subduing alms-bowl / tiger-taming bowl: Buddhist allusions to subduing dragons and taming tigers, symbolizing mastering Spirit and gathering Essence and Breath.
  • Yellow Dame: True Intention; see True Intention (zhenyi).
  • Yang fire / yin tally: Advancing the yang fire and withdrawing the yin tally—the two halves of fire phasing (huohou).
  • Sovereign Contemplation (Guanzizai): An epithet of Avalokiteśvara Bodhisattva, playing simultaneously on the literal sense of “contemplating in sovereign freedom.”
  • Just-so (ruru): A Buddhist term for the unmoving, unchanging original substance of True Suchness.

Putting It into Practice

  1. A human body is hard to obtain. “Only after cultivating across countless kalpas do you gain a human body; once lost, ten thousand kalpas will not easily recover it”—cherish the present moment.
  2. First sever the Three Corpses. Becoming an immortal is not hard; the difficulty lies in first subduing one’s own greed, anger, and delusion.
  3. The true mystery is for oneself alone to see. Realization in cultivation is deeply personal; there is no need to seek external validation.
  4. No thought, no ideation, original substance just-so. The final song is the culmination of the whole cycle: at the summit of practice, even “practice” itself is set down.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Drawn from the meaning of ‘the moon at heaven’s edge emerging in the geng quarter’”: small subtitle note in the original edition.
  2. “Clinging to wife and children is like a sheep keeping company with a pack of tigers”: means that attachment to household entanglements leaves one like a sheep wandering among tigers, in peril without realizing it.
  3. “Immortal Maiden Zhang herself once bowed to Patriarch Lü”: a female immortal of popular legend who studied under Lü Dongbin; her exact identity is unrecorded.
  4. “Cowherd and Weaver Girl laugh together… joins in the dim and obscure”: Cowherd and Weaver Girl symbolize the yin and yang within one’s own body (Spirit and Breath), and “joining in the dim and obscure” echoes the Daodejing’s “dim and obscure, yet within it there is a thing”—all taking place strictly within one’s own person.
  5. “Eighteen disciples sport with the Maṇi jewel”: preserved as in the original text; “eighteen disciples” probably refers to the Eighteen Arhats.
  6. “Partridges sing guarding the Muddy Pellet”: a metaphor for Spirit guarding the upper Cinnabar Field.
  7. “Formless Realm” (arūpadhātu): One of the Buddhist Three Realms (Desire, Form, and Formless), borrowed here as a metaphor for the state beyond shape and sign.

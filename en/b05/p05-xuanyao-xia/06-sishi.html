V · Essentials of the Mystery (Part Two)

Daoqing of the Four Seasons · Daoqing of the Five Watches (Sets One and Two)

Daoqing of the Four Seasons and two sets of Daoqing of the Five Watches — light a stick of incense, play a tune on the zither; practice without ceasing

About This Chapter

  • Daoqing of the Four Seasons: Four songs for spring, summer, autumn, and winter, each painting a seasonal scene and an immortal’s legend, and each landing on the refrain: “I only say: at leisure, light a stick of incense”—and play a tune on the zither, play a game of chess, read a scroll, or paint a plum tree. This is the lightest, most relaxed group in the volume, capturing the unhurried daily life of a Daoist practitioner.
  • Daoqing of the Five Watches: Part Two includes several sets of Five Watches songs arranged from the first watch to the fifth. Li Xiyue reminds readers: “The Five Watches must be read flexibly; they simply mean uninterrupted work”—not that one practices one exercise at the first watch and another at the second, but a metaphor for continuous cultivation throughout the entire process.
    • First set: First watch—herding the Green Ox alone and closing the Six Gates; second watch—calling the phoenix to nest and leading the tiger back to its den; third watch—as the first yang just sprouts, gathering a lingzhi blossom and sending it into the Yellow Court; fourth watch—irrigating the Three Fields with all thoughts gone; fifth watch—changing the tripod, dividing the Elixir, and nine years of returning to the Origin.
    • Second set: First watch—tethering the monkey-mind and horse-intention fast; second watch—“a realm of pure emptiness, unstained by dust”; third watch—the Living Zi Hour; fourth watch—“neither coveting wealth nor loving fame”; fifth watch—casting off rank, riches, and honors, “never again longing for wayside willows or wall-top blossoms.”

Note: In the original collection, four Daoqing Inscribed at the Green Ram Palace follow Daoqing of the Four Seasons. Because they are framed primarily in the idiom of the yin-yang dual-cultivation school, they are omitted in keeping with this site’s editorial policy.

Original Text and Translation

§1Daoqing of the Four Seasons · Spring
Original (Chinese)

春色可人，可人，桃杏花开满眼新；山园风物嫩，看来倒也无忧闷；仙喜的是洞府去游春，子晋先生吹玉笙，玉笙吹与知音听；俺则道闲来时，焚一炷香，抚一曲琴。

Spring colors are charming, so charming—peach and apricot blossoms fill the eyes with freshness. The mountain garden’s scenery is tender and green; looking upon it brings neither worry nor gloom. What immortals love is strolling through grotto-mansions in spring, Master Wang Zijin playing his jade reed-pipe—playing his jade pipe for a true confidant to hear. As for me, I only say: at leisure, light a stick of incense and play a tune on the zither.

§2Daoqing of the Four Seasons · Summer
Original (Chinese)

夏赏荷池，荷池，两个鸳鸯水面飞；摆列成双对，清风明月闲游戏；仙喜的是吕祖遇钟离，二翁留下长生地，终南山上乘云去；俺则道闲来时，焚一炷香，下一盘棋。

In summer admiring the lotus pond, the lotus pond—a pair of mandarin ducks skim across the water’s surface, arrayed side by side, sporting at leisure beneath cool breeze and bright moon. What immortals love is Patriarch Lü meeting Zhongli Quan: the two old masters left behind the ground of long life and rode away on clouds from Mount Zhongnan. As for me, I only say: at leisure, light a stick of incense and play a game of chess.

§3Daoqing of the Four Seasons · Autumn
Original (Chinese)

秋景云疏，云疏，远岫苍黄木叶枯；夜看银河布，牛郎到把织女度；仙喜的是卖卜隐成都，君平先生挈玉壶，日得百钱把酒沽；俺则道闲来时，焚一炷香，看一卷书。

In autumn the clouds grow sparse, so sparse—distant peaks turn pale yellow and tree leaves wither. By night watching the Milky Way spread across the sky, as the Cowherd ferries the Weaving Maiden over. What immortals love is telling fortunes in seclusion in Chengdu: Master Yan Junping carrying his jade flask, earning a hundred coins a day to buy his wine. As for me, I only say: at leisure, light a stick of incense and read a scroll.

§4Daoqing of the Four Seasons · Winter
Original (Chinese)

冬景雪飞，雪飞，万里关山似玉堆；和靖掩庐睡，天寒鹤守孤山内；仙喜的是湘子度文公，屡劝回头不肯回，蓝关路上才相会；俺则道闲来时，焚一炷香，画一树梅。

In winter the snow flies, flies—ten thousand miles of mountain passes piled like jade. Lin Hejing shuts his thatch hut to sleep while in the bitter cold his crane keeps watch on Solitary Hill. What immortals love is Han Xiangzi delivering Han Wengong: urged again and again to turn back, he refused until they met at last on the snowy road to Lan Pass. As for me, I only say: at leisure, light a stick of incense and paint a plum tree.

§5Daoqing of the Five Watches
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕题义：五更须活看，只是功夫不息之义，勿谓一更是一更功夫，二更是二更功夫也，余类推。

(Original Note) On the title: The “Five Watches” must be read flexibly—they simply signify uninterrupted effort. Do not assume that the first watch means one specific exercise for the first watch, or the second watch an exercise for the second watch; the rest follow the same principle.

§6Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set One) · First Watch
Original (Chinese)

一更初，独牧青牛，勿纵狂行，不放闲游；我这里换景移情，攀花折柳，密炼潜修；闭六门无为静守，擒五贼有法拘囚；匹配刚柔，耐得春秋；炁盛神全，采药何愁。

At the start of the first watch, herd the Green Ox alone: do not let it bolt wildly or wander idly about. Here I shift the scene and transform emotional attachment, “plucking blossoms and breaking willows,” refining in secret and cultivating quietly. Closing the Six Gates, guard in non-action and stillness; seizing the Five Thieves, hold them captive with proper method. Pair the firm and the yielding, endure the passing springs and autumns; when Breath is full and Spirit complete, why worry about gathering the Medicine?

§7Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set One) · Second Watch
Original (Chinese)

二更里，匹配调和，逐散诸阴，赶退群魔；俺只要招凤来巢，唤龟还窟，引虎归窝；看铅生须知谨守，逢月现认得真么；下手莫错，望远时过，赤水含珠，造化无那。

In the second watch, pair and harmonize, scattering all yin energies and driving back the host of demons. I only aim to summon the phoenix back to its nest, call the spirit tortoise back to its grotto, and lead the White Tiger home to its den. Seeing Lead arise, know how to guard it carefully; when the moon appears, do you recognize it truly? Do not mistime your move—once the full moon passes, the moment is gone. The Red Water holds the Pearl; creation’s marvel is boundless.

§8Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set One) · Third Watch
Original (Chinese)

三更中，一阳才萌，赤县门开，真炁方升；这时节微露铅华，初含玉蕊，半吐金精；锁心猿龙吟云应，拴意马虎啸风生；采颗芝英，送入黄庭，封固无虞，百日功灵。

In the middle of the third watch, the single yang has just begun to sprout; the gate of the Red County opens and True Breath begins to rise. At this moment the Lead sheen faintly shows, jade stamens first form, and Golden Essence half unfolds. Lock the monkey-mind and the dragon chants as clouds answer; tether the horse-intention and the tiger roars as winds arise. Gather a single lingzhi blossom and send it into the Yellow Court; seal it securely with no anxiety, and in a hundred days the work becomes spiritually efficacious.

§9Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set One) · Fourth Watch
Original (Chinese)

四更残，饮罢醍醐，乘槎张骞，笑煞麻姑；凭这点灌溉三田，融通百脉，润泽肌肤；周流遍牢关土釜，升降毕谨守如初；念虑皆无，声色尽除；温养胎仙，十月功夫。

As the fourth watch wanes, having drunk the ghee-nectar—like Zhang Qian riding his raft, laughing with Magu—rely on this single drop to irrigate the Three Cinnabar Fields, open through the hundred channels, and moisten skin and flesh. Once it has circulated everywhere, tightly close the Earthen Cauldron; once ascent and descent are finished, guard carefully just as at the start. Every thought and worry gone, sights and sounds all cleared away: gently nurture the Embryo Immortal—ten months of work.

§10Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set One) · Fifth Watch
Original (Chinese)

五更终，添汞抽铅，换鼎分丹，移丹上田；从今后阳长阴消，性成命全，体固身坚；静调神一周渐大，勤面壁九载还元；行满功圆，八百三千，与道合真，便是神仙。

At the end of the fifth watch, add Mercury and draw out Lead, change the tripod, divide the Elixir, and move the Elixir to the upper Field. From this time forth yang grows and yin diminishes, inner nature is perfected and life is whole, body firm and frame strong. Quietly regulating Spirit, each Celestial Circuit grows wider; diligently gazing at the wall, nine years restore the Origin. Conduct fulfilled and merit complete—eight hundred good deeds and three thousand merits—uniting in truth with the Way, one is a Spiritual Immortal.

§11Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set Two) · First Watch
Original (Chinese)

一更里，铅汞全，三尸六贼都游散；心猿意马牢拴定，铅鼎温温水不寒，诸魔不敢抬头看；安神息，任天然，龙自吟，虎自欢；这桩妙理行持惯，遍身水火配离坎；成仙成圣何嗟叹，做个长生也不难；曾记得，火龙直指得还丹，逍遥自在，自在神仙。

In the first watch, Lead and Mercury are whole, and the Three Corpses and Six Thieves scatter away. Monkey-mind and horse-intention are tethered fast; the Lead tripod is gently warm, the water never cold, and none of the demons dare lift their heads to look. Settle Spirit and breath, leaving them to spontaneity; the Dragon chants on its own, the Tiger delights on its own. Once practiced in this wondrous principle, water and fire throughout the whole body pair Li and Kan. Why sigh over becoming an immortal or sage? Attaining long life is not hard either. Still I remember Master Fire Dragon’s direct pointing by which I gained the Reverted Elixir—carefree and at ease, a carefree Spiritual Immortal!

§12Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set Two) · Second Watch
Original (Chinese)

二更里，丹诏来，乘龙跨凤青霄外；大还到手人人爱，方信金丹好药材；母见娇儿共一堆，安稳睡，且妆呆；清虚地，不染埃，从今灭却寃家债；做一个长生不老，养就了杏脸桃腮。

In the second watch, the cinnabar summons arrives—riding a dragon and striding a phoenix beyond the azure empyrean. Once the Great Reverted Elixir is in hand, everyone treasures it, believing at last what fine medicine the Golden Elixir is. Mother and cherished child huddle together, sleeping peacefully, playing the fool for a while. In a realm of pure emptiness, unstained by dust, from this day on all karmic debts are wiped clean—becoming one who lives long without aging, nourished to apricot-blossom face and peach-bloom cheeks.

§13Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set Two) · Third Watch
Original (Chinese)

三更里，活子时，仙家美景现华池；灵龟吸尽金乌髓，丹凤啣来玉兔脂；玄明酒，醉如痴，群阴尽，艳阳期；一枝春色金花丽，佳人有意心相许，郎君把玩两情怡；得遇了还元返本，寿与天齐。

In the third watch, at the Living Zi Hour, the immortals’ fair vista appears in the Flowery Pool. The spirit tortoise drinks dry the marrow of the Golden Crow; the cinnabar phoenix carries in its beak the fat of the Jade Hare. Intoxicated as if entranced on Mysterious-Bright wine: all yin is exhausted and radiant yang arrives. One spray of spring splendor, the Golden Flower radiant; the “fair maiden” is willing and pledges her heart, the “young lord” cherishes it and both hearts delight—having met the Way of returning to the Origin and restoring the root, one’s lifespan equals Heaven’s.

§14Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set Two) · Fourth Watch
Original (Chinese)

四更里，更漏深，铅生癸后阴阳分；正值一弦金水满，恰似莺花二月春；不贪财，不爱名；饮琼浆，听玉音；碧天连水清波净，虚白堂前拴意马，无影树下锁心猿；三回九转真人现，得遇了先天大道，寿比乾坤。

In the fourth watch, the water-clock runs deep; Lead is born after Gui-water, and yin and yang are clearly distinguished. Right at the first-quarter moon, Golden Water brims full, just like a second-month spring of orioles and blossoms. Neither coveting wealth nor loving fame; drinking jade nectar and listening to jade tones. Azure sky joins clear water in spotless ripples; before the Hall of Empty Whiteness tether the horse-intention; beneath the Shadowless Tree lock up the monkey-mind. Three turns and nine cycles, and the True Person appears—having met the Great Way of Prior Heaven, one’s lifespan rivals Qian and Kun.

§15Daoqing of the Five Watches (Set Two) · Fifth Watch
Original (Chinese)

五更里，采得他，功名富贵都抛下；一心盼望蓬莱景，十洲三岛便为家；免欲火，无牵挂，这长生，在自家；养育恩情休要差，逢人莫说艰难话，刀兵虎全然不怕；任你是艳色垂帘，再不恋路柳墙花。

In the fifth watch, once you have gathered “it,” cast aside all rank, riches, and honors. With a single heart looking toward the vistas of Penglai, take the Ten Continents and Three Isles as your home. Free from the fire of desire, without a single entanglement, this long life lies right within your own home. Never fall short in repaying those who nurtured you; when meeting others, never complain of hardship; blades, weapons, and tigers hold no terror at all. Even if dazzling beauty hangs behind beaded curtains, never again long for wayside willows or wall-top blossoms.

Key Terms

  • Green Ox (青牛): Laozi rode a green ox out through the pass; inner alchemists use it to symbolize the mind or Original Breath, so “herding the Green Ox alone” means guarding one’s mind and thoughts.
  • Six Gates (六门): The gates of the six sense roots—eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind.
  • Five Thieves (五贼): The cravings stirred up by the five senses.
  • Red Water holds the Pearl (赤水含珠): Metaphor for True Yang within Kan, alluding to the Zhuangzi story of the Yellow Emperor losing his Dark Pearl by the Red Water.
  • Lingzhi blossom (芝英): The flower of the spirit mushroom (lingzhi), symbolizing the Medicine.
  • Magu (麻姑): A legendary female immortal who thrice saw the eastern sea turn into mulberry fields.
  • Embryo Immortal (胎仙): Metaphor for the Holy Embryo.
  • Eight hundred and three thousand (八百三千): Eight hundred good deeds and three thousand merits, referring to virtuous conduct and merit.
  • Living Zi Hour (活子时): The moment when the first yang stirs, not tied to clock midnight; see Living Zi Hour.
  • Flowery Pool (华池): Inside the mouth, beneath the tongue, where saliva wells up.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Lighting incense at leisure is both practice and life. Daoqing of the Four Seasons does not depict grim asceticism, but serene ease: playing the zither, playing chess, reading, painting plum blossoms—an unscattered mind is itself the Way.
  2. Practice without ceasing. The Five Watches are not five separate exercises, but continuity at every moment.
  3. Close the Six Gates and seize the Five Thieves. Guarding the senses so they do not drag the mind away is how each set of Five Watches begins.
  4. Never again long for wayside willows or wall-top blossoms. The closing line of the second set is its true landing point: severing craving and attachment.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Master Wang Zijin playing his jade reed-pipe”: Wang Ziqiao (courtesy name Zijin), crown prince of King Ling of Zhou, who loved to imitate the phoenix’s call on the reed-pipe and later became an immortal.
  2. “Patriarch Lü meeting Zhongli Quan”: The famous legend of Lü Dongbin encountering Zhongli Quan and attaining the Way.
  3. “Telling fortunes in seclusion in Chengdu: Master Yan Junping”: Yan Zun (courtesy name Junping), a Han-dynasty recluse who told fortunes in Chengdu, closing his shop to read the Laozi each day as soon as he had earned a hundred coins to live on.
  4. “Lin Hejing shuts his thatch hut to sleep while in the bitter cold his crane keeps watch on Solitary Hill”: Lin Bu (posthumously Master Hejing), a Song-dynasty recluse on Solitary Hill in Hangzhou, famed for “taking plum blossoms as his wife and cranes as his sons.”
  5. “Han Xiangzi delivering Han Wengong… until they met at last on the snowy road to Lan Pass”: The legend of Han Xiangzi guiding his grand-uncle Han Yu (posthumously Wen) to the Way, alluding to Han Yu’s famous line “Snow blocks Lan Pass; my horse will not go on.”
  6. “Shift the scene and transform emotional attachment, ‘plucking blossoms and breaking willows’”: Understood here as transforming emotional consciousness and refining the mind amid bustling scenes, directly connected to “refining in secret and cultivating quietly” and “closing the Six Gates” that follow.
  7. “In the third watch… the ‘fair maiden’ is willing and pledges her heart, the ‘young lord’ cherishes it and both hearts delight”: “Fair maiden” and “young lord” symbolize Mercury and Lead (Li and Kan) within one’s own body, and “both hearts delight” symbolizes the harmonious union of Spirit and Breath. This is a yin-yang metaphor, not a literal romance—as confirmed by the closing line of the fifth song in the same set: “never again long for wayside willows or wall-top blossoms.”
  8. “In the fifth watch, once you have gathered ‘it’”: “It” (ta 他) refers to Prior Heaven Breath.
  9. “Mother and cherished child huddle together”: Metaphor for Original Spirit (mother) and the formed Elixir (child) abiding together.
  10. “Blades, weapons, and tigers hold no terror at all”: The original reads daobing hu quanran bupa (刀兵虎全然不怕), likely missing one character (such as lang 狼, “wolves,” after “tigers”).

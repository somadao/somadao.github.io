V · Essentials of the Mystery (Part Two)

Song of the Celestial Immortal

Song of the Celestial Immortal — “To study immortality, one must study Celestial Immortality”: a long lyric tracing the entire path from laying the foundation, refining the self, gathering the medicine, and gentle nurturing to nine years of wall-gazing and returning to emptiness

About This Chapter

Most of the works collected in Part Two of Essentials of the Mystery are daoqing (“Daoist sentiment songs”)—popular admonitory songs sung by Daoists to Yuan–Ming song-lyric melodies, rich in vernacular diction, strongly rhythmic, and meant to be chanted aloud. This volume is the most accessible and warmly human portion of Zhang Sanfeng’s writings.

This chapter presents Song of the Celestial Immortal (Tianxian yin). Its tune title comes from the Awakening to Reality (Wuzhen pian): “To study immortality, one must study Celestial Immortality; only the Golden Elixir is the truest and most direct.” Li Xiyue notes that vulgar editions call this tune Lyrics of the Southern Palace, which is actually inappropriate: the Southern Palace belongs to the talisman-and-register tradition, and those who reach it still undergo rebirth, whereas Zhang Sanfeng is teaching the Great Way of the Celestial Immortal, hence the title Song of the Celestial Immortal. The original collection contains five variants (five songs), with the last four differing slightly in prosody.

This chapter includes the second, third, and fifth variants. All three describe the same path:

  • Second variant: Begins with studying the Daodejing and Awakening to Reality—water within metal, heaven within earth; beheading the Three Corpses and seizing the Five Thieves; sealing the mouth (Dui), forgetting words, and gazing at the wall for nine years.
  • Third variant: First regulating the breathing evenly, keeping mind and intention vigilant, and working diligently at refining the self; “as though foolish, as though dull, sitting silently, forgetting words, and keeping to poverty.”
  • Fifth variant: Refusing to let the monkey-mind and horse-intention run loose, keeping far from sights and sounds, and firmly guarding true constancy; “Who knows how vast Qian and Kun are within stillness? I love how long the sun and moon last within the gourd.”

Note: Ascending the High Terrace at the opening of Part Two, as well as the first and fourth variants of Song of the Celestial Immortal, are framed primarily in the idiom of the yin-yang dual-cultivation school and are omitted in keeping with this site’s editorial policy.

Original Text and Translation

§1Song of the Celestial Immortal
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕俗作南宫词，原名天仙引，按：悟真云「学仙须是学天仙，惟有金丹最的端」原名取此，望人甚深也，后四调稍有不同，即以词家分体论，此篇作第一体。

(Original Note) Vulgar editions title this Lyrics of the Southern Palace, but its original title is Song of the Celestial Immortal. As Awakening to Reality says, “To study immortality, one must study Celestial Immortality; only the Golden Elixir is the truest and most direct.” The original name is taken from this line, expressing a profound hope for practitioners. The following four tunes differ slightly; following the lyricists’ convention of distinguishing prosodic variants, the opening piece serves as the first variant.

§2Song of the Celestial Immortal · Second Variant
Original (Chinese)

看归根复命篇，观养性修真卷；方知金里水，才识地中天；微妙玄玄，原来生杀隔一线，龙虎隐二弦；讲玄牝道德仙经，穷戊己悟真圣典；论庚方生金所在，说兑位产药川源，分明直指于赤县；如露如电，如雾如烟，只等的乌龟吐气，白虎喷涎；斩三尸境灭魔潜，擒五贼马卧猿眠；追神炁入归元海，运真气周流涌泉；采金精纳入丹田，自然贯穿，浑身百节周流遍；心似火，意如电，养育金丹汞渐添；闭兑忘言，九年面壁功无间；八极神游遍大千，七返婴儿自出现；六贼遁焉，五行数全，四海人知归阆苑。

Read the chapters on returning to the root and restoring life; study the scrolls on nourishing inner nature and cultivating reality. Only then do you know the water within metal; only then do you recognize heaven within earth. Subtle, wondrous, mystery upon mystery: life and death are separated by no more than a hair’s breadth, and Dragon and Tiger lie hidden in the two quarter-moons. Speaking of the Mysterious Female is the immortal scripture of the Daodejing; exhausting the meaning of Wu and Ji is the sacred classic Awakening to Reality. Discussing the Geng quarter—where metal is born—and speaking of the Dui position—the river-source that produces the Medicine—it points plainly and directly to the “Red County.” Like dew, like lightning, like mist, like smoke, simply wait for the Black Tortoise to exhale its breath and the White Tiger to spray its saliva. Behead the Three Corpses, and illusory realms vanish while demons slink away; seize the Five Thieves, and the horse lies down while the monkey sleeps. Draw Spirit and Breath into the Sea of Return to the Origin; move True Breath to circulate all the way to the Bubbling Spring; gather the Golden Essence and draw it into the Cinnabar Field. Naturally it pierces through and circulates throughout every single joint of the entire body. With mind like fire and intention like lightning, nurture the Golden Elixir as Mercury gradually increases. Seal the mouth (Dui) and forget speech; gaze at the wall for nine years with unbroken effort. Spirit roams the eight extremities across the great chiliocosm; after the Seven Returns, the Infant appears of itself. The Six Thieves flee, the number of the Five Phases is complete, and people across the Four Seas know you have returned to the immortal gardens of Langyuan.

§3Song of the Celestial Immortal · Third Variant
Original (Chinese)

先调呼吸匀，后把金木并；铅精藏牝户，汞液隐玄门；造化之根，杳冥中有信，恍惚无见闻；养药苗常令炉全，取黄芽不教鼎损；使元神不离方寸，觅金水灌入昆仑；仗慧剑追逼群魔，闭三宝持心意谨；擒五贼炼己功勤，断贪嗔袪除万虑，遇时景不染一尘。

First regulate the breathing until it is even, then unite Metal and Wood. The Essence of Lead is hidden in the Female Gate; the fluid of Mercury is concealed in the Mysterious Gate. The root of creation holds a sure token within the obscure depths, with neither sight nor sound in the dim and indistinct. Nurturing the seedling of the Medicine, keep the furnace always intact; taking the Yellow Sprout, never let the tripod be damaged. Keep Original Spirit from leaving the Square Inch; seek the Golden Water and pour it into Kunlun. Wielding the sword of wisdom, pursue and drive out the host of demons; sealing the Three Treasures, guard mind and intention with care. Seize the Five Thieves and labor diligently at refining the self; sever greed and anger, cast off ten thousand anxieties, and when encountering passing scenes, remain unstained by a single speck of dust.

§4
Original (Chinese)

我只待，曲江上月吐庚申，形山里踏雪寻春；采金精方离赤水，和真息周流一身，运己汞包裹阳精；炉火长温，时时刻刻加精进；如愚蠢，似痴钝，默坐忘言且守贫，保养全真；俺向那，深山面壁无人问，静室灰心惟俺亲；调神息，凭咱训，不言十月怀孕，只言九载辛勤，今日行足功圆，方证本。

I only wait for the moon over the Winding River to emerge at Geng-Shen, and to tread the snow in the Mountain of Form in search of spring. Gathering the Golden Essence as it first leaves the Red Water, blending it with True Breath to circulate through the whole body, I move my own Mercury to wrap around the yang Essence. Keep the furnace fire steadily warm, applying diligent effort moment by moment. As though foolish, as though dull, sit silently, forget speech, and keep to simple poverty, preserving Complete Reality. Off in the deep mountains I gaze at the wall with no one to ask after me; in a quiet chamber, with mind like cold ashes, only my true self is my companion. Regulating Spirit and breath by my own instruction—saying nothing of ten months of pregnancy, speaking only of nine years of arduous toil—today, with conduct fulfilled and work complete, I finally realize the original source.

§5Song of the Celestial Immortal · Fifth Variant
Original (Chinese)

不容意马狂，岂把心猿放，三尸无扰攘，六贼尽归降；魔境俱忘，保养的精神壮，调和的气血强；诸虑忘离却贪嗔，万缘尽不生妄想；遣神女侍炉铸剑，藉金水配对柔刚；凭匠手生擒，活捉如翻掌，开刀圭锁钥，解龙虎绳缰；饮延年仙酒，吃续命琼浆；丹入腹遍体生香，情归性换骨回阳。

Never allow the horse of intention to bolt wildly; how could one let the monkey of the mind run loose? The Three Corpses stir up no commotion, and the Six Thieves all surrender. Illusory demonic scenes are completely forgotten; Essence and Spirit are nurtured to vigor, Breath and blood harmonized to strength. All worries forgotten, greed and anger cast off; ten thousand entanglements exhausted, no deluded thoughts arise. Dispatch the “divine maiden” to attend the furnace and forge the sword, borrowing Golden Water to pair the yielding and the firm. By the master craftsman’s hand, seize them alive—catching them as easily as turning over one’s palm; unlock the key of the Elixir Measure (daogui) and undo the reins of Dragon and Tiger. Drink the immortal wine that lengthens years; partake of the jade nectar that extends life. As the Elixir enters the belly, fragrance fills the entire body; emotion returns to inner nature, transforming the bones and restoring pure yang.

§6
Original (Chinese)

休心景牢封土釜，灭意春深闭洞房；远声色固守真常，隄防损伤，如遇使纵休轻丧；十月后婴儿降，调理的通灵会神，独步仙乡；谁知静里乾坤大，我爱壶中日月长；任你人间是非谤，披云衣鹤氅，捧丹书玉章，紫府神仙齐庆赏。

Still the scenes of the mind and tightly seal the Earthen Cauldron; extinguish the spring-stirrings of intention and deeply close the bridal chamber. Keep far from sights and sounds, firmly guard true constancy, and guard against loss or injury—even if tempted toward indulgence, never lightly squander your treasure. After ten months the Infant is born, nurtured until it is spiritually penetrating and united with Spirit, striding alone through the realm of immortals. Who knows how vast Qian and Kun are within stillness? I love how long the sun and moon last within the gourd. Let the human world wag its tongue with praise and slander: draped in cloud-robes and a crane-feather cloak, holding the cinnabar writ and jade tallies, I am celebrated by all the immortals of the Purple Mansion.

§7
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕天仙引亦是祖师创调，命名之义极为雅正，盖必须加此乃是金丹大道，若作南宫调词则小矣，南宫乃符箓之派，与金丹不同，且到南宫者，尚有轮回，方得成道，太上度人经云「末学道浅，仙品未充，运应灭度，身经太阴，临过之时，同学至人，为其行香诵经十遍，以度尸形，如法，魂神迳上南宫，随其学功，计日而得更生，转轮不灭，方得神仙。」据此，则南宫之流乃夺舍投胎之辈也，天仙妙旨，岂可以南宫名哉？又，此词不可分段，兹分两段者，亦因可分之处而分之也，张邓刻本分为三段，一段名鹊桥仙，二段名玉女摇仙佩，三段作尾声，盖唱道情者为之也，且鹊桥、玉女乃是古曲名，祖师所作既不拘拘旧谱，则孟浪安名有何意味？今照汪本正之。

(Original Note) Song of the Celestial Immortal is likewise a tune created by the Patriarch himself, and the meaning of its title is exceedingly refined and orthodox: one must add the words “Celestial Immortal” for it to represent the Great Way of the Golden Elixir, whereas calling it Southern Palace Lyrics diminishes it. The Southern Palace belongs to the talisman-and-register lineage and differs from the Golden Elixir; moreover, those who reach the Southern Palace still must pass through rebirth before attaining the Way. The Scripture of Universal Salvation (Taishang duren jing) states: “Later students whose Dao is shallow and whose immortal rank is not yet filled, when their allotted span ends and their body passes through Great Yin—at the hour of passing, fellow perfected practitioners burn incense and recite the scripture ten times to deliver their mortal form according to the rite; their ethereal soul and spirit ascend straight to the Southern Palace, where according to their degree of attainment, after a reckoned number of days they obtain rebirth, turning through the wheel without perishing, and only then attain spiritual immortality.” By this evidence, the Southern Palace stream consists of those who undergo rebirth and re-embodiment; how could the sublime purport of the Celestial Immortal be named after the Southern Palace? Furthermore, this lyric ought not to be broken into stanzas; dividing it here into two sections merely follows natural pauses. The Zhang and Deng woodblock editions split it into three parts—naming the first Immortal at the Magpie Bridge, the second Jade Maiden Shaking Her Immortal Pendants, and the third Coda—probably the doing of daoqing singers. Since Magpie Bridge and Jade Maiden are ancient tune titles, and the Patriarch’s compositions did not cling rigidly to old metrical templates, what sense is there in recklessly attaching such names? It is hereby corrected according to the Wang edition.

Key Terms

  • Daoqing (道情): Admonitory Daoist songs chanted to popular melodies; most pieces in Part Two belong to this genre.
  • Celestial Immortal (天仙): Inner alchemists divide immortals into five ranks—ghost, human, earthly, spiritual, and celestial—with the Celestial Immortal being the highest.
  • Southern Palace (南宫): In Daoist tradition, practitioners of talismans and scripture recitation ascend in soul and spirit to the Southern Palace after death to await rebirth after a fixed term. Li Xiyue cites this to distinguish it from the Golden Elixir Way of the Celestial Immortal.
  • Water within metal, heaven within earth (金里水、地中天): Metaphors for the true yang hidden within Kan and within yin.
  • Red County (赤县): Literally the sacred land of China; borrowed by inner alchemists to denote the place within the body where the Medicine is generated.
  • Three Corpses and Five Thieves (三尸、五贼): The Three Corpses are three parasitic entities traditionally said to dwell in the body and stir up desires; the Five Thieves denote the cravings aroused by the five senses.
  • Sealing Dui (闭兑): The trigram Dui corresponds to the mouth; closing the mouth and refraining from speech.
  • Winding River and Mountain of Form (曲江、形山): The Winding River refers to the abdomen; the Mountain of Form refers to the body.
  • Geng-Shen (庚申): The new moon emerging in the Geng quarter, symbolizing the first stirring of one yang.
  • Earthen Cauldron and bridal chamber (土釜、洞房): Both refer to the Central Palace and the Yellow Court.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Sit silently, forget speech, and keep to simplicity. Speaking little, staying quiet, and wanting little—this is a way of life that recurs throughout the daoqing songs.
  2. Keep far from sights and sounds; firmly guard true constancy. The fifth variant states refining the self very plainly: do not indulge, do not cling, and guard against loss.
  3. Vast is the cosmos within stillness. Once you grow truly quiet, the inner world opens up and becomes far wider than before.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Awakening to Reality says, ‘To study immortality, one must study Celestial Immortality; only the Golden Elixir is the truest and most direct’”: From the heptasyllabic quatrains of Awakening to Reality (Wuzhen pian).
  2. “Read the chapters on returning to the root and restoring life; study the scrolls on nourishing inner nature and cultivating reality”: General references to inner-alchemical classics rather than specific book titles.
  3. “Simply wait for the Black Tortoise to exhale its breath and the White Tiger to spray its saliva”: Metaphor for the stirring of Prior Heaven Breath.
  4. “Dispatch the ‘divine maiden’ to attend the furnace and forge the sword”: “Divine maiden” symbolizes the true yin within the body (the yin inside Li); paired with the following line “borrowing Golden Water to pair the yielding and the firm,” both are metaphors for yin and yang within one’s own body.
  5. “Extinguish the spring-stirrings of intention and deeply close the bridal chamber”: In inner alchemy, “bridal chamber” (dongfang) refers to the Central Palace and Yellow Court, not its ordinary worldly sense.
  6. “Even if tempted toward indulgence, never lightly squander your treasure”: The original reads ru yu shi zong xiu qing sang (如遇使纵休轻丧), meaning that even when encountering temptations toward indulgence, one must never lightly lose one’s true vitality.
  7. “Scripture of Universal Salvation”: The Yuanshi wuliang duren shangpin miaojing; the quotation is abridged.
  8. “Zhang and Deng woodblock editions” and “Wang edition”: Earlier printed editions consulted by Li Xiyue.
  9. Works in this volume omitted in accordance with this site’s editorial policy (as framed primarily in the yin-yang dual-cultivation idiom): Ascending the High Terrace; the first and fourth variants of Song of the Celestial Immortal; the second and third pieces of A Spray of Blossoms; the first piece of Fine Golden Flower; A Formula of Heaven’s Secret; the second stanza of Clear Chants of the Grotto-Heaven; the two Lyrics of the Hall of Spring Beauty; and the four Daoqing Inscribed at the Green Ram Palace.

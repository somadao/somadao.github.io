V · Essentials of the Mystery (Part Two)

Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Seven Daoist Songs on Leaving the Household

Nine Watches Daoist Songs and Seven Daoist Songs on Leaving the Household — the gate that gave me birth is the road that brings my death; seeing through the clouds and mud of worldly ways

About This Chapter

  • Nine Watches Daoist Songs: The night has no “nine watches”; Li Xiyue notes that geng here is read in the fourth tone (gèng), meaning “cultivating in successive turns to complete the number of nine revolutions.” It opens by borrowing four lines from Zhongli Quan: “The gate that gave me birth is the road that brings my death—how many are awake, how many truly see? In the night an iron-willed man reflects alone: long life without death is forged by one’s own hand.” The nine lyrics trace the arc of a human life: departing Ancient Spirit Mountain to descend into the mortal world, growing up to follow the Six Thieves and ruin one’s own true Master; in the third watch abstaining from sexual misconduct; in the fourth watch turning back in middle age to seek an enlightened master; from the fifth watch onward cutting through worldly attachments and locking up the monkey of the mind, culminating in guarding True Lead in the eighth watch and assiduously refining the Three Families in the ninth. Of all the cycles, this one most closely echoes Buddhist exhortations urging people to turn back.
  • Seven Daoist Songs on Leaving the Household: Each lyric begins with “Sighing for leaving the household, how [wondrous / secluded / profound…] it truly is.” Li Xiyue remarks that Daoists often copied and adapted these seven lyrics as their own, while others suspected they were written by Luo Hongxian—neither is right, since the final song’s line “fifty-two lines of words within the mystery” clearly refers to the Song of Daoist Sentiment (fifty-two lines) in this very collection, proving they were indeed transmitted by Zhang Sanfeng. The seven songs portray the resolve of the renunciant: seeing through hollow fame, refusing to cling to spouse or children, steadying the mind in walking, standing, sitting, and lying down, needing no more than a gourdful of rice, and offering kindness wherever one goes.

Original Text and Translation

§1Nine Watches Daoist Songs
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕时无九更，而此以九更名者，更，更音去声，言更番修炼，以成九转之数也。

(Original Note) Nighttime hours have no “nine watches”; the reason this piece is titled Nine Gèng is that geng is read in the departing (fourth) tone, meaning to cultivate in successive turns so as to fulfill the number of nine revolutions.

§2
Original (Chinese)

生我之门死我路，几个惺惺几个悟；夜来铁汉自思量，长生不死由人做。

The gate that gave me birth is the very road that brings my death—how many are clear-eyed, how many awaken? By night an iron-willed man reflects within himself: long life without death is made by human effort.

§3
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕此乃钟祖句，亦祖师引来以作九更起首者。

(Original Note) These are lines by Patriarch Zhongli, likewise cited by Patriarch Zhang to open the Nine Watches.

§4Nine Watches Daoist Songs · First Watch
Original (Chinese)

一更里，回心向善，为生死其实艰难；自从离了古灵山，混沌初分下世间；西方有本，丢下根原；来在东土，性命落凡；失迷了，老母当初未生前。

In the first turn, turn the heart back toward goodness—settling birth and death is hard indeed. Ever since departing Ancient Spirit Mountain, descending into the mortal world when primordial chaos first divided, you had your true root in the West yet cast away your origin; arriving in the Eastern Land, inner nature (xing) and life (ming) fell into the mundane, losing sight of the primordial Mother before you were ever born.

§5Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Second Watch
Original (Chinese)

二更里，成人长大，城市中立下根芽；如饥知渴知上下，不知生死何处发；二房居住，不识真假；不修出路，只顾眼下；顺六贼，坏了自己主人家。

In the second turn, growing to adulthood, you plant your roots and shoots amid the cities. Knowing hunger and thirst, knowing high and low, you have no idea whence birth and death arise. Dwelling in the “second chamber,” unable to tell true from false, you cultivate no way out and care only for the moment before your eyes—following the Six Thieves until you ruin your own Master of the house.

§6Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Third Watch
Original (Chinese)

三更里，邪淫休念，牢锁定意马心猿；花街柳巷少贪顽，别人妻女成婚眷；尽是破戒，尽是魔缠；二鬼来勾，不敢倒看，入地府，那时难见如来面。

In the third turn, put away all thoughts of sexual misconduct and lock fast the horse of thought and monkey of the mind. Do not dally in brothel lanes and willow alleys, or covet other men’s wives and daughters as partners—all of this breaks the precepts and binds you to demons. When the two underworld constables come to summon your soul, you will not even dare look back; once cast into the netherworld, how hard it will be then to behold the face of the Tathāgata.

§7Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Fourth Watch
Original (Chinese)

四更里，贤愚难辨，惺悟的识破机关；看见世人不久远，百岁光阴急如箭；及早回头，正是中年；拜求明师，口诀相传，指与你，从前本来娘生面。

In the fourth turn, wise and foolish are hard to tell apart, yet the awakened see right through the snare. Seeing that no mortal lasts for long and a hundred years fly swiftly as an arrow, turning back early—right in middle age—is just in time. Bow and seek an enlightened master to pass down the oral formula, pointing out to you the original face you bore from your mother’s womb.

§8Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Fifth Watch
Original (Chinese)

五更里，皈依如来，为生死斩断恩爱；观见世事好伤怀，恐怕惹下轮回债；心猿锁定，休教损坏；提出正念，无边无界，趁香风，及时正好早归来。

In the fifth turn, take refuge in the Tathāgata and sever worldly attachments for the sake of birth and death. Looking upon worldly affairs fills the heart with sorrow, fearing to incur fresh debts of rebirth. Lock up the monkey of the mind and never let it suffer harm; raise right mindfulness, boundless and without limit, and riding the fragrant breeze return home while there is still time.

§9Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Sixth Watch
Original (Chinese)

六更里，细认本宗，将猿猴锁在房中；若来若去莫放松，昼夜挂剑守深宫；四门上锁，压定妖风；看破红尘，尽是浮空，主人翁，时时常把真经诵。

In the sixth turn, carefully recognize the true lineage and lock the monkey inside the chamber. Whether coming or going, never slacken; hang the sword day and night to guard the inner palace. Bolt all four gates to quell every ghostly wind; seeing right through the red dust as nothing but floating emptiness, let the true Master continually recite the True Scripture at every hour.

§10Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Seventh Watch
Original (Chinese)

七更里，要脱尘埃，多亏了恩师携带；受持三规并五戒，发下洪誓永不开；世事皆小，生死事大；斩断轮回，金刚不坏，滚浪里，现出一枝白莲来。

In the seventh turn, escaping the world’s dust owes everything to a gracious master’s guiding hand. Uphold the Three Rules and Five Precepts, swearing a solemn vow never to break them. Worldly affairs are all small; the matter of birth and death is great. Severing the cycle of rebirth, indestructible as diamond, right amid the rolling waves a single stalk of white lotus emerges.

§11Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Eighth Watch
Original (Chinese)

八更里，把守真铅，古弥陀倒坐玄关；呼吸元阳上下转，前后三三一担担；黄河倒卷，泥牛耕田；鹊巢灌顶，地涌金莲，须弥顶，透出一道灵光现。

In the eighth turn, guard True Lead as the Ancient Amitābha sits inverted in the Mysterious Pass. Breathing Original Yang up and down in revolution, three front and three back are shouldered on a single carrying pole. The Yellow River rolls backward and the clay ox plows the field; the magpie’s nest anoints the crown, golden lotuses spring from the earth, and from the summit of Mount Sumeru a shaft of numinous light shines forth.

§12Nine Watches Daoist Songs · Ninth Watch
Original (Chinese)

九更里，苦炼三家，红炉中火候不差；无影树下现金花，性命二字两头发；三车搬运，赵州斟茶；去到西方，参拜佛家，小婴儿，见娘呵呵喜笑煞。

In the ninth turn, diligently refine the Three Families with unerring fire phasing (huohou) in the glowing furnace. Beneath the shadowless tree the Golden Flower appears, and the two words “inner nature” (xing) and “life” (ming) bloom from both ends. The Three Carts transport the load while Zhaozhou pours the tea; arriving in the West to pay homage to the Buddha’s household, the little Infant sees its mother and laughs aloud with sheer delight.

§13Seven Daoist Songs on Leaving the Household
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕出家七首，羽流多套袭以为己作，又有疑为罗洪先所作者，非也，末篇所云「五十二句玄中理，明明白白说与君。」盖指道情歌也，据此观之，的系丰师所传无疑，继阅汪本，更为可证。

(Original Note) These seven songs on leaving the household have often been copied and adapted by Daoists as their own compositions, and some have suspected them to be by Luo Hongxian—this is incorrect. The closing song’s line “fifty-two lines of principle within the mystery, spoken plainly and clearly to you” refers to the Song of Daoist Sentiment. Judging from this, they are unmistakably transmitted by Master Sanfeng; later inspecting the Wang edition confirmed this even more firmly.

§14Leaving the Household · No. 1
Original (Chinese)

叹出家，到也奇，看破了世路云泥；一心不染红尘事，任凭他浮名美利，任凭他爱子娇妻；劳形不如归山去，俺怎肯终日奔驰，俺怎肯终日寻思；修行当发冲天志，做一个慷慨男子，打破了生死机关，无烦无恼无忧虑。

(No. 1) Sighing for leaving the household—how remarkable it is, having seen through the clouds-and-mud ups and downs of worldly roads! With a single heart unstained by the affairs of red dust, let others chase hollow fame and handsome profit, let them cling to cherished children and tender wives. Rather than wearing out this frame, better to return to the mountains—how could I consent to scurry about all day, how could I consent to scheme and brood all day? In cultivation one must raise a sky-piercing resolve and be a magnanimous, stout-hearted person, smashing right through the mechanism of birth and death, free of vexation, free of distress, free of every care.

§15Leaving the Household · No. 2
Original (Chinese)

叹出家，到也幽，断却了妄念忧愁；人生那得无尘垢，俺怎肯图利贪求，俺怎肯空自罢休；断然不落无常手，发洪誓去把师求，发洪誓时把真修；自然有日功成就，任凭我跨鹤乘舟，任凭我散步优游，真玄道妙谁参透。

(No. 2) Sighing for leaving the household—how serene and secluded it is, having severed deluded thoughts and sorrow! How could mortal life ever be free of dust and grime? Yet how could I consent to scheme for profit and greed, or consent to give up empty-handed? Resolutely refusing to fall into the grip of impermanence, I swear a great vow to go seek a master, and having sworn the vow I truly cultivate. Naturally a day will come when the work reaches fulfillment—then I may freely mount a crane or sail a skiff, freely stroll at leisure; who can fathom the marvel of the truly mysterious Way?

§16Leaving the Household · No. 3
Original (Chinese)

叹出家，到也深，学些儿借假修真；行住坐卧把心定，爱的是养气提神，喜的是木降金升；灵光现出圆如镜，顷刻间窍窍通灵；黄河水运转昆仑，自然认得真玄牝。

(No. 3) Sighing for leaving the household—how profound it is, learning a little of borrowing the false to cultivate the true! Whether walking, standing, sitting, or lying down, keep the mind steady; what I love is nourishing Breath and raising Spirit, what delights me is Wood descending and Metal ascending. The numinous light appears as round as a mirror, and in an instant every aperture opens with clarity; the Yellow River’s waters revolve up to Kunlun, and naturally you recognize the true Mysterious Female.

§17Leaving the Household · No. 4
Original (Chinese)

叹出家，到也玄，看破了打坐参禅；主人现是何物件，玄中理默默无言，动静处添汞抽铅；如痴如醉神不倦，进火时文武相煎，温养时子后午前，水火既济同烹炼。

(No. 4) Sighing for leaving the household—how mysterious it is, having seen all the way through quiet sitting and Chan inquiry! When the true Master appears, what sort of “thing” is it? The principle within the mystery is silent and beyond words; amid movement and stillness, add Mercury and draw Lead. As if entranced, as if tipsy, Spirit never tires; when advancing the fire, civil and martial heats simmer in turn; during warm nourishment between zi and wu, water and fire meet in Jiji (Already Across) and cook together.

§18Leaving the Household · No. 5
Original (Chinese)

叹出家，倒也精，准备着猛将强兵；隄防六贼来搬运，任凭他驾雾腾云，任凭他惯战争能；全凭妙用将他胜，举慧眼万骑齐奔，提慧剑斩断魔精；三尸束手魔王顺，现出了赤胆忠心；自然见富国安民，一战功成皆宁静。

(No. 5) Sighing for leaving the household—how keen and refined it is, keeping fierce generals and sturdy troops at the ready! Guard against the Six Thieves coming to pilfer and stir trouble; let them ride the mist and mount the clouds, let them boast of battle-hardened prowess—rely entirely on wondrous function to defeat them. Raise the eye of wisdom and ten thousand horsemen charge as one; lift the sword of wisdom and sever the demonic spirits. The Three Corpses surrender bound, the Demon King submits, and a loyal, wholehearted devotion shines forth; naturally the realm grows prosperous and its people dwell in peace—one battle won, and all is still.

§19Leaving the Household · No. 6
Original (Chinese)

叹出家，倒也高，学了些散淡逍遥；顺逆颠倒通玄妙，一瓢饭能吃多少，三杯酒面像仙桃；花街柳巷呵呵笑，小葫芦常挂在腰，万灵丹带上几包；到处与人行方便，遇缘时美酒佳肴，淡薄时饮水箪瓢；富贵穷通由天造，任凭他身挂紫袍，任凭他骏马金貂；转眼难免无常到，三寸气顷刻缥缈，一家人哭哭叫叫；那管你子贤孙孝，算将来修道为高，延年寿病减灾消；无忧无虑无烦恼，等时来到步云霄，会八仙去上仙桥，那时方显玄中妙。

(No. 6) Sighing for leaving the household—how lofty it is, having learned a measure of unhurried, carefree wandering! Forward, reverse, and inverted, all penetrate the wondrous mystery; how much can one eat beyond a single gourdful of rice? After three cups of wine, one’s cheeks glow like immortal peaches. Passing through flower streets and willow lanes with an easy laugh, a little gourd always hanging at the waist and a few packets of myriad-cure elixir tucked along—everywhere doing kindnesses for others: when circumstance provides, enjoying fine wine and savory dishes; in lean times, drinking plain water from a bamboo basket and gourd. Wealth and poverty, rise and fall are shaped by Heaven; let others drape themselves in purple robes of office or boast of fine steeds and golden sable—in the blink of an eye none escapes impermanence when three inches of breath vanish in an instant and the whole family wails and cries, caring nothing for how worthy your sons or filial your grandsons may be! Reckon it all up and cultivating the Way stands highest: prolonging years and lifespan, diminishing illness and dissolving calamity, free of anxiety, worry, and vexation. When the hour arrives, step up into the clouds and meet the Eight Immortals on the Immortal Bridge—only then does the wonder within the mystery shine in full.

§20Leaving the Household · No. 7
Original (Chinese)

叹出家，到也真，洗心源必要清净；玄中理方可见明，修真养性谁来问；俺也曾，过了些崎山峻岭，走了些州县府城，大都尘市和光混；有一等，不犯腥不犯淫；有一等，宽怀忍气财分明；西南国上，把朋来进；昔日里醉似昏昏，醒眼看四海苍生；红尘滚滚金花嫩，天边月谁人认真；世上事那件分明，人人抱着修仙兴；五十二句玄中语，明明白白说与君；拜明师要访高人，殷勤了，才得长生赠。

(No. 7) Sighing for leaving the household—how genuine it is: to wash the fountainhead of the mind requires pure stillness! Only then can the principle within the mystery grow clear—yet who comes asking how to cultivate the true and nourish inner nature? I too have crossed steep crags and lofty ridges, walked through prefectures, counties, and great walled towns, mingling my light with the dust of bustling markets. Some people abstain strictly from rank foods and lust; others keep a generous heart, curb anger, and stay scrupulously clear in money matters. In the “southwest country,” bring the “companion” in. Once I was befuddled as if drunk; now with awakened eyes I look out upon all beings across the four seas—amid the surging red dust the Golden Flower is fresh and tender, yet who truly recognizes the moon at heaven’s edge? Which worldly affair is ever truly clear, even while everyone harbors a fancy for cultivating immortality? Fifty-two lines of words within the mystery, spoken plainly and clearly to you: bow to an enlightened master and seek out a lofty adept—only through earnest devotion will you receive the gift of long life.

Key Terms

  • Gèng (turn / alternation): Alternating in turns; Li Xiyue notes that “Nine Gèng” signifies cultivating in successive turns through the nine revolutions.
  • Ancient Spirit Mountain / Primordial Mother: Expressions common in late imperial popular religious texts, referring to the human being’s original source.
  • Second chamber (erfang): A metaphor for the Posterior Heaven body and mind.
  • Two constables (ergui): Underworld spirits sent to summon the soul at death.
  • Three Rules and Five Precepts: A general reference to Daoist and Buddhist ethical precepts.
  • Three and three (sansan): A numerological inner-alchemy term (here pointing to the three passes along the front and back channels).
  • Three Carts: Sheep-cart, deer-cart, and ox-cart, used in inner alchemy as metaphors for the circulation of the River Chariot.
  • Zhaozhou pours the tea: Alludes to the famous Chan gong’an “Zhaozhou’s ‘Go have a cup of tea.’”
  • Mysterious Female (xuanpin): See Mysterious Opening / Mysterious Pass (xuanqiao / xuanguan).
  • Southwest country / gaining a companion: From the Book of Changes, hexagram Kun ☷ (“in the southwest one gains companions”), used in inner alchemy for the moment when the first yang arises and the tender sprout of the Medicine appears; see Two Poems in the Ancient Style in this book.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Long life without death is made by human effort. Whether one cultivates or not rests entirely with oneself.
  2. Abstain from sexual misconduct. The third song of Nine Watches states it most plainly: brothel lanes and coveting others’ spouses “all break the precepts and bind you to demons.”
  3. Turning back in middle age is right on time. There is no need to regret starting late.
  4. How much can one eat beyond a gourdful of rice? Song No. 6 on leaving the household: material needs are actually very few; offering kindness to others wherever you go is itself cultivation.
  5. Borrow the false to cultivate the true. Use this body—which will age and die—to cultivate the unborn, undying true nature.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Lines by Patriarch Zhongli”: Zhongli Quan, one of the Eight Immortals and traditionally revered as the teacher of Lü Dongbin.
  2. “Ever since departing Ancient Spirit Mountain… losing sight of the primordial Mother before you were ever born”: the diction of this song resembles Ming–Qing popular religious precious scrolls (baojuan, with imagery such as the Unborn Venerable Mother); preserved verbatim.
  3. “Covet other men’s wives and daughters as partners”: means lusting after others’ spouses, a grave breach of precept.
  4. “Ancient Amitābha sits inverted in the Mysterious Pass”: a metaphor for the Original Spirit (yuanshen) resting securely within the Mysterious Pass.
  5. “Three front and three back are shouldered on a single carrying pole”: as in the original text, symbolizing the three passes along the front Conception Vessel and back Governing Vessel.
  6. “Suspected them to be by Luo Hongxian”: Luo Hongxian (1504–1564), a Ming scholar and statesman styled Nian’an.
  7. “Seen all the way through quiet sitting and Chan inquiry”: means seeing through to the genuine essence of quiet sitting and Chan, not rejecting seated practice.
  8. “Passing through flower streets and willow lanes with an easy laugh”: carries the same meaning as A Spray of Flowers earlier in this volume (“amid the blossoms, tapping bamboo and strumming the zither with a mind like still water”)—moving through worldly splendor with an unshaken heart.
  9. “In the ‘southwest country,’ bring the ‘companion’ in”: interpreted here via “gaining companions in the southwest,” signifying gathering the Medicine when the first yang arises; see Two Poems in the Ancient Style in this book (“the moon appears in the geng quarter; in the southwest one gains companions”).
  10. “Fifty-two lines of words within the mystery”: refers to the Song of Daoist Sentiment in Part One of Volume Four, whose text itself says “fifty-two lines for you to seek.”

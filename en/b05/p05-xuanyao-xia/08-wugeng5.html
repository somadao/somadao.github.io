V · Essentials of the Mystery (Part Two)

Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · Golden Elixir Flowers

Set V of the Five Watches Daoist Songs (twelve songs) — joyfully chanting the Golden Elixir flowers, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall

About This Chapter

This set of Five Watches Daoist Songs consists of twelve lyrics—two for each of the five watches, with the final two depicting daybreak. In form it closely resembles folk Buddhist chants: each song counts “Golden Elixir flower, tally one,” “tally two,” all the way to twelve, and closes with the refrain: “Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant the name of a Buddha: Namo [such-and-such] Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall.”

It begins with a seven-character quatrain as a prelude: “Reversing the Yellow River, a single channel opens; nourished gentle and pliant as a newborn babe. Should people of the world ask for the road to long life, I point smiling eastward across the emerald sea to Penglai.”

Li Xiyue’s commentary highlights the meaning of “the blossom opens and the leaves fall”: it is a metaphor for “yang arising and yin being exhausted”—when an ordinary flower blooms, leaves sprout alongside it, whereas when this Golden Elixir flower blooms, the leaves fall away (yin is exhausted and yang stands pure).

In content, this cycle weaves Buddhist and Daoist idioms into one: “Refine the Golden Elixir right at the bustling crossroads—no need for water, fire, or charcoal, only awaken to quiet sitting and Chan inquiry”; “lock the monkey of the mind within the Square Inch”; “wielding a shadowless sword, drive the Six Thieves to the Demon King’s hall”; and “this long life belongs to every single person.”

Original Text and Translation

§1Five Watches Daoist Songs (V)
Original (Chinese)

倒卷黄河一脉通，养来柔弱似婴童；世人若问长生路，笑指蓬莱碧海东。

Reversing the Yellow River, a single vessel opens through; nourished until gentle and pliant like an infant child. If people of the world ask for the path to long life, I smile and point toward Penglai across the emerald eastern sea.

§2Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 1
Original (Chinese)

一更里，修行要仔细，休教意马走东西；走东西，猿猴锁在方寸地，这青牛老子能骑，金丹花儿勾一，宝剑插在炉里；龙虎交，会坎离，水火颠倒成既济；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无混沌世界佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the first watch, cultivate with care and do not let the horse of thought wander east and west. Wander east and west—lock the monkey fast within the Square Inch (fangcun), for this green ox Laozi himself can ride. Golden Elixir flower, tally one: plant the precious sword straight inside the furnace; Dragon and Tiger unite, Kan and Li converge, and water and fire invert to form Jiji (Already Across). Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Buddha of the Primordial Chaos World, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§3
Original (Chinese)

〔原评〕花开叶落，比阳生阴尽之时；若是凡花，则花开叶亦生矣，余倣此。

(Original Commentary) “The blossom opens and the leaves fall” is a metaphor for the moment when yang arises and yin is exhausted; were it an ordinary flower, leaves would grow along with the blossom. The same applies to all the remaining songs.

§4Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 2
Original (Chinese)

一更里，修行提正念，十字街前炼金丹；炼金丹，不用水火不用炭，只要悟打坐参禅；金丹花儿勾二，黄婆引去婴儿；上泥丸，透玄关，婴儿姹女两团圆；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无释迦牟尼佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the first watch, cultivate by raising right mindfulness and refining the Golden Elixir right at the crossroads. Refining the Golden Elixir requires neither water, fire, nor charcoal—only awakening to quiet sitting and Chan inquiry. Golden Elixir flower, tally two: the Yellow Dame leads the Infant away; rising to the Muddy Pellet (niwan) and piercing the Mysterious Pass, the Infant and the Fair Maiden are reunited as one. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Śākyamuni Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§5Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 3
Original (Chinese)

二更里，修行要团圆，姹女婴儿在两边；在两边，黄公黄婆为媒眷，将二家结就姻缘；金丹花儿勾三，说起生死不难；说不难，却又难，不在身边在那边；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无西方如来佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the second watch, cultivate toward reunion while the Fair Maiden and the Infant stand on opposite sides. On opposite sides—the Yellow Lord and Yellow Dame serve as matchmakers, joining the two families in union. Golden Elixir flower, tally three: to speak of transcending birth and death is not hard; yet though we say it is not hard, it is hard after all—if it is not right beside you, then where else could it be? Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Tathāgata of the West, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§6Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 4
Original (Chinese)

二更里，修行要心专，手提一根无影剑；无影剑，六贼赶至魔王殿，将三尸斩首目前；金丹花儿勾四，我今遇着明师；与咱指，一条路，时时刻刻用功夫；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无大肚弥勒佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the second watch, cultivate with single-minded focus, gripping a shadowless sword in hand. Shadowless sword—drive the Six Thieves all the way to the Demon King’s hall, and behead the Three Corpses right before your eyes. Golden Elixir flower, tally four: today I have met an enlightened master; he points out a single path for me, to apply the work at every single moment. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Big-Bellied Maitreya Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§7Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 5
Original (Chinese)

三更里，修行要防危，休将六贼搬弄你；搬弄你，聚气凝神总不移，烧纸钱送将出去；金丹花儿勾五，我今得了功夫；采先天，补后天，滚出云门天外天；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无接引准提佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the third watch, cultivate while guarding against peril and never let the Six Thieves toy with you. Toy with you—gather Breath and concentrate Spirit without ever wavering, and burn spirit-money to send them packing. Golden Elixir flower, tally five: today I have attained the true work; gathering Prior Heaven to replenish Posterior Heaven, rolling right out of Cloud Gate into the heaven beyond Heaven. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Guiding Cundī Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§8Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 6
Original (Chinese)

三更里，修行莫漏机，黄河倒卷上天梯；上天梯，玄关站定青牛蹄，颠倒颠取坎填离；金丹花儿勾六，狮子炼成火猴；我今想，去云游，翻过甲子到瀛洲；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无十八罗汉佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the third watch, cultivate without leaking the heavenly mechanism as the Yellow River reverses and climbs the ladder to Heaven. Climbs the ladder to Heaven—plant the green ox’s hooves firmly at the Mysterious Pass, reversing the reversal to draw from Kan and fill Li. Golden Elixir flower, tally six: the lion is refined into the fiery monkey; now I long to wander like a cloud, passing beyond the sixty-year cycle to reach the Isle of Yingzhou. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Eighteen Arhats Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§9Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 7
Original (Chinese)

四更里，修行要用心，双树林中点慧灯；点慧灯，照见世尊极乐国，他去门前等一等；金丹花儿勾七，空中有人提携；骑青牛，过玄关，炼颗金丹圆又圆；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无燃灯古老佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the fourth watch, cultivate with attentive heart and light the lamp of wisdom in the Twin Śāla Grove. Light the lamp of wisdom—it illuminates the World-Honored One’s Land of Ultimate Bliss, where he waits a while before the gate. Golden Elixir flower, tally seven: in the empty sky someone reaches down to lift you up; riding the green ox through the Mysterious Pass, you refine a Golden Elixir round upon round. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Ancient Dīpaṃkara Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§10Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 8
Original (Chinese)

四更里，修行要用功，须看西南起巽风；起巽风，水火既济颠倒用，将药火齐入炉中；金丹花儿勾八，婴儿姹女荣华；主人翁，认得他，打成一片是作家；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无大鹏金翅佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the fourth watch, cultivate with earnest effort and watch for the Wind of Xun ☴ rising in the southwest. Rising Wind of Xun—invert water and fire into Jiji (Already Across), placing Medicine (yao) and fire together inside the furnace. Golden Elixir flower, tally eight: the Infant and the Fair Maiden share radiant splendor; recognize the true Master and merge with him into one unbroken whole—that is a true adept. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Great Golden-Winged Garuḍa Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§11Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 9
Original (Chinese)

五更里，修行金鸡叫，迷人不是这条道；这条道，铁树开花蕊不少，完满了自有根梢；金丹花儿勾九，说起人人皆有；说起有，却又无，说无说有永不休；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无孔雀明王佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the fifth watch, cultivate as the golden rooster crows—the deluded do not walk this path. This path: the iron tree bursts into bloom with abundant stamens, and once perfected it naturally has both root and crown. Golden Elixir flower, tally nine: truth be told, every single person possesses it; yet say it exists and it is absent, and arguing whether it is or is not will never end. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Peacock Wisdom-King Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§12Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 10
Original (Chinese)

五更里，修行太阳红，须防火候一场空；一场空，霞光万道金莲涌，完满了自然成功；金丹花儿勾十，我今来在家里；见主人，笑嬉嬉，我问真人在那里；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无长耳定光佛，南无花开叶儿落。

In the fifth watch, cultivate as the sun glows crimson, and guard carefully lest the fire phasing (huohou) come to nothing. Come to nothing—ten thousand rays of rosy light shine forth and golden lotuses well up; once full and complete, success follows naturally. Golden Elixir flower, tally ten: today I have arrived home; seeing the Master grinning happily, I ask: “Where is the True Person?” Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Long-Eared Dīpaṃkara Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§13Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 11
Original (Chinese)

念罢五更到天明，正东闪上小桃红；小桃红，东生西落催人老，躲三灾报答师恩；金丹花儿勾十一，我今来在深山里；搭一座，草茅庵，降龙伏虎自然宽；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无阿弥陀佛，南无花开叶儿落。

Having chanted through the five watches until dawn breaks, a blush of peach-blossom pink flashes due east. Peach-blossom pink—rising in the east and setting in the west, it hurries mortals into old age; escape the Three Calamities to repay your teacher’s grace. Golden Elixir flower, tally eleven: today I have come deep into the mountains; building a thatched hermitage, subduing the dragon and taming the tiger, all is naturally spacious and at ease. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Amitābha Buddha, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

§14Five Watches Daoist Songs (V) · No. 12
Original (Chinese)

念罢五更一坦明，一道红光是至真；是至真，黄龙透出三关顶，到今日跨鹤飞升；金花十二一齐开，玉皇老子丹书来；穿仙衣，坐莲台，炼就金身全不坏；金丹花儿快乐念，念上一声佛，南无无量受福佛，南无花开叶儿落。

Having chanted through the five watches, all is bright and open—a single beam of crimson light is the supreme truth. Supreme truth—the Yellow Dragon breaks straight through the summit of the Three Passes, and this very day you mount a crane and soar on high. All twelve Golden Flowers bloom at once as the cinnabar edict of the Jade Emperor and Laozi arrives; clad in immortal robes, seated on a lotus throne, you have refined a golden body wholly indestructible. Joyfully chant the Golden Elixir flower, chant a Buddha’s name: Namo Buddha of Immeasurable Blessing, Namo, the blossom opens and the leaves fall!

Key Terms

  • Tally one, tally two… (gou yi, gou er…): A counting refrain from folk balladry, adding one more “Golden Elixir flower” with each song.
  • Namo (nā mó): A Buddhist expression of refuge and homage.
  • The blossom opens and the leaves fall: As Li Xiyue explains, a metaphor for yang arising and yin being exhausted.
  • Green ox: A metaphor for original Breath and the true mind; “Laozi himself can ride” alludes to the legend of Laozi riding a green ox.
  • Shadowless sword: A metaphor for the sword of wisdom that severs deluded thoughts.
  • Three Corpses and Six Thieves: Desires dwelling within the body and the greed of the six sense roots.
  • Wind of Xun ☴: Breathing; see Two Poems in the Ancient Style in this book.
  • Three Calamities: The Daoist calamities of wind, fire, and water; see Discourse Pointing Out Delusions on the Ascent to Heaven in this book.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Refine the Golden Elixir right at the crossroads. There is no need to flee everyday life: “no need for water, fire, or charcoal, only awaken to quiet sitting and Chan inquiry.”
  2. Lock the monkey of the mind within the Square Inch. Every single song begins by gathering the mind back home.
  3. Long life belongs to every person. “Truth be told, every single person possesses it; yet say it exists and it is absent”—our innate nature is complete in everyone, yet cannot be grasped as a tangible thing.
  4. Guard lest the fire phasing come to nothing. When practice nears completion, vigilance is needed most of all.

Translator's Notes

  1. This cycle has no separate overarching title; in the original edition it immediately follows the previous Five Watches Daoist Songs under the same title. To distinguish it clearly, this site labels it Set V and numbers the twelve lyrics “No. 1” through “No. 12.”
  2. The second lyric in the original also begins “In the first watch, cultivate by raising right mindfulness,” pairing with the first lyric under the first watch; the cycle contains two lyrics per watch, reproduced here as in the original.
  3. “This green ox Laozi himself can ride”: beneath this line the original edition prints a small note, “repeat line; same below,” indicating that the corresponding line in each song was repeated when sung.
  4. “Yellow Lord and Yellow Dame serve as matchmakers”: both Yellow Lord and Yellow Dame symbolize True Intention (zhenyi).
  5. “Burn spirit-money to send them packing”: draws on the folk custom of sending off spirits, used playfully as a metaphor for dismissing the Six Thieves.
  6. “The lion is refined into the fiery monkey”: preserved as in the original text, symbolizing the monkey of the mind being tamed through the fire of refinement.
  7. Some of the Buddha names chanted in this cycle come from Buddhist scriptures (Śākyamuni, Amitābha, Dīpaṃkara, Maitreya, etc.), while others are folk coinages (such as “Buddha of the Primordial Chaos World,” “Great Golden-Winged Garuḍa Buddha,” and “Buddha of Immeasurable Blessing”); all are translated as in the original.

The Rootless Tree (Part One)

Twenty-Four Daoqing Songs of the Rootless Tree (Part One) — two introductory lyrics and Songs 1–12: even old age, it turns out, has a medicine to heal it

About This Chapter

The twenty-four songs of the Rootless Tree (Wugen shu) are Zhang Sanfeng’s most widely circulated work; every song opens with “Rootless tree, the blossom is just […].” Li Xiyue’s introductory note explains that “rootless tree” refers to the Lead-breath (Prior Heaven Breath) within the human body. It grows its roots out of emptiness and nonbeing: both Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven arise as “being born from nonbeing,” which is why the songs say “spoken of as rootless, yet it has a root.” All twenty-four songs urge readers to come “beneath the rootless tree and closely savor the immortal blossoms”—the subtleties of the Medicine, fire phasing, grafting, and gathering are all contained within.

This chapter contains the two introductory lyrics composed by Zhang Sanfeng (Partridge Sky and Cry of the Flower Seller), Li Xiyue’s note on the title, and Songs 1 through 12.

How to read this cycle of songs: The Rootless Tree uses abundant worldly metaphors—blossoms and wine, man and woman, husband and wife, grafting. Because of this, some readers across the centuries have forced yin-yang sexual-harvesting interpretations onto it. Yet Zhang Sanfeng himself states with complete clarity in Pointing Out Delusions on Ascending to Heaven (included in this book) that heaven and earth, Qian and Kun, man and woman, and inner and outer furnace and tripod “are all metaphors for the inner and outer yin and yang of my own single body, with no literal man and woman at all,” and that using a female cauldron is “utterly devoid of reason.” In the Qing dynasty, Li Hanxu (the very re-editor of this collection) also wrote a dedicated pure-stillness commentary on the Rootless Tree. This site follows that reading and annotates each song accordingly: everywhere “man and woman,” “yin and yang,” or “husband and wife” appear, they refer to Spirit and Breath, inner nature and life, Li and Kan within one’s own body.

Safety Note: Any teaching that uses the Rootless Tree to promote “male–female dual cultivation” or “harvesting yin to replenish yang” directly contradicts Zhang Sanfeng’s own words. Do not be deceived.

Highlights of the first twelve songs:

  • Songs 1–2: Urging people to turn back and cultivate; “even old age, it turns out, has a medicine to heal it”—one can cultivate even in advanced years, yet must “begin at once.”
  • Song 3: “Neither violating meat-and-pungents nor violating lust,” yet also saying “without blossoms and without wine the Way is not accomplished”—blossoms and wine are metaphors.
  • Songs 4–5: Rejecting “sitting silently in solitary cultivation”—clinging to a dead emptiness with no union of yin and yang will not work; “going forward makes an ordinary mortal, reversing makes an immortal; it lies solely in turning upside down right at the center.”
  • Songs 8–12: When the Lead blossom appears and Gui-water ends; not found in minerals or herbs (“the five metals and eight stones are all false”); remaining unmoved by emotion in the face of enticing scenes.

Original Text and Translation

§1Two Self-Inscribed Lyrics on the Rootless Tree · Partridge Sky
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕按此调五六两句当作三字句，偕一韵乃是鹧鸪天，一本作「难与世人条辨论」，以二句为一句，则又似瑞鹧鸪也，兹两存之。另，瑞鹧鸪调与七律体同。

(Original Note) Lines five and six of this tune should be three-character lines sharing a single rhyme to match Partridge Sky (Zhegu tian). Another edition reads nan yu shiren tiao bianlun (“hard to argue point by point with worldly folk”), combining the two lines into one, which resembles Auspicious Partridge (Rui zhegu). Both readings are noted here. In addition, the prosody of Auspicious Partridge is identical to a seven-character regulated verse.

§2
Original (Chinese)

道法流传有正邪，入邪背正遍天涯；飞腾罕见穿云凤，陷溺多成落井蛙；难与辨，乱纷哗，都将赤土作丹砂；要知端的通玄路，细玩无根树下花。

Daoist teachings handed down include both the orthodox and the heterodox; those who turn from the true and enter the crooked fill the whole earth. Rarely seen is the phoenix soaring through the clouds; far more sink down to become frogs fallen in a well. Hard to argue with them—such noisy confusion!—all mistaking red clay for cinnabar. If you wish to know the true, exact road into the mystery, closely savor the blossoms beneath the rootless tree.

§3Two Self-Inscribed Lyrics on the Rootless Tree · Cry of the Flower Seller
Original (Chinese)

无根树下说真常，六道含灵共一光；会得威音前后事，本无来去貌堂堂。

Beneath the rootless tree I speak of True Constancy: all sentient beings in the six realms share one single Light. Once you fathom what came before and after the Buddha Majestic Sound, there is originally neither coming nor going, and one’s true visage stands dignified and clear.

§4Twenty-Four Daoqing Songs of the Rootless Tree
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕题义：无根树者，指人身之铅气也，丹家于虚无境内养出根株，先天后天都自无中生有，故曰说到无根却有根也，炼后天者，须要入无求有，然后以有投无，炼先天者，又要以有入无，然后自无返有，修炼根蒂如是而已，二十四首皆劝人无根树下细玩仙花，其药物气候裁接采取之妙，备载其中，此道情之不朽者也。

(Original Note) On the title: “Rootless tree” refers to the Lead-breath within the human body. Inner alchemists cultivate roots and trunk within the realm of emptiness and nonbeing; both Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven arise as being born from nonbeing, hence the song says “spoken of as rootless, yet it has a root.” Those who refine Posterior Heaven must enter nonbeing to seek being, and then cast being into nonbeing; those who refine Prior Heaven must enter nonbeing by means of being, and then return from nonbeing to being—the very root and stem of cultivation are nothing other than this. All twenty-four songs urge people to savor the immortal blossoms beneath the rootless tree; the subtleties of the Medicine, fire times, grafting, and gathering are fully recorded therein. This is an immortal masterpiece of daoqing.

§5The Rootless Tree · Song 1
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正幽，贪恋红尘谁肯修；浮生事，苦海舟，荡去飘来不自由；无边无岸难泊系，长在鱼龙险处游；肯回首，是岸头，莫待风波坏了舟。

Rootless tree, the blossom is quiet and secluded—clinging to the red dust, who is willing to cultivate? The affairs of floating life are a boat on the sea of suffering, tossing to and fro with no freedom of its own. Boundless and shoreless, with nowhere to moor or tie up, drifting always through perilous waters of fish and dragons. Be willing to turn your head—there is the shore! Do not wait until wind and waves smash the boat to pieces.

§6The Rootless Tree · Song 2
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正微，树老将新接嫩枝；梅寄柳，桑接梨，传与修真作样儿；自古神仙栽接法，人老原来有药医；访明师，问方儿，下手速修犹太迟。

Rootless tree, the blossom is faint and subtle—when a tree grows old, graft a fresh tender branch onto it. Plum grafted onto willow, mulberry grafted onto pear: handed down as a pattern for those who cultivate reality. From antiquity, immortals have had a method of grafting and renewal; even old age, it turns out, has a medicine to heal it. Seek out an enlightened teacher, ask for the prescription—even if you begin at once, it is already almost too late.

§7The Rootless Tree · Song 3
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正青，花酒神仙古到今；烟花寨，酒肉林，不犯荤腥不犯淫；犯淫丧失长生宝，酒肉穿肠道在心；打开门，说与君，无花无酒道不成。

Rootless tree, the blossom is fresh and green—“blossom-and-wine immortals” have existed from ancient times to today. Amid brothel-camps and forests of wine and meat, neither violate meat-and-pungents nor violate lust. Violating lust loses the treasure of long life; wine and meat pass through the gut while the Way abides in the heart. Opening the door, I tell you plainly: without “blossoms” and without “wine,” the Way is not accomplished.

§8The Rootless Tree · Song 4
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正孤，借问阴阳得类无；雌鸡卵，难抱雏，背了阴阳造化炉；女子无夫为怨女，男子无妻是旷夫；叹迷徒，太模糊，静坐孤修气转枯。

Rootless tree, the blossom stands alone—may I ask, have yin and yang found their true counterpart? An unfertilized hen’s egg can hardly hatch a chick, for it runs counter to the creative furnace of yin and yang. A woman without a husband is a grieving woman; a man without a wife is a lonely bachelor. Alas for deluded students, so muddled in mind: sitting silently in solitary cultivation, their Breath only withers dry.

§9The Rootless Tree · Song 5
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正偏，离了阴阳道不全；金隔木，汞隔铅，阳寡阴孤各一边；世上阴阳男配女，生子生孙代代传；顺为凡，逆为仙，只在中间颠倒颠。

Rootless tree, the blossom leans to one side—apart from yin and yang, the Way is incomplete. Metal parted from Wood, Mercury parted from Lead, solitary yang and lone yin each stuck on its own side. In the world, yin and yang mean man paired with woman, bearing sons and grandsons generation after generation. Going forward makes an ordinary mortal; reversing makes an immortal—it lies solely in turning upside down right at the center.

§10The Rootless Tree · Song 6
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正新，产在坤方坤是人；摘花戴，采花心，花蕊层层艳丽春；时人不达花中理，一诀天机值万金；借花名，作花身，句句敲爻说得真。

Rootless tree, the blossom is brand new—produced in the Kun quarter, and Kun is the human body. Pluck the blossom to wear, gather the heart of the blossom; petal upon petal, stamens glowing in spring splendor. People of the age do not fathom the principle within the blossom—this single formula of Heaven’s secret is worth ten thousand pieces of gold. Borrowing the name of “blossom” to depict its form, every line rings out like the Song of Striking the Lines (Qiaoyao ge), spoken true.

§11The Rootless Tree · Song 7
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正繁，笑貌娇容赛粉团；防猿马，劣更顽，挂起娘生铁面颜；提出青龙真宝剑，摘尽墙头朵朵鲜；趁风帆，满载还，怎肯空行到宝山。

Rootless tree, the blossoms are thick and lush—smiling grace and delicate beauty surpassing powdered jade. Guard against the monkey-mind and horse-intention, unruly and stubborn as they are; put on the iron face your mother bore you with, unmoved by anything. Draw forth the true precious sword of the Azure Dragon and pluck every fresh blossom along the wall-top. Catching the wind in your sails, return with a full cargo—how could one visit Treasure Mountain and walk away empty-handed?

§12The Rootless Tree · Song 8
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正飞，卸了重开有定期；铅花现，癸尽时，依旧西园花满枝；对月才经收拾去，又向朝阳补衲衣；这玄微，世罕知，须共神仙仔细推。

Rootless tree, the blossoms are flying—falling and blooming anew at an appointed time. The Lead blossom appears just as Gui-water ends, and once more the western garden is filled with blossoms on every bough. Having just gathered them in facing the moon, turn again to the morning sun to mend your patched robe. This subtlety is rarely known in the world; one must examine it carefully together with an immortal.

§13The Rootless Tree · Song 9
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正开，偃月炉中摘下来；延年寿，减病灾，好结良朋备法财；从兹可成天上宝，一任群迷笑我呆；劝贤才，休卖乖，不遇明师莫强猜。

Rootless tree, the blossom is open wide—plucked right out of the Crescent-Moon Furnace. Lengthening years of life, reducing illness and misfortune: make good companions and prepare the means for practice (facai). From this one can form the treasure of Heaven, letting the deluded crowd laugh and call me a fool. I urge you, worthy talents: stop showing off your cleverness; until you meet an enlightened teacher, do not force wild guesses.

§14The Rootless Tree · Song 10
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正圆，结果收成滋味全；如朱橘，似弹丸，护守隄防莫放闲；学些草木收头法，复命归根返本源；选灵地，结道庵，会合先天了大还。

Rootless tree, the blossom is full and round—bearing fruit and harvested, its flavor complete. Like a vermilion tangerine, like a pellet: guard and watch over it, never letting your vigilance slacken. Learn something of how grasses and trees draw back to their roots—restoring life, returning to the root, going back to the original source. Choose a numinous site, build a Daoist hut, unite with Prior Heaven, and complete the Great Reversion.

§15The Rootless Tree · Song 11
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正亨，说到无根却有根；三才窍，二五精，天地交时万物生；日月交时寒暑顺，男女交时孕始成；甚分明，说与君，只恐相逢认不真。

Rootless tree, the blossom flourishes smoothly—spoken of as rootless, yet it has a root. The opening of the Three Powers, the essence of Two and Five: when heaven and earth unite, the myriad things are born; when sun and moon unite, cold and heat move in harmony; when man and woman unite, conception takes place. All this is crystal clear as I tell it to you—I fear only that when you meet it face to face, you will fail to recognize it truly.

§16The Rootless Tree · Song 12
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正佳，对景忘情玩月华；金精旺，耀眼花，莫在园中错拣瓜；五金八石皆为假，万草千方总是差；金虾蟆，玉老鸦，认得真铅是作家。

Rootless tree, the blossom is at its finest—forgetting emotion before the scene, delighting in the splendor of the moon. Golden Essence surges bright, dazzling the eyes; do not pick the wrong melon in the garden! The five metals and eight stones are all false; ten thousand herbs and a thousand prescriptions are all wide of the mark. Golden toad and jade crow—only one who recognizes True Lead is a true master of the craft.

Key Terms

  • Rootless tree (无根树): Explained by Li Xiyue as the Lead-breath—Prior Heaven Breath—within the human body; called “rootless” because it arises out of emptiness and nonbeing.
  • Blossom (花): Metaphor for the Medicine seedling when Prior Heaven Breath stirs, also called Lead blossom or Golden Flower.
  • Grafting (栽接): Inner alchemists use botanical grafting as a metaphor for replenishing Posterior Heaven with Prior Heaven and restoring youth to old age.
  • Majestic Sound (威音): Buddha Majestic Sound (Bhīṣmagarjitasvararāja), the primordial Buddha in Buddhist scriptures; “before and after Majestic Sound” symbolizes one’s original face.
  • Kun quarter (坤方): The southwest; in inner alchemy it indicates the place in the body where the Medicine is produced (the abdomen, lower Cinnabar Field), corresponding to the “Winding River” in On a Single Grain of Millet in this book.
  • Crescent-Moon Furnace (偃月炉): Inner-alchemical metaphor for the lower Cinnabar Field, where the Medicine is generated.
  • Three Powers, Two and Five (三才、二五): Heaven, Earth, and Humanity; Two and Five refer to the two breaths of yin and yang and the essences of the Five Phases, from Zhou Dunyi’s Explanation of the Diagram of the Supreme Ultimate: “The essences of the Two and the Five wondrously unite and congeal.”
  • Five metals and eight stones (五金八石): Mineral ingredients used in external alchemy (waidan).

Putting It into Practice

  1. Turning back is the shore—the sooner the better. Songs 1 and 2 are the most down-to-earth: life is fleeting, and even old age has a medicine, but one must start without delay.
  2. Neither violate meat-and-pungents nor violate lust. Song 3 explicitly calls for keeping away from lust. “Blossoms” and “wine” are metaphors, not license for indulgence.
  3. Avoid dead emptiness. Songs 4 and 5 criticize “sitting silently in solitary cultivation, their Breath only withers dry”: merely sitting rigidly without understanding is not cultivation. One must understand how yin and yang (Spirit and Breath) unite within the body.
  4. Forget emotion before the scene. Songs 7 and 12: when encountering alluring scenes—including blissful sensations arising inside the body—keep the mind as unmoved as an iron mask.
  5. Do not seek in external substances. “The five metals and eight stones are all false; ten thousand herbs and a thousand prescriptions are all wide of the mark.”

Translator's Notes

  1. “Two Self-Inscribed Lyrics on the Rootless Tree”: In the original collection, Partridge Sky and Cry of the Flower Seller precede the cycle of twenty-four songs, with Li Xiyue’s note appended to the first.
  2. “There is originally neither coming nor going, and one’s true visage stands dignified and clear”: Metaphor for inner nature as unborn and undying.
  3. “Plum grafted onto willow, mulberry grafted onto pear”: Ancient horticultural grafting, borrowed by inner alchemists to symbolize renewing the Posterior Heaven body with Prior Heaven Breath.
  4. “‘Blossom-and-wine immortals’ have existed from ancient times to today”: “Blossom” symbolizes the Prior Heaven Lead blossom (Medicine seedling), and “wine” symbolizes the water of True Unity (saliva, jade nectar). Since the very next line says “neither violate meat-and-pungents nor violate lust,” this clearly does not refer to worldly wine and courtesans.
  5. “May I ask, have yin and yang found their true counterpart?” and “A woman without a husband is a grieving woman; a man without a wife is a lonely bachelor”: Read according to the pure-stillness lineage, yin and yang denote Spirit and Breath (Li and Kan, inner nature and life) within the body, and “finding their counterpart” means Spirit and Breath pairing with each other. Clinging to an empty void without Spirit and Breath uniting is “lone yin and solitary yang,” hence “sitting silently in solitary cultivation, their Breath only withers dry.” Because Zhang Sanfeng explicitly rejects using a female cauldron in Pointing Out Delusions on Ascending to Heaven, these lines are not read as sexual intercourse.
  6. “In the world, yin and yang mean man paired with woman, bearing sons and grandsons generation after generation; going forward makes an ordinary mortal, reversing makes an immortal”: Human procreation illustrates the forward (ordinary) course; cultivation requires reversal—instead of letting Spirit and Breath leak outward, turning them inward, hence “solely in turning upside down right at the center.”
  7. “Produced in the Kun quarter, and Kun is the human body”: Kun corresponds to the southwest and to the abdomen, denoting the site within the body where the Medicine arises; “Kun is the human” means the Medicine lies right inside the human body. Heterodox schools twist this into a reference to a female partner, contrary to Zhang Sanfeng’s intent.
  8. “Smiling grace and delicate beauty surpassing powdered jade”: Describes the alluring vision when the Medicine seedling (Lead blossom) stirs—immediately followed by “guard against the monkey-mind and horse-intention” and “put on the iron face your mother bore you with”: however enchanting the scene, the mind stays unmoved.
  9. “Falling and blooming anew at an appointed time” and “The Lead blossom appears just as Gui-water ends”: Metaphor for the cyclical timing of Prior Heaven Breath, gathered just as Gui-water (turbid Posterior Heaven water) subsides and Prior Heaven is about to emerge.
  10. “Having just gathered them in facing the moon, turn again to the morning sun to mend your patched robe”: Metaphor for following the gathering of the Medicine immediately with gentle nurturing.
  11. “When heaven and earth unite, the myriad things are born… when man and woman unite, conception takes place”: Uses the three unions of heaven-and-earth, sun-and-moon, and man-and-woman to illustrate how Spirit and Breath unite to form the Elixir, driving home the principle of “rootless yet having a root.”
  12. “Do not pick the wrong melon in the garden”: Metaphor for not misidentifying the Medicine.
  13. “Golden toad and jade crow”: The toad in the moon and the golden crow in the sun, symbolizing Lead and Mercury.
  14. “Make good companions and prepare the means for practice (facai)”: On facai (“means/resources for practice”), see On Verification of the Return in this book.

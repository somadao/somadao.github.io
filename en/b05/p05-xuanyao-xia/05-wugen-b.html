The Rootless Tree (Part Two)

Twenty-Four Daoqing Songs of the Rootless Tree (Part Two) — Songs 13–24: emptiness is form, form is emptiness; see right through to True Emptiness within form

About This Chapter

Continuing from the previous chapter, this chapter presents Songs 13 through 24 of the Rootless Tree. The way of reading them is the same as in Part One: phrases such as “man and woman,” “husband and wife,” and “maiden of the eastern house, lad of the western cottage” are all metaphors for yin and yang within one’s own body (Spirit and Breath, Mercury and Lead); see the introduction to Part One for details.

The arc of the second half of the cycle:

  • Songs 13–18: Across the River of Desire, walking step by step into the lair of Dragon and Tiger; the Lead tripod gently warm, replacing the dusty old bowels completely; tallies and fire simmering together, passing the Three Passes and piercing the Muddy Pellet; “Essence, Breath, and Spirit cooked in one single tripod”; dragon-slaying sword and tiger-binding cord sweeping away all 3,600 side paths.
  • Songs 19–22: Lead cast into Mercury, yin paired with yang; gathering the first branch of Prior Heaven while watching carefully lest the fire phasing escape the furnace; born in the central Wu-Ji homeland, where the Yellow Dame offers ghee-nectar wine; guarding the Yellow Court and nourishing the Valley Spirit—“a man carrying a child makes everyone laugh!”
  • Songs 23–24: Returning at last to emptiness: “Emptiness is form, form is emptiness; see right through to True Emptiness within form”; “shadowless and formless, impossible to paint”; “forging True Emptiness to return to Great Nonbeing.” The entire cycle moves from “the blossom is quiet and secluded” to “the blossom is gone”—tracing precisely the journey from being into nonbeing.

Original Text and Translation

§1The Rootless Tree · Song 13
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正多，遍地开时隔爱河；难攀折，怎奈何，步步行行龙虎窝；采得黄花归洞去，紫府题名永不磨；笑呵呵，白云阿，准备天梯上大罗。

Rootless tree, the blossoms are abundant—blooming across the land, yet separated by the River of Desire. Hard to reach and pluck—what can be done? Walk step by step into the lair of Dragon and Tiger. Once you gather the yellow blossom and return to your grotto, your name is inscribed in the Purple Mansion, never to be effaced. Laughing heartily amid the white clouds, prepare the heavenly ladder to ascend to the Grand Canopy Heaven (Daluo).

§2The Rootless Tree · Song 14
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正香，铅鼎温温现宝光；金桥上，望曲江，月里分明见太阳；吞服乌肝并兔髓，换尽尘埃旧肚肠；名利场，恩爱乡，再不回头空自忙。

Rootless tree, the blossom is fragrant—the Lead tripod glows softly warm, revealing precious light. Standing on the Golden Bridge and gazing down at the Winding River, inside the moon you clearly see the sun. Swallow the crow’s liver together with the hare’s marrow, replacing every trace of your dust-stained old guts. Arenas of fame and profit, realms of fondness and attachment—never turn back to busy yourself in vain again.

§3The Rootless Tree · Song 15
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正鲜，符火相煎汞与铅；临炉际，景现前，采取全凭渡法船；匠手高强牢把舵，一任洪波海底翻；过三关，透泥丸，早把通身九窍穿。

Rootless tree, the blossom is vivid and fresh—tallies and fire simmer Mercury and Lead together. Approaching the furnace, as the vision appears before you, gathering relies wholly on the Dharma ferryboat. With a master craftsman’s firm hand gripping the helm, let huge billows surge up from the ocean floor! Passing the Three Passes and piercing the Muddy Pellet, in no time all nine apertures of the entire body are opened through.

§4The Rootless Tree · Song 16
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正浓，认取真铅正祖宗；精炁神，一鼎烹，女转成男老变童；欲向西方擒白虎，先往东家伏青龙；类相同，好用功，外药通时内药通。

Rootless tree, the blossom is rich and deep—recognize True Lead as the orthodox ancestor. Essence, Breath, and Spirit are cooked in a single tripod; female turns to male, and the aged becomes a child. If you wish to go west to seize the White Tiger, first go to the eastern house to subdue the Azure Dragon. Being of the same kind, the work goes smoothly: once the Outer Medicine flows through, the Inner Medicine flows through as well.

§5The Rootless Tree · Song 17
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正娇，天应星兮地应潮；屠龙剑，缚虎绦，运转天罡斡斗杓；煅炼一炉真日月，扫尽三千六百条；步云霄，任逍遥，罪垢凡尘一笔消。

Rootless tree, the blossom is tender and lovely—Heaven responds with stars and Earth responds with tides. Dragon-slaying sword and tiger-binding cord turn the Celestial Dipper and pivot its handle. Forging one furnace of true sun and moon sweeps clean all three thousand six hundred side paths. Striding into the clouds, carefree at will, every stain of fault and worldly dust is wiped away with one stroke.

§6The Rootless Tree · Song 18
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正高，海浪滔天月弄潮；银河路，透九霄，槎影横空泊斗梢；摸着织女支机石，踏遍牛郎驾鹊桥；遇仙曹，胆气豪，盗得瑶池王母桃。

Rootless tree, the blossom climbs high—ocean waves billow to heaven as the moon plays with the tide. Along the Milky Way, piercing up to the Nine Heavens, the shadow of a raft spans the sky and moors at the tip of the Dipper. Touching the Weaving Maiden’s loom-prop stone, treading across the Cowherd’s Magpie Bridge, meeting the ranks of immortals with bold and lofty spirit, you steal the Queen Mother’s peaches from the Jasper Pool.

§7The Rootless Tree · Song 19
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正双，龙虎登坛战一场；铅投汞，阴配阳，法象玄珠无价偿；此是家园真种子，返老还童寿命长；上天堂，极乐方，免得轮回见阎王。

Rootless tree, the blossoms bloom in pairs—Dragon and Tiger mount the altar to wage a battle. Lead cast into Mercury, yin paired with yang: the symbolic Mysterious Pearl is beyond all price. This is the true seed of one’s own home garden, turning old age back to youth and lengthening one’s span. Ascending to Heaven, to the land of utmost bliss, spared from rebirth and from facing King Yama.

§8The Rootless Tree · Song 20
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正奇，月里栽培片晌时；拏云手，步云梯，采取先天第一枝；饮酒带花神气爽，笑煞仙翁醉似泥；托心知，谨护持，惟恐炉中火候飞。

Rootless tree, the blossom is wondrous and rare—tended in the moonlight for just a fleeting moment. Reaching out a cloud-grasping hand, stepping onto a cloud-ladder, pluck the foremost branch of Prior Heaven. Drinking wine with blossoms tucked in your hair, Spirit and Breath feel brisk and clear—how funny the old immortal looks, drunk as mud! Entrust it to one who knows your heart, and guard it with utmost care, fearing only lest the fire phasing fly out of the furnace.

§9The Rootless Tree · Song 21
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正黄，产在中央戊己乡；东家女，西舍郎，配合夫妻入洞房；黄婆劝饮醍醐酒，每日醺蒸醉一场；这仙方，返魂浆，起死回生是药王。

Rootless tree, the blossom is pure yellow—born in the central homeland of Wu and Ji. The maiden of the eastern house and the lad of the western cottage are joined as husband and wife and enter the bridal chamber. The Yellow Dame urges them to drink ghee-nectar wine, growing warmly tipsy in a daily round of intoxication. This immortal recipe, this soul-returning elixir, raises the dead and brings back life—it is the King of Medicines.

§10The Rootless Tree · Song 22
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正明，月魄天心逼日魂；金乌髓，玉兔精，二物擒来一处烹；阳火阴符分子午，沐浴加临卯酉门；守黄庭，养谷神，男子怀胎笑煞人。

Rootless tree, the blossom shines bright—the moon’s corporeal soul and the heart of Heaven press upon the sun’s ethereal soul. Marrow of the Golden Crow and essence of the Jade Hare: seize both things and cook them in a single place. Yang fire and yin tally divide at Zi and Wu; ablution comes at the gates of Mao and You. Guarding the Yellow Court and nourishing the Valley Spirit—a man carrying a child makes everyone laugh!

§11The Rootless Tree · Song 23
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正红，摘尽红花一树空；空即色，色即空，识透真空在色中；了了真空色相灭，法相长存不落空；号圆通，称大雄，九祖超升上九重。

Rootless tree, the blossom is crimson red—pluck every red blossom, and the whole tree stands empty. Emptiness is form, form is emptiness; see right through to True Emptiness within form. Lucid and clear, within True Emptiness all phenomenal forms dissolve, yet the Dharma-form endures forever without falling into dead emptiness. Named Complete Penetration, titled Great Hero—nine generations of ancestors ascend to the Ninefold Heaven.

§12The Rootless Tree · Song 24
Original (Chinese)

无根树，花正无，无影无形难画图；无名姓，却听呼，擒入中间造化炉；运起周天三昧火，煅炼真空返太无；谒仙都，受天符，才是男儿大丈夫。

Rootless tree, the blossom is gone—shadowless and formless, impossible to paint. Without surname or given name, yet it answers when called; draw it into the central furnace of creation. Rouse the Samādhi fire of the Celestial Circuit, forge True Emptiness, and return to Great Nonbeing. Paying homage at the Immortal Capital and receiving Heaven’s tallies—only this is a true hero of a person!

Key Terms

  • River of Desire (爱河): A Buddhist term likening emotional craving to a river that drowns people; here the blossoms bloom “across the River of Desire,” meaning one must cross over desire to gather them.
  • Lair of Dragon and Tiger (龙虎窝): Metaphor for the place within the body where Spirit and Breath meet.
  • Crow’s liver and hare’s marrow (乌肝兔髓): From the Golden Crow in the sun and the Jade Hare in the moon, symbolizing Mercury and Lead.
  • Three Passes and nine apertures (三关、九窍): The Caudal Funnel, Spine-Straddling Pass, and Jade Pillow; the apertures of the body.
  • Eastern house and western cottage (东家、西舍): Eastern Wood (Azure Dragon, Mercury, inner nature) and Western Metal (White Tiger, Lead, emotion/vitality); see On the Two Earths, Wu and Ji in this book.
  • Three thousand six hundred side paths (三千六百条): Metaphor for myriad minor heterodox techniques.
  • Wu-Ji homeland (戊己乡): The Central Palace; see Wu and Ji (True Intention).
  • Valley Spirit (谷神): From Daodejing, chapter 6: “The Valley Spirit never dies; it is called the Mysterious Female.”
  • A man carrying a child (男子怀胎): Metaphor for conceiving the Holy Embryo; see Holy Embryo.
  • Nine generations of ancestors ascending (九祖超升): A Daoist saying that when one person attains the Way, nine generations of ancestors are elevated to heaven.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Cross the River of Desire. Song 13 says the blossoms bloom “across the River of Desire”—passion and craving are the greatest obstacles to cultivation, and only by crossing them can you “gather the yellow blossom.”
  2. Essence, Breath, and Spirit cooked in one tripod. The three are originally one unified whole; resting steadily in the center is “cooking them in one tripod.”
  3. Sweep away all side paths. Song 17’s “sweeps clean all three thousand six hundred side paths”: the true Way is simple; every elaborate contrivance can be set aside.
  4. Return at last to emptiness. The final two songs move from “crimson red” to “gone”: every tangible phenomenon must eventually be released. “See right through to True Emptiness within form”—not fleeing daily life in search of emptiness, but seeing through appearances right in the midst of life.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Amid the white clouds” (baiyun a 白云阿): So in the original; a (阿) is a rhythmic modal particle.
  2. “Inside the moon you clearly see the sun”: Metaphor for true yang within yin.
  3. “Female turns to male, and the aged becomes a child”: Metaphor for exhausting yin into pure yang and reversing aging.
  4. “If you wish to go west to seize the White Tiger, first go to the eastern house to subdue the Azure Dragon”: First subdue one’s own heart-mind (Azure Dragon, eastern house), and only then can one gather Prior Heaven Breath (White Tiger, west).
  5. “Being of the same kind, the work goes smoothly: once the Outer Medicine flows through, the Inner Medicine flows through as well”: On “Outer Medicine” and “Inner Medicine,” see On the Two Earths, Wu and Ji in this book—Wu Earth is the Outer Medicine and Ji Earth is the Inner Medicine, both within one’s own body.
  6. “Heaven responds with stars and Earth responds with tides”: Metaphor for the resonance between the human body and heaven and earth.
  7. “Touching the Weaving Maiden’s loom-prop stone, treading across the Cowherd’s Magpie Bridge”: Alludes to the legend of Zhang Qian sailing a raft up the Milky Way and obtaining the Weaving Maiden’s loom-prop stone; “Magpie Bridge” also alludes to the junction of the Conception and Governing vessels inside the body.
  8. “The maiden of the eastern house and the lad of the western cottage are joined as husband and wife and enter the bridal chamber”: East is Wood-Mercury (the yin inside Li, hence “maiden”); West is Metal-Lead (the yang inside Kan, hence “lad”); the Yellow Dame (True Intention) acts as matchmaker, uniting them in the Central Palace (“bridal chamber”). Every image is an inner-body metaphor.
  9. “A man carrying a child makes everyone laugh”: Because the Holy Embryo is a metaphor, he says it “makes everyone laugh.”
  10. “Emptiness is form, form is emptiness”: Adapts the Heart Sutra’s “form is emptiness, emptiness is form.”
  11. “Named Complete Penetration, titled Great Hero”: Epithets of the Buddha, borrowed as metaphors for attaining the Way.

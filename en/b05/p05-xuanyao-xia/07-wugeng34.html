V · Essentials of the Mystery (Part Two)

Five Watches Daoist Songs (III & IV)

Sets III and IV of the Five Watches Daoist Songs — entering the meditation hall, sitting on the rush cushion, and recalling one’s original face before one’s parents were born

About This Chapter

  • Set III: Carries a distinct Chan Buddhist flavor. In the first watch, one enters the meditation hall and guards the white-jade light of the Spirit Terrace; in the second watch, one sits upon the rush cushion, “recalling the time before one’s parents were born, when the original face continually appears”; in the third watch, the first yang arises, free of all attachments and ever pure and still; in the fourth watch, one “seeks the ancestral lineage, where the Great Meaning of the Patriarch’s coming from the West abides,” watching over one’s own true Master; in the fifth watch, the single opening of the Mysterious Pass is realized and the Yang Spirit merges with Great Emptiness.
  • Set IV: Opens with a lyric to the tune West River Moon as a prelude (Li Xiyue notes that this is an ancient immortal’s lyric borrowed by the Patriarch to begin the set). Each watch is sung in a brisk, lively cadence drawn from folk storytelling and balladry—“just look at you,” “advisors, ministers, pawns, cannons deployed, chariots and horses advancing, check!”—likening the subduing of demons and the guarding of the Celestial Circuit to a game of Chinese chess.

Original Text and Translation

§1Five Watches Daoist Songs (III) · First Watch
Original (Chinese)

一更里，入禅房，清净身心不用忙，心猿意马休轻放；守定灵台白玉光，无事真人里面藏；主翁端坐昆仑上，黄婆勾引入洞房；婴儿姹女配成双，三家会合曲江上。

In the first watch, enter the meditation chamber, keep body and mind pure and still without haste, and do not lightly let loose the monkey of the mind or the horse of thought. Steadfastly guard the white-jade light of the Spirit Terrace, for the carefree True Person hides within. The true Master sits upright atop Mount Kunlun, while the Yellow Dame guides and invites them into the bridal chamber; the Infant and the Fair Maiden are matched as a pair, and the Three Families converge upon the Winding River.

§2Five Watches Daoist Songs (III) · Second Watch
Original (Chinese)

二更里，上蒲团，思念父母未生前，本来面目常发现；采取先天补后天，三关运转至泥丸；华池神水频吞咽，水火相交煖下田；偃月炉中至宝煎，三回九转把丹炼。

In the second watch, take your seat on the rush cushion, contemplate the state before your parents were born, and your original face will continually manifest. Gather Prior Heaven to replenish Posterior Heaven, revolving through the Three Passes all the way to the Muddy Pellet (niwan). Frequently swallow the divine water of the Flowery Pool so that water and fire meet and warm the lower Cinnabar Field. Within the Crescent-Moon Furnace, simmer the supreme treasure; through three cycles and nine revolutions, refine the Elixir to completion.

§3Five Watches Daoist Songs (III) · Third Watch
Original (Chinese)

三更里，一阳生，坎离交媾结婚姻，无牵无挂常清净；海底泥牛直上奔，绵绵一炁透昆仑；金水夫妻来交并，白雪生长在黄庭；炼就金丹不坏身，方才识透玄关性。

In the third watch, the first yang arises; Kan and Li unite in inner “marriage,” free of every entanglement and ever pure and still. The clay ox from the bottom of the sea charges straight upward, and an unbroken thread of One Breath penetrates to Mount Kunlun. The “husband and wife” of Metal and Water join together, and white snow grows within the Yellow Court (huangting). Once the Golden Elixir is refined into an indestructible body, only then do you truly penetrate the nature of the Mysterious Pass.

§4Five Watches Daoist Songs (III) · Fourth Watch
Original (Chinese)

四更里，觅宗风，西来大意在其中，时时常把功夫用；皓月当空彻顶红，照看自己主人翁；方知炉内铅投汞，玲珑塔里现真宗；金丹炼就了真空，千年万载身不动。

In the fourth watch, seek the ancestral style—the Great Meaning of the Patriarch’s coming from the West lies right within it—and apply the work at every moment without cease. A bright moon hangs high in the sky, glowing crimson right through the crown, watching over your own true Master. Only then do you know how Lead enters Mercury inside the furnace, and how the true lineage reveals itself within the exquisite pagoda. When the Golden Elixir is perfected and true emptiness is fully realized, for thousands and ten thousands of years the body remains unmoved.

§5Five Watches Daoist Songs (III) · Fifth Watch
Original (Chinese)

五更里，合天机，玄关一窍少人知，谁人识得生死地；全凭戊己产婴儿，金光灿烂现牟尼；至宝收在丹田里，养就灵根与天齐；阳神妙体同太虚，黍珠一粒包天地。

In the fifth watch, accord with the heavenly mechanism: few know the single opening of the Mysterious Pass, and who truly recognizes the ground of life and death? Rely entirely on the Earth of wu and ji to bring forth the Infant; golden light blazes forth, revealing the Maṇi jewel. Store this supreme treasure within the Cinnabar Field and nourish the spiritual root until its lifespan equals Heaven’s. The wondrous body of the Yang Spirit becomes one with Great Emptiness, and a single millet-grain pearl encompasses Heaven and Earth.

§6Five Watches Daoist Songs (IV)
Original (Chinese)

静中观面观象，搜寻道窍根源；太乙炉中运周天，三昧真火锻炼；箭射九重铁鼓，三关运转泥丸；拏著宝月配日眠，此时铅汞相见。

In stillness, observe the face and observe the image, searching out the root and source of the Dao’s opening. Revolve the Celestial Circuit (zhoutian) within the Furnace of Supreme Unity, tempering it with the Samādhi True Fire. An arrow pierces nine layers of iron drums as the breath turns through the Three Passes to the Muddy Pellet (niwan). Holding the precious moon to sleep beside the sun—at this very moment Lead and Mercury meet.

§7
Original (Chinese)

〔原按〕右调西江月，此乃古仙词唱，祖师借来作五更道情引首，故仍之。

(Original Note) The lyric above, to the tune West River Moon, is a song by an ancient immortal which the Patriarch borrowed to serve as the prelude to these Five Watches Daoist Songs; therefore it is retained here.

§8Five Watches Daoist Songs (IV) · First Watch
Original (Chinese)

一更里，马稳莫放猿猴跳；气静神清，自然心地扫；看守黄庭，运转先天道；清静闲观，透出玄中妙。乘一时，才心定，性在天边海底命，悟着青铜镜；青铜镜，无象光明铁陀硬；你看你，变乾坤，采日精，尽都听，法王令。

In the first watch, steady the horse and do not let the monkey leap about; when Breath is calm and Spirit is clear, the ground of the mind is naturally swept clean. Guard the Yellow Court (huangting) and turn the Way of Prior Heaven; in pure stillness look on at ease, and the wonder within the mystery shines through. Seizing that single moment, the mind at last grows settled: inner nature (xing) lies at heaven’s edge and existence (ming) at the bottom of the sea, awakening to the bronze mirror. That bronze mirror is formless yet radiant, hard as solid iron. Just look at you: transforming Qian ☰ and Kun ☷, gathering the essence of the sun, everything obeys the Dharma King’s command.

§9Five Watches Daoist Songs (IV) · Second Watch
Original (Chinese)

二更里，炉内万朵莲花放；炼就黄芽，一点从天降；死中拨活，无象却有象；普照十方，到处皆明亮。看天沟，明耀耀，牧放群羊拍手笑，早把云梦跳；云梦跳，脚踏灵台高声叫；你看你，领金牌，把名表，得证了，无为道。

In the second watch, ten thousand lotus blossoms open inside the furnace; once the Yellow Sprout is refined, a single point descends from Heaven. Stirring life out of death, it has no form yet shows a form, shining universally across the ten directions so every place is bright. Look at the Heavenly Channel gleaming brilliantly; herding the flock of sheep, clapping hands and laughing, you have already leaped clear of the Yunmeng marsh. Leaped clear of Yunmeng, stepping upon the Spirit Terrace and calling out aloud—just look at you: receiving the golden tally, your name inscribed on the register, you have realized the Way of non-action.

§10Five Watches Daoist Songs (IV) · Third Watch
Original (Chinese)

三更里，调理巍巍全不动；一枕孤眠，识破黄粱梦；白鸦来朝，太极光明洞；海水枯干，颗颗珍珠弄。紫阳宫，独自立，水晶宫里闲游戏；好个九品无生地，都帅大堂金刚列；你看你，上天梯，怎得知，珊瑚石，真琉璃。

In the third watch, regulate everything until it stands towering and utterly unmoved; sleeping solitary on a single pillow, see right through the Yellow Millet Dream. The white crow comes to pay court in the luminous grotto of the Supreme Ultimate; the seawater dries up, and pearl after pearl rolls in play. Standing alone in the Purple Yang Palace, sporting at leisure inside the Crystal Palace—what a fine nine-grade realm of the Unborn, with vajra guardians lined up in the commander’s great hall! Just look at you: climbing the ladder to Heaven, how could outsiders ever know—coral stone, true lapis lazuli.

§11Five Watches Daoist Songs (IV) · Fourth Watch
Original (Chinese)

四更里，猛勇要把魔王战；无象宫中，使出双尾剑；战退魔王，万里成一片；体貌纵横，又入蓬莱院。这消息，谁知道，自己思量自己笑；好个玄中妙，玄中妙，一副棋子盘中闹；你看你，士相卒，摆列炮，进车马，将军照。

In the fourth watch, fiercely and courageously do battle with the Demon King; in the Formless Palace, wield the double-tailed sword. Drive back the Demon King until ten thousand miles merge into one unbroken expanse; moving freely in every direction, you enter once more the courtyard of Penglai. Who knows this news? Pondering it to yourself, you laugh to yourself! What a marvel within the mystery—a marvel within the mystery, like a full set of chessmen bustling across the board! Just look at you: advisors, ministers, pawns, cannons lined up in array, chariots and horses advancing—check!

§12Five Watches Daoist Songs (IV) · Fifth Watch
Original (Chinese)

五更里，方醒自觉心开悟；急紧加功，再进竿头步；奔到紫阳宫，透出漕溪路；这些功夫，等闲休分诉。提金容，把剑首，自己收拾休教漏；放出蛟龙斗，蛟龙斗，莫把羊儿饿饿瘦；你看你，隄防很，贼不偷，守走了，周天候。

In the fifth watch, waking at last, you sense your own mind open in awakening; urgently redouble the work and step further past the top of the hundred-foot pole. Press onward to the Purple Yang Palace and break through onto the road of Caoxi—of such practice, do not lightly speak to casual listeners. Lift the golden countenance, grasp the hilt of the sword, gather everything back into yourself and let nothing leak away. Release the flood-dragons to contend—while the flood-dragons contend, never let the little sheep go hungry and grow lean! Just look at you: guarded so tightly that thieves cannot steal a thing, keeping intact the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit.

Key Terms

  • Spirit Terrace (lingtai): The mind-heart.
  • True Master (zhuweng, zhurenweng): One’s original true nature; Original Spirit (yuanshen).
  • Yellow Dame, Infant and Fair Maiden, Three Families: True Intention serves as go-between to unite Lead and Mercury (Spirit and Breath), bringing the Three Families into one; see True Intention (zhenyi).
  • Before one’s parents were born / original face: Chan Buddhist expressions referring to one’s innate nature prior to the arising of body and mind.
  • Great Meaning of the coming from the West: The Chan expression “the Patriarch’s intention in coming from the West,” referring to the mind-to-mind Dharma transmitted by Bodhidharma.
  • Clay ox (niniu): A metaphor for true Breath; “the clay ox from the bottom of the sea charges straight upward” signifies the ascent of Breath from the lower origin.
  • Maṇi: The wish-fulfilling Maṇi jewel, a metaphor for the Elixir.
  • Furnace of Supreme Unity (Taiyi lu): A metaphor for the Cinnabar Field.
  • Caoxi: The seat of Huineng, Sixth Patriarch of Chan, standing for the path of sudden awakening; written 漕溪 in the original text.
  • Celestial Circuit phasing (zhoutian hou): The fire phasing (huohou) of the Celestial Circuit (zhoutian).

Putting It into Practice

  1. Recognize your original face as soon as you sit on the cushion. Set III, Second Watch: when you sit down, first contemplate who you were “before your parents were born”—this is a direct way to draw the mind back to its quietest ground.
  2. Free of entanglement, ever pure and still. The prerequisite for all inner work is a mind free from clinging and worry.
  3. Ponder it yourself and laugh to yourself. Realizations in cultivation cannot be put into words, nor need they be: “of such practice, do not lightly speak to casual listeners.”
  4. Guarding intact is itself the Celestial Circuit. At the close of Set IV: “guarded so tightly that thieves cannot steal a thing, keeping intact the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit”—when you guard strictly against leakage, the proper fire phasing is naturally present within.

Translator's Notes

  1. “The Yellow Dame guides and invites them into the bridal chamber,” “Kan and Li unite in marriage,” and “the husband and wife of Metal and Water join together” are all metaphors for the inner conjunction of Kan and Li, Lead and Mercury within one’s own body, consistent with the Discourse Pointing Out Delusions on the Ascent to Heaven, which states that “there are no forms of man and woman.”
  2. “Holding the precious moon to sleep beside the sun”: 拏 (ná) is a variant of 拿 (“to hold”). It symbolizes pairing the moon (Kan, Lead) with the sun (Li, Mercury).
  3. “Inner nature lies at heaven’s edge and existence at the bottom of the sea”: inner nature (xing) resides above (heaven’s edge) and life/existence (ming) resides below (bottom of the sea); see the Discourse on a Single Grain of Millet earlier in this book: “Inner nature is at heaven’s edge; life is at the bottom of the sea.”
  4. “Formless yet radiant, hard as solid iron”: tietuo (铁陀, an iron weight or lump) symbolizes indestructible firmness.
  5. “Already leaped clear of the Yunmeng marsh”: Yunmeng was a vast ancient marsh, used here as a metaphor for the realm of delusion.
  6. “The white crow comes to pay court”: symbolizes yang within yin, or the manifestation of Elixir light.
  7. “Nine-grade realm of the Unborn”: borrows from Pure Land Buddhist terms for the “nine grades of rebirth” and “forbearance of the unborn Dharma.”
  8. “Never let the little sheep go hungry and grow lean”: rendered as in the original text (饿饿瘦), meaning not to neglect gentle warm nourishment.
  9. “Guarded so tightly”: in the original text 隄防很, the character 很 stands for 狠 (strict, thorough); preserved verbatim in the classical quote.

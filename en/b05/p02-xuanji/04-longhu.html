II · Plain Talks on the Mysterious Mechanism

On Dragon, Tiger, Lead, and Mercury · Analysis of the Characters Wuzhen · Rhapsody on Knife and Ruler

Commentary on the Nine Emperors Elixir Classic (On Dragon, Tiger, Lead, and Mercury), Analysis of the Characters Wuzhen, and Rhapsody on Knife and Ruler — taming dragon and tiger, refining the eyes to collect the mind, and roaming the world with a knife and ruler

About This Chapter

This chapter collects three short pieces, each with a distinct voice:

  • Commentary on the Nine Emperors Elixir Classic: On Dragon, Tiger, Lead, and Mercury is Zhang Sanfeng’s commentary on a passage concerning the “extraction and addition of dragon, tiger, lead, and mercury” in the Nine Emperors Elixir Classic. Its tone reads like a lively storyteller: “Tiger, tiger, tiger! What care I though you open wide your jaws and bare your fangs?” and “Dragon, dragon, dragon! Though you be a creature of lust!” The dragon is my Mysterious Pass (spirit), the tiger is “the other’s” Mysterious Female (breath); the dragon is prone to lust, so I refuse to leak a single drop of water; the tiger is prone to devouring, so I refuse to leak a spark of True Fire. Taming the dragon and subduing the tiger so they meet like wind and clouds inside the Three Fields—that is the “Great Way of mingling.” The closing line is the most famous: “Merely cast mercury into lead, and you beget sons and daughters; do not cast lead into mercury—rather bring lead to mercury—and you become a buddha or an immortal.” Moving forward produces offspring; reversing produces an immortal.
  • Analysis of the Characters Wuzhen dissects the graphs wu (awakening) and zhen (reality): wu takes the “heart-mind” radical, so one must first cultivate the mind; zhen takes the “eye” radical, so “whoever wishes to refine the Dao must first refine the eyes”—the eyes gather breath and collect the mind, for “the pivot lies in the eyes.”
  • Rhapsody on Knife and Ruler is a brief prose-poem. Zhang Sanfeng always carried a knife (shears) and a ruler on his wanderings; when a guest wondered why, he composed this rhapsody: the knife splits primal chaos, the ruler measures Penglai; fire work is measured inch by inch, embryonic nurturing guarded moment by moment. It closes with a quatrain: “One knife, one ruler, roaming to heaven’s edge; homeless across the four seas, yet everywhere at home; patched monk’s robe mended into shreds of cloud, cradling the Great Cinnabar within my sleeve.”

Original Text and Translation

§1Commentary on the Nine Emperors Elixir Classic — On Dragon, Tiger, Lead, and Mercury
Original (Chinese)

道君论龙虎铅汞抽添，正要后世有根有缘者，从此下手。虽曰神仙还是神仙做，吾却偏曰：「凡人亦可做神仙，只怕不明金丹理，方入地狱为兽员。」

When the Lord of the Dao discoursed on the extraction and addition of dragon, tiger, lead, and mercury, it was precisely so that people of later ages with capacity and affinity might begin their work right here. Though people say, “It takes an immortal to become an immortal,” I insist on saying: “Ordinary mortals too can become immortals—the only fear is failing to understand the principle of the Golden Elixir, and thus falling into the hells to join the ranks of beasts.”

§2
Original (Chinese)

此龙属阳，自阳一失却是一阴；此虎属阴，自阴有宝却是一阳。龙即我之玄关也；虎即彼之玄牝也。龙性好淫，我却不泄一水，添一点土，偏要成一个瑶字，成为玉液至宝；虎性好吃，我却不泄真火，加一东字，偏要成个炼字，收为金液至宝。

This dragon belongs to yang; once yang is lost, it becomes a yin. This tiger belongs to yin; because yin holds a treasure, it becomes a yang. The dragon is my Mysterious Pass; the tiger is “the other’s” Mysterious Female. The dragon’s nature is fond of lust (yin), yet I refuse to leak a single drop of water and instead add a touch of earth, deliberately turning it into the character yao (“precious jade”) to become the supreme treasure of the Jade Liquid. The tiger’s nature is fond of devouring, yet I refuse to leak True Fire and instead add the component “east” (dong), deliberately forming the character lian (“refine”) to harvest it as the supreme treasure of the Golden Liquid.

§3
Original (Chinese)

虎虎虎，那怕你张口漏牙，把人亡魂丧胆，我却有伏虎手段，将你为空中色，色中空，用龙一戏，把你为龙虎风云会，不怕你不为我把你虎穴中虎子得来，入我三田之中。龙龙龙，纵任你是淫欲之物，我却有降龙手段，那怕你变化无穷，我用一哪吒金刚圈降住你，抽你筋做为一条养性接命的金带，时时系着，那怕你不去向太极真人前，请一点真一不二法门来，与我为混合之大道也。

Tiger, tiger, tiger! What care I though you open wide your jaws and bare your fangs, scaring people out of their wits? I have the skill to subdue tigers: turning you into “form within emptiness, emptiness within form,” letting the dragon play with you once, I bring you into the wind-and-cloud meeting of dragon and tiger—never fear you won’t fetch the tiger cub from your lair for me and deliver it into my Three Fields!

Dragon, dragon, dragon! Though you be a creature of lust, I have the skill to tame dragons: no matter how endlessly you shift shapes, I pin you down with Nezha’s diamond hoop, draw out your sinew to fashion a golden belt for nourishing inner nature and extending life, and keep it buckled round me at every moment—never fear you won’t go before the True Person of the Great Ultimate to beg a single drop of the non-dual dharma gate of True Oneness, uniting with me in the Great Way of mingling!

§4
Original (Chinese)

铅即两弦之铅，汞即我身天地之汞，有日月之光明，天地才成地天泰，不为天地否，人身自父母生来，原有一汞一铅男女交媾之理。故云「乾为父，坤为母，男女媾精，万物化醇。」易为先天之太极。人不明此一理，只把汞去投铅，生男生女，不把铅来投汞，成佛成仙。这个铁馒头打得破，何难为三丰中之三丰也！

Lead is the lead of the two crescent moons; mercury is the mercury of heaven and earth within my own body. Only with the radiance of sun and moon do heaven and earth form Earth-over-Heaven Peace (Tai) rather than Heaven-over-Earth Obstruction (Pi). Born from father and mother, the human body inherently contains the principle of one mercury and one lead—the union of male and female. Hence it is said: “Qian is the father, Kun is the mother; male and female mingle their essences, and the myriad things transform and ripen.” In the Changes, this is the Great Ultimate of Prior Heaven.

People do not grasp this single principle: they only cast mercury into lead, begetting sons and daughters, instead of bringing lead to mercury to become buddhas and immortals. If you can bite through this “iron bun,” what difficulty is there in becoming a Sanfeng among Sanfengs!

§5Analysis of the Characters Wuzhen
Original (Chinese)

噫！真何由悟哉？世人皆欲悟真，而悟之不真，非悟也；真之不悟，非真也。今即字义解明之：悟者，觉也，求也；真者，实也，正也；人所知之也。夫岂知悟真二字，书义亦深乎？悟从君心，先当以修心为本，太上云「吾从无量劫来，观心得道」是也。

Alas! By what means is Reality (zhen) awakened to (wu)? Everyone in the world wishes to awaken to reality, yet awakening that is not real is no awakening, and reality without awakening is not real. Let us explain it through the meaning of the characters themselves: wu means to awaken, to seek; zhen means real, upright—that much everyone knows. Yet who realizes that the graphic structure of the two characters wu and zhen also holds deep meaning? The character wu takes the “heart-mind” radical, so one must first make cultivating the mind the foundation—just as the Most High says, “From measureless kalpas past, I attained the Dao by contemplating the mind.”

§6
Original (Chinese)

真者何也？六书云「人受气以生，目最先，神之所聚，无非实也，故从目从七，七者化也；又从乚（音浩），乚者气之形也。」可知欲炼道者，须先炼目：炼目乃能聚气，炼目并可收心，所谓机在目者是也。识得此机便见真机，识得此机便知息机，识得此机便入妙机。悟如是，真如是也，请以告天下之悟真者。

And what of zhen (“reality”)? The Six Categories of Script say: “When a human being receives breath to be born, the eyes form first; they are where spirit gathers, wholly real. Therefore zhen is composed of ‘eye’ (mu) and ‘seven’ (qi), seven signifying transformation; and also of yin (乚, pronounced hao), which is the form of breath.”

From this we know that whoever wishes to refine the Dao must first refine the eyes: refining the eyes gathers breath, and refining the eyes also collects the mind—this is what is meant by “the pivot lies in the eyes.” Know this pivot and you see the true pivot; know this pivot and you understand the pivot of breath; know this pivot and you enter the wondrous pivot. Awakening is like this, and reality is like this—let me announce it to all under heaven who seek to awaken to reality.

§7Rhapsody on Knife and Ruler
Original (Chinese)

三丰先生，常携刀尺以遨游，空乎两大，浩乎十州，客有怪者，不知其由。先生乃为之赋曰：「是刀也能开混沌，斯尺也用絜蓬莱，故相随而不失，知造化之剪裁。尔其百点明星，双叉皎雪，绳墨从之，锋芒割若，分修短兮合宜，剪水雪兮快绝，期妙用之无方，岂微能之足述。

Master Sanfeng always carried a knife and a ruler as he roamed about, empty and free between heaven and earth, vast across the Ten Continents. A guest thought it strange and did not know the reason, so the Master composed this rhapsody for him:

“This knife can split primal chaos; this ruler serves to measure Penglai. Therefore they accompany me and are never lost, knowing the tailoring of creation itself. Behold the hundred flecks of bright stars upon the blade, the twin prongs gleaming white as snow! Chalkline and ink follow them, the keen edge slices clean—dividing long and short in fitting proportion, snipping water and snow with crisp precision. Its wondrous application knows no fixed direction; how could minor craft suffice to describe it?

§8
Original (Chinese)

至如裁妙理，削尘嚣，量度数，别昏朝，火功寸寸，风信刁刁，胎养刻刻，羽衣飘飘，度龙门之万仞，如虎剑之两条，梦益州而不愿，与方丈而同超。刀兮刀兮，妙之又妙，尺兮尺兮，要所必要。匪欧冶之能镕，匪公输之能造，与我偕行，任他嘲笑，将求织女之云绡，缝出仙翁之衣帽。」

“As for tailoring wondrous principle, paring away worldly clamor, measuring degrees and cycles, distinguishing dusk from dawn: fire work measured inch by inch, wind-tidings rustling soft, embryonic nurturing guarded moment by moment, feathered robes fluttering free! Crossing the ten-thousand-fathom Dragon Gate like a pair of tiger-swords, declining even a dream of governing Yizhou, transcending together with Mount Fangzhang. O knife, O knife, wondrous beyond wonder! O ruler, O ruler, essential among essentials! No Ou Yezi could smelt it, no Gongshu Ban could craft it. Walking side by side with me, let others mock as they will—I shall ask the Weaver Girl for cloud-silk to stitch an immortal patriarch’s robe and cap.”

§9
Original (Chinese)

歌曰：

一刀一尺遍天涯，四海无家却有家；破衲补成云片片，袖中笼住大丹砂。

And he sang:

One knife, one ruler, roaming to heaven’s edge; Homeless across the four seas, yet everywhere at home; My tattered monk’s robe mended into shreds of white cloud, Cradling the Great Cinnabar within my sleeve.

Key Terms

  • Dragon and Tiger: Here the dragon is “my Mysterious Pass” (spirit, mercury) and the tiger is “the other’s Mysterious Female” (breath, lead). The dragon is prone to lust (spirit easily strays outward) and the tiger is prone to devouring (breath easily dissipates); taming the dragon and subduing the tiger means collecting spirit and gathering breath; see Dragon and Tiger.
  • Bringing lead to mercury vs. casting mercury into lead: Casting mercury into lead is forward motion—spirit leaking away with breath to beget children; bringing lead to mercury is reverse motion—breath returning to spirit to form the elixir; see reversal.
  • Earth-over-Heaven Peace (Tai) / Heaven-over-Earth Obstruction (Pi): Two hexagrams of the Book of Changes. Earth above and Heaven below is Peace (communion); Heaven above and Earth below is Obstruction (blockage). Alchemists use them to symbolize the conjunction or separation of water and fire, Kan and Li.
  • Three Fields (santian): Upper, middle, and lower Cinnabar Fields.
  • The pivot lies in the eyes (ji zai mu): A frequent alchemical maxim; refining the eyes means reverting the light, collecting the mind, and gathering breath; see Reverting the Light.
  • Fire work (huogong), wind-tidings (fengxin), embryonic nurturing (taiyang): All alchemical terms—fire phasing, the wind of breathing, and the ten months of nurturing the embryo.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Collecting spirit and gathering breath is taming the dragon and subduing the tiger. Not letting thoughts gallop outward (the dragon) and not letting essence and breath dissipate (the tiger) form the bedrock of all practice.
  2. Cultivate the mind first. The character wu (“awakening”) takes the “heart-mind” radical—whatever method you study, cultivating the mind is the root.
  3. Start with the eyes. “Whoever wishes to refine the Dao must first refine the eyes”: in daily life look less and stare less at screens; during quiet sitting lower the curtains of the eyes and gaze inward—this is the simplest method to collect the mind.
  4. Measure the work inch by inch. “Fire work inch by inch, embryonic nurturing moment by moment”—do not rush, do not chase marvels, accumulate steadily day by day.

Translator's Notes

  1. Nine Emperors Elixir Classic (Jiuhuang danjing): Titled in the original “Commentary on the Nine Emperors Elixir Classic”; the exact text referenced is uncertain, and this piece comments on its passage concerning the extraction and addition of dragon, tiger, lead, and mercury.
  2. “Join the ranks of beasts” (wei shouyuan): So printed in the original, meaning to become one of the beasts.
  3. “Add a touch of earth, deliberately turning it into the character yao” / “add the component ‘east,’ deliberately forming the character lian”: Playful character dissections whose exact mechanics the original leaves unstated. The gist is: strip the water radical from yin (淫, “lust,” i.e. “leak not a drop of water”) and replace it with earth/jade elements to yield yao (瑶, “jade,” Jade Liquid); combine “fire” with “east” (東, visually close to the right-hand component 柬 of traditional 煉) to yield lian (炼, “refine,” Golden Liquid). Take the spirit of the wordplay.
  4. “The tiger is ‘the other’s’ Mysterious Female”: Following the pure-stillness method, “the other” (bi) is understood as breath within one’s own body (Kan, life), paired with “I” (Li, spirit), not another person. Pointing Out Delusions on the Ascent to Heaven in this same volume says “the other side within my own body” (woshen bijia), explicitly locating it within one’s own person.
  5. “Born from father and mother, the human body inherently contains the principle of one mercury and one lead—the union of male and female”: This explains that because the human body comes from parents, it inherently contains yin and yang; the following line (“merely cast mercury into lead, begetting sons and daughters”) describes ordinary procreation (forward flow), not sexual cultivation.
  6. “Qian is the father, Kun is the mother” is from Book of Changes, “Discussion of the Trigrams” (Shuogua); “male and female mingle their essences, and the myriad things transform and ripen” is from the “Appended Remarks” (Xici xia), where standard editions read huasheng (“transform and are born”), as also quoted in this book’s Treatise on the Great Way.
  7. “Iron bun” (tie mantou): A Chan idiom for a hard barrier difficult to break through.
  8. “Wu takes the ruler’s heart” (wu cong junxin): So printed in the original. The character 悟 combines “heart” (忄) and “I” (吾), so it should read “wu takes my heart” (wu cong wuxin); jun (君) is likely a misprint for wu (吾), retained in the original quote.
  9. “Composed of ‘eye’ and ‘seven,’ seven signifying transformation; and also of 乚 (pronounced hao)”: In the Shuowen jiezi, 真 combines 匕 (“change”), 目 (“eye”), 乚, and 八; “seven” (七) is doubtless a corruption of 匕, and 乚 is glossed in Shuowen as pronounced yin rather than hao; both are preserved as printed.
  10. Rhapsody on Knife and Ruler: Phrases such as “snipping water and snow” and “twin prongs gleaming white as snow” describe a pair of shears; the “knife” Zhang Sanfeng carried was apparently shears/scissors.
  11. “Declining even a dream of governing Yizhou”: Alludes to the Jin-dynasty general Wang Jun, who dreamed of three knives (suggesting the character 州) and became Governor of Yizhou—meaning no envy for worldly office.
  12. “Ou Yezi”: Famous swordsmith of the Spring and Autumn period; “Gongshu”: Gongshu Ban, i.e., Lu Ban, patron of carpenters and craftsmen.

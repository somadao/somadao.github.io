II · Plain Talks on the Mysterious Mechanism

Commentary on Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Tablet

Commentary on Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Tablet — twenty lines of one hundred characters, tracing the full path from nourishing breath and forgetting speech to Five Breaths returning to the Origin and fathoming creation

About This Chapter

The Hundred-Character Tablet is traditionally attributed to Lü Dongbin: five characters per line, twenty lines totaling exactly one hundred characters, making it one of the most widely circulated short classics in inner alchemy. Zhang Sanfeng wrote a line-by-line commentary on it—this Commentary on Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Tablet. The complete poem reads:

Nourish breath, forget speech and guard; Subdue the mind, acting without acting. In movement and stillness know the Ancestor; With nothing to do, whom else would you seek?

True Constancy must respond to things; Responding to things, do not lose your way. Not losing your way, inner nature abides of itself; When nature abides, breath returns of itself.

When breath returns, the elixir congeals of itself; Within the gourd, pair Kan and Li. Yin and yang engender reversal and return; Universal transformation—a single clap of thunder.

White clouds pay court at the crown; Sweet Dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru. Drinking oneself of the wine of long life— Such carefree ease, who can know it?

Sitting and listening to the stringless tune, Clearly fathoming the pivot of creation. All told, these twenty lines Are truly a ladder ascending to Heaven.

Zhang Sanfeng’s commentary arranges these hundred characters into a complete roadmap:

  1. Entry (first four lines): Forget speech and guard the One to nourish breath; lower the curtains of the eyes and close the mouth to subdue the mind; recognize the “Ancestor” (below the heart and above the kidneys, where thoughts arise), let mind and breath rely on each other, and regulate breathing down to the breath of no-breath.
  2. Responding to things (next four lines): The Way of True Constancy does not flee worldly affairs, but “responds when things come and retains nothing once they pass”; responding without delusion, inner nature abides of itself and breath returns of itself.
  3. Forming the elixir (next four lines): Breath returns so the two breaths unite in the central palace, pairing Kan and Li; attaining utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, the single yang returns, and True Breath pierces the Caudal Funnel, rises to the Muddy Pellet, and turns into Sweet Dew descending into the Yellow Court.
  4. Warm nurturing and attainment (final eight lines): Sweet Dew descends the Twelve-Storied Tower as the “wine of long life”; when the work matures, the Five Breaths return to the Origin, the Three Flowers gather at the Summit, and wisdom arises spontaneously.

At the close of the text, Li Hanxu adds a postscript: Patriarch Lü transmitted this tablet out of a vast vow to save humanity, and Patriarch Zhang annotated it out of the same great vow—“Patriarch Zhang’s heart is Patriarch Lü’s heart.”

Safety Note: Phenomena mentioned in the commentary—such as “both kidneys like boiling soup, the bladder like scorching fire” or “inside the abdomen like a fierce wind roaring”—are natural signs occurring only when the work matures of itself; never guide breath with intention to chase or imitate them. The commentary’s own oral key states it clearly: “Fixating with intention errs at every turn; doing nothing falls into dead emptiness”—neither force with intention nor sink into blank torpor. For beginners, putting the opening lines (“forget speech, subdue the mind, mind and breath relying on each other, respond to things without delusion”) into practice is already plenty.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

养气忘言守，

凡修行者，先须养气，养气之法，在乎忘言守一，忘言则气不散，守一则神不出，诀曰：「缄舌静，抱神定。」

降心为不为；

凡人之心，动荡不已，修行人心欲入静，贵乎制伏两眼，眼者心之门户，须要垂帘塞兑，一切事体，以心为剑，想世事无益于我，火烈顿除，莫去贪着，诀云：「以眼视鼻，以鼻视脐，上下相顾，心息相依。」着意玄关，便可降伏思虑。

“Nourish breath, forget speech and guard,” — Whoever cultivates the Way must first nourish breath. The method of nourishing breath lies in forgetting speech and guarding the One: forget speech and breath does not scatter; guard the One and spirit does not stray outward. The oral formula says: “Seal the tongue in stillness; embrace the spirit in steadiness.”

“Subdue the mind, acting without acting;” — The ordinary person’s mind tosses and churns without cease. When a practitioner wishes the mind to enter stillness, the key is mastering the two eyes. The eyes are the gateway of the mind; one must lower the curtains of the eyes and close the mouth (dui). In all affairs, take the mind as a sword: reflect that worldly concerns bring no real benefit to you, and the fierce blaze of heart-fire vanishes at once—do not crave or cling. The oral formula says: “With the eyes look to the nose, with the nose look to the navel; above and below watch over each other, mind and breath relying on each other.” Resting awareness gently at the Mysterious Pass, discursive thoughts are subdued.

§2
Original (Chinese)

动静知宗祖，

动静者，一阴一阳也。宗祖者，生身之处也。修行人当知，父母未生之前，即玄牝也。一身上下乾坤八卦五行四象聚会之处，乃天地未判之先，一点灵光而成，即太极也。心之下，肾之上，仿佛之内念头无息，所起之处即是宗祖，所谓动静者，调和真气，安理真元也。盖呼接天根，吸接地根，即阖户之谓坤，辟户之谓乾，呼则龙吟云起，吸则虎啸风生，一阖一辟，一动一静，贵乎心意不动，任其真息往来，绵绵若存，调息至无息之息，打成一片，斯神可凝，丹可就矣。

无事更寻谁。

若能养气忘言守，降伏身心，神归炁穴，意注规中，混融一炁，如鸡抱卵，如龙养珠，念兹在兹，须臾不离，日久工深，自然现出黍米之珠，光耀如日，默化元神，灵明莫测即此是也。

“In movement and stillness know the Ancestor,” — Movement and stillness are one yin and one yang; the Ancestor is the place where the body is born. The practitioner should know that before father and mother gave us birth, there is the Mysterious Female—the meeting place of Qian and Kun, the Eight Trigrams, the Five Phases, and the Four Emblems throughout the body above and below, formed from a single spark of numinous light before heaven and earth were divided, which is the Great Ultimate. Below the heart and above the kidneys, in that dim space where thoughts cease and from which thoughts arise—that is the Ancestor.

What is called “movement and stillness” means harmonizing True Breath and ordering the True Origin. Exhaling connects to the root of heaven; inhaling connects to the root of earth—that is, “closing the gate is called Kun, opening the gate is called Qian.” Exhaling, the dragon chants and clouds rise; inhaling, the tiger roars and wind springs up. Through one closing and one opening, one movement and one stillness, what matters most is that mind and intention remain unmoved, allowing True Breath to come and go on its own, unbroken as though barely there, regulating the breath until it reaches the “breath of no-breath” and merges into a single piece. Then spirit can congeal and the elixir can be accomplished.

“With nothing to do, whom else would you seek?” — If you can nourish breath, forget speech, guard the One, subdue body and mind, return spirit to the Cavity of Breath, and rest intention in the Center of the Compass, fusing into One Breath—like a hen brooding her egg, like a dragon tending its pearl—mindful of this alone, never leaving it for an instant, then as days pass and the work deepens, the pearl of millet grain appears spontaneously, radiant as the sun, silently transforming the Original Spirit, numinous and bright beyond measure. This is what the line means.

§3
Original (Chinese)

真常须应物，应物要不迷；

此道乃真常之道，以应事易于昏迷，故接物不可迷于尘事，若不应接则空寂虚无，须要来则应之，事去不留，光明正大乃是不迷，真性清静，元神凝结，诀曰：「着意头头错，无为又落空。」

“True Constancy must respond to things; responding to things, do not lose your way;” — This Way is the Way of True Constancy. Because responding to affairs easily makes one dazed and entangled, one must not lose oneself in dusty matters when engaging with things; yet refusing to engage at all falls into barren voidness. What is needed is to respond when things come and keep nothing once they pass—bright, upright, and open, that is “not losing your way.” Then true nature stays clear and still, and the Original Spirit congeals. The oral formula says: “Fixating with intention errs at every turn; doing nothing falls into dead emptiness.”

§4
Original (Chinese)

不迷性自住，性住气自回。

凡人性烈如火，喜怒哀乐爱恶欲憎，变态无常，但有触动便生妄想，难以静性，必要有真惩忿则火降，真寡欲则水升，身不动名曰炼精，炼精则虎啸，元神凝固，心不动名曰炼气，炼气则龙吟，元气存守，意不动名曰炼神，炼神则二炁交，三元混，元炁自回矣。三元者，精炁神也；二炁者，阴阳也。修行人应物不迷，则元神自归，本性自住矣。性住则身中先天之炁自回，复命归根，有何难哉！诀曰：「回光返照，一心中存，内想不出，外想不入。」

“Not losing your way, inner nature abides of itself; when nature abides, breath returns of itself.” — An ordinary person’s temperament blazes like fire; joy, anger, sorrow, pleasure, love, dislike, craving, and hatred shift ceaselessly, and at the slightest stimulus delusive thoughts spring up, making it hard to quiet one’s nature. One must truly curb anger so heart-fire descends, and truly lessen desire so kidney-water rises.

When the body does not stir, that is refining essence; refining essence makes the “tiger roar” and Original Spirit congeals firmly. When the mind does not stir, that is refining breath; refining breath makes the “dragon chant” and Original Breath stays preserved. When intention does not stir, that is refining spirit; refining spirit causes the two breaths to unite and the three origins to merge, and Original Breath returns of itself. The three origins are essence, breath, and spirit; the two breaths are yin and yang. When the practitioner responds to things without delusion, Original Spirit returns of itself and original nature abides of itself. Once nature abides, the Prior Heaven breath within the body returns spontaneously—what difficulty is there in restoring life and returning to the root? The oral formula says: “Revert the light to illuminate within, keep one mind centered inside; inner thoughts do not go out, outer thoughts do not come in.”

§5
Original (Chinese)

气回丹自结，壶中配坎离；

修行人，性不迷尘事则炁自回，将见二炁升降于中宫，阴阳配合于丹鼎，忽觉肾中一缕热炁上冲心府，情来归性，如夫妇配合，如痴如醉，二炁絪缊，结成丹质，而炁穴中水火相交，循环不已，则神驭炁、炁留形，不必杂术自长生，诀曰：「耳目口三宝，闭塞勿发通；真人潜深渊，浮游守规中。」直至丹田气满，结成刀圭也。

“When breath returns, the elixir congeals of itself; within the gourd, pair Kan and Li;” — When the practitioner’s nature is unconfused by worldly affairs, breath returns of itself. Soon you will witness the two breaths rising and descending in the central palace, yin and yang uniting in the elixir tripod. Suddenly you feel a warm stream of breath rising from the kidneys up into the palace of the heart; feelings return to inner nature, like husband and wife uniting, entranced as though intoxicated. The two breaths mingle in misty fusion and condense into the substance of the elixir; within the Cavity of Breath water and fire conjoin in an unbroken cycle, so spirit governs breath and breath preserves the bodily form—needing no miscellaneous techniques, one naturally achieves long life. The oral formula says: “Ears, eyes, and mouth—the three treasures—seal them shut and do not let them leak; the True Person dives into the deep abyss, floating freely while guarding the Center of the Compass.” Continue until the Cinnabar Field is full of breath and forms the Knife-Tip Measure.

§6
Original (Chinese)

阴阳生反复，普化一声雷。

功夫到此，神不外驰，炁不外泄，神归炁穴，坎离已交，愈加猛烈精进，致虚之极，守静之笃，身静于杳冥之中，心澄于无何有之乡，则真息自住，百脉自停，日月停景，璇玑不行，太极静而生动，阳产于西南之坤，（坤即腹也，又名曲江。）忽然一点灵光，如黍米之大，赫然光透，即药生消息也。两肾如汤煎，膀胱如火炙，腹中如烈风之吼，腹内如震雷之声，即复卦天根现也。天根既现，即固心王以神助之，则其炁如火，逼金上行穿过尾闾，轻轻运，默默举，一团和气，如雷之震，上升泥丸，周身涌跃，即天风姤卦也。由月窟至印堂，眉中露出元光，即太极动而生阴，化成神水甘露，内有黍米之珠，落在黄庭之中，点我离中灵汞，结成圣相之体，行周天火候一度，烹之炼之，丹自结矣。

“Yin and yang engender reversal and return; universal transformation—a single clap of thunder.” — When the work reaches this point, spirit no longer races outward and breath no longer leaks out; spirit has returned to the Cavity of Breath and Kan and Li have already conjoined. Press forward all the more diligently, “attaining utmost emptiness and holding fast to stillness”: the body rests still in the dim and obscure, the mind grows limpid in the land of nothing-whatever. True Breath then pauses of itself, the hundred pulses cease of themselves, sun and moon halt their radiance, and the celestial pivot stops turning.

At the extreme of stillness, the Great Ultimate engenders movement, and yang is born in the southwest quarter of Kun (original note: Kun is the abdomen, also called the Winding River). Suddenly a single point of numinous light, the size of a millet grain, shines brilliantly through—this is the tiding that the medicine has been born. Both kidneys feel like boiling soup, the bladder like scorching fire; inside the abdomen a fierce wind roars and thunder rumbles—this is the Return (Fu) hexagram, the appearance of the Root of Heaven. Once the Root of Heaven appears, steady the mind-king and assist it with spirit: the breath, like fire, drives the metal upward through the Caudal Funnel Pass, moved gently and lifted silently, a single ball of harmonious breath vibrating like thunder as it ascends to the Muddy Pellet, surging joyfully through the whole frame—this is the Heaven-over-Wind Encounter (Gou) hexagram.

Passing from the Moon’s Cavern to the Hall of Seal between the brows, primal light shines forth: this is the Great Ultimate generating yin at the peak of movement, transforming into numinous water and Sweet Dew holding the millet-grain pearl within. It drops into the Yellow Court, transmuting the numinous mercury within my Li trigram to form the body of the sage image. Conduct one round of Celestial Circuit fire phasing, simmering and refining it, and the elixir congeals of itself.

§7
Original (Chinese)

白云朝顶上，甘露洒须弥；

到此地位，药即得矣。二炁结刀圭，关窍开通，火降水升，一炁周流，从太极中动天根过玄谷关，升二十四椎骨节至天谷关，月窟阴生，香甜美味，降下重楼，无休无息，名曰甘露洒须弥，诀曰：「甘露满口，以目送之，以意迎之，送下丹釜，凝结元炁以养之。」

“White clouds pay court at the crown; Sweet Dew sprinkles Mount Sumeru;” — Having reached this station, the medicine is obtained. The two breaths condense into the Knife-Tip Measure, passes and cavities open, fire descends and water rises, and One Breath circulates around the body: from the Great Ultimate the Root of Heaven stirs, passes the Mysterious Valley Pass, climbs the twenty-four vertebrae to the Celestial Valley Pass; in the Moon’s Cavern yin is born, fragrant, sweet, and delicious, descending the Twelve-Storied Tower without cease—this is called “Sweet Dew sprinkling Mount Sumeru.” The oral formula says: “When Sweet Dew fills the mouth, escort it with the eyes and welcome it with intention, sending it down into the elixir cauldron, congealing Original Breath to nourish it.”

§8
Original (Chinese)

自饮长生酒，逍遥谁得知。

养炁到此，骨节已开，神水不住，上下周流往来不息，时时吞咽谓之长生酒，诀曰：「流珠灌养灵根性，修行之人知不知。」

“Drinking oneself of the wine of long life—such carefree ease, who can know it?” — When breath-nurturing reaches this stage, the bone joints have already opened, numinous water flows unceasingly, circulating up and down without end; swallowing it continually is called “the wine of long life.” The oral formula says: “The flowing pearl irrigates and nourishes the numinous root of nature—do cultivators know this or not?”

§9
Original (Chinese)

坐听无弦曲，明通造化机；

功夫到此，耳听仙乐之音，又有钟鼓之韵，五气朝元，三花聚顶，如晚鸦来栖之状，心田开朗，智慧自生，明通三教经书，默悟前生根本，预知未来休咎，目视万里，大地山河如在掌中，已得六通之妙，此乃实有之也。吾行实到此际，若有虚言，以误后学，天必诛之，遇之不行，罪遭天谴，非与师遇，此事难知。

“Sitting and listening to the stringless tune, clearly fathoming the pivot of creation;” — When the work reaches this point, the ears hear strains of immortal music along with the resonance of bells and drums; the Five Breaths return to the Origin and the Three Flowers gather at the Summit, like evening crows flying home to roost (all stray thoughts settling into one place). The field of the heart opens bright and clear, wisdom arises spontaneously; one sees clearly through the scriptures of all Three Teachings, silently comprehends the root of past lives, foreknows future fortune and misfortune, sees ten thousand miles, holds the mountains and rivers of the great earth as though in the palm of one’s hand, and attains the marvel of the six spiritual penetrations. All of this truly exists: my own practice actually reached this point—if I speak a single false word to mislead later students, may Heaven strike me down! Yet to encounter it and fail to practice incurs Heaven’s rebuke; without meeting a master, this matter is hard to know.

§10
Original (Chinese)

都来二十句，端的上天梯。

自养炁忘言，至此二十句，皆是吕祖真正口诀工夫，无半点虚伪，乃修行上天之阶梯，得悟此诀与注者，可急行之，勿妄漏泄，勿示匪人，以遭天谴，珍重奉行，克登天阙。

“All told, these twenty lines are truly a ladder ascending to Heaven.” — From “nourish breath, forget speech” down to this line, all twenty lines are Patriarch Lü’s authentic oral formulas and practical work, without the slightest falsity—truly a ladder by which cultivation ascends to Heaven. Whoever grasps these formulas and commentary should lose no time in practicing them; do not divulge them lightly or show them to unworthy people lest you incur Heaven’s rebuke. Treasure and carry them out faithfully, and you will ascend to the Celestial Palace.

Original Postscript
Original (Chinese)

吕祖抱度人洪愿而传此百字碑，张祖抱度人大愿而注此百字碑，张祖之心即吕祖之心也。故曰纯阳、三丰乃神仙中耳目。——西月跋

(Postscript by Li Hanxu) Patriarch Lü transmitted this Hundred-Character Tablet out of a vast vow to deliver humanity, and Patriarch Zhang annotated this Hundred-Character Tablet out of a great vow to deliver humanity; Patriarch Zhang’s heart is Patriarch Lü’s heart. Therefore it is said that Pure Yang (Lü Dongbin) and Sanfeng are the ears and eyes among the immortals.

Key Terms

  • Forgetting speech and guarding the One (wangyan shouyi): Closing the mouth in silence and keeping the mind anchored in oneness—the entry method for nourishing breath.
  • Lowering the curtains and closing the mouth (chuilian saidui): Half-closing the eyes like lowered curtains and closing the mouth (dui denotes the mouth); see lowering the curtains of the eyes.
  • Ancestor: Explained here as “below the heart and above the kidneys, in that dim space where thoughts cease and from which thoughts arise”—that is, the Mysterious Female, the Mysterious Pass.
  • Root of Heaven (tiangen) and Moon’s Cavern (yueku): From Shao Yong’s poem “Idly coming and going between the Root of Heaven and the Moon’s Cavern.” Here the Return (Fu) hexagram (one yang born) is the Root of Heaven, and the Encounter (Gou) hexagram (one yin born) is the Moon’s Cavern.
  • Three Origins (sanyuan) and Two Breaths (erqi): The Three Origins are essence, breath, and spirit; the Two Breaths are yin and yang.
  • Refining essence, refining breath, refining spirit: Defined here as “body not stirring is refining essence; mind not stirring is refining breath; intention not stirring is refining spirit.”
  • Mysterious Valley Pass (Xuangu guan) and Celestial Valley Pass (Tiangu guan): Here True Breath ascends from the Mysterious Valley Pass up the twenty-four vertebrae to the Celestial Valley Pass; Celestial Valley is the upper Cinnabar Field (Muddy Pellet); see Celestial Valley.
  • Mount Sumeru (Xumi): The cosmic mountain of Buddhism, borrowed here to refer to the crown of the head.
  • Five Breaths return to the Origin / Three Flowers gather at the Summit: The breaths of the five viscera returning to the central origin, and essence, breath, and spirit gathering at the crown—marks of mature cultivation.
  • Six spiritual penetrations (liutong): The six Buddhist supernatural faculties—divine eye, divine ear, knowledge of others’ minds, recollection of past lives, psychic travel, and exhaustion of outflows.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Speak less and guard the One. “Forget speech and breath does not scatter; guard the One and spirit does not stray outward”—cutting back on needless talk in daily life is the simplest way to nourish breath.
  2. Mind and breath relying on each other. “With the eyes look to the nose, with the nose look to the navel; above and below watch over each other, mind and breath relying on each other”—this is the single most practical line in the chapter for beginners.
  3. Respond without losing yourself; let matters go once passed. Cultivation does not mean fleeing daily life: deal conscientiously with what arrives, and do not hold onto it once it is over.
  4. Curb anger and lessen desire. “Truly curb anger so heart-fire descends; truly lessen desire so kidney-water rises”—losing one’s temper less and curbing craving are more fundamental than any technique.
  5. Neither force with intention nor sink into blankness. “Fixating with intention errs at every turn; doing nothing falls into dead emptiness”—in quiet sitting, neither push breath around by force nor drift into dull stupor.
  6. Do not chase phenomena. Light, thunder, heat, and celestial music appear naturally if and when the stage arrives; do not look for them beforehand, and do not cling to them if they occur.

Translator's Notes

  1. Hundred-Character Tablet: Traditionally attributed to Lü Dongbin (Master Pure Yang), already circulating by the Yuan dynasty; Zhang Sanfeng’s commentary is preserved in Volume Three, Plain Talks on the Mysterious Mechanism, of the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng.
  2. “Seal the tongue in stillness; embrace the spirit in steadiness”: Jian (缄) means to seal shut.
  3. “Lower the curtains and close the mouth”: Dui means the mouth, echoing Daodejing, Chapter 52: “Close the apertures, shut the gates.”
  4. “Closing the gate is called Kun, opening the gate is called Qian”: From Book of Changes, “Appended Remarks, Part One” (Xici shang): “Therefore closing the gate is called Kun, opening the gate is called Qian; one closing and one opening is called change.”
  5. “Attaining utmost emptiness and holding fast to stillness”: Adapted from Daodejing, Chapter 16.
  6. “The mind grows limpid in the land of nothing-whatever”: “Land of nothing-whatever” (wuheyou zhi xiang) comes from Zhuangzi, “Free and Easy Wandering,” denoting a boundless expanse of emptiness.
  7. “Root of Heaven” and “Moon’s Cavern”: From Shao Yong’s Song of Observing Things (Guanwu yin): “When Qian meets Xun, behold the Moon’s Cavern; where Earth meets Thunder, see the Root of Heaven; idly coming and going between Root of Heaven and Moon’s Cavern, all thirty-six palaces are spring.”
  8. “Ears, eyes, and mouth—the three treasures—seal them shut and do not let them leak; the True Person dives into the deep abyss, floating freely while guarding the Center of the Compass”: Quoted from the Zhouyi Cantongqi (Seal of the Unity of the Three).
  9. “Mingle in misty fusion” (yinyun, 絪缊): Variant of 氤氲, describing the dense intermingling of breaths.
  10. “Like evening crows flying home to roost”: A vivid image of myriad thoughts settling quietly into one place.
  11. “Do not show them to unworthy people” (feiren): So in the original, meaning people of crooked character.
  12. Signed at the end “Postscript by Xiyue”: Xiyue is Li Xiyue (Li Hanxu), the re-editor of this collection; rendered here as “Original Postscript.”

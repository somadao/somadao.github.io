II · Plain Talks on the Mysterious Mechanism

Pointing Out Delusions on the Ascent to Heaven

Pointing Out Delusions on the Ascent to Heaven — using women as vessels has no basis whatsoever; Zhang Sanfeng recounts failing in chamber practice, encountering yin demons, and returning to refine himself

About This Chapter

Pointing Out Delusions on the Ascent to Heaven is the frankest and most important piece in Plain Talks on the Mysterious Mechanism. It stands out for two reasons:

  1. An explicit refutation of “female vessels” (nüding). Zhang Sanfeng states that certain “blind people of shallow root,” seeing the ancient sages speak of outer yin and yang, outer furnace and tripod, and outer medicine, “become deluded into taking women as vessels,” and even infer from “Xuanyuan casting nine tripods” that nine partners are required—“all the more absurd to the extreme”; “as for the matter of using female vessels, there is not a shred of truth in it!” Near the end he repeats: heaven above and earth below, Qian and Kun, Kan and Li, male and female, inner and outer furnace and tripod “are all metaphors for the inner and outer yin and yang of my own single body; there is no literal form of man and woman at all.” Harvesting a woman’s essence for one’s own gain—“how does this differ from the murderers of the world?” These lines are the most reliable anchor for reading all of Zhang Sanfeng’s alchemical works.
  2. The author’s own account of a failure. Zhang Sanfeng relates that early on, having already obtained the oral formulas, medicine, and fire phasing, he entered the chamber to undertake the great work without having refined himself amid worldly dust, “before my breath-channels were deeply settled, and before my foundation had attained the Three Completions.” Disturbed by outer sights and sounds, his Original Spirit scattered and he fell prey to yin demons: daytime was bearable, but at night “whether my eyes were open or shut, I saw ghosts and spirits coming and going,” and he nearly lost his life. He then “temporarily set aside his former work,” obeyed his master’s instruction, and returned to the marketplace to refine himself and accumulate lead before finally succeeding.

At the end of the essay, Li Hanxu adds a note explaining that this piece is actually the general introduction to the “Daoist ballads” (daoqing) in the second part of Volume Four, Essentials of the Mystery—“Pointing Out Delusions on the Ascent to Heaven” being the collective title of those songs.

Safety Note: The lesson of Zhang Sanfeng’s own experience is stark: pursuing advanced practices before the foundation is solid leads to serious trouble. If visual or auditory hallucinations, terror, insomnia, or loss of bodily control appear during quiet sitting, stop immediately, return to ordinary daily life, and seek medical care if needed. Never attempt prolonged, high-intensity solitary retreats on your own.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

道也者，生天地、育万物，放之则包罗虚空，敛之则退藏于密，两仪日月五行，都是道中之造化耳。物物各具一太极——即道也；人人心上有先天——亦道也。五行顺而生人生物，五行逆而成仙成佛。故云「五行顺则法界火坑，五行逆则大地七宝。」这五行之精，秘于四大形山不内不外之密处，只是百姓日用而不知耳。民可使由之——顺行也；不可使知之——逆行也。夫鱼在水中不知其为水也；人居气内不知其为气也。此譬喻果能潜心究竟，回光返照，明心见性，果证仙佛，夫复何难哉！

The Dao generates heaven and earth and nurtures the myriad things; released, it encompasses the entire void; gathered in, it withdraws into secrecy. The Two Modes, sun and moon, and Five Phases are all nothing but creative workings within the Dao. Every single thing possesses its own Great Ultimate—that is the Dao; every human heart holds Prior Heaven—that too is the Dao. When the Five Phases move forward (downstream), they produce human beings and creatures; when the Five Phases move in reverse, they produce immortals and buddhas. Hence it is said: “When the Five Phases move forward, the dharma realm is a pit of fire; when the Five Phases move in reverse, the great earth becomes the seven jewels.”

The essence of these Five Phases lies hidden in the secret place—neither inside nor outside—of the four-element mountain of the body, yet ordinary folk use it every day without knowing it. “The people may be made to follow it”—that is forward motion; “they cannot be made to know it”—that is reverse motion. A fish lives in water without knowing it is water; a human being dwells in breath without knowing it is breath. If you can quietly fathom this metaphor to the bottom, revert the light to illuminate within, clarify the mind and see your inner nature, what difficulty remains in realizing immortalhood or buddhahood?

§2
Original (Chinese)

今人学道，个个自卖聪明，自夸伶俐，自称会家，终无了悟。又有一等小根盲人，见先圣所言外阴阳、外炉鼎、外药物，执迷女子为鼎器，则又可哀已也。某见酷好炉火者，百无一成，又以轩辕铸九鼎而成道，以为必用鼎器九人，更谬之甚矣。尝见有进过五七鼎亦无成就者，且人念头一动，先天淳朴即散，先天既丧，后天虽存，究何益于身心，不过聊存其四大而已。这样下愚，岂知天不言而四时行、百物生之妙哉！

People studying the Dao today all peddle their own cleverness, boast of their sharpness, and style themselves experts, yet never reach true awakening. Worse still is a class of blind people of shallow root who, seeing the ancient sages speak of outer yin and yang, outer furnace and tripod, and outer medicine, become deluded into taking women as vessels—that is truly pitiful.

I have seen those obsessed with furnace-and-fire external alchemy: not one in a hundred succeeds. Others, hearing that Xuanyuan cast nine tripods and attained the Dao, suppose one must use nine human vessels—absurdity carried to the extreme! I have seen men go through five or seven such “vessels” and still accomplish nothing. Moreover, the very instant a lustful thought stirs, Prior Heaven’s unspoiled simplicity scatters; once Prior Heaven is lost, even if Posterior Heaven remains, what real benefit is it to body and mind? It merely props up the four-element frame a little longer. How could such benighted fools fathom the wonder of “Heaven says nothing, yet the four seasons proceed and the hundred things are born”?

§3
Original (Chinese)

夫人身造化同乎天地，但不知天何得一以清，地何得一以宁，且又不知主张造化的是谁，若能以清静为体，镇定为基，天心为主，元神为用，巧使盗机，返还天真，归根复命，岂患不至天地圣位？至用女鼎一节事，万无此理！

The creative workings of the human body are identical to those of heaven and earth. Those people simply do not know how heaven obtained the One to become clear or how earth obtained the One to become tranquil, nor do they know who presides over creation and transformation. If one can take clarity and stillness as the substance, composure as the foundation, the Celestial Heart as master, and the Original Spirit as function, skillfully wielding the mechanism of stealing back what was lost, returning to Heaven’s genuineness and reverting to the root to restore life—why fear not reaching the sage station of heaven and earth? As for the matter of using female vessels, there is not a shred of truth in it!

§4
Original (Chinese)

假使有缘之士，得遇真师，先行玉液还丹炼己和光，操持涵养回光返照，此即见性明心之事也。既见其性，更求向上之事，乃金液还丹情来归性，直到真空地位，大用现前，龙女献一宝珠，金光发现，至此方为一得永得，亥子之交，剥复之间，于太阳初动时兴功，手探月窟，足蹑天根，回风混合，从此有百日功灵之妙，此金液还丹乃阴阳五行之大道也。除此玉液金液性命双修清静自然之道，余皆旁门小法。

Suppose a person with karmic affinity meets a true master: first they practice the Jade Liquid Returning Elixir, refining the self and tempering their light, maintaining steady cultivation and reverting the light to illuminate within—this is the work of seeing inner nature and clarifying the mind. Once inner nature is seen, they seek the higher work: the Golden Liquid Returning Elixir, where feelings return to inner nature, all the way to the station of true emptiness where great function manifests before them, “the Dragon Girl offers a precious pearl,” and golden light appears—only then is it once attained, attained forever.

At the juncture of Hai and Zi, between Splitting Apart (Bo) and Return (Fu), just as the Great Yang first stirs, they launch the work: “the hand probes the Moon’s Cavern, the foot treads the Root of Heaven,” turning the wind back to mingle as one. From then on unfolds the marvel of a hundred days of numinous work. This Golden Liquid Returning Elixir is the Great Way of yin-yang and the Five Phases. Apart from this Way of Jade Liquid and Golden Liquid—dual cultivation of inner nature and life, clear, still, and natural—everything else is a minor side path.

§5
Original (Chinese)

某于一身内外，安炉立鼎，攒簇口诀，药物火候，细微已得，不知虚空法度，便去入室行外药入腹大事，发火行功到秘密处，虚空有万神朝礼，仙音戏顶，此事鬼神难明，怎奈自己不能炼己于尘俗，未得积铅于市尘，气脉又未甚大定，基址也未得三全，理虽融而性未见，故万物发现凶险，心神恍惚不能做主，又因外边无知音道侣护持看守，触其声色，惊散元神，激鼎翻炉，劣了心猿，走了意马，神不守舍，气不归元，遭其阴魔，（何为阴魔，我不细言后学不知，皆因真阳一散，阴气用事，昼夜身中神鬼为害，不论睁眼合眼，皆看见鬼神来往，即耳中亦听得鬼神吵闹，白日间觉犹可，到晚来最难过，不敢静定一时。）我身彼家——海底命主兑金之戊土，冲返五脏，气血皆随上腾，身提不着地，杀身丧命，真乃鬼家活计也。

I myself had already mastered setting up the furnace and tripod inside and outside my single body, the oral formulas of gathering and clustering, and the minutiae of medicine and fire phasing. Yet without knowing the “measures of the void,” I went straight into the chamber to carry out the great affair of bringing the outer medicine into the belly. When I kindled the fire and pushed the work to its secret juncture, myriad spirits paid homage in the void and immortal music played above my crown—something even ghosts and spirits can scarcely fathom. Alas, I had not refined myself amid worldly dust nor accumulated lead in the marketplace; my breath-channels were not yet deeply settled, my foundation had not attained the Three Completions, and though I understood the principle, I had not truly seen my inner nature. Consequently, all phenomena manifested in menacing forms, and my mind-spirit grew dazed and could no longer stay master.

Moreover, because there were no kindred Daoist companions outside to guard and watch over me, I was struck by outer sounds and sights; my Original Spirit scattered in alarm, “upsetting the tripod and overturning the furnace.” The monkey-mind turned unruly, the horse of intention bolted, spirit abandoned its dwelling, breath failed to return to the origin, and I fell prey to yin demons. (Original note: What are yin demons? If I do not spell it out, later students will not know. It comes wholly from True Yang scattering so that yin breath takes command: day and night spirits and ghosts torment one within the body; whether eyes are open or shut, one sees ghosts and spirits passing to and fro, and hears them clamoring in the ears. By day it feels barely tolerable, but at night it is hardest to endure—one dares not sit still for even a single hour.) The “other side” within my own body—the master of life at the bottom of the sea, the Wu-Earth of Dui-Metal—surged back into the five viscera, qi and blood rushed upward with it, my body felt lifted off the ground, nearly costing me my life. Truly it was a business fit for ghosts!

§6
Original (Chinese)

某乃暂弃前功，遵师训指，大隐市尘积铅尘俗，摄情归性杀机返覆，自幼至老被天地人物盗去的天真，今于虚无中、尘色内，却要夺盗返还于我天性之中，方得元精元炁元神之三全，至是乃心明理融，理融见性，身心大定，五行攒簇，才去行上等事而了大道，想前代贤哲，多有中道而废，皆因未曾炼己持心，金来归性，以至二候得药，于四候进火之时，不知虚空法度，粗心大意，是以白玉蟾有再砍秋筠节之叹焉！

I thereupon temporarily set aside my former work and followed my master’s instructions: living as a great recluse amid the dust of the marketplace, accumulating lead in ordinary worldly life, gathering feelings back into inner nature, and reversing the killing mechanism. All the Heaven-given genuineness stolen from me by heaven, earth, people, and things from childhood to old age—now, within emptiness and amid dusty appearances, I stole it back and returned it to my celestial nature, thereby attaining the Three Completions of Original Essence, Original Breath, and Original Spirit. Only then was the mind bright and principle thoroughly assimilated; with principle assimilated I saw my nature; body and mind entered great stillness, the Five Phases clustered together, and only then did I proceed to the supreme work and complete the Great Way.

Thinking back on worthies and sages of former generations, many failed halfway—all because they had not refined the self, guarded the mind, and brought metal back to inner nature; thus after obtaining the medicine at the second phase, when advancing the fire at the fourth phase, they did not know the measures of the void and acted carelessly. That is why Bai Yuchan voiced his sigh of “having to cut the autumn bamboo joints all over again!”

§7
Original (Chinese)

谁知虚空消息至微至凶至恶，若是擒捉不住定不饶人。若是学人，知一身内外两个真消息，了然无碍，方去操持涵养，克去己私复还天理，则还丹工夫至简至易，终日采吾身外之黄芽，以候先天之琼浆，此正是饮酒戴花悟长生之妙也。

Who would have known that the tidings of the void are utterly subtle, utterly fierce, and utterly perilous—if you fail to hold them fast, they will surely show no mercy. If a student knows the two true tidings of inside and outside one’s own body, thoroughly clear and unhindered, and then applies steady cultivation and nurturing, overcoming selfish desire to restore Celestial Principle, then the work of the Returning Elixir is supremely simple and easy: all day long gathering the Yellow Sprout beyond the physical frame to await the Prior Heaven jade nectar—this is the very marvel of “drinking wine and wearing flowers” while awakening to long life.

§8
Original (Chinese)

若混元一事，则毋意毋必，毋固毋我，恁生恁死，忘人忘物，摄境积铅，法财两用，岂不致俗子之笑谤哉？如游手好闲不务生理，终日穿街过巷玩景怡情，淫房酒肆兀坐忘言，岂不动世人之惊疑哉？是以必资通都大邑有力之家以为外护，目击道存，韬光晦迹。故仙云「要贪天上宝，须用世间财。」

As for the affair of “primal mingling” (hunyuan), one must have no foregone conclusions, no arbitrary certainties, no obstinacy, no ego—indifferent to life or death, forgetting others and forgetting things, gathering the environment to accumulate lead, employing both dharma and wealth. How could that fail to draw the mockery and slander of the vulgar? Appearing idle and without livelihood, strolling streets and lanes all day enjoying the scenery, sitting upright and speechless even in brothels and wine-shops—how could that fail to arouse the astonishment and suspicion of worldly folk? Therefore one must rely on a capable household in a great metropolis as an outer protector, realizing the Dao at a glance while concealing one’s light and hiding one’s tracks. Hence an immortal said: “To covet the treasure up in heaven, you must spend worldly wealth.”

§9
Original (Chinese)

夫天上宝，非指青天之上而言也，乃吾身上九阳鼎之宝也。故轩辕铸九鼎而飞升，世之迷徒，一闻天上宝三字，遂执天上日月为水火，乃于月出庚方，用两目行度数以采之为真水真铅，于日出卯时，亦运两目采之为真火真汞。夫天上地下、乾坤坎离、男女内外炉鼎，皆喻吾一身之内外阴阳而言，并无男女等相。仙云「凡有所相，皆是虚妄，还丹本无质。」至哉斯言尽矣！

Now “the treasure up in heaven” does not refer to the blue sky above; it refers to the treasure of the Nine-Yang Tripod atop one’s own body—which is why Xuanyuan cast nine tripods and ascended in flight. Deluded people in the world, the moment they hear the words “treasure up in heaven,” fix upon the literal sun and moon in the sky as water and fire: when the moon rises in the Geng quarter they move their two eyes through counted cycles to harvest it as True Water and True Lead; when the sun rises at the Mao hour they likewise turn their two eyes to harvest it as True Fire and True Mercury.

In truth, heaven above and earth below, Qian and Kun, Kan and Li, male and female, inner and outer furnace and tripod—all are metaphors for the inner and outer yin and yang of my own single body; there is no literal form of man and woman at all. An immortal said: “Whatever has a form is illusory; the Returning Elixir originally has no material substance.” Utmost indeed—those words say it all!

§10
Original (Chinese)

况世间学好的人，必不为损人利己之事，宇宙间男女所赖以生而不死者，惟此一点阳精而已，岂有学仙的人，采女人之精而利己之身哉？此与世之杀人者有何异焉？又，先圣言彼家男女、两家两国，及内外炉鼎等说，若人不得正传，其不错认者几希矣。

Furthermore, any decent person in the world who strives for goodness refuses to harm others for personal gain. Between heaven and earth, the one thing by which men and women live and do not die is this single point of yang essence—how could someone studying to become an immortal harvest a woman’s essence to benefit their own body? How does that differ from the murderers of the world?

Moreover, when the ancient sages spoke of “the other household, man and woman,” “two families and two kingdoms,” and “inner and outer furnace and tripod,” if one has not received authentic transmission, few indeed escape misinterpreting them.

§11
Original (Chinese)

某曾遇明师耳提面命，抉破虚空内外两个真消息，不敢私于一己，冒禁相付，把三教经书一身天地人之造化、药物、火候、日月交合盈满度数，尽都抉破，不立文字但说真言，使学者无错认迷修之误。是书在处，有神物护持，若无缘下流见之，亦不过瞽唱之文词耳。是金丹大道万劫难遇，正是踏破铁鞋无觅处，得来全不费功夫。学者果能涵养于造次颠沛流离之际，保此方寸不失，是天理复矣。天理既复，然后求向上外药入腹事，顷刻湛然，脱胎换骨，浑然化一道金光，大地成宝，身外生身，阳神脱体，持空养虚，此是五龙大蛰法，受诸逍遥，超出风水火之三劫，不在生老病死苦中矣。今人不去修行，有贪图炉火外丹服食者，此又迷之甚矣！

I once met an enlightened master who instructed me face to face, laying bare the two true tidings of inside and outside the void. Not daring to keep it selfishly to myself, I braved the prohibition to hand it down—breaking open the creative workings of heaven, earth, and humanity within one body as found in the scriptures of the Three Teachings, together with the medicine, fire phasing, and the exact cycles of waxing and union of sun and moon—not setting up ornate rhetoric, only speaking plain truth so that students will not fall into error and blind practice. Wherever this book rests, numinous beings guard it; yet if an unready, base person reads it, it seems no more than the lyrics of a blind street-singer.

This Great Way of the Golden Elixir is scarcely met in ten thousand kalpas—truly “wearing out iron shoes with nowhere to find it, yet when it arrives it takes no effort at all.” If a student can cultivate and nurture the mind even in moments of haste, upheaval, and displacement, guarding this Square Inch so it is never lost, then Celestial Principle is restored. Once Celestial Principle is restored, seek the higher affair of bringing the outer medicine into the belly: limpid in an instant, shedding the mortal frame and exchanging the bones, melting wholly into a single ray of golden light, the great earth turning to jewels, a body born beyond the body, the Yang Spirit departing the form, upholding emptiness and nourishing vacuity—this is the “Great Hibernation Method of the Five Dragons,” enjoying boundless freedom, stepping beyond the three calamities of wind, water, and fire, no longer bound within the suffering of birth, aging, sickness, and death. People today refuse to cultivate themselves, yet hanker after furnace-and-fire external alchemy to swallow—that is delusion carried to the utmost!

Original Note
Original (Chinese)

此篇乃玄要下篇道情总说，登天指迷即道情词曲之总名也。篇末云「下流见之，不过瞽唱文词」，即此可知为道情总说。

(Note by Li Hanxu) This essay is the general introduction to the “Daoist ballads” (daoqing) in the second part of Essentials of the Mystery; “Pointing Out Delusions on the Ascent to Heaven” is the collective title of those songs and lyrics. From the remark near the end—“if a base person reads it, it seems no more than the lyrics of a blind street-singer”—we know that this serves as the general introduction to the ballads.

Key Terms

  • Female vessel (nüding) / vessel (dingqi): Side-path practices that use women as “vessels” for sexual gathering. This essay states unequivocally that “there is not a shred of truth in it,” and equates harvesting a woman’s essence for personal gain with murder.
  • Outer yin and yang / outer furnace and tripod / outer medicine: Figurative expressions in alchemical texts; this essay declares that “all are metaphors for the inner and outer yin and yang of my own single body.”
  • Jade Liquid Returning Elixir / Golden Liquid Returning Elixir: Here Jade Liquid denotes refining the self, tempering one’s light, and seeing inner nature and clarifying the mind, while Golden Liquid denotes feelings returning to inner nature and permanent attainment; together they are called “the Way of dual cultivation of inner nature and life, clear, still, and natural.”
  • Measures of the void (xukong fadu): Mastery of the shifting phenomena within emptiness during advanced chamber practice; Zhang Sanfeng says his own early failure stemmed from not knowing these.
  • Yin demons: Explained in the essay’s original note as the ghostly visions arising when “True Yang scatters so that yin breath takes command.”
  • Refining the self (lianji) and accumulating lead (jiqian): Tempering mind and nature and storing Prior Heaven breath amid worldly life—indispensable groundwork before entering the chamber.
  • Primal mingling (hunyuan): Here referring to the stage of living as a recluse hidden in the marketplace, cultivating amid ordinary society.
  • Daoist ballads (daoqing): Songs chanted by Daoists to exhort the world; the second part of Essentials of the Mystery consists largely of pieces in this form.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Any teaching that treats the opposite sex as a “vessel” can be rejected as a side path on the basis of this essay. Zhang Sanfeng’s own words are “not a shred of truth in it” and “how does this differ from murderers?”
  2. When the foundation is not solid, it is better to step back. Zhang Sanfeng himself “temporarily set aside his former work” and returned to daily life to refine himself. Stepping back and pausing when discomfort arises in practice is not failure—it is the right path.
  3. Self-refinement happens in daily life. “Refine the self amid worldly dust, accumulate lead in the marketplace”—keeping the mind unruffled among people, work, and everyday responsibilities is more fundamental than hiding away to sit.
  4. Do not take “heaven above” literally. Staring at the physical sun and moon to “harvest qi” is explicitly called the folly of deluded people. Heaven, earth, sun, moon, man, and woman in alchemical books are all metaphors for yin and yang within one’s own body.
  5. Have reliable support. One reason Zhang Sanfeng gave for his failure was having “no kindred Daoist companions outside to guard and watch over me.”

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text comes from Volume Three, Plain Talks on the Mysterious Mechanism, of the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng. The pronoun mou (“I myself”) is the author’s self-reference.
  2. “When the Five Phases move forward, the dharma realm is a pit of fire; when the Five Phases move in reverse, the great earth becomes the seven jewels”: A saying frequently quoted by alchemists; exact source unidentified.
  3. “The people may be made to follow it; they cannot be made to know it”: From Analects, “Taibo.” Here borrowed to distinguish forward and reverse movement, a characteristic alchemical reinterpretation.
  4. “How heaven obtained the One to become clear, how earth obtained the One to become tranquil”: Adapted from Daodejing, Chapter 39.
  5. “Xuanyuan cast nine tripods”: Legend tells that the Yellow Emperor (Xuanyuan) cast tripods at the foot of Mount Jing and ascended on a dragon upon their completion. Side-path practitioners twisted “nine tripods” to mean nine women, which this essay refutes.
  6. “The ‘other side’ within my own body—the master of life at the bottom of the sea, the Wu-Earth of Dui-Metal”: Here “the other side” (bijia) is explicitly qualified as “within my own body” (woshen), referring to the Prior Heaven breath at the bottom of the sea. This demonstrates that in the author’s own usage, bijia lies within a single body.
  7. “Bai Yuchan voiced his sigh of ‘having to cut the autumn bamboo joints all over again’”: Bai Yuchan (Fifth Patriarch of the Southern Lineage) lamented in his writings losing one’s footing mid-work and having to start over; exact source unidentified.
  8. “No foregone conclusions, no arbitrary certainties, no obstinacy, no ego”: From Analects, “Zihan”: “There were four things the Master was entirely free from: foregone conclusions, arbitrary certainties, obstinacy, and ego.”
  9. “To covet the treasure up in heaven, you must spend worldly wealth”: A common alchemical adage, cited in the original as “an immortal said.”
  10. “Whatever has a form is illusory”: From the Diamond Sutra (fan suoyou xiang, jie shi xuwang), printed here with word order fan yousuo xiang, preserved as written; “the Returning Elixir originally has no material substance” is an alchemical maxim.
  11. “Wearing out iron shoes with nowhere to find it, yet when it arrives it takes no effort at all”: A well-known proverb from the Song dynasty onward.
  12. The closing “Note” (an) was added by the re-editor Li Hanxu, translated here as “Original Note.”

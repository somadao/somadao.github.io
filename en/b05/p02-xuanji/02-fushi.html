II · Plain Talks on the Mysterious Mechanism

On Ingesting the Great Elixir · On the Single Grain of Millet

On Ingesting the Great Elixir and On the Single Grain of Millet — when the inner elixir is complete, the outer elixir is accomplished; the names of the millet-grain jewel across the Three Teachings

About This Chapter

These two short essays are paired together and speak of the same single reality, which alchemists call the “pearl of millet grain” or the “luminous precious pearl.”

  • On Ingesting the Great Elixir first dispels a misconception: what the texts call “ingestion” (fushi), “outer elixir,” and “yellow and white” does not mean smelting worldly gold, silver, lead, and mercury into pills to swallow. “When the inner elixir is complete, the outer elixir is accomplished”—first accumulate essence and store up breath within yourself until breath is full and spirit is whole; when day and night are ever bright and the medicinal sprout arises of itself, the so-called “outer elixir” manifests naturally. Zhang Sanfeng puts it bluntly: “Gold seeds gold, silver seeds silver; outside there is no other numinous spirit”; trying to refine Posterior Heaven metals and chemical potions “will fail ten thousand times out of ten thousand.” The second half counsels students: before meeting an enlightened master, read the classics quietly, accumulate merit and good deeds, keep the heart humble, and avoid mediocre teachers who cling to side paths.
  • On the Single Grain of Millet goes on to explain the different names this single jewel bears in the Three Teachings: in Daoism, True Lead and True Mercury; in Buddhism, the true emptiness and wondrously awakened nature; in Confucianism, the Ultimateless that is yet the Great Ultimate. It then uses heaven, earth, sun, and moon as metaphors: the sun is yang containing yin, the moon is yin containing yang, and the human body likewise holds its own true sun and moon—the three ethereal souls belong to inner nature (xing), the seven corporeal souls to life (ming), and “the two words xing and ming exhaust ten thousand scrolls of alchemy texts.” It closes with a reminder that nasal mucus, saliva, semen, fluids, qi, and blood are all Posterior Heaven turbid yin, not the True Yin of the alchemists.

Reading Note: Right at the start of On the Single Grain of Millet, the text declares that the mysterious pearl of millet grain “must not be fixed upon Qian and Kun, sun and moon, or man and woman; only inside and outside one’s own body do you set up the furnace and establish the tripod.” This is a master key to all of Zhang Sanfeng’s alchemical writings.

Original Text and Translation

§1On Ingesting the Great Elixir
Original (Chinese)

三清俸禄，玉皇廪给，非仙圣贤哲焉能受得，如许旌阳、葛仙翁、殷真君等八百余家，俱是成道之后，方炼服食以度群迷。仙经云「内丹成，外丹就。」此言人得正传，先积精累炁，收积内外法财，养得炁满神全，金光出现，昼夜常明，如是则吾身内丹成，而吾身外丹亦发相矣。

The stipends of the Three Pure Ones and the granary allowances of the Jade Emperor—who but immortals, sages, worthies, and wise ones could possibly receive them? Figures such as Xu Jingyang, Immortal Patriarch Ge, and Perfected Lord Yin, along with more than eight hundred others, all refined the “elixir of ingestion” only after attaining the Dao, in order to deliver the deluded multitude. The immortal scripture says: “When the inner elixir is complete, the outer elixir is accomplished.” This means that once one receives the authentic transmission, first accumulates essence and gathers breath, stores up inner and outer dharma wealth, and nurtures them until breath is full, spirit is whole, golden light appears, and day and night are ever bright—then the inner elixir of one’s body is complete, and the outer elixir of one’s body likewise manifests its form.

§2
Original (Chinese)

凡看书不可按图索骏。学者于昼夜常明之时，药苗已生，方可采吾身外之药，配吾身中之雌雄，到这般时候，方可炼服食金丹，（此丹如黍米一粒，落于地则金光烛天，方名神丹，若不通神，敢说是外丹服食。）以得金丹入口，周天火候发现，顷刻湛然，撒手无碍，才是金蝉脱壳，默朝上帝，中遇仙举，受其天禄，万神朝礼，能折天补地，摘星握月，驱雷转斗，呼风唤雨，举意万神使，觑天地如手掌相似，这福德胜三辈天子，智慧胜七辈状元。

Whenever you read books, do not look for a fine steed solely by checking a picture. When the student reaches the stage of being ever bright day and night and the medicinal sprout has already emerged, only then may one gather the medicine beyond one’s physical body to pair with the male and female within one’s own body; only at such a time may one refine the Golden Elixir of ingestion. (Original note: This elixir is like a single grain of millet; when dropped to the ground its golden light illuminates the heavens—only then is it called the Divine Elixir. If it does not penetrate the divine, how dare anyone call it the outer elixir of ingestion?)

Once the Golden Elixir enters the mouth, the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit manifests; in an instant all is limpid and one lets go without obstruction. Only then is it the “golden cicada shedding its shell,” silently paying court to the Sovereign on High, recommended along the way by immortals, receiving Heaven’s stipend, with myriad spirits paying homage—able to fold heaven and mend earth, pluck stars and grasp the moon, drive thunder and turn the Dipper, summon wind and call rain; at a single stir of intention myriad spirits obey, and heaven and earth appear no larger than one’s palm. Such blessing and merit surpass three lifetimes as emperor; such wisdom surpasses seven lifetimes as top imperial scholar.

§3
Original (Chinese)

此理奥妙，天机深远。金种金，银种银，外边无有别灵神，此黄白之术，不是凡间金银为母过气，果得正传，认得黄芽白雪（此为黄白），能产先天大药，方可为母过气，以炼神丹，但是金银水药都属后天，且又不知真阴真阳同类，万万无成。慨世学者真假不辨，不遇正人，都是盲修瞎炼。

This principle is profound and subtle, its celestial mechanism deep and far-reaching. “Gold seeds gold, silver seeds silver; outside there is no other numinous spirit.” This art of “yellow and white” is not taking ordinary worldly gold and silver as a seed-mother to transmit qi for transmutation. Only if one truly obtains the orthodox transmission, recognizes the Yellow Sprout and White Snow (original note: this is the true “yellow and white”), and can produce the Great Medicine of Prior Heaven, may that serve as the seed-mother to transmit qi and refine the Divine Elixir. As for worldly metals, mercury, and chemical drugs, all belong to Posterior Heaven; moreover, those who work with them know nothing of True Yin, True Yang, and “of the same kind”—they will fail ten thousand times out of ten thousand. Alas, students in the world cannot tell true from false; never meeting an authentic master, all of them cultivate blindly and refine in the dark.

§4
Original (Chinese)

实修性命之士，若未遇真师，且潜心看书。夫古圣丹书，不空说一字，妄言一句，只是后人不识邪正，又不知圣贤书中都是隐语譬喻，遭遇庸师，执认旁门，毒药入心，又无通变，似是而非，自高自是，声音颜色拒人千里之外，则高人望望然去之，况仙圣乎？

Those who earnestly cultivate inner nature and life, if they have not yet met a true teacher, should for the time being study the books with quiet concentration. The alchemical books of the ancient sages never speak a single idle word or utter a single false sentence; it is only that later people cannot tell crooked from straight, and do not realize that the books of sages and worthies are written entirely in veiled language and metaphor. Falling in with mediocre teachers, they cling to side paths; poison enters the mind, they lack flexibility, they mistake the specious for the true, and grow arrogant and self-righteous. Their very voice and expression hold others a thousand miles at bay, so that persons of high attainment turn away and leave them from afar—how much more so the immortals and sages?

§5
Original (Chinese)

学者未遇正人时，当小心低意，积功累行，遇魔莫退，遭谤勿嗔，重道轻财，一遇正人笃志苦求，抉破一身内外两个真消息，忽然醒悟诸书，才不为人迷惑。若是志人君子实心为命，扫尽旁门，重正心猿，重立志气，低心下意，经魔历难，苦求明师，穷取生身受气初，初者是元始祖炁也。此炁含著一点真阳真阴。夫真阳真阴，产于天地之先，混元之始，这颗灵明黍米宝珠，悬在虚空明明丽丽，但未有明师指破的人，如在醉梦相似。离此一着，都是旁门。此灵明宝珠，于虚空之中，包含万象，潜藏万有，发生万物，都是这个。

Before meeting an authentic master, the student ought to be careful and humble, accumulate merit and virtuous deeds, refuse to retreat when meeting demonic trials, feel no anger when slandered, and value the Dao above wealth. Once one meets an authentic person, entreat them with steadfast resolve until the two true tidings of inside and outside one’s own body are laid bare; suddenly all the classics awaken into clarity, and only then can one no longer be led astray by others.

If a person of resolve and integrity sincerely seeks to settle life, they must sweep away all side paths, straighten the monkey-mind anew, set their aspiration anew, lower their pride, endure trials and difficulties, earnestly seek an enlightened master, and fathom “the very beginning when the body received breath.” That “beginning” is the Primordial Ancestral Breath, which holds within it a single point of True Yang and True Yin. True Yang and True Yin are born before heaven and earth, at the dawn of primal unity—this luminous precious pearl of millet grain hangs suspended in the void, clear and resplendent; yet those who have not had an enlightened master point it out are as though lost in a drunken dream. Apart from this one move, everything is a side path. This luminous precious pearl, within the void, embraces ten thousand phenomena, harbors ten thousand existences, and generates ten thousand things—all of it is this.

§6On the Single Grain of Millet
Original (Chinese)

此物在道门中，喻真铅真汞，一得真得，不可着于乾坤、日月、男女上，只于己身内外，安炉立鼎，炼己持心，明理见性之时，攒簇发火，不出半刻时辰，立得黍米玄珠现于曲江之上，刀圭入口，顷刻一窍开百脉齐开，浑身筋骨，五脏血肉，都化成气，与外水银相似，到此时候，用百日火功，方有灵妙，一得永得无有返还，住世留形，炼神还虚与道为一矣。

In the Daoist gate, this thing is symbolized as True Lead and True Mercury; once truly obtained, it is truly obtained. It must not be fixed upon Qian and Kun, sun and moon, or man and woman; only inside and outside one’s own body do you set up the furnace and establish the tripod, refine the self and guard the mind. When principle is clear and inner nature is seen, gather, cluster, and ignite the fire; in less than half a double-hour, the mysterious pearl of millet grain appears at once above the Winding River. Once the Knife-Tip Measure enters the mouth, in an instant one opening unlocks and the hundred channels open together; sinews and bones, the five viscera, blood and flesh throughout the body all dissolve into breath, like liquid mercury outside. Having reached this stage, apply a hundred days of fire work and spiritual marvels follow: once attained it is attained forever, never to regress; dwelling in the world while retaining the form, one refines spirit to return to emptiness and becomes one with the Dao.

§7
Original (Chinese)

此物在佛门中，说是真空真妙觉性，下手端的，在炼魔见性，片晌工夫，发起三昧真火，返本还元，一体同观，大地成宝，霞光万道，五眼六通，炼金刚不坏之身，了鬼神不测之妙也。

In the Buddhist gate, this thing is called the awakened nature of true emptiness and true wonder. The exact starting point lies in refining away demons and seeing one’s nature; in a brief moment of work one kindles the Samādhi True Fire, returns to the root and reverts to the origin, beholding all as one single body. The great earth turns to jewels, ten thousand rays of rosy light shine forth, the five eyes and six penetrations unfold, the indestructible vajra body is forged, and one fulfills the wonder unfathomable to ghosts and spirits.

§8
Original (Chinese)

此物在儒门中，说是无极而太极，依外天地而论，无极是天地周围，日月未判之前，四维上下，混混沌沌如阴雾水气，直至时到，气满相激才是太极，是时也，日月既生，清浊自分，在上为天，在下为地，天之清气为纯阳，地之浊气为纯阴，雨露从天降是阳能生阴，万物从地生是阴能生阳，天地是个虚无，包藏无穷尽、无边际，天之星宿神祇，动静转轮各有方位，地下万物，按四时八节自然发生，总论只是虚空。

In the Confucian gate, this thing is called “the Ultimateless and yet the Great Ultimate.” Speaking by analogy with the outer heaven and earth: the Ultimateless is the expanse around heaven and earth before sun and moon were separated, all four directions, above and below, a turbid chaos like overcast mist and watery vapor; only when the time arrives and breath brims full and stirs itself does it become the Great Ultimate. At that moment sun and moon are born, clear and turbid part of themselves, what rises above is heaven and what settles below is earth; heaven’s clear breath is pure yang, earth’s turbid breath is pure yin. Rain and dew fall from heaven—that is yang generating yin; the myriad creatures sprout from earth—that is yin generating yang. Heaven and earth are one vast emptiness, containing what has no end and no boundary; the stars, constellations, and spirits of heaven move, rest, and revolve each in its proper station; the myriad things upon earth arise naturally according to the four seasons and eight seasonal nodes. Summed up, it is nothing but empty space.

§9
Original (Chinese)

夫日月是天地之精，上照三十三天，下照九极万泉，东西运转，上下升降，寒暑往来，日是纯阳之体，内含一点真阴之精，属青龙、姹女、甲木、水银、金乌、三魂，即是外，月是纯阴之体，内含着一点真阳之气，属白虎、婴儿、庚金、朱砂、玉兔、七魄，即是内，人身造化同天地，故人身亦有真日月，道在迩，人何求之远也？

Sun and moon are the essence of heaven and earth, shining upward over the thirty-three heavens and downward over the nine extremities and myriad springs, revolving east and west, ascending and descending, bringing cold and heat in alternation. The sun has a body of pure yang containing within it a single point of True Yin essence, corresponding to the Azure Dragon, the Maiden, Jia-Wood, quicksilver, the Golden Crow, and the three ethereal souls—this is the “outer.” The moon has a body of pure yin containing within it a single point of True Yang breath, corresponding to the White Tiger, the Infant, Geng-Metal, cinnabar, the Jade Hare, and the seven corporeal souls—this is the “inner.” The creative workings of the human body are identical to those of heaven and earth, so the human body too has its true sun and moon. The Dao is right at hand—why do people seek it afar?

§10
Original (Chinese)

三魂属性，性在天边，七魄属命，命在海底，内外通来，性命两个字，了却万卷丹书，性属神是阴，命属气是阳，故曰一阴一阳之谓道，千经万卷皆是异名，然真性命及幻法象，若不得真传，则又不可知耳。仙云「四大一身皆属阴，未知何物是阳精。」又云「涕唾精津气血液，七般灵物总属阴。」此说乃后天渣质之浊阴，非真阴也。真阴与真阳相对，真阴既不知，焉能知真阳乎？今之学者，不惟不知真阳，亦且不知真阴，若知真阴亦必知真阳矣。

The three ethereal souls belong to inner nature, and inner nature lies at the rim of heaven; the seven corporeal souls belong to life, and life lies at the bottom of the sea. Once inner and outer communicate, the two words “inner nature and life” (xing and ming) exhaust ten thousand scrolls of alchemical books. Inner nature belongs to spirit and is yin; life belongs to breath and is yang—hence it is said, “One yin and one yang are called the Dao.” A thousand scriptures and ten thousand scrolls are all merely different names for this. Yet as for true inner nature and life versus illusory phenomena, without authentic transmission one cannot tell them apart.

An immortal said: “This whole body of the four elements belongs to yin; who knows what thing is the yang essence?” And again: “Mucus, saliva, semen, bodily fluids, qi, blood, and humors—all seven numinous substances belong to yin.” This refers to the turbid yin of Posterior Heaven dregs, not True Yin. True Yin stands in counterpart to True Yang; if one does not even know True Yin, how could one know True Yang? Students today not only do not know True Yang—they do not even know True Yin; were they to know True Yin, they would surely know True Yang as well.

§11
Original (Chinese)

不遇明师，焉能猜度。学者穷取一身中天地人三才之妙，穷一身内外真炉鼎之端的，及一身内外阴阳之真消息，如不得旨，一见诸书异名，心无定见，执诸旁门，无有辨理，既不知穷理则心不明，心既不明则不能见性，既不见性焉能至命，故古人云「只为金丹无口诀，教君何处结灵胎。」

Without meeting an enlightened master, how could one guess it? Let the student fathom the marvel of the Three Powers—heaven, earth, and humanity—within one’s own body, fathom the true furnace and tripod inside and outside one’s own body, and fathom the true tidings of yin and yang inside and outside one’s own body. Without grasping the essential point, as soon as you encounter the diverse synonyms in various books, your mind has no settled compass, you latch onto side paths, and you have no principle by which to discriminate. Not knowing how to fathom principle, the mind is not clear; when the mind is not clear, one cannot see inner nature; not seeing inner nature, how can one reach destiny? Therefore the ancients said: “Only because the Golden Elixir has no written oral key—where can it tell you to form the numinous embryo?”

Key Terms

  • Ingestion (fushi) / Outer Elixir (waidan): In this essay, “ingesting the Golden Elixir” and “outer elixir” do not mean consuming mineral or metallic drugs, but the Great Medicine manifesting “beyond the physical body” (in emptiness) after the inner elixir is accomplished—hence “when the inner elixir is complete, the outer elixir is accomplished.”
  • Yellow and White / Yellow Sprout and White Snow: Worldly “yellow and white arts” mean transmuting base metals into gold and silver; this essay says the true yellow and white are the Yellow Sprout and White Snow—the Great Medicine of Prior Heaven generated within the body; see Yellow Sprout.
  • Seed-mother (mu) and transmitting qi (guoqi): Terms from external alchemy, using a small quantity of “mother” substance to transmute another. Borrowed here to say that only the Great Medicine of Prior Heaven can serve as the seed-mother.
  • Pearl of millet grain: The elixir seed condensed from the One Breath of Prior Heaven, which this essay says “hangs suspended in the void, clear and resplendent,” bearing different names across the Three Teachings.
  • Winding River (Qujiang): As the Commentary on Patriarch Lü’s Hundred-Character Tablet in this same volume notes, “Kun is the abdomen, also called the Winding River”—referring to the abdominal region.
  • Three ethereal souls (hun) and seven corporeal souls (po): This essay assigns the yin within the sun to the three hun (inner nature) and the yang within the moon to the seven po (life); see ethereal and corporeal souls.
  • Posterior Heaven turbid yin: The seven substances—mucus, saliva, semen, bodily fluids, qi, blood, and humors—which alchemists distinguish from True Yin.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Do not seek the medicine outside yourself. “Gold seeds gold, silver seeds silver; outside there is no other numinous spirit”—the elixir is not in minerals or herbs, nor in another person’s body, but solely within yourself.
  2. Do not fix upon man and woman. On the Single Grain of Millet states plainly that the mysterious pearl “must not be fixed upon Qian and Kun, sun and moon, or man and woman.” Any teaching that tells you to harvest something from the opposite sex can be judged a side path by Zhang Sanfeng’s own words.
  3. Before meeting a master, study quietly and cultivate character. Be careful and humble, accumulate merit and good deeds, do not retreat before trials, do not rage at slander, value the Dao above wealth—these can be practiced whether or not one has a teacher.
  4. Guard against arrogance and self-righteousness. Zhang Sanfeng identifies the greatest flaw of mediocre teachers and students as “mistaking the specious for the true, growing arrogant and self-righteous,” which drives away genuine masters. Remaining humble when reading alchemical classics matters more than memorizing terminology.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Xu Jingyang, Immortal Patriarch Ge, and Perfected Lord Yin”: Xu Xun (Magistrate of Jingyang), Ge Xuan or Ge Hong (commonly called Immortal Patriarch Ge), and Perfected Lord Yin (a legendary immortal whose exact identity is uncertain). “More than eight hundred” is a round figure.
  2. “Do not look for a fine steed solely by checking a picture”: The original reads an tu suo jun (按图索骏), a variant of the standard idiom an tu suo ji (按图索骥), with the same meaning.
  3. The parenthetical remarks (“This elixir is like a single grain of millet…” and “this is the true ‘yellow and white’”) are interlinear small-print notes in the original edition, preserved and translated here.
  4. “Recommended along the way by immortals” (zhong yu xianju): So in the original, meaning meeting immortals who recommend one’s elevation.
  5. “Gather the medicine beyond one’s physical body to pair with the male and female within one’s own body”: Following the pure-stillness method, this site understands it as the One Breath of Prior Heaven arriving from emptiness to pair with the spirit and breath within the body, matching “outside there is no other numinous spirit” immediately below.
  6. “Once truly obtained, it is truly obtained” (yide zhende): So in the original; below it reads “once attained, attained forever” (yide yongde).
  7. “Nine extremities and myriad springs” (jiuji wanquan): So in the original, referring broadly to the nine depths beneath the earth.
  8. “Inner nature belongs to spirit and is yin; life belongs to breath and is yang”: The assignment of yin and yang to xing and ming varies across alchemical texts; more commonly xing is yang (Li, spirit) and ming is yin (Kan, breath). Here the text follows its own preceding scheme (“yin within the sun belongs to the three hun; yang within the moon belongs to the seven po”), translated as written.
  9. “This whole body of the four elements belongs to yin; who knows what thing is the yang essence?”: From Zhang Boduan’s Folios on Awakening to Reality (Wuzhen pian), where standard editions read bu zhi (“do not know”) for wei zhi.
  10. “Only because the Golden Elixir has no written oral key—where can it tell you to form the numinous embryo?”: From the Folios on Awakening to Reality quatrain: “Though your cleverness surpass Yan Hui and Min Ziqian, without meeting a true master do not force guesses; only because the elixir classics contain no oral key—where can they tell you to form the numinous embryo?” Standard editions read danjing (“elixir classics”) for jindan (“Golden Elixir”).

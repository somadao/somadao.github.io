Preface by Li Hanxu

Li Hanxu's preface — dispelling illness and nourishing life are themselves the Way of the immortals, and why Zhang Sanfeng withdrew deep into mountain ravines, fearing only to be known

About This Chapter

This preface to the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng was written by its re-editor Li Hanxu (sobriquets Changyi Shanren and Hanxusheng, also known as Li Xiyue) and dated to the Double Seventh Festival of the jiachen year of Daoguang (1844). Brief as it is, the preface develops three main ideas:

  1. Dispelling illness and nourishing life are themselves the Way of the immortals. Both Cheng Yi and Ouyang Xiu of the Song dynasty maintained that while methods for warding off illness and prolonging life exist, broad-daylight ascension and immortals do not. Li Hanxu replies that dispelling illness and nourishing life are already the Way of the immortals, citing the Scripture of the Yellow Court: "Immortals and Daoists possess no uncanny magic; they accumulate essence and store up breath to become Perfected." Immortals are not born that way; they are forged bit by bit.
  2. Why Zhang Sanfeng remained in hiding. Living through the upheaval between the Yuan and Ming dynasties, he preserved his life and inner nature rather than seeking fame among rulers. To him, summonses from courts and prefectures only "cloud luminous emptiness and injure the Valley Spirit," so he brushed them aside and hid away.
  3. How this book came into being. Early in the Yongzheng reign of the Qing, Wang Xiling (courtesy name Mengjiu) served as an official in Sichuan and recounted that Zhang Sanfeng manifested in his compound; Wang then collected Zhang's writings and deeds into a volume. Later Liu Zhuo'an obtained Wang's book, added supplements, and printed it. On that foundation Li Hanxu compiled the Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng we read today.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

程子谓：「却病延年则有，白日飞升则无。」欧阳公谓：「养生之术则有，神仙之事则无。」余以为却病养生即仙道也。《黄庭经》云：「仙人道士非有神，积精累气以成真。」诚乎允之论哉！今夫谷者，虚境也；神者，灵觉也。虚灵不昧则成儒，谷神不死则成道，仙何异焉？

Master Cheng Yi said: "Dispelling illness and prolonging life exist; ascending to heaven in broad daylight does not." Lord Ouyang Xiu said: "Arts for nourishing life exist; the affairs of immortals do not." Yet in my view, dispelling illness and nourishing life are themselves the Way of the immortals. As the Scripture of the Yellow Court states: "Immortals and Daoists possess no uncanny magic; they accumulate essence and store up breath to become Perfected." What a fair and balanced verdict!

Now "valley" refers to the realm of emptiness, and "spirit" refers to luminous awareness. When luminous emptiness remains unclouded, one becomes a true Confucian; when the Valley Spirit never dies, one realizes the Way. How could the immortal be any different?

§2
Original (Chinese)

三丰先生，在元明鼎革间，独全性命于乱世，不求闻达于诸侯，深造以道，欲其自得，默念州县劳人币书招我，皆所以蔽虚灵而损谷神也，屏之弃之，避之藏之，真所谓世有神仙，彼必深潜崖壑，惟畏人知者。先生之节贞至矣！风雪可眠，非徒却病；须眉不老，何但养生。光之有日月之明，大之有沧海之水，又安知不有脱壳生翰、白日飞升，成仙于青霄之外者哉？

Master Sanfeng lived through the dynastic transition from Yuan to Ming. Alone he preserved his inner nature and life in a chaotic age, seeking no renown among the lords of the realm. He went deeply into the Way, wishing only to attain it for himself. Silently he reflected that prefectural and county officials toiling to summon him with gifts and letters would only cloud his luminous emptiness and injure his Valley Spirit; so he brushed them aside, cast them off, evaded them, and hid himself away. Truly this fulfills the saying: "If there are immortals in the world, they are sure to plunge deep into cliffs and ravines, fearing only that people might know of them."

How steadfast and pure was the Master's integrity! To sleep peacefully amid wind and snow is far more than warding off illness; to keep beard and brows unaging is scarcely mere hygiene. With brightness rivaling sun and moon, and vastness matching the waters of the blue sea, how could we know there is no shedding of the mortal shell, sprouting of wings, and rising in broad daylight to become an immortal beyond the azure clouds?

§3
Original (Chinese)

雍正初，汪公梦九观察剑南，先生以法身显化其署，即谓「五百年犹在人间」可也。公知神仙之道信而有征，于是以先生文章出处辑为成书。盖欲与却病、延年者大其观也。卓庵刘君得汪书而补纪之，刊版传世，后之颂其诗、读其书者，应知仙道之不虚也夫！

——道光甲辰七夕，长乙山人涵虚生叙。

Early in the Yongzheng reign, Lord Wang Mengjiu served as Surveillance Commissioner in Jiannan (Sichuan), and the Master manifested in his Dharma body within the official compound—one might truly say that "after five hundred years he was still among humankind." Convinced that the Way of the immortals was trustworthy and attested, Lord Wang gathered the Master's writings and biographical records into a book, intending to broaden the horizons of those who speak only of dispelling illness and extending life. Master Liu Zhuo'an later obtained Wang's book, added further records, and had it carved on printing blocks for posterity. Those who later recite his poems and read his writings should know that the Way of the immortals is no empty fancy!

—Preface written on the Double Seventh Festival of the jiachen year of Daoguang (1844), by Hanxusheng, the Mountain Recluse of Changyi.

Key Terms

  • Valley Spirit (gushen): from Chapter 6 of the Daodejing, "The Valley Spirit never dies; it is called the Mysterious Female." This preface explains "valley" as the realm of emptiness and "spirit" as luminous awareness.
  • Unclouded luminous emptiness (xuling bumei): from Zhu Xi's Commentary on the Great Learning, "Luminous virtue is what human beings receive from Heaven—empty, luminous, and unclouded."
  • Accumulating essence and storing up breath (jijing leiqi): a phrase from the Scripture of the Yellow Court, meaning to build up Essence and Breath without letting them dissipate.
  • Manifestation in the Dharma body (fashen xianhua): Daoist language for an immortal or Perfected One appearing in a formless spiritual body.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Nourishing life is itself cultivating the Way. Do not imagine immortality as something impossibly remote; caring well for your body and keeping essence and breath from draining away already puts you on the path.
  2. Step back from noise and entanglement. Zhang Sanfeng declined imperial summonses because fame, obligation, and social clamor "cloud luminous emptiness and injure the Valley Spirit." We need not retreat into mountain caves today, yet we can trim away needless social friction and constant streams of distraction.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Master Cheng Yi said: 'Dispelling illness and prolonging life exist; ascending to heaven in broad daylight does not'": Master Cheng refers to the Song Neo-Confucian Cheng Yi; the gist appears in the Surviving Works of the Two Chengs (Ercheng yishu), though the wording here is not strictly verbatim and is preserved as printed.
  2. "Lord Ouyang Xiu said: 'Arts for nourishing life exist; the affairs of immortals do not'": a similar sentiment appears in Ouyang Xiu's Preface to the Corrected Scripture of the Yellow Court; retained as printed.
  3. "Immortals and Daoists possess no uncanny magic; they accumulate essence and store up breath to become Perfected": from the Scripture of the Inner Landscape of the Yellow Court (Huangting neijing jing).
  4. "Prefectural and county officials toiling to summon him with gifts and letters": preserved as worded in the original edition, referring to local magistrates sent with gifts and documents to invite him forth.
  5. "If there are immortals in the world, they are sure to plunge deep into cliffs and ravines, fearing only that people might know of them": a well-known traditional saying whose earliest source is untraced.
  6. "Lord Wang Mengjiu served as Surveillance Commissioner in Jiannan": Wang Xiling (courtesy name Mengjiu), an early Qing official who served as circuit intendant in Sichuan and compiled an early collection of Zhang Sanfeng's works; Scroll 1 of this collection preserves his Basic Biography of Master Sanfeng.
  7. "Master Liu Zhuo'an": biographical details unknown; according to this preface, he supplemented and published Wang's compilation.
  8. "Jiachen year of Daoguang": 1844. Both "Mountain Recluse of Changyi" (Changyi Shanren) and "Hanxusheng" are sobriquets of Li Hanxu.

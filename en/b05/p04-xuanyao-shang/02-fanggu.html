Part IV · Essentials of the Mystery (Part I)

Two Chapters in Imitation of the Ancients · Ladder to Heaven · Oral Instructions in Person · Reply to the Yongle Emperor

Two Chapters in Imitation of the Ancients, Ladder to Heaven, Oral Instructions in Person, Reply to the Yongle Emperor — four-character synthesis, autobiographical quest, formula for replenishing depletion, and abundant Way and flourishing virtue as the finest plan

About This Chapter

The opening four works of the upper scroll of Essentials of the Mystery:

  • Two Chapters in Imitation of the Ancients: Four-character verse modeled on the Cantongqi. The first chapter traces the arc from the "Primordial Ancestral Breath" to the inversion of Kan and Li and Spirit born through Jiji (Already Across); the second moves from "the Great Way of Heaven and Earth, holding harmony and embracing the center" through advancing and withdrawing the fire, completing the ten-month count, and fulfilling the work of life. Li Xiyue notes that popular editions merged both chapters into one under the title "General Treatise on Inner and Outer Fire Phasing of the Golden Elixir," and only after obtaining Immortal Wang's edition did he see them properly separated.
  • Ladder to Heaven: A five-character ancient-style poem narrating Zhang Sanfeng's own quest for the Way: taking leave of his parents' graves, parting from wife and children, resigning office to roam seas and peaks, and reaching Mount Zhongnan to receive transmission from Master Fire Dragon. The second half treats refining the self, gathering the Medicine, fire phasing, ten months, and nine years.
  • Oral Instructions in Person: "Torn clothes are mended with cloth, a failing tree is banked with earth; with what shall human depletion be mended? With the pivot of yin-yang creation"—human depletion is repaired through the creative mechanism of yin and yang.
  • Reply to the Yongle Emperor: Tradition holds that Emperor Chengzu of the Ming sought out Zhang Sanfeng, and this poem was his answer: "What is the formula for long life? Abundant Way and flourishing virtue are the finest plan; restrain desire, clarify the mind, calm thoughts and cares—what exotic technique do immortals ever possess?"

Original Text and Translation

§1Two Chapters in Imitation of the Ancients
Original (Chinese)

（三丰自注：参同云「吾不敢虚说，仿效古人文。」题名取此。）

元始祖气，朴朴昏昏；元含无朕，始浑无名；混沌一破，太乙吐萌；两仪合德，日月晦明；乾交坤变，坤索乾成；异名同出，一本共根；内外虚实，刚柔平均；阴阳燮理，变化分形；真精真气，恍惚杳冥；坎离颠倒，运施五行；既济生神，未济死临；仙道谨守，鬼道邪倾；人希天道，速避鬼门；由仙希天，道炁长存。

(Zhang Sanfeng's own note: The Cantongqi says, "I dare not speak idly; I imitate the writings of the ancients." The title is taken from this.)

The Primordial Ancestral Breath is uncarved and dim; the origin holds no sign, the beginning is an undifferentiated whole without name. Once primal chaos breaks open, the Great Unity puts forth its sprout; the Two Modes unite in virtue, and sun and moon alternate dark and bright. Qian joins with Kun and transforms; Kun draws upon Qian and completes. Different in name, they issue from the same source, one stem sharing a single root. Inner and outer, empty and full, firm and yielding are balanced evenly; yin and yang are harmonized, transforming into distinct forms. True Essence and True Breath dwell in the dim and obscure; Kan and Li are inverted, setting the Five Phases in motion. Jiji (Already Across) gives birth to Spirit; Weiji (Not Yet Across) brings death near. The Way of immortals guards carefully; the way of ghosts veers crooked and collapses. If a person aspires to the Way of Heaven, let them swiftly shun the gate of ghosts; ascending from immortality toward Heaven, the Breath of the Way abides forever.

§2In Imitation of the Ancients · Part II
Original (Chinese)

天地大道，含和抱中；玄玄之祖，妙妙之宗；玄妙贯通，劈破鸿蒙；窍门汇籥，朱雀烧空；庚方月现，西南得朋；笑倾玄酒，宴饮黄中；二八成就，乌兔混融；神光默默，黄屋玄翁；巽风鼓吹，满鼎霞红；水火进退，朝屯暮蒙；子午运用，卯酉无功；十月数足，卦象翻终；了命功全，纯阳气冲；神几妙用，道法无穷。

The Great Way of Heaven and Earth holds harmony and embraces the center; it is the ancestor of mystery upon mystery, the source of marvel upon marvel. Mystery and marvel interpenetrate, cleaving open primal mist; the gates of the orifices converge as a bellows, and the Vermilion Bird sets the sky ablaze. In the geng quarter the crescent moon appears; in the southwest one gains companions. Smiling, one pours the Mysterious Wine and feasts in the Central Yellow. When the two eights are complete, the Golden Crow and Jade Hare blend as one; the Light of Spirit shines silently, and in the Yellow Chamber sits the Mysterious Elder. The Wind of Xun fans and blows until the entire tripod glows rosy crimson. Water and Fire advance and withdraw—Zhun at dawn and Meng at dusk; active at Zi and Wu, resting without effort at Mao and You. When the ten-month count is full, the hexagram images run their course to completion; the work of fulfilling life is whole, and pure yang Breath surges upward. Subtle in spiritual pivot and wondrous in function, the Way and its methods are inexhaustible.

§3
Original (Chinese)

〔原评〕此二章俗本皆作一首，题作「金丹内外火候总论」，心窃非之，后得汪仙藏本，不觉爽然。

(Original commentary) Popular editions all combine these two chapters into a single piece titled "General Treatise on Inner and Outer Fire Phasing of the Golden Elixir." Privately I thought this wrong; later, upon obtaining Immortal Wang's edition, I felt an immediate sense of clarity.

§4Ladder to Heaven
Original (Chinese)

大元飘蓬客，拂拂髯如戟；一曲上天梯，可当飞空锡；回思访道初，不转心如石；弃官游海岳，辛苦寻丹秘；辞我亡亲墓，乡山留不得；别我中年妇，出门天始白；舍我丱角儿，掉头离大宅；人所难毕者，行人已做毕；人所难割者，行人皆能割；欲证长生果，冲举乘仙鹤；后天培养坚，两足迈于役；悠悠催我心，流年驹过隙。

A drifting wanderer of the Great Yuan, whiskers bristling stiff as halberds—this single song, Ladder to Heaven, may serve as a flying staff through the sky. Looking back to when I first sought the Way, my mind was immovable as stone. I resigned office to roam the seas and sacred peaks, toiling in search of the elixir's secret. I bade farewell to my late parents' graves—neither home nor native hills could hold me back. I parted from my middle-aged wife, stepping out the gate as the sky first turned pale. I left behind my young tufted-haired child, turning my head to leave the family mansion. What others find hard to finish, this traveler has already finished; what others find hard to sever, this traveler can sever—solely to realize the fruit of long life and ascend in broad daylight astride an immortal crane. With Posterior Heaven firmly nurtured, my two feet set out on the road; the long stretch of years pressed upon my heart, fleeting time like a white colt glimpsed through a crack.

§5
Original (Chinese)

翘首终南山，对天三叹息；天降火龙师，玄音参一一；知我内丹成，不讲筑基业；赐我外丹功，可怜谆告切；炼己忘世情，采药按时节；先天无觔两，火候无爻策；只将老嫩分，但把文武别；纯以真意求，刀圭难缕晰；十月抱元胎，九年加面壁；换鼎复生孙，骑龙起霹雳；天地坏有时，仙翁寿无极。

Gazing up toward Mount Zhongnan, I sighed three times to Heaven. Heaven sent down Master Fire Dragon, whose mysterious teachings I penetrated one by one. Knowing my internal elixir was already accomplished, he did not speak of laying the foundation; instead he bestowed on me the work of the outer elixir, instructing me with earnest compassion. In refining the self, forget worldly attachments; in gathering the Medicine, follow the right timing. Prior Heaven has no weights or ounces; fire phasing has no lines or stalks—simply distinguish old from tender, simply differentiate gentle from martial fire. Seek purely with True Intention; the subtlety of the Knife-Tip (daogui) cannot be itemized in detail. For ten months embrace the primordial embryo; add nine years of facing the wall. Changing the tripod brings forth a grandson anew, riding the dragon amid rolling thunder. Even Heaven and Earth have their time of ruin, yet the immortal elder's lifespan is boundless.

§6
Original (Chinese)

〔原评〕沉雄顿挫，可作古歌行读。

(Original commentary) Deep, vigorous, and rhythmically cadenced—it can be read as an ancient ballad.

§7Oral Instructions in Person
Original (Chinese)

圣师亲口诀，名方万古遗；传与世间人，能有几人知；衣破用布补，树衰以土培；人损将何补，阴阳造化机；取将坎中丹，金花露一枝；庆云开天际，祥光塞死基；归己昏昏默，如醉亦如痴；大丹如黍米，脱壳镇无为；优游天地廓，万象掌中珠；人能服此药，寿与天地齐；如若不延寿，吾言皆是非。

Oral instructions directly from the sage master, a famed prescription handed down for ten thousand ages; passed to people in the world, how many truly understand it? When clothes tear, patch them with cloth; when a tree weakens, bank it with earth; when a human body is depleted, with what shall it be mended? With the pivot of yin-yang creation. Draw forth the elixir within Kan, and a single branch of the Golden Flower appears; auspicious clouds open along the horizon, and radiant light seals off the root of death. Returning to oneself, dim and silent, as though tipsy and entranced; the Great Elixir, like a grain of millet, sheds its shell and rests serenely in non-action. Wandering at ease across the expanse of Heaven and Earth, the myriad phenomena are like a pearl in the palm. Whoever can ingest this Medicine will match Heaven and Earth in lifespan; if it does not prolong life, then all my words are false.

§8Reply to the Yongle Emperor
Original (Chinese)

天机不肯轻轻泄，犹恐当今欠猛烈；千磨万难费辛勤，吾今传与天地脉；皇帝寻我问金丹，祖师留下长生诀；长生之诀诀何如，道充德盛即良图；节欲澄心澹神虑，神仙那有异功夫。

Heaven's secret will not be lightly divulged, lest the present ruler fall short of fierce resolve; through a thousand trials and ten thousand hardships, at great toil, I now transmit the very pulse of Heaven and Earth. The Emperor sought me out to ask about the Golden Elixir; the patriarchs left behind the formula for long life. What is that formula for long life? Abundant Way and flourishing virtue are the finest plan; restrain desire, clarify the mind, and calm thoughts and cares—what strange or exotic technique do immortals ever have?

§9
Original (Chinese)

〔原评〕此下又有五言一首云「金丹重一觔，闭目静存神；只在家中取，何劳向外寻；炼成离女汞，吞尽坎男精；金丹并火候，口口是元音。」系吕祖所作，盖当时书以答永乐皇帝者。

(Original commentary) Following this, there was originally another five-character poem: "The Golden Elixir weighs one catty; close your eyes and quietly preserve Spirit. Take it right within your own house—why toil to seek outside? Refine the Mercury of the Li maiden; swallow whole the Essence of the Kan youth. Both Golden Elixir and fire phasing—every word is the primordial sound." It was composed by Patriarch Lü, and was presumably written out alongside this piece in reply to the Yongle Emperor.

Key Terms

  • In Imitation of the Ancients (Fanggu): The author's own note explains that the title comes from the Cantongqi: "I dare not speak idly; I imitate the writings of the ancients."
  • Jiji and Weiji: Two hexagrams of the Book of Changes. Water above Fire is Jiji (Already Across, water and fire in conjunction); Fire above Water is Weiji (Not Yet Across, water and fire separated). Alchemists use Jiji to symbolize the union of heart and kidneys.
  • Moon appears in the geng quarter; gaining companions in the southwest: On the third day of the lunar month, the new crescent moon appears in the west (geng); "gaining companions in the southwest" comes from the Judgment of the Kun hexagram. Alchemists use this to signify the first stirring of yang and the tender sprouting of the Medicine.
  • Zhun and Meng: Hexagrams paired by alchemists with morning and evening fire phasing, from the Cantongqi's phrase "Zhun at dawn, Meng at dusk."
  • No effort at Mao and You: Mao and You are the hours of ablution (muyu), when the fire is paused and active effort ceases.
  • Knife-Tip (daogui): A metaphor for the union of the two Earths (wu and ji); see Treatise on the Two Earths Wu and Ji in this book.
  • Work of the outer elixir (waidan gong): This poem says Master Fire Dragon "knew my internal elixir was accomplished, did not speak of laying the foundation, and bestowed the work of the outer elixir," matching Author's Preface to Essentials of the Mystery. Here "outer elixir" is understood in light of On Ingesting the Great Elixir in this book: the Great Medicine that manifests after internal elixir completion, not mineral/chemical alchemy.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Letting go is the threshold of cultivation. Ladder to Heaven describes leaving parents' graves, wife, and child—we need not imitate the ancients' literal renunciation of household life, yet the resolve that "what others find hard to sever, this traveler can sever" merits deep reflection: genuinely letting go of clinging attachment.
  2. To mend depletion, first stop depleting. Oral Instructions in Person says human depletion is mended through the pivot of yin-yang creation—the prerequisite is first ceasing further drainage.
  3. "Abundant Way and flourishing virtue are the finest plan." This line can serve as a footnote to the entire scroll: restraining desire, clarifying the mind, and cultivating quiet simplicity are themselves the practice.

Translator's Notes

  1. "The origin holds no sign" (yuan han wu zhen): Zhen means subtle sign or omen.
  2. "The gates of the orifices converge as a bellows" (qiaomen hui yue): The original prints hui yue (彙籥), simplified as 汇籥; likely close in sense to tuoyue (bellows), referring to the opening and closing apertures of breath, retained as written.
  3. "Feast in the Central Yellow" (yanyin huangzhong): Central Yellow refers to the central Yellow Court.
  4. "In the Yellow Chamber sits the Mysterious Elder" (huangwu xuanweng): Yellow Chamber is the Central Palace; Mysterious Elder refers to Original Spirit.
  5. The two original commentaries and the note after Reply to the Yongle Emperor are all comments by the editor Li Xiyue (Li Hanxu), printed in small characters in the original and mostly signed "Yue."
  6. "Immortal Wang's edition" (Wangxian cangben): An older edition cited several times by Li Xiyue; the identity of Immortal Wang is unknown.
  7. "Whiskers bristling stiff as halberds" (fufu ran ru ji): Describes stiff, bristling facial hair.
  8. "Serve as a flying staff through the sky" (ke dang feikong xi): Xi is the pewter ringed staff carried by wandering monks and Daoists; "flying staff" refers to cloud-wandering.
  9. "Young tufted-haired child" (guanjiao er): Guanjiao refers to the ancient hairstyle of tying a young child's hair into two horn-like tufts, signifying early childhood.
  10. "Set out on the road" (liangzu mai yuyi): Yuyi means going forth on a journey or mission.
  11. "Changing the tripod brings forth a grandson anew" (huanding fu sheng sun): An alchemical metaphor for further refinement and transformation after initial elixir completion; see poems 29 (Changing the Tripod and Dividing the Embryo) and 30 (Transforming and Penetrating Spirit) in Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir in this scroll.
  12. "Reply to the Yongle Emperor": Emperor Chengzu of the Ming (Zhu Di, reign title Yongle) repeatedly sent envoys to seek Zhang Sanfeng without success. Whether this poem was actually composed in reply to an imperial summons has long been questioned.
  13. The poem quoted in the commentary—"The Golden Elixir weighs one catty... every word is the primordial sound"—states "take it right within your own house; why toil to seek outside?", explicitly directing the practitioner to seek the elixir within oneself.

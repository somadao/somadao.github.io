Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir (Part I)

Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir (Part I) — from nourishing the Dao and returning to truth, seeking the Mysterious Pass, and laying the foundation and refining the self, to recognizing Prior Heaven

About This Chapter

Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir is a cycle of seven-character regulated verses, each bearing its own subtitle and arranged in the progressive order of cultivation—virtually a complete compendium of internal alchemy written in poetry. This chapter presents the first eighteen poems (omitting Poems 12 and 17); the following chapter presents the remaining eighteen.

The arc of this first half unfolds as follows:

  • Establishing resolve (Poems 1–4): Nourishing the Dao and returning to truth, leaving the dusty world for seclusion, sweeping away sensory scenes to cultivate the mind, and battling the demon of sleep. "Turn back and shine the light inward—that is my home"; "rouse your spirit and sit until dawn."
  • Seeking the Mysterious Pass (Poems 5–7): The Mysterious Pass is "neither the heart nor the kidneys," nor is it found in the bladder, the anal tract, the spleen and stomach, or the Muddy Pellet—"it turns out to be only the place of luminous awareness"; "if in refining the elixir you wish to find the winter solstice, you must seek it within the stillness of the Spirit Terrace."
  • Laying the foundation and refining the self (Poems 8–11): Forging the spirit sword (the sword of wisdom), replenishing Breath and blood, remaining unstained by a single speck of dust, and forgetting all myriad anxieties. "How laughable is stubborn emptiness, sitting until one's hair turns white as snow"—mere blank sitting accomplishes nothing, even when one's hair has turned white.
  • Recognizing Prior Heaven (Poems 13–18): The tripod and vessel, True Lead, and Great Medicine of Prior Heaven are none of them external: "If you wish to know the true matter of the Golden Elixir, put your effort into the place before the body was born"; "such a supreme treasure exists in every household; it is only that foolish people hold biased views."

Note: Poem 12 (Starting the Practice of Refining the Self, "The principle of bodies apart yet spirits united is most detailed") and Poem 17 (Condensing Prior Heaven, "At the Gate of the Female a single grain of True Lead stirs") follow predominantly Yin-Yang dual-cultivation teachings and are omitted in accordance with this site's editorial policy.

Original Text and Translation

§1Poem 1 · Nourishing the Dao and Returning to Truth
Original (Chinese)

落魄江湖数十秋，逢师咬破铁馒头；十分佳味谁调蜜，半夜残灯可着油；信道形神堪入妙，方知性命要全修；自从会得些儿后，忘却人间万斛愁。

For decades I drifted impoverished across rivers and lakes, until I met a master and finally bit through the "iron bun." This exquisite flavor—who blended it with honey? A guttering lamp at midnight can still be given oil. Convinced that both bodily form and Spirit can enter the wondrous realm, I came to know that inner nature (xing) and life (ming) must be cultivated together in full. Ever since I grasped just this little bit, I have forgotten ten thousand bushels of worldly sorrow.

§2Poem 2 · Leaving the Dusty World for Seclusion
Original (Chinese)

一片闲心绝世尘，寰中寂静养精神；素琴弹落天边月，玄酒倾残瓮底春；五气朝元随日长，三花聚顶逐时新；炼成大药超凡去，仔细题诗警后人。

With a single expanse of unhurried mind, I cut off the dust of the world, quietly nourishing Essence and Spirit within the vast expanse of heaven and earth. My plain zither strums down the moon at the edge of the sky; the dark wine drains the last spring at the bottom of the jar. The Five Breaths return to the Origin, growing day by day; the Three Flowers gather at the Summit, renewing hour by hour. Having refined the Great Medicine and transcended the mundane, I carefully inscribe these poems to awaken those who come after.

§3Poem 3 · Sweeping Away Scenes to Cultivate the Mind
Original (Chinese)

纷纷内外景如麻，有地驰驱事可夸；撒手不迷真捷径，回头返照即吾家；六根清净无些障，五蕴虚空绝点瑕；了了忘忘方寸寂，一轮明月照南华。

Inner and outer scenes swarm in tangles like hemp, giving the mind fields to gallop through and exploits to boast of. Letting go without losing one's way is the true shortcut; turning back to shine the light inward is my home. The six sense roots are pure and clear, without the slightest obstruction; the five aggregates are empty space, devoid of the tiniest flaw. Lucidly aware yet effortlessly forgetting, the Square Inch of the heart rests in stillness—a single round bright moon illuminating Nanhua.

§4Poem 4 · Battling the Demon of Sleep
Original (Chinese)

气昏嗜卧害非轻，才到初更困倦生；必有事焉常恐恐，只教心要强惺惺；纵当意思形如醉，打起精神坐到明；着此一鞭须猛省，做何事业不能成。

Clouded Breath and a craving to lie down do no small harm; scarcely has the first watch arrived before drowsiness sets in. One must "always be engaged with it," ever vigilant and cautious, urging the mind to force itself to remain brightly awake. Even when thought grows dull and the body feels drunk, rouse your spirit and sit straight through until dawn. Strike yourself with this whip and wake up sharply—what undertaking could possibly fail to succeed?

§5Poem 5 · First Quest for the Mysterious Pass
Original (Chinese)

一孔玄关要路头，非心非肾最深幽；膀胱谷道空劳索，脾胃泥丸莫漫搜；神气根基常恍惚，虚无窟里细探求；原来只是灵明处，养就还丹跨鹤游。

The single aperture of the Mysterious Pass is the fountainhead of the vital path—neither the heart nor the kidneys, it is deepest and most hidden. Searching for it in the bladder or the anal tract is wasted labor; do not carelessly hunt for it in the spleen, stomach, or Muddy Pellet. The root and foundation of Spirit and Breath dwell always in the dim and indistinct; seek it carefully within the Cave of Emptiness. It turns out to be none other than that place of luminous awareness—nourish the Reverted Elixir to completion there, and ride a crane to roam the skies.

§6Poem 6 · Second Quest for the Mysterious Pass
Original (Chinese)

傀儡当场会点头，应知总是线来抽；抽他虽是依人力，使我人抽又孰谋；原赖主公常月白，期教到处好风流；炼丹若要寻冬至，须向灵台静里求。

A puppet on stage knows how to nod its head, yet you should know it is always strings that pull it. Pulling the puppet relies on human strength, yet who directs this person who pulls the strings? All along it depends on the Master within remaining ever as clear as moonlight, bringing grace and vitality wherever one turns. If in refining the elixir you wish to find the "winter solstice" (the return of the first yang), you must seek it within the stillness of the Spirit Terrace.

§7Poem 7 · General Discourse on the Mysterious Pass
Original (Chinese)

身即乾坤勿外求，虚灵一窍最深幽；二三自许同为侣，一四何妨与共俦；五土建中司发育，巽风起处定刚柔；自从识得还元妙，六六宫中春复秋。

The body itself is Qian and Kun—do not seek outside; the single empty, numinous opening is deepest and most secluded. "Two" and "three" naturally pledge themselves as companions; why should "one" and "four" not also pair together? Five-Earth establishes the central palace and governs generation and growth; where the Wind of Xun arises, firmness and yielding are settled. Ever since recognizing the marvel of returning to the Origin, throughout the thirty-six palaces spring follows autumn in endless renewal.

§8Poem 8 · Forging the Spirit Sword
Original (Chinese)

师传铸作青蛇法，坤鼎乾炉煅炼成；非铁非金生杀气，无形无影自通灵；掣来匣外乾坤窄，收入胸中芥子生；万两黄金无觅处，隐然身畔斩妖精。

My master passed down the art of forging the Green Snake sword, tempered to completion in the tripod of Kun and furnace of Qian. Neither iron nor metal, yet it generates a keen, decisive edge; formless and shadowless, it is naturally imbued with spirit. Drawn from its scabbard, all Qian and Kun seem cramped; sheathed back into the breast, it shrinks smaller than a mustard seed. Not even ten thousand ounces of gold can buy it—quietly by my side, it slays every phantom and demon.

§9Poem 9 · Laying the Foundation in Posterior Heaven
Original (Chinese)

气败血衰宜补接，明师亲授口中诀；华池玉液逐时吞，桃坞琼浆随日吸；绝虑忘思赤子心，归根复命仙人业；丹田温暖返童颜，笑煞顽空头似雪。

When Breath is depleted and blood is weakened, they ought to be replenished and rejoined; an enlightened master personally imparts the oral formula: swallow the jade liquid of the Flowery Pool hour by hour; sip the fine nectar of the Peach Grove day by day. Cut off anxiety and forget discursive thought to regain the newborn infant's heart; return to the root and restore life—this is the work of the immortals. As the Cinnabar Field grows warm and a childlike complexion returns, how laughable are those trapped in stubborn emptiness, sitting idly until their hair turns white as snow.

§10Poem 10 · Refining the Self in Posterior Heaven
Original (Chinese)

炼己功夫谁得知，精灵常与我相随；一尘不染心俱静，万虑皆忘性若痴；邪贼无由侵内境，学人终日侍严师；饥来解饮长生酒，每日醺醺醉似泥。

Who truly understands the work of refining the self? Pure spiritual lucidity walks constantly at my side. Unstained by a single speck of dust, the mind is utterly still; forgetting all myriad anxieties, one's inner nature seems almost simple-minded. Wicked thieves have no means to invade the inner realm, for the student attends a strict master all day long. When hungry, one knows how to drink the wine of longevity, pleasantly tipsy every day, mellow as clay.

§11Poem 11 · Refining the Self and Obtaining the Medicine
Original (Chinese)

炼己功夫继筑基，心头万虑已忘之；一轮月色相为伴，午夜雷声独自知；雪向静中飞白点，芽从虚处长黄枝；夺他阳气归来孕，产个千年跨鹤儿。

The work of refining the self follows upon laying the foundation, once the myriad anxieties of the heart have been forgotten. A single round moon keeps me company; the rumble of midnight thunder is known to myself alone. Within stillness, white snow scatters pale flakes; from emptiness, the Yellow Sprout puts forth golden shoots. Seizing that yang Breath and bringing it home to gestate, one gives birth to a thousand-year crane-riding child.

§12Poem 13 · The Tripod and Vessel of Prior Heaven
Original (Chinese)

一从识破鸿蒙窍，认得乾坤造化炉；不用神功调水火，自然灵气透肌肤；朝朝黄鹤藏金鼎，夜夜银蟾灌玉壶；要识金丹端的事，未生身处下功夫。

Once you see through the Opening of Primordial Chaos and recognize the creative furnace of Qian and Kun, there is no need for strenuous feats to adjust water and fire—numinous Breath naturally permeates skin and flesh. Morning after morning, the yellow crane hides within the golden tripod; night after night, the silver toad fills the jade pot. If you wish to know the exact truth of the Golden Elixir, apply your effort at the place before this body was born.

§13Poem 14 · The True Lead of Prior Heaven
Original (Chinese)

举世人多好入玄，入玄不识这先天；五千日内生黄道，三十时辰认黑铅；不在乾坤分判后，只于父母未生前；此般至宝家家有，自是愚人识见偏。

Most people in the world love to enter the mysterious, yet having entered the mystery they fail to recognize this Prior Heaven. Within five thousand days the Yellow Path is born; within thirty double-hours Black Lead is recognized. It is not after Qian and Kun separated, but solely before one's father and mother were born. Such a supreme treasure exists in every household; it is only that foolish people hold biased views.

§14Poem 15 · The Great Medicine of Prior Heaven
Original (Chinese)

信道金丹理最精，先天一点少人明；不拘贫富家家有，无论贤愚种种生；吞向腹中方有孕，将来掌上却无形；世人问我难回答，遥指天边月出庚。

Believe that the principle of the Golden Elixir is the finest of all, yet few understand that single point of Prior Heaven. Regardless of wealth or poverty, every household possesses it; whether sage or fool, it arises in every person alike. Only when swallowed into the belly does pregnancy begin; placed in the palm of the hand, it has no visible form. When worldly people question me, it is hard to answer—I simply point afar to the horizon, where the new moon emerges in the Geng direction.

§15Poem 16 · Seizing and Capturing Prior Heaven
Original (Chinese)

笑睹神州有妙玄，耳边切切细相传；凿开混沌寻金汞，劈破鸿蒙捉水铅；黍米一珠含北海，蟾光万道照西川；若人采得吞归腹，何虑凡夫不作仙。

Smiling, I see that this divine realm holds a wondrous mystery, whispered closely and carefully from ear to ear: chisel open Primal Chaos to seek Golden Mercury; cleave apart Primordial Undifferentiation to capture Water Lead. A single millet-sized pearl contains the entire Northern Sea; ten thousand beams of lunar toad-light illuminate the Western River. If someone gathers it and swallows it into the belly, why worry that an ordinary mortal cannot become an immortal?

§16Poem 18 · Recognizing and Gathering the Medicine
Original (Chinese)

日红海底山头月，灵山会上寻茄色；花开鸟唱一天春，颠倒龙涎配虎血；神光射入玉壶田，密数坤申子午诀；刀圭百日大丹成，丹成永做蓬莱客。

A red sun at the bottom of the sea, a bright moon above the mountain peak—seek that purple hue at the assembly on Spirit Mountain. Flowers bloom and birds sing in a skyful of spring; inverted, dragon saliva is paired with tiger blood. The light of Spirit shoots into the field of the jade pot, secretly counting the formulas of Kun-Shen and Zi-Wu. A spatula-tip of elixir over a hundred days brings the Great Elixir to completion—and once the elixir is complete, one becomes forever a guest of Penglai.

Key Terms

  • Iron bun (tie mantou): A Chan Buddhist expression used as a metaphor for a difficult barrier or enigma hard to crack.
  • Five Breaths return to the Origin; Three Flowers gather at the Summit: The Breaths of the five viscera returning to the central Origin, and Essence, Breath, and Spirit converging at the crown; see Five Breaths return to the Origin and Three Flowers gather at the Summit.
  • Always be engaged with it (bi you shi yan): From Mencius ("Gongsun Chou I"), meaning that the mind maintains a constant, steady attentiveness.
  • Brightly awake (xingxing): Clear, alert, and watchful.
  • Mysterious Pass (xuanguan): Poem 5 of this cycle states that it is "neither the heart nor the kidneys" and "turns out to be only the place of luminous awareness"; see Mysterious Pass.
  • Winter solstice (dongzhi): The moment in the year when the first yang returns; alchemists use it as a metaphor for stillness reaching its extreme and giving birth to movement—the first stirring of yang.
  • Spirit Terrace (lingtai): The mind-heart.
  • Two-three, one-four, and five-earth: Numbers from the River Chart (Hetu): 2 and 7 (Fire) pair with 3 and 8 (Wood) as one couple (Wood–Fire); 1 and 6 (Water) pair with 4 and 9 (Metal) as the other couple (Metal–Water); 5 and 10 (Earth) occupy the center. Alchemists use this to describe the meeting of the Three Families.
  • Spirit sword / Sword of wisdom: A metaphor for the discriminating wisdom that severs deluded thoughts.
  • Jade liquid of the Flowery Pool: Saliva in the mouth.
  • Stubborn emptiness (wankong): Sitting in mere blank vacancy without knowing the true pivot of life—frequently criticized by internal alchemists.
  • Yellow Sprout and White Snow: Images of the first sprouting of Prior-Heaven Breath; see Yellow Sprout.
  • Prior Heaven (xiantian): One's original state before father and mother were born; Poem 14 states, "It is not after Qian and Kun separated, but solely before one's father and mother were born."
  • Black Lead (heiqian): The yang within Kan; Prior-Heaven Breath.
  • Moon emerging in Geng: On the third day of the lunar month, the crescent moon first appears in the western Geng direction, symbolizing the first birth of yang.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Turning back to shine the light inward is my home. Cease chasing external things and turn awareness back toward the mind—this is the beginning of all cultivation (Poem 3).
  2. Countering the demon of sleep. Poem 4 teaches rousing one's spirit and staying brightly awake—when torpor strikes during quiet sitting, straightening the spine and opening the eyes for a moment is far more effective than grimly trying to endure it.
  3. The Mysterious Pass is not a physical organ. Poem 5 explicitly rules out the bladder, anal tract, spleen, stomach, and Muddy Pellet: "It turns out to be only the place of luminous awareness."
  4. Refining the self means forgetting anxious thoughts. "Unstained by a single speck of dust, the mind is utterly still; forgetting all myriad anxieties, one's inner nature seems almost simple-minded."
  5. Every household holds the supreme treasure. The treasure of Prior Heaven is innately present in everyone; there is no need to seek it outside, nor does it distinguish between rich and poor, wise and foolish.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original title of this cycle is Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir, with each poem carrying a subtitle and a circled numeral in the source edition. Here they are numbered Poem 1, Poem 2, and so forth, retaining the original subtitles verbatim.
  2. "A single round bright moon illuminating Nanhua": Nanhua refers to Zhuangzi (the Perfected Being of Nanhua), evoking a realm of clear, unencumbered emptiness.
  3. "Searching for it in the bladder or the anal tract is wasted labor": "Anal tract" (gudao) means the anus. Some practitioners of the era identified these anatomical spots as the Mysterious Pass; this poem rejects them all at once.
  4. "A puppet on stage knows how to nod its head": The wooden puppet symbolizes the physical body, while the one pulling the strings symbolizes the Master within—Original Spirit (yuanshen).
  5. "'Two' and 'three' naturally pledge themselves as companions; why should 'one' and 'four' not also pair together?": Understood through River Chart numerology; see Key Terms.
  6. "Throughout the thirty-six palaces spring follows autumn": Alludes to Shao Yong's famous line, "All thirty-six palaces are filled with spring."
  7. "Green Snake sword": Legend credits Lü Dongbin with a Green Snake sword; alchemists use it as a symbol for the sword of wisdom.
  8. "Fine nectar of the Peach Grove": A poetic metaphor for saliva.
  9. "Within five thousand days the Yellow Path is born; within thirty double-hours Black Lead is recognized": Alchemical numerological expressions interpreted differently across lineages; the gist is that Prior-Heaven Breath has its proper timing of emergence, and one must recognize the moment when the first yang stirs. Retained as written.
  10. "Seek that purple hue at the assembly on Spirit Mountain": The source text writes "eggplant color" (qiese), i.e., purple, symbolizing purple Breath and purple-gold radiance.
  11. "Secretly counting the formulas of Kun-Shen and Zi-Wu": Kun-Shen denotes the southwest, echoing "finding companions in the southwest" in Two Poems in the Ancient Style in this collection; Zi and Wu refer to advancing and retreating fire phasing.

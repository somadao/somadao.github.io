Twenty-Four Poems on the Golden Elixir (Part I)

Twenty-Four Poems on the Golden Elixir (Part I) — modeling the twenty-four solar terms: Prior and Posterior Heaven, lunar phases, setting up furnace and tripod, and water and fire

About This Chapter

Twenty-Four Poems on the Golden Elixir is another cycle of seven-character regulated verses. As Li Xiyue remarks, it is composed "to model the twenty-four solar terms"—twenty-four poems corresponding to the twenty-four seasonal nodes of the year. Unlike Thirty-Six Poems, this cycle carries no individual subtitles and leans more heavily toward imagery of fire phasing and Lead and Mercury. At the head of the cycle, Li Xiyue also notes specially that Liao Fusheng of Luzhou compiled poems by ancient immortals into Refined Words to Awaken to the Dao without naming their authors in order to pass them off as his own, and that he restored the text according to the authentic edition of Immortal Wang (that commentary is placed at the end of the next chapter).

This chapter presents the first twelve poems. Key points:

  • Poems 1–2: Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven share a single root; the moon has no light of its own and borrows the sun's light—using lunar phases to explain yin and yang.
  • Poems 3–4: Yang returns on the seventh day; the bellows (breathing) puffs and blows, drawing Kun-fire up to the summit of Kunlun and then swallowing it back into the Earthen Cauldron.
  • Poems 5–6: Setting up the furnace and tripod—water fears drying out and fire fears growing cold; "off by a hair's breadth, the work will not succeed."
  • Poems 7–10: Gathering Metal from the land of Kun and planting it in the house of Qian; water and fire within the body are yin and yang themselves; "who can fathom one's own inner tidings? Do not interject idle talk before others."
  • Poems 11–12: "Settling birth and death lies not in ordinary spirit and breath; the work of inner nature and life lies in Mercury and Lead"; refining the self to seek the true.

Original Text and Translation

§1Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 1
Original (Chinese)

采取先天炼后天，循环二炁共根源；欲知有象原无象，须识初弦与下弦；杳杳乾坤将判处，冥冥父母未生前；服之混沌犹如梦，变化婴儿寿万年。

Gather Prior Heaven to refine Posterior Heaven—the two Breaths circulate from one shared root. If you wish to know that "having form" was originally "formless," you must recognize the first quarter and the last quarter moons. In the dimness just before Qian and Kun divide, in the obscurity before father and mother were born: ingest it, and primal chaos feels like a dream, transforming into an infant who lives ten thousand years.

§2Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 2
Original (Chinese)

月本无光借日光，每从晦朔定阴阳；蟾乌交媾合真质，牛女相期入杳茫；自是魂灵应魄圣，从他地久与天长；学人解得玄中妙，紫府高悬姓字香。

The moon has no light of its own and borrows the sun's light; always from the dark moon and new moon are yin and yang determined. The lunar toad and solar crow unite to fuse the true substance; the Cowherd and Weaver Girl keep their tryst, entering the vast obscurity. Naturally the numinous ethereal soul responds to the sage corporeal soul, enduring as long as earth and heaven. When a student grasps this marvel within the mystery, their fragrant name hangs high in the Purple Palace.

§3Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 3
Original (Chinese)

七日阳来下鹊桥，上桥夫妇任逍遥；逆回海水流天谷，倒转风帆运斗杓；手握乾坤分造化，时凭年月步周遭；神仙手段常如此，那与庸夫斗舌苗。

On the seventh day yang returns and descends the Magpie Bridge; ascending the bridge, husband and wife roam freely at ease. Reversing the ocean tide to flow up into the Heavenly Valley, turning back the sails to revolve the Handle of the Dipper. Holding Qian and Kun in hand to apportion creation, pacing the full circuit in step with years and months. Such are ever the methods of the immortals—how could they stoop to bandy words with vulgar folk?

§4Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 4
Original (Chinese)

橐籥吹嘘藉巽风，搬来坤火自鸿蒙；徐徐摄上昆仑顶，渐渐吞回土釜宫；铅汞相投成至宝，精神凝合变婴童；将来跳出乾坤外，不属璇玑造化中。

The bellows puffs and blows by means of the Wind of Xun, transporting Kun-fire out of primordial chaos. Gently drawing it up to the summit of Kunlun, gradually swallowing it back into the palace of the Earthen Cauldron. Lead and Mercury unite to form the supreme treasure; Essence and Spirit congeal into a young child. In time one leaps beyond Qian and Kun, no longer subject to the turning of the celestial sphere and creation.

§5Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 5
Original (Chinese)

安炉立鼎炼金丹，水怕干兮火怕寒；既未煅时常守护，屯蒙行处要勤看；抽铅添汞须加紧，虑险防危莫放宽；毫发差殊功不就，半途而废复行难。

Setting up the furnace and standing the tripod to refine the Golden Elixir: water fears drying out and fire fears growing cold. Before the firing begins, guard it constantly; where Tun and Meng proceed, watch with diligence. Subtracting Lead and adding Mercury must be pressed closely; guarding against peril and anticipating danger must never slacken. Off by a hair's breadth, the work will not succeed; abandon it halfway, and taking it up again is hard indeed.

§6Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 6
Original (Chinese)

龙升虎降转河车，赤火擒来制白砂；二炁凝胎铅自减，三花聚顶汞还加；开炉漫搅成钟乳，起鼎应知是马牙；两物齐拏休纵放，放之失却美金花。

Dragon ascends and tiger descends, turning the Waterwheel; seizing red fire to subdue white cinnabar. As the two Breaths congeal the embryo, Lead naturally diminishes; as the Three Flowers gather at the Summit, Mercury increases still more. Opening the furnace and stirring gently yields stalactite essence; lifting the tripod reveals what you should know as horse-tooth niter. Grasp both things together and never let them slip—release them, and you lose the lovely Golden Flower.

§7Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 7
Original (Chinese)

采回坤地水金多，种在乾家入爱河；阳火阴符依进退，铅龙汞虎自调和；浆收东位成甘露，酒饮西方醉绮罗；但要至诚勤爱护，胎圆十月化青娥。

Gathering back from the land of Kun where water holds abundant metal, plant it in the house of Qian to enter the river of love. Yang fire and yin tallies follow advance and retreat; the Lead Dragon and Mercury Tiger naturally harmonize. The juice gathered in the east becomes Sweet Dew; the wine drunk in the west intoxicates amid fine silks. Only maintain utmost sincerity and tender vigilance, and when the embryo rounds out at ten months it transforms into the Azure Maiden.

§8Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 8
Original (Chinese)

夺得乾坤一点精，阴阳交媾自然成；夫妻会合攒三姓，戊己交加簇五行；朔望屯蒙鸡兔跃，晦弦既未虎龙争；地天收在玄关内，运转河车霹雳声。

Seize a single point of Essence from Qian and Kun, and yin and yang unite naturally to completion. Husband and wife meet to gather the Three Surnames; Wu and Ji intertwine to cluster the Five Phases. At new and full moons, Tun and Meng set rooster and rabbit leaping; at dark and quarter moons, Ji-Ji and Wei-Ji set tiger and dragon contending. Gather Earth-over-Heaven (Tai) within the Mysterious Pass, and turn the Waterwheel with the boom of a thunderbolt.

§9Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 9
Original (Chinese)

身中水火即阴阳，二炁相孚化紫光；日日醍醐延命酒，时时吞咽返魂浆；玄机不许庸人识，大药须令志士尝；九转功完还太始，坤柔炼尽变乾刚。

Water and fire within the body are yin and yang themselves; the two Breaths answer each other in trust and transform into purple light. Day by day one drinks the ghee-like wine that prolongs life; hour by hour one swallows the nectar that recalls the soul. This mysterious pivot is not for the vulgar to know; the Great Medicine is meant for the resolute aspirant to taste. When the nine-cycle work is complete, one returns to the Great Beginning—all Kun's yin softness refined away, transformed into Qian's pure yang firmness.

§10Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 10
Original (Chinese)

火性炎炎水性流，河车搬运自然周；昆仑片玉原无价，沧海明珠竟暗投；三昧初从离下发，一符始自坎中浮；自家消息谁能会，莫向人前插话头。

Fire by nature blazes upward and water flows down; carried by the Waterwheel, they complete their circuit naturally. A shard of jade from Kunlun is truly priceless, yet bright pearls from the blue sea are cast away in the dark. Samādhi fire first rises from beneath Li; a single tally begins to float up from within Kan. Who can fathom one's own inner tidings? Do not interject idle talk before others.

§11Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 11
Original (Chinese)

修仙须要修天仙，金液神丹仔细看；添在离宫抽在坎，寄于兑位种于乾；死生了当非神气，性命功夫在汞铅；世上纷纷谈道者，几人于此达真诠。

To cultivate immortality, one must cultivate Celestial Immortality—examine closely the Golden Liquid Divine Elixir. Add into the palace of Li, draw from the position of Kan; lodge it in the seat of Dui, plant it within Qian. Settling birth and death lies not in ordinary spirit and breath; the work of inner nature and life lies in Mercury and Lead. Among all the crowds in the world who chatter about the Dao, how many truly reach this authentic key?

§12Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 12
Original (Chinese)

炼己寻真固不难，先擒兔髓配乌肝；牵龙就虎归根窍，制汞投铅复命关；金气往来通夹脊，河车搬运上泥丸；夫妻共入黄婆舍，火候调停自结丹。

Refining the self to seek truth is truly not difficult: first capture the rabbit's marrow to pair with the crow's liver. Lead the dragon to the tiger to return to the root opening; subdue Mercury and cast it into Lead to restore the pass of life. Metal Breath moves back and forth through the Spine-Straddling Pass; the Waterwheel conveys it upward to the Muddy Pellet. Husband and wife enter together into the Yellow Matron's cottage—with fire phasing well balanced, the elixir congeals of itself.

Key Terms

  • First quarter and last quarter moons (chuxian, xiaxian): The waxing half-moon (eighth day) and waning half-moon (twenty-third day), used by alchemists to symbolize the moments when yin and yang are evenly balanced.
  • Dark moon and new moon (huishuo): The end of the lunar month and the beginning of the next, when moonlight vanishes completely—symbolizing extreme stillness.
  • Toad and Crow (chanwu): The toad in the moon and the three-legged crow in the sun, symbolizing yin and yang, Lead and Mercury.
  • Magpie Bridge (queqiao): The upper bridge where the tongue touches the palate and the lower bridge near the perineum—the junctions connecting the Conception and Governing vessels; see Magpie Bridge.
  • Handle of the Dipper (doushao): The handle of the Northern Dipper, symbolizing the pivot of cyclical rotation.
  • Bellows (tuoyue): A smith's bellows, symbolizing breathing.
  • Armillary stars (xuanji): The first four stars of the Northern Dipper, symbolizing the turning of the heavens.
  • Tun, Meng, Ji-Ji, and Wei-Ji: Four hexagrams of the Book of Changes paired by alchemists with daily or monthly fire phasing.
  • Stalactite essence and horse-tooth niter (zhongru, maya): External alchemy terms borrowed by internal alchemists to describe signs of the elixir's completion.
  • Rabbit's marrow and crow's liver: The jade rabbit in the moon and golden crow in the sun, symbolizing Lead and Mercury.

Putting It into Practice

  1. "Having form" was originally "formless." All the varied metaphors—Lead and Mercury, dragon and tiger, husband and wife—are "images" pointing toward the formless Prior Heaven. When reading this cycle, do not get entangled in the literal words.
  2. Guard against peril; miss not a hair's breadth. "Water fears drying out and fire fears growing cold"—neither excess nor deficiency will do. Quiet sitting works the same way: too tight produces agitation and dryness; too slack invites torpor.
  3. Keep your own inner tidings to yourself. Experiences arising in practice are for you alone to know—do not broadcast or boast of them to others (Poem 10).

Translator's Notes

  1. "On the seventh day yang returns and descends the Magpie Bridge": Alludes to the Fu (Return) hexagram in the Book of Changes, "returning in seven days."
  2. "How could they stoop to bandy words with vulgar folk?": Literally "contest tongue-sprouts" (shemiao), meaning the tongue.
  3. "Lifting the tripod reveals what you should know as horse-tooth niter": Horse-tooth niter (mayaxiao) is a crystalline saltpetre of external alchemy, borrowed here as a metaphor.
  4. "Plant it in the house of Qian to enter the river of love": "River of love" (aihe) is originally a Buddhist expression, borrowed here to depict the mutual attraction and union of Lead and Mercury.
  5. "When the embryo rounds out at ten months it transforms into the Azure Maiden": "Azure Maiden" (qing'e) symbolizes the numinous embryo after pure yin has been transformed into yang; alchemists also call it "Azure Maiden" or "Immortal Lady."
  6. "Settling birth and death lies not in ordinary spirit and breath": Means that transcending birth and death does not depend on mundane spirit and breath, but on Prior-Heaven Mercury and Lead (True Spirit and True Breath).
  7. All occurrences of "husband and wife" and "union" in this cycle are yin-yang metaphors referring to Lead and Mercury, Spirit and Breath within one's own body—consistent with Treatise on Pointing Out Delusions in Ascending to Heaven, "there are no forms of man and woman whatsoever."

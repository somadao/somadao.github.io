Part IV · Essentials of the Mystery, Upper Scroll

Eight Poems on the Great Elixir · Poem on the Viburnum Flower · Seven-Character Quatrains

Eight Poems on the Great Elixir (written in the Wudang Daoist chamber for his disciples), Poem on the Viburnum Flower, and Seven-Character Quatrains — the numinous medicine within the body is neither metal nor stone; neither forgetting nor forcing

About This Chapter

The closing group of the Upper Scroll of Essentials of the Mystery:

  • Eight Poems on the Great Elixir, carrying the original sub-note "Written in the Wudang Daoist chamber to instruct his disciples," was composed in his Daoist retreat on Mount Wudang for his students. The very first poem declares: "The numinous medicine within the body is neither metal nor stone; how could the divine cinnabar inside the belly be quicksilver?" Poem 3 outlines the clear levels of the Initial, Middle, and Final Passes alongside the Lower, Middle, and Upper Cinnabar Fields; Poem 6 warns, "Ten thousand side-doors and Daoist arts all come to naught without knowing fire phasing; first fathom the subtle principles to reach true awakening, then bow to an enlightened master to receive the oral formula"; Poem 7 teaches, "To seek truth, one must recognize the Opening of Emptiness—the work lies solely where intention arrives" and "as if forgetting yet as if retaining, guard the altar and hearth."
  • Poem on the Viburnum Flower: Celebrates the famous qionghua (viburnum) blossom of Yangzhou, using the flower to evoke the clear, unworldly elegance of the immortals.
  • Four Seven-Character Quatrains: "Worldly people cling to this tiny physical frame, failing to recognize the single mysterious pearl"; the final quatrain concludes, "If you have ever grasped that unbroken, continuous intent, that is the very moment of neither forgetting nor forcing"—summing up fire phasing in Mencius's phrase "neither forgetting nor forcing," a fitting epilogue to the entire Upper Scroll.

Note: There were originally five seven-character quatrains; the fifth ("One must seek one's own kind so the two delight together") follows predominantly Yin-Yang dual-cultivation teachings and is omitted in accordance with this site's editorial policy.

Original Text and Translation

§1Great Elixir · Poem 1
Original (Chinese)

学道修真出世尘，遨游云水乐天真；身中灵药非金石，腹内神砂岂水银；采炼功夫依日月，烹煎火候配庚辛；黄婆媒娉三家合，饮酒观花遍地春。

Studying the Dao and cultivating truth to step beyond worldly dust, roaming through clouds and waters to delight in heavenly simplicity. The numinous medicine within the body is neither metal nor stone; how could the divine cinnabar inside the belly be quicksilver? The work of gathering and refining follows sun and moon; the fire phasing of simmering and decocting aligns with Geng and Xin. With the Yellow Matron arranging the betrothal, the Three Families unite—drinking wine and viewing blossoms, spring fills the entire earth.

§2Great Elixir · Poem 2
Original (Chinese)

采聚他家一味铅，捉精炼炁补先天；前弦八两后弦八，内药还时外药还；紫府玄宫垂宝露，黄芽白雪化金蟾；神仙妙用常如此，火里能栽九节莲。

Gather and collect that single flavor of Lead from "the other house," seizing Essence and refining Breath to replenish Prior Heaven. Eight ounces at the first quarter and eight ounces at the last quarter; when the inner Medicine reverts, the outer Medicine reverts too. From the Purple Palace and Mysterious Hall precious dew descends; Yellow Sprout and White Snow transform into the golden toad. Such is ever the wondrous craft of the immortals: even in the midst of fire they can plant the nine-jointed lotus.

§3Great Elixir · Poem 3
Original (Chinese)

初关中关与后关，下田中田与上田；层次原来分井井，火功须要法乾乾；室窗透亮三更到，昼夜通红九鼎全；文武阴阳勤转炼，阖开万窍好朝天。

The Initial Pass, Middle Pass, and Final Pass; the Lower Field, Middle Field, and Upper Field. The stages are laid out in crystal-clear order; the fire work must emulate "Qian-Qian," unceasing self-strengthening. Light streams through the chamber window as the third watch arrives; glowing red day and night, the nine tripods reach completion. Diligently revolve and refine with civil and martial fire, yin and yang—opening and closing all ten thousand apertures, ready to attend before Heaven.

§4Great Elixir · Poem 4
Original (Chinese)

身内功夫我自知，天机玄妙有谁窥；初寻龙虎来争战，又见龟蛇喜唱随；天地倒颠观否泰，火符起止在虚危；南辰北斗映前后，日月乌蟾来往飞。

The work within my body I know for myself—who can peek into Heaven's subtle mystery? At first I seek Dragon and Tiger clashing in battle; then I watch Tortoise and Serpent harmonizing in joyful duet. Inverting heaven and earth, observe Pi (Stagnation) and Tai (Peace); the start and halt of the fire tallies rest in the mansions Xu and Wei. Southern Dipper and Northern Dipper illuminate front and back, while sun and moon—crow and toad—fly to and fro.

§5Great Elixir · Poem 5
Original (Chinese)

修真大道乾坤祖，采取阴阳造化功；要制天魂生白虎，须擒地魄产青龙；运回至宝归中舍，变化阳神入上宫；一炁凝成丹一粒，人能吞服貌如童。

The Great Way of cultivating truth takes Qian and Kun as its ancestor, gathering the creative work of yin and yang. To subdue the heavenly ethereal soul gives birth to the White Tiger; to capture the earthly corporeal soul produces the Green Dragon. Convey the supreme treasure back to the central dwelling, transforming into the Yang Spirit to enter the upper palace. A single Breath congeals into one grain of elixir—whoever swallows it regains the countenance of a child.

§6Great Elixir · Poem 6
Original (Chinese)

道法傍门有万千，不知火候总徒然；先穷妙理将真悟，后拜明师把诀传；欲使三家情意合，只凭一点道心坚；朝朝煅炼精神炁，结就真神上九天。

Daoist arts and side-doors number in the tens of thousands, yet without knowing fire phasing all come to naught. First fathom the subtle principles to reach genuine awakening; afterward bow to an enlightened master to receive the oral formula. To bring the Three Families into heartfelt harmony depends solely on one firm point of Dao-mind. Tempering Essence, Spirit, and Breath morning after morning, one forms the True Spirit and ascends to the Nine Heavens.

§7Great Elixir · Poem 7
Original (Chinese)

寻真要识虚无窍，功夫只在意所到；往来顺逆炼阴阳，升降坎离在颠倒；恍恍惚惚太极生，杳杳冥冥婴儿兆；出玄入牝由自然，若忘若存守坛灶。

To seek truth, one must recognize the Opening of Emptiness; the work lies solely where intention arrives. Going and coming, forward and reverse, refine yin and yang; the ascent and descent of Kan and Li lie in inversion. In the dim and indistinct, the Supreme Ultimate is born; in the deep and obscure, the sign of the infant appears. Emerging from the Mysterious and entering the Female follows spontaneity—as if forgetting yet as if retaining, guard the altar and hearth.

§8Great Elixir · Poem 8
Original (Chinese)

知先达后炼金丹，火冷水干做不全；上德无为成至圣，下功有作在周天；一阳动处窥天地，二品合时生佛仙；月里栽花无片晌，蟾光现出照西川。

Knowing what comes first and mastering what follows to refine the Golden Elixir—if fire grows cold or water dries up, it can never be completed. Superior virtue acts through non-doing to become a supreme sage; foundational work acts with effort within the Celestial Circuit. Where the single yang stirs, glimpse heaven and earth; when the two substances unite, Buddhas and immortals are born. Planting flowers in the moon takes not a single moment—the lunar toad's radiance appears and illuminates the Western River.

§9Poem on the Viburnum Flower
Original (Chinese)

琼枝玉树属仙家，未识人间有此花；清致不沾凡雨露，高标长带古烟霞；历年既久何曾老，举世无双莫浪夸；几欲载回天上去，拟从博望借灵槎。

Boughs of alabaster and trees of jade belong to the immortals' realm—who knew the human world possessed such a blossom? Its pure elegance stays unstained by common rain and dew; its lofty bearing forever carries ancient mists and rosy clouds. Through so many passing years, when has it ever grown old? Peerless in all the world—that is no idle boast. More than once I have longed to bear it back to Heaven, thinking to borrow the spirit raft from Marquis Bowang.

§10Quatrain 1
Original (Chinese)

真心浩浩无穷极，无限神仙从里出；世人耽着小形骸，一颗玄珠人不识。

The True Mind is vast and boundless, without end or limit; numberless immortals emerge from within it. Worldly people cling blindly to this tiny physical frame, failing to recognize the single mysterious pearl.

§11Quatrain 2
Original (Chinese)

佛印指出虚而觉，丹阳诀破无中有；捉住元初那点真，万古千秋身不朽。

Chan Master Foyin pointed out "empty yet aware"; Perfected Master Danyang revealed "presence within absence." Grasp firmly that primal point of truth, and through ten thousand ages one's being never decays.

§12Quatrain 3
Original (Chinese)

溯流一直上蓬莱，散布甘泉润九垓；从此丹田沾润泽，黄芽遍地一齐开。

Moving upstream straight up to Penglai, scattering sweet springs to moisten the nine regions of the earth. From this moment the Cinnabar Field drinks in rich moisture, and Yellow Sprouts bloom everywhere all at once.

§13Quatrain 4
Original (Chinese)

年月日时空有着，卦爻觔两亦支离；若曾会得绵绵意，正是勿忘勿助时。

Clinging to years, months, days, and hours is empty attachment; hexagram lines and ounces of weight are likewise fragmented. If you have ever grasped that unbroken, continuous intent, that is the very moment of "neither forgetting nor forcing."

Key Terms

  • Geng and Xin: Western Metal in the Heavenly Stems, symbolizing Lead.
  • Yellow Matron and Three Families: True Intention serving as matchmaker to unite Essence, Breath, and Spirit (or body, mind, and intention).
  • The other house (tajia): An alchemical expression paired with "my own house." In Treatise on Pointing Out Delusions in Ascending to Heaven in this collection, Zhang Sanfeng speaks of "the other house within my own body," referring to Prior-Heaven Breath within oneself.
  • First quarter and last quarter (qianxian, houxian): The waxing and waning half-moons, symbolizing yin and yang in equal measure; "eight ounces" and "eight ounces" symbolize matching in exact proportion.
  • Three Passes and Three Fields: The Caudal Funnel (weilü), Spine-Straddling Pass (jiaji), and Jade Pillow (yuzhen); and the Lower, Middle, and Upper Cinnabar Fields.
  • Qian-Qian: From the Qian hexagram of the Book of Changes, "the noble person exerts himself tirelessly all day long" (qianqian), unceasing self-strengthening.
  • Xu and Wei: Two of the twenty-eight lunar mansions located due north in the Zi position, symbolizing the beginning and stopping point of fire phasing.
  • Heavenly ethereal soul and earthly corporeal soul: Metaphors for True Yin within Li and True Yang within Kan.
  • Emerging from the Mysterious and entering the Female: The Gate of the Mysterious Female, the passage through which breath goes out and comes in; see Mysterious Female (Mysterious Opening).
  • Neither forgetting nor forcing (wuwang wuzhu): From Mencius ("Gongsun Chou I"): "Always be engaged with it, yet do not expect results; let the mind neither forget nor force its growth." Alchemists use this to describe fire phasing: neither neglect it nor force it.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The numinous medicine within the body is neither metal nor stone. The very first poem rejects external alchemy and the ingestion of mineral drugs.
  2. Understand the principles first, then seek a teacher. "First fathom the subtle principles to reach genuine awakening; afterward bow to an enlightened master to receive the oral formula"—only by studying and clarifying the principles first can you distinguish genuine teachers from false ones.
  3. As if forgetting yet as if retaining. Both Poem 7 and the final quatrain speak to the exact same balance: awareness barely there and barely absent—neither forgetting nor forcing. That is fire phasing.
  4. Do not cling to the physical shell. "Worldly people cling blindly to this tiny physical frame, failing to recognize the single mysterious pearl"—cultivation is not merely about pampering the physical body, but about recognizing the True Mind within.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Written in the Wudang Daoist chamber to instruct his disciples": A small-character note beneath the title in the original edition. Mount Wudang was Zhang Sanfeng's place of Daoist cultivation.
  2. "Gather and collect that single flavor of Lead from 'the other house'": Following Zhang Sanfeng's usage "the other house within my own body," this site understands it as Prior-Heaven Breath within oneself, not another person.
  3. "Even in the midst of fire they can plant the nine-jointed lotus": A metaphor for nurturing the elixir embryo amidst fire phasing.
  4. "Inverting heaven and earth, observe Pi (Stagnation) and Tai (Peace)": Heaven below and Earth above form Tai (free communication); the reverse forms Pi (obstruction).
  5. "Superior virtue acts through non-doing to become a supreme sage; foundational work acts with effort within the Celestial Circuit": Adapts Chapter 38 of the Classic of the Way and Virtue (Daode jing), "Superior virtue does not act and has no motive to act."
  6. "Thinking to borrow the spirit raft from Marquis Bowang": Marquis Bowang refers to Zhang Qian, who according to legend sailed a raft in search of the source of the Yellow River and reached the Milky Way.
  7. "Chan Master Foyin pointed out 'empty yet aware'; Perfected Master Danyang revealed 'presence within absence'": Foyin refers to the Song-dynasty Chan master Liaoyuan; Danyang refers to Ma Yu (Master Danyang), one of the Seven Perfected of Quanzhen.
  8. "Moving upstream straight up to Penglai": The original edition writes su with the variant character 泝, equivalent to 溯 ("moving upstream").
  9. For the list of works omitted from this scroll, see Translator's Note 9 of the opening chapter of this section, Author's Preface to Essentials of the Mystery.

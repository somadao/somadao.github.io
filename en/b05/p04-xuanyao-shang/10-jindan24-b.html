Twenty-Four Poems on the Golden Elixir (Part II)

Twenty-Four Poems on the Golden Elixir (Part II) — entering the chamber, guarding the city and fighting in the field, completing ten months of work, hiding one's light among ordinary folk, and bowing to the Star Lords

About This Chapter

This chapter presents Poems 13 through 24 of Twenty-Four Poems on the Golden Elixir, together with Li Xiyue's commentary on the cycle. Key points:

  • Poems 13–17: Opening and closing Qian and Kun like a bellows; Wu and Ji in the central palace serving as matchmaker; guarding the city and fighting in the field, entering the chamber to guard against peril; uniting the Three Surnames and clustering the Five Phases.
  • Poems 18–19: The true furnace and true tripod emit the true pivot, gathering the Medicine at the close of the Hai hour; once ten months of work are complete, "adding more fire will surely harm the elixir"—when practice reaches maturity, one must know when to stop.
  • Poems 20–21: Congealing the elixir and emerging from the shell; "subduing the tiger and taming the dragon require mingling with ordinary folk"—concealing one's brilliance and cultivating quietly amid the dusty world.
  • Poem 22: Names in a single breath major alchemical classics—Jinbi jing, Cantong qi, Zhixuan pian, Cuixu pian, Yinfu jing, Daode jing, Ruyao jing, and Wuzhen pian—declaring that what they earnestly urge again and again is nothing other than "the first quarter and the last quarter."
  • Poems 23–24: Fire phasing is fundamentally spontaneous; the cycle closes with devoutly bowing to the Star Lords to watch over and protect one's person.

Original Text and Translation

§1Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 13
Original (Chinese)

阖辟乾坤橐籥形，屯蒙呼吸坎离精；鼎和四象真铅降，炉备三才妙汞生；一有两无同变化，两无一有共相成；时人要识玄中妙，配合青娥仔细论。

Opening and closing Qian and Kun take the shape of a bellows; Tun and Meng match the breath as Kan and Li exchange their essence. The tripod harmonizes the Four Emblems and True Lead descends; the furnace completes the Three Powers and wondrous Mercury is born. One presence with two absences transforms together; two absences with one presence mutually fulfill each other. If people of our day wish to understand this marvel within the mystery, let them discuss closely how to pair with the Azure Maiden.

§2Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 14
Original (Chinese)

中宫戊己自知音，二物媒来共一心；姹女用吹无孔笛，金公为抱没弦琴；深深密密谁能测，杳杳冥冥孰解寻；指日还丹成就后，总教大地尽黄金。

Wu and Ji in the central palace are natural confidants, matchmaking the two substances into a single heart. The Lovely Maiden blows upon a holeless flute; the Lord of Metal cradles a stringless zither. Deep and secret—who can fathom it? Dim and obscure—who knows how to seek it? Soon, once the Reverted Elixir is brought to completion, the entire great earth will be turned to gold.

§3Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 15
Original (Chinese)

温温铅鼎透帘帏，认定人身活子时；虎啸一声忙采取，龙吟初动急施为；守城野战天翻地，入室防危坎复离；夺得团团龟凤髓，请君服食赴瑶池。

The warm lead tripod shines softly through the curtains—recognize firmly the body's own Living Zi Hour. At a single roar of the tiger, hasten to gather; at the first stirring of the dragon's chant, act without delay. Guarding the city and fighting in the field turn heaven and earth upside down; entering the chamber to guard against peril brings Kan back to Li. Having seized the rounded marrow of tortoise and phoenix, I invite you to ingest it and set out for the Jade Pond.

§4Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 16
Original (Chinese)

入室虚心炼大丹，神功妙用不为难；能窥天巧参元气，解饮刀圭夺紫丸；朔望符来三姓合，晦弦火退五行攒；羿妃纵会奔蟾窟，争似青娥驾彩鸾。

Enter the chamber with an empty heart to refine the Great Elixir—its spiritual work and subtle application are not difficult. Able to glimpse Heaven's craft and fathom Original Breath, knowing how to drink a spatula-tip and capture the purple pellet. When the tallies of new and full moon arrive, the Three Surnames unite; when fire recedes at dark and quarter moon, the Five Phases cluster. Even though Yi's consort knew how to flee to the lunar toad's grotto, how could that compare with the Azure Maiden riding a bright-plumed simurgh?

§5Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 17
Original (Chinese)

木中砂汞水中金，漫向离宫坎位寻；只就乾坤分主客，还依龙虎定浮沉；故能金木成三姓，遂使夫妻共一心；庭院归来相聚会，黄婆媒妁是知音。

Cinnabar-mercury within wood and metal within water—do not casually hunt for them in the palaces of Li and Kan. Simply distinguish host and guest by Qian and Kun, and determine rising and sinking by Dragon and Tiger. Thus Metal and Wood form the Three Surnames, and husband and wife come to share a single heart. Returning to the courtyard to gather and meet, the Yellow Matron as matchmaker is the true confidant.

§6Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 18
Original (Chinese)

真炉真鼎发真机，采药须凭亥尽时；铅母氤氲光欲动，金胎跳跃火临期；休忘气候调真息，但守虚无运坎离；临阵莫教轻纵敌，兢兢业业更防危。

The true furnace and true tripod emit the true pivot; gathering the Medicine must rely on the moment as the Hai hour ends. The Mother of Lead swirls in misty radiance, its light about to stir; the Golden Embryo leaps as the fire reaches its appointed time. Do not forget the seasonal timing while regulating True Breath; simply guard emptiness and circulate Kan and Li. On the battlefield, never carelessly let the foe slip away—remain reverently alert and doubly on guard against peril.

§7Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 19
Original (Chinese)

十月功完造化坚，若加火候必伤丹；仙房气血浑忘却，宝鼎金炉不用看；面壁九年形脱壳，身超三界体生翰；祇缘黍米吞归腹，行满功成跨凤鸾。

When ten months of work are complete and creation stands firm, adding further fire phasing will surely harm the elixir. Forget altogether the Breath and blood of the immortal chamber; there is no longer any need to watch over the precious tripod and golden furnace. After nine years facing the wall, the form sheds its husk; the body transcends the Three Realms and grows immortal wings. All because a single millet-grain was swallowed into the belly—merit fulfilled and work complete, one mounts phoenix and simurgh.

§8Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 20
Original (Chinese)

上吞下咯两弦弦，逐节隄防入玉田；往往来来宾返主，夫夫妇妇倒和颠；结丹已喜婴儿兆，出壳皆凭圣母全；远近遨游看四正，东西南北任周旋。

Swallowing above and drawing up below between the two quarter moons, guarding step by step as it enters the Jade Field. Coming and going, the guest turns back into the host; husband and wife invert and reverse. Once the elixir congeals, rejoice in the sign of the infant; emerging from the husk depends entirely on the Holy Mother's care. Roaming near and far to survey the four cardinal quarters, moving freely at will through east, west, south, and north.

§9Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 21
Original (Chinese)

韬光晦迹隐红尘，有作谁知妙更真；伏虎降龙须混俗，超凡入圣乃惊人；深深秘秘修丹道，白白明明显至神；药境玄机俱泄尽，古今由此达天津。

Sheathe your light and conceal your traces, hiding in the red dust—who knows that purposeful work holds a marvel truer still? Subduing the tiger and taming the dragon require mingling with ordinary folk; transcending the mundane to enter sagehood astonishes the world. Deeply and secretly cultivate the Way of the Elixir; brightly and plainly manifest supreme Spirit. Medicine, realms, and mysterious pivots are all laid bare here—from antiquity to the present, all reach the Heavenly Ford by this path.

§10Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 22
Original (Chinese)

金碧参同及指玄，翠虚性命与忠仙；阴符宝字逾三百，道德灵文贯五千；入药镜中推橐籥，悟真篇内究蹄筌；金丹切近叮咛语，总论前弦与后弦。

The Jinbi jing, Cantong qi, and Zhixuan pian, the Cuixu pian on inner nature and life, and the works of Zhongxian; the precious words of the Yinfu jing surpass three hundred, and the numinous text of the Daode jing runs through five thousand. Within the Ruyao jing one traces the bellows; within the Wuzhen pian one investigates snare and fish-trap. All the most intimate, earnest injunctions on the Golden Elixir discourse continually on the first quarter and the last quarter.

§11Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 23
Original (Chinese)

火候功夫本自然，能通此妙即神仙；五行攒簇盗天地，八卦循环作圣贤；造化炉中烹日月，乾坤鼎内产金莲；有人识得玄微理，随我飞升朝帝前。

The work of fire phasing is fundamentally spontaneous—whoever penetrates this marvel is at once an immortal. Clustering the Five Phases steals the secret of heaven and earth; cycling the Eight Trigrams makes one a sage. In the furnace of creation one simmers sun and moon; in the tripod of Qian and Kun blooms the golden lotus. Should anyone recognize this subtle, mysterious principle, follow me in flight to attend before the Celestial Sovereign.

§12Twenty-Four Poems · Poem 24
Original (Chinese)

虔诚稽首拜星君，顶礼星辰护本身；二十八宿齐朗耀，三百六度尽分陈；上圣能攒年月日，中宫保合气精神；照临应许增遐寿，掩映还同二曜新。

Devoutly bowing my head to the ground, I pay homage to the Star Lords, prostrating to the stars and constellations to guard my own body. All twenty-eight lunar mansions shine brightly together; all three hundred and sixty degrees are laid out in clear array. The supreme sages can gather years, months, and days, preserving and uniting Breath, Essence, and Spirit within the central palace. Beneath their shining watch, long life is surely granted—their radiance mingling, ever renewed like sun and moon.

§13
Original (Chinese)

〔原评〕此二十四首金丹诗，以象二十四气也，泸州廖复盛刊刻讹误，勦袭沽名，尝辑古仙诗歌为一部，名曰醒道雅言，而于群真姓字大半不书，欲使未见者诧为己作，正如丰翁所谓「鬼计悭贪窃道玄」者也，今照汪仙真本改正，以复旧观，庶使好道者不致迷误耳。

(Original commentary) These twenty-four poems on the Golden Elixir are modeled after the twenty-four solar terms. Liao Fusheng of Luzhou printed them full of errors and plagiarized them to fish for fame: he once compiled poems and songs by ancient immortals into a single volume entitled Refined Words to Awaken to the Dao (Xingdao yayan), omitting the names of most of the Perfected authors so that readers unfamiliar with them would marvel at them as his own compositions—precisely the sort of person Old Master Feng described as "scheming with ghostly greed to steal the mysteries of the Dao." I have now corrected the text according to the authentic edition of Immortal Wang to restore its original form, hoping that lovers of the Dao will not be led astray.

Key Terms

  • Opening and closing (hepi): Contracting and expanding, symbolizing breathing.
  • One presence with two absences (yiyou liangwu): An alchemical numerological expression symbolizing the mutual transformation of yin and yang, presence and absence.
  • Holeless flute / Stringless zither: Metaphors for the formless, soundless marvel.
  • Living Zi Hour (huozishi): The moment when the first yang stirs, independent of clock time; see Living Zi Hour.
  • Guarding the city and fighting in the field: "Guarding the city" symbolizes gentle, motionless nurturing; "fighting in the field" symbolizes gathering the Medicine and advancing the fire.
  • Three Surnames (sanxing): Body, mind, and intention; Essence, Breath, and Spirit; or the three families of Wood–Fire, Metal–Water, and Earth.
  • Spatula-tip / Purple pellet (daogui, ziwan): Metaphors for the elixir.
  • Yi's consort fleeing to the lunar toad's grotto: The legend of Chang'e flying to the moon.
  • Heavenly Ford (tianjin): The ford of the Milky Way, symbolizing the realm of the immortals.
  • Snare and fish-trap (tiquan): From "once you catch the rabbit, forget the snare; once you catch the fish, forget the trap"—symbolizing verbal teachings and expedients.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Know when to stop once the work is ripe. "When ten months of work are complete and creation stands firm, adding further fire phasing will surely harm the elixir"—every practice has its point of "enough"; going too far is as harmful as falling short.
  2. Cultivate quietly in the midst of ordinary life. "Subduing the tiger and taming the dragon require mingling with ordinary folk"—there is no need to set oneself apart eccentrically; do your quiet work within ordinary daily life.
  3. Read as many alchemical classics as you like, they all point to the same thing. Poem 22 calls the roll of the great classics across the dynasties and sums up all their injunctions as "the first quarter and the last quarter"—the balance of yin and yang and the exact measure of timing.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Discuss closely how to pair with the Azure Maiden": "Azure Maiden" (qing'e) symbolizes True Yin within Li.
  2. "The warm lead tripod shines softly through the curtains—recognize firmly the body's own Living Zi Hour": The Living Zi Hour is the moment when the first yang stirs within one's own body, not restricted to the midnight Zi watch.
  3. "Even though Yi's consort knew how to flee to the lunar toad's grotto, how could that compare with the Azure Maiden riding a bright-plumed simurgh?": Means that Chang'e stealing the elixir to flee to the moon cannot compare with refining the Great Elixir to completion and ascending on a simurgh.
  4. "The Jinbi jing, Cantong qi, and Zhixuan pian, the Cuixu pian on inner nature and life, and the works of Zhongxian": Refers to Jinbi guwen longhu shangjing, Zhouyi cantong qi, Zhixuan pian (attributed to Chen Tuan or Bai Yuchan), and Cuixu pian (by Chen Nan); "inner nature and life" (xingming) may refer to a work such as Xingming guizhi, while "Zhongxian" is unidentified and retained as written.
  5. "The precious words of the Yinfu jing surpass three hundred": The Classic of the Hidden Tally (Yinfu jing) contains just over three hundred characters.
  6. "Swallowing above and drawing up below between the two quarter moons": Rendered from the original wording; the general sense is mutual swallowing above and below in resonance with the two quarter phases.
  7. "Emerging from the husk depends entirely on the Holy Mother's care": "Holy Mother" (shengmu) symbolizes Original Spirit and True Intention that gently nurture the Holy Embryo.
  8. "Scheming with ghostly greed to steal the mysteries of the Dao" quoted in the original commentary: "Old Master Feng" refers to Zhang Sanfeng; the line comes from another fascicle of this collection and is retained as written.
  9. "Liao Fusheng of Luzhou": A Qing-dynasty figure of unknown biography who printed Refined Words to Awaken to the Dao (Xingdao yayan).

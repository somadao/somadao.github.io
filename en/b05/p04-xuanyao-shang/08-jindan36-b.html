Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir (Part II)

Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir (Part II) — True Lead and True Mercury, meeting of the Three Families, fire phasing, shedding the embryo, wall-gazing, and completing the Dao to save others

About This Chapter

Continuing from the previous chapter, this chapter presents Poems 19 through 36 of Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir (omitting Poem 35). The arc of this second half unfolds as follows:

  • Lead and Mercury (Poems 19–22): True Lead is the "radiance within the moon," extracting white from black; True Mercury is the "essence within the sun," fire hidden within wood. Lead and Mercury unite: "casting Mercury into Lead is like bathing the sun; restraining Mercury with Lead is like adding oil to a lamp." Poem 22 declares, "Do not seek the supreme medicine in mountain forests; you must search for True Lead amid the city"—the same meaning as Treatise on Pointing Out Delusions in Ascending to Heaven, "refine the self amid worldly dust, accumulate Lead in the marketplace": tempering oneself within ordinary life.
  • Congealing the elixir (Poems 23–26): Samādhi True Fire blazes forth and a peal of thunder arrives; the Yellow Matron acts as matchmaker and the Three Families meet; the nine-cycle Great Reversion; fire phasing wherein "year, month, day, and hour are gathered into a single moment."
  • Accomplishment (Poems 27–34): "Once the elixir is complete, my life no longer rests with Heaven"; nine years of wall-gazing, "silent and dim, just like refining the self"; changing the tripod and dividing the embryo, transmuting to spiritual penetration, nine-cycle numinous transformation, and cultivating the Celestial Origin; finally roaming free and at ease, completing the Dao and ferrying others across.
  • General ode on outer affairs (Poem 36): Using the metaphor of "husband and wife bearing a son, and the great-great-grandson founding a new estate" to depict the successive stages of internal alchemy.

Note: Poem 35 (General Ode on Inner Affairs, "Borrowing another's lead tripod to seek the true seed") and the appended Ode to the Prior-Heaven Tripod follow predominantly Yin-Yang dual-cultivation teachings and are omitted in accordance with this site's editorial policy.

Original Text and Translation

§1Poem 19 · Pointing Directly to True Lead
Original (Chinese)

真铅本是月中华，西北相生共一家；雌里怀雄成至宝，黑中取白见灵芽；金多水少方为贵，阴盛阳衰未足夸；更识其间包戊土，时时炉火起丹砂。

True Lead is fundamentally the radiance within the moon, generated in the northwest as one single household; the female embracing the male becomes the supreme treasure, and extracting white from black reveals the numinous sprout. Only when metal is abundant and water sparse is it precious; where yin flourishes and yang wanes, there is nothing to boast of. Recognize further that Wu-Earth is enclosed within it, so the furnace fire continually raises up cinnabar.

§2Poem 20 · Pointing Directly to True Mercury
Original (Chinese)

真汞原来日里精，东三南二自相生；木中藏火非闲说，雄里怀雌是宝珍；莫使一毫阴浊染，须教全体太阳明；其间己土培元气，炼得灵芽渐长成。

True Mercury is originally the essence within the sun, East-Three and South-Two naturally generating one another; fire concealed within wood is no idle saying, and the male embracing the female is a rare jewel. Let not a hair's breadth of turbid yin stain it, but make the entire body shine like the great sun. Within it, Ji-Earth nurtures Original Breath, refining the numinous sprout until it gradually matures.

§3Poem 21 · Lead and Mercury Uniting
Original (Chinese)

真铅真汞两相投，似漆如胶意未休；以汞投铅如浴日，将铅制汞若添油；铅调汞性常依傍，汞爱铅情乐泳游；内外五行攒簇定，结成赤白大丹头。

True Lead and True Mercury throw themselves into each other, clinging like lacquer and glue with endless devotion. Casting Mercury into Lead is like bathing the sun; restraining Mercury with Lead is like adding oil to a lamp. Lead tempers Mercury's nature, leaning ever close; Mercury delights in Lead's affection, swimming joyfully within. Once the inner and outer Five Phases are gathered and settled, they congeal into the red-and-white nucleus of the Great Elixir.

§4Poem 22 · The Wondrous Use of Inversion
Original (Chinese)

寻真访道有何难，只要人心识倒颠；休向山林寻至药，必须城市觅真铅；青龙锁住离交坎，白虎牵回兑入乾；此术效他行将去，自然有路上青天。

What difficulty is there in seeking truth and inquiring into the Dao? One only needs the human heart to recognize the principle of inversion. Do not seek the supreme medicine in mountain forests; you must search for True Lead amid the city. Lock the Green Dragon so Li joins with Kan; lead the White Tiger back so Dui enters Qian. Follow this method and practice it steadily, and naturally a road will open straight into the azure heavens.

§5Poem 23 · Harmonizing the Elixir Nucleus
Original (Chinese)

既生黄道始生荄，必有真阳应候回；三昧火从离位发，一声雷自震宫来；气神和合养灵质，心命相依结圣胎；透得里头消息子，三关九窍一时开。

Once the Yellow Path is born, the root begins to sprout, and True Yang inevitably returns in response to the season. Samādhi True Fire blazes forth from the position of Li; a single peal of thunder arrives from the palace of Zhen. Breath and Spirit harmonize to nourish the numinous substance; mind and life rely on each other to form the Holy Embryo. Penetrate the inner tidings of this process, and the Three Passes and nine apertures open all at once.

§6Poem 24 · Meeting of the Three Families
Original (Chinese)

央请黄婆善作媒，无中生有何栽培；却因姹女当时待，勾引郎君自外来；两窍相通无隔碍，中宫聚会不分开；翕然好合春无限，产个婴儿号圣胎。

Entreat the Yellow Matron to serve well as matchmaker—how does one cultivate bringing being out of non-being? Because the Lovely Maiden awaits at the appointed moment, drawing the Young Lord in from outside. The two apertures open into each other without obstruction; meeting in the central palace, they never separate again. Harmoniously united in boundless spring, they give birth to an infant named the Holy Embryo.

§7Poem 25 · The Nine-Cycle Great Reversion
Original (Chinese)

九转还丹下手功，要知山下出泉蒙；安炉妙用凭坤土，运火功夫藉巽风；兑虎震龙才混合，坎男离女更和同；自从四象归中后，造化机缄在我躬。

In beginning the work of the nine-cycle Reverted Elixir, one must know the meaning of the hexagram Meng, "a spring emerging beneath the mountain." The wondrous art of setting up the furnace relies on Kun-Earth; the work of circulating the fire depends on the Wind of Xun. Scarcely have the Tiger of Dui and Dragon of Zhen merged before the Boy of Kan and Girl of Li harmonize even more closely. Ever since the Four Emblems returned to the center, the hidden spring of creation lies within my own person.

§8Poem 26 · The Subtleties of Fire Phasing
Original (Chinese)

年月日时攒一刻，一刻不刻阴阳别；抽添符候兔鸡临，升降功夫龙虎烈；虑险防危罢火功，称铢分两同爻策；自然数足合周天，日日如斯行十月。

Year, month, day, and hour are gathered into a single moment, and within that moment—beyond ordinary moments—yin and yang divide. The tally-times of subtracting and adding arrive at Mao (Rabbit) and You (Rooster); the work of ascending and descending grows fiercest as Dragon and Tiger contend. Guarding against peril and anticipating danger, one halts the fire work; weighing grains and ounces matches the hexagram lines and counting stalks. Naturally the count is fulfilled in accord with the Celestial Circuit—practiced thus day after day for ten months.

§9Poem 27 · Shedding the Embryo and Spiritual Transformation
Original (Chinese)

丹成我命不由天，陵谷随他有变迁；荣辱无干随处乐，利名不挂逐时颠；但知壶内乾坤景，谁记人间甲子年；欲问归踪何处是，醉中遥指白云边。

Once the elixir is complete, my life no longer rests with Heaven; let hills and valleys shift as they will. Honor and disgrace touch me not—I find joy wherever I go; fame and profit never weigh on my mind—I play the carefree madman from moment to moment. Knowing only the scenery of Qian and Kun within the gourd, who keeps count of the sixty-year cycles of the human world? Should you ask where my homeward trail leads, tipsy I point afar toward the edge of the white clouds.

§10Poem 28 · Nine Years Facing the Wall
Original (Chinese)

九年面壁养神体，默默昏昏如炼己；无束无拘得自由，随缘随分能知止；心同日月大辉光，我与乾坤为表里；打破虚空不等闲，收拾六合一黍米。

Nine years facing the wall to nourish the spirit body—silent and dim, just like refining the self. Free of all bonds and constraints, one attains true liberty; flowing with circumstances and knowing one's measure, one knows when to stop. The mind shines with vast radiance like sun and moon; Qian and Kun and I are each other's outer and inner facets. Shattering empty space is no ordinary feat—gathering the six directions of the cosmos into a single grain of millet.

§11Poem 29 · Changing the Tripod and Dividing the Embryo
Original (Chinese)

重安炉鼎立坤乾，巧手移丹入上田；道德崇高神益迈，虚空粉碎法无边；从今易舍还成质，以后分胎又入玄；儿养孙兮孙养子，老翁老母一齐仙。

Setting up furnace and tripod anew to establish Kun and Qian, a deft hand moves the elixir into the upper Cinnabar Field. With Dao and Virtue lofty, Spirit soars higher still; empty space shatters to dust, and spiritual power knows no bounds. From now on, changing dwellings still forms a real body; thereafter, dividing the embryo enters the mystery once more. Son raises grandson and grandson raises son—the old father and old mother become immortals together.

§12Poem 30 · Transmuting to Spiritual Penetration
Original (Chinese)

养得玄孙渐长成，强宗胜祖善谋营；昨宵灯下逢佳偶，今日堂前产俊英；个个孩儿森玉树，飘飘仙嗣簇金茎；一班胜似连城璧，无税良田只自耕。

Nourishing the great-great-grandson until he gradually grows to maturity, strengthening the lineage beyond its forebears and skilled in stewardship. Last night beneath the lamp he met a worthy spouse; today before the hall are born gifted heirs. Every child stands tall and lush as a jade tree; airy immortal descendants cluster round the golden stalk. This whole troop surpasses jade discs worth walled cities—fertile tax-free fields, tilled entirely by oneself.

§13Poem 31 · Nine-Cycle Numinous Transformation
Original (Chinese)

九转丹砂岁月深，养成舍利鬼神钦；一炉白雪浑如玉，满鼎黄芽胜似金；曾见鸾飞和凤舞，但看虎啸与龙吟；五金八石皆成宝，还炼天元拔宅升。

Nine cycles of cinnabar over deep, long years nourish a relic revered by ghosts and spirits alike. A furnace of White Snow, pure and seamless as jade; a tripod brimming with Yellow Sprout, surpassing gold. Having witnessed simurghs soaring and phoenixes dancing, now watch only the tiger roar and the dragon chant. Five metals and eight minerals all turn into treasure; refining further the Celestial Origin, one ascends with the whole household.

§14Poem 32 · Cultivating the Celestial Origin
Original (Chinese)

天元丹品问谁知，有自无生世所稀；天地为炉真造化，阴阳作药自玄微；鸡餐变鹤青云去，犬食成龙白昼飞；到此方称高妙极，许君携手一同归。

Who knows the rank of the Celestial Origin elixir? Born from being into non-being, it is rare in this world. Taking heaven and earth as the furnace is true creation; taking yin and yang as Medicine is naturally subtle and mysterious. Chickens that taste it turn into cranes and depart into the azure clouds; dogs that eat it become dragons and fly off in broad daylight. Only at this point can it be called the pinnacle of loftiness and wonder—granting that you and I may go home hand in hand.

§15Poem 33 · Roaming Free and Unfettered
Original (Chinese)

道人久已泯耳目，潇洒自如脱拘束；朝从扶桑日头起，暮去昆仑云脚宿；青牛过关知几年，此道分明在眼前；昨夜瑶琴三叠后，一天风冷月娟娟。

The Daoist long ago dissolved eyes and ears, roaming with effortless grace, free of all restraint. At dawn he rises where the sun climbs over Fusang; at dusk he lodges beneath the cloud-fringed base of Kunlun. Who knows how many years have passed since the blue ox crossed the pass? Yet this Dao stands plainly right before your eyes. Last night, after three refrains upon the jade zither, a cool wind filled the sky and the moon shone serene and pure.

§16Poem 34 · Completing the Dao and Saving Others
Original (Chinese)

铁笛双吹破晓烟，相逢又隔几多年；曾将物外无为事，付在毫端不尽传；白发数茎君已老，青云几度我当先；世间穷究只如此，何若同游归洞天。

Two iron flutes blow together, parting the mists of dawn—how many years have passed since we last met? I once committed those effortless matters beyond things to the tip of my brush, yet could never finish transmitting them. With strands of white hair, you have already grown old, while I have walked ahead through the azure clouds time and again. Push worldly affairs to their utmost end and they amount to no more than this—why not journey together back to the grotto-heavens?

§17Poem 36 · General Ode on Outer Affairs
Original (Chinese)

真铅真汞结夫妻，两意交欢产个儿；幼子成人离祖业，玄孙主事建新基；恢宏家道成三业，大展门庭第九枝；满室堆金何足羡，但看拔宅住瑶池。

True Lead and True Mercury unite as husband and wife; in mutual harmony they give birth to a child. The young son grows to adulthood and leaves the ancestral estate; the great-great-grandson takes charge and builds a new foundation. Expanding the family fortune, he achieves the three accomplishments; spreading the household wide, he reaches the ninth branch. What is worth envying in a room piled high with gold? Just watch the whole household ascend to dwell by the Jade Pond.

Key Terms

  • True Lead and True Mercury: Poems 19 and 20 describe Lead as the "radiance within the moon" and "female embracing male," and Mercury as the "essence within the sun" and "male embracing female"—that is, the yang within Kan and the yin within Li.
  • Wu-Earth and Ji-Earth: The earth enclosed within Lead is Wu-Earth; the earth cultivated within Mercury is Ji-Earth. See Treatise on the Two Earths Wu and Ji in this collection.
  • Yellow Matron (huangpo): A metaphor for True Intention, acting as matchmaker for Lead and Mercury; see Yellow Matron (True Intention).
  • Meeting of the Three Families (sanjia xiangjian): The union of Essence, Breath, and Spirit (or body, mind, and intention) into one.
  • A spring emerging beneath the mountain (Meng): From the Image commentary to the Meng hexagram in the Book of Changes: "Beneath the mountain emerges a spring: Meng."
  • Rabbit and Rooster: Mao (Rabbit) and You (Rooster), the times of ablution (muyu).
  • Lines and counting stalks (yaoce): The divination stalks and line-numbers of the hexagrams, symbolizing the exact measure of fire phasing.
  • Changing the tripod and dividing the embryo / Transmuting to spiritual penetration: Further refinement and transformation after the elixir is formed, which alchemists depict through the metaphor of begetting sons and grandsons.
  • Celestial Origin Elixir (tianyuan dan): The highest grade among the three elixirs—Celestial Origin, Earthly Origin, and Human Origin—spoken of by alchemists.
  • The blue ox crossing the pass: The legend of Laozi riding a blue ox out through the Hangu Pass.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Refine yourself within ordinary life. "Do not seek the supreme medicine in mountain forests; you must search for True Lead amid the city"—there is no need to flee into the mountains; tempering mind and nature amid city life and daily work is itself accumulating Lead.
  2. When the Four Emblems return to the center, creation lies within oneself. All cultivation ultimately comes to rest in the "center"—a mind neither leaning nor biased.
  3. Accomplishment means ease and freedom. "Honor and disgrace touch me not—I find joy wherever I go; fame and profit never weigh on my mind"—this is both the goal of internal alchemy and the test of practice: becoming progressively freer and less calculating.
  4. Wall-gazing is sustained refining of the self. "Nine years facing the wall to nourish the spirit body—silent and dim, just like refining the self."

Translator's Notes

  1. "Only when metal is abundant and water sparse is it precious; where yin flourishes and yang wanes, there is nothing to boast of": Only when Metal (yang) is abundant and Water (yin) sparse within Lead is it True Lead.
  2. "East-Three and South-Two naturally generating one another": River Chart numerology—East-Three is Wood and South-Two is Fire, Wood generating Fire.
  3. "Do not seek the supreme medicine in mountain forests; you must search for True Lead amid the city": Yin-Yang dual-cultivation schools often cite this line as support; following Zhang Sanfeng's own Treatise on Pointing Out Delusions in Ascending to Heaven ("refine the self amid worldly dust, accumulate Lead in the marketplace"), this site understands it as cultivating mind and nature within ordinary worldly life.
  4. "Lock the Green Dragon so Li joins with Kan; lead the White Tiger back so Dui enters Qian": A metaphor for drawing from Kan to fill Li and returning to the pure body of Qian.
  5. "Because the Lovely Maiden awaits at the appointed moment, drawing the Young Lord in from outside": The Lovely Maiden symbolizes True Mercury within Li, and the Young Lord (Lord of Metal) symbolizes True Lead within Kan—both reside within one's own body.
  6. "Year, month, day, and hour are gathered into a single moment": Alchemists often speak of "compressing the year into a month, the month into a day, the day into a double-hour," condensing a full year's fire phasing into a single period.
  7. "Relic (sheli, Skt. śarīra)": Originally a Buddhist term, here used as a metaphor for the elixir.
  8. "Five metals and eight minerals all turn into treasure": Five metals and eight minerals are substances of external alchemy, here symbolizing the elixir's power to transmute all things.
  9. "Chickens that taste it turn into cranes" and "dogs that eat it become dragons": Parallel to "chickens eat it and become phoenixes; dogs eat it and become dragons" in Treatise on the Verifications of Reversion in this collection—both are hyperbolic metaphors.
  10. "After three refrains upon the jade zither": "Three refrains" (sandie) refers to a zither piece repeated three times.
  11. Poem 36 uses the worldly imagery of founding a family and establishing an estate to symbolize the layered progressions of internal alchemy; rendered faithfully from the original.

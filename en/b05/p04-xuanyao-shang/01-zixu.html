Author's Preface to Essentials of the Mystery

Author's Preface to Essentials of the Mystery — Zhang Sanfeng recounts his quest for the Way, his meeting with Master Fire Dragon, and his emergence from the mountains to complete cultivation

About This Chapter

Essentials of the Mystery (Xuanyao pian) is juan 4 of The Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng (Sanfeng quanji), collecting Zhang Sanfeng's songs, poems, and lyrics on internal alchemy in two parts (upper and lower). This chapter is his own preface, dated the eleventh year of the Yongle reign of the Ming dynasty (1413).

The preface recounts three things:

  1. The hardship of seeking the Way: Seeing through the impermanence of wealth and honor, he abandoned rank and worldly ambition, wandered over lakes and seas, and visited many teachers—yet everything he was taught turned out to be "side-door minor methods" that brought no benefit to body or mind.
  2. Meeting a master and receiving the teaching: During the Yanyou era of the Yuan dynasty he entered Mount Zhongnan and met Master Fire Dragon (said to be an eminent disciple of Chen Tuan), who transmitted everything in full—from refining the self, obtaining the Medicine, fire phasing, and warm nurturing to shedding the embryo, spiritual transformation, and completing emptiness. When his master said that cultivating this Way requires "both the method and the financial means," and Zhang had already exhausted his family fortune, the master also imparted the art of alchemical transmutation.
  3. Leaving the mountains and attaining completion: Mingling his light with the ordinary world, he first accomplished the foundational cultivation, then chose an auspicious site to build a thatched hermitage, sat in a quiet chamber to empty his mind and nurture Spirit, and attained the Way through ten months of warm nurturing and nine years of facing the wall. Finally he compiled his songs, treatises, poems, and lyrics on cultivation into this book, titled Essentials of the Mystery (also known as Swift Essentials).

Editorial note on selection: A portion of the songs and poems in Essentials of the Mystery uses the vocabulary of the yin-yang (paired) school ("tripod and vessel," "bedchamber," "same kind," and so forth). Following this site's editorial standard, works centered on that approach are omitted in their entirety, and only works in the pure-stillness (qingjing) lineage—readable in the light of Zhang Sanfeng's Clarifying Delusions on Ascending to Heaven—are included. Omitted pieces are listed in the translator's notes for each chapter. As the general preface to the entire book, this piece is retained; expressions such as "tripod and vessel" and "the other's Lead" are understood in accordance with Zhang Sanfeng's own words in Clarifying Delusions on Ascending to Heaven: "all are metaphors for the inner and outer yin and yang of my own single body, with no distinction of male and female whatsoever."

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

玄素叹人生光阴有限，富贵无常，若风灯草露，存没倏忽，自古及今，比比皆然，深可惊省。以是日夕希慕大道，弃功名，撇势利，云游湖海，遍访名师，所授虽多，总皆旁门小法，行于身心无所益也；考诸丹经而又不合，与道乖违，徒劳勤苦，性命惶惶，不得一遇至人，以了生平之愿！

I, Zhang Xuansu, lament that the span of human life is limited and that wealth and rank are impermanent—like a lamp in the wind or dew on the grass, appearing and vanishing in an instant. From antiquity to the present, this has been so everywhere, enough to startle one awake. Therefore, morning and night I longed for the Great Way; I cast aside office and fame, set aside power and profit, wandered like a cloud across lakes and seas, and visited renowned teachers everywhere. Though what I received was plentiful, it was all side-door minor methods, yielding no benefit when practiced in body and mind. When tested against the alchemical classics, none of it accorded; it ran contrary to the Way, wasting my toil and hardship while my inner nature and life remained anxious and unsettled—never once encountering a Perfected One to fulfill the wish of my entire life!

§2
Original (Chinese)

延祐间，幸天怜我，初入终南，得遇火龙先生，询是图南高弟，绿鬓朱颜，俨乎物外神仙，春秋不知其几许矣。玄素异之，礼拜师事，跪问大道。蒙师慈悲，鉴我精诚，初指炼己功夫，次传得药口诀，再示火候细微，与夫温养节度、脱胎神化、了当虚空之旨，无不一一备悉，真所谓口口相传，心心相授，得闻斯道，何幸如之！

During the Yanyou reign of the Yuan dynasty, Heaven mercifully took pity on me: on first entering Mount Zhongnan, I met Master Fire Dragon. Upon inquiry, I learned he was a senior disciple of Chen Tunan (Chen Tuan); with raven-black hair and a ruddy countenance, he appeared every bit an immortal beyond the mundane world, of uncounted years. Struck by how extraordinary he was, I bowed in reverence, took him as my teacher, and knelt to ask about the Great Way. Through the master's compassion, seeing my wholehearted sincerity, he first pointed out the work of refining the self, next transmitted the oral instructions for obtaining the Medicine, and then revealed the subtleties of fire phasing, together with the measured degrees of warm nurturing, shedding the embryo and spiritual transformation, and the ultimate purport of completing emptiness. Nothing was left out; every point was explained in full. Truly this was what is called transmission from mouth to mouth and bestowal from mind to mind. To be able to hear this Way—what good fortune could compare!

§3
Original (Chinese)

又云：「造斯道者，必须法财两用，而后可以有为。」乃玄素以勤于游访，兼颇好善，倾囊倒箧殆尽，安能造斯道哉！不觉每日忧形于色。师遂怪而问之，玄素又挥泪跪告，重蒙怜悯，授以丹砂点化之术，及赐汞见立干之药。玄素遂出山双修性命，乃拜辞恩师。

He further said: "Whoever would accomplish this Way must make use of both the method and material means; only then can one take effective action." Yet because I had traveled diligently in search of teachers and was also fond of charitable deeds, I had emptied my purse and chests nearly bare—how could I possibly accomplish this Way? Unawares, worry showed on my face every day. The master found this strange and asked me why; weeping, I knelt again and told him. Once more moved to compassion, he imparted to me the art of cinnabar transmutation and bestowed a substance that "dries Mercury on sight." I then left the mountains to practice dual cultivation of inner nature and life, bowing in farewell to my gracious master.

§4
Original (Chinese)

于是和光混俗，觅真铅八两，真汞半斤，同入造化炉中，锻炼日魂月魄，攒簇五行，和合四象，水火配对，金木调停，真土凝结，交媾温养，转制分胎，三次超脱，九转以周，自见黄芽白雪，明干点化，妙不可言。到此何患无财以了大事！特择善地，起盖茅庵，端坐静室，虚心养神，安神养气，气慧神清，方求鼎器。

Thereupon I dimmed my light and mingled with the ordinary world, sought out "eight ounces of True Lead and half a catty of True Mercury," and placed them together in the furnace of creation—tempering the sun's ethereal soul and the moon's corporeal soul, gathering the Five Phases, harmonizing the Four Emblems, pairing Water and Fire, reconciling Metal and Wood, congealing True Earth, joining and warmly nurturing, transforming and dividing the embryo, transcending three times, and completing the nine revolutions. Naturally Yellow Sprouts and White Snow appeared, dry and luminous in transmutation, subtle beyond words. Reaching this stage, why fear lacking the means to settle the great affair?

Accordingly, I specially chose an auspicious site, built a thatched hermitage, and sat upright in a quiet chamber, emptying my mind to nurture Spirit and settling Spirit to nurture Breath, until Breath was lucid and Spirit clear—only then did I seek the "tripod and vessel" (setting up the furnace and tripod to enter the great work).

§5
Original (Chinese)

暮饮蟠桃酒，朝餐玉液波，如醉如痴，若婴儿赤子，补气补血，丹田温煖，返老还童。自觉汞有半斤，可待他铅八两。是数者，须候月之明圆，其铅花自然露见，下手擒龙捉虎，采彼先天一点真铅，吞入腹中，能干我汞，徐徐火候烹煎，自有斤两法度，既合周天之数，又必爻策无差，进火退符知复姤，虑险防危识卯酉，十月功完圣胎显象，九年面壁与道合真，跨鹤青天如大路，任他沧海变桑田——此大丈夫功成名遂之时也！始信有此出世之法，虽有拱璧以先驷马，争如坐进此道。皆因广积阴功，累行方便，得遇至人而成也。

At dusk I drank the wine of the Peaches of Immortality, at dawn I sipped the waves of Jade Fluid—as though tipsy, as though entranced, like a newborn infant—replenishing Breath and blood, warming the Cinnabar Field, and returning from old age to youth. Sensing within myself that Mercury had reached its half-catty, I could await the eight ounces of "the other's Lead." In this reckoning, one must wait until the moon is bright and full, when the Lead Blossom naturally reveals itself; then one sets to work seizing the Dragon and capturing the Tiger, gathering that single speck of True Lead of Prior Heaven and swallowing it into the belly so that it dries and fixes my Mercury. Gently simmering with fire phasing, there is an exact measure of weights and degrees: it accords with the numbers of the Celestial Circuit and makes no error in lines and stalks—knowing Fu (Return) and Gou (Meeting) for advancing the fire and withdrawing the tally, and recognizing Mao and You to guard against peril and danger. When ten months of work are complete, the Holy Embryo manifests its form; after nine years of facing the wall, one merges in truth with the Way. Riding a crane across the azure sky as though walking a highway, let the blue seas turn into mulberry fields—this is the hour when a person of great resolve achieves merit and fulfillment! Only then did I truly believe that such a world-transcending method exists: even though one has jade disks borne before four-horse chariots, how can that compare with sitting and advancing in this Way? All of this came from widely accumulating hidden merit and continually practicing kindness, whereby I was able to meet a Perfected One and attain completion.

§6
Original (Chinese)

玄素幸荷天庇，得以有成，虽不敢妄泄真传，亦不敢缄默闭道，因是作为修炼内外金丹歌论诗词，编次成录，以觉后学，名曰《玄要篇》（一曰：《捷要篇》）。所论其行道之工夫，与得道之口诀，及成道之旨趣，诚无有切于此者矣。倘有志之士，得遇是书，虽不得玄素之亲传，又奚异玄素之面授也哉！

——永乐十一年癸巳岁孟秋月既望日元邋遢道人张玄素三丰自序

By good fortune I have enjoyed Heaven's protection and attained completion. Though I dare not recklessly divulge the true transmission, neither do I dare remain silent and seal off the Way. Therefore I have composed these songs, treatises, poems, and lyrics on cultivating the inner and outer Golden Elixir, arranging them into a single record to awaken later students, entitled Essentials of the Mystery (also called Swift Essentials). As for what it sets forth concerning the practice of walking the Way, the oral instructions for obtaining the Way, and the purport of accomplishing the Way, nothing could be more direct and pertinent. Should a person of resolve encounter this book, even without receiving my personal transmission, how would it differ from my instructing them face to face!

— Author's preface by the Yuan-dynasty Scruffy Daoist, Zhang Xuansu Sanfeng, on the sixteenth day of the seventh lunar month in the guisi year, the eleventh year of the Yongle reign (1413).

Key Terms

  • Master Fire Dragon (Huolong xiansheng): Zhang Sanfeng's legendary teacher; this preface identifies him as an eminent disciple of Chen Tuan (courtesy name Tunan).
  • Both method and means (fa cai liang yong): "Method" (fa) refers to oral instructions and practice; "means" (cai) refers to the material support, dwelling, and protection needed for sustained cultivation.
  • Eight ounces of True Lead, half a catty of True Mercury: In traditional Chinese weight, eight ounces and half a catty are each half of a sixteen-ounce catty—a standard alchemical metaphor for Lead and Mercury (Breath and Spirit) matched in equal measure.
  • Drying Mercury on sight (gong jian li gan): Mercury solidifies immediately upon meeting True Lead—a metaphor for Spirit settling into stillness the moment it unites with the Breath of Prior Heaven.
  • Fu and Gou, Mao and You: The hexagram Fu (one yang rising) and the hexagram Gou (one yin rising) mark the turning points for advancing the fire and withdrawing the tally; Mao and You mark the hours of ablution (muyu).
  • Jade disks before four-horse chariots: From chapter 62 of the Daodejing: "Though one has jade disks carried before teams of four horses, it does not compare with sitting and advancing in this Way."

Putting It into Practice

  1. Seeing through impermanence is the starting point of seeking the Way. Zhang Sanfeng's point of departure is precisely that "wealth and rank are impermanent, appearing and vanishing in an instant."
  2. Side-door minor methods bring no benefit to body or mind. Learning many techniques without real benefit is a common experience. The test of any method is whether it genuinely makes body and mind steadier and clearer.
  3. Do the work while mingling in the ordinary world first. He first "dimmed his light and mingled with the world" before choosing a quiet retreat—consistent with "refining the self amid worldly dust" in Clarifying Delusions on Ascending to Heaven.
  4. Accumulate virtue and practice kindness. At the end of the preface, he credits his success to "widely accumulating hidden merit and continually practicing kindness."

Translator's Notes

  1. The source text stands at the head of juan 4 of The Complete Works of Zhang Sanfeng, originally titled Author's Preface to Essentials of the Mystery.
  2. "During the Yanyou reign": Reign era of Emperor Renzong of the Yuan dynasty, 1314–1320.
  3. "Master Fire Dragon, a senior disciple of Tunan": Chen Tuan (courtesy name Tunan, sobriquet Fuyaozi) was a celebrated Daoist of the Five Dynasties and early Song. Master Fire Dragon's biography appears historically only in legends surrounding Zhang Sanfeng.
  4. "Seek the tripod and vessel," "await the eight ounces of the other's Lead," "gather that single speck of True Lead of Prior Heaven": These are standard yin-yang metaphors in alchemical literature. This site reads them through the pure-stillness method: "tripod and vessel" refers to establishing the furnace and tripod within one's own body, and "the other's Lead" / "that" refers to the Breath of Prior Heaven, not another person. In Clarifying Delusions on Ascending to Heaven in this book, Zhang Sanfeng states explicitly: "As for using a female tripod, there is not the slightest basis for such a thing," and "there is no distinction of male and female whatsoever."
  5. "Left the mountains to practice dual cultivation of inner nature and life": "Dual cultivation of inner nature and life" (xingming shuangxiu) means cultivating inner nature (mind-nature) and life (Essence and Breath) together—not paired sexual practice.
  6. "Transcending three times, completing the nine revolutions": Standard alchemical phrasing referring to repeated cycles of refinement in elixir cultivation.
  7. "Sixteenth day of the seventh lunar month" (mengqiu yue jiwang ri): Mengqiu is the first month of autumn (seventh lunar month), and jiwang is the sixteenth day.
  8. "Scruffy Daoist of the Yuan" (Yuan Lata daoren): Zhang Sanfeng's sobriquet "Lata" ("Scruffy" or "Unkempt") came from his indifference to outward appearance.
  9. Works in this scroll (Essentials of the Mystery, Part I) omitted under this site's editorial policy are: Song of Sitting in Meditation (already included in Book 2, Part 8, linked instead); Song of the Great Way, Song of the True Bellows, Song of the Single Opening of the Mysterious Pass, Song of Refining Lead (the version beginning "The Most High Way, declared once more"), Song of the Golden Elixir, Song of Dispelling Delusions on the Golden Fluid Reverted Elixir, and Song of Pointing Out Delusions on the Dragon-Tiger Reverted Elixir; poems 12 (Refining the Self and Starting the Work), 17 (Condensing Prior Heaven), and 35 (General Verse on Inner Matters) of Thirty-Six Poems on the Golden Elixir, together with the appended poem Ode to the Prior-Heaven Tripod; and the fifth of the Five Seven-Character Quatrains. Because those pieces rely primarily on yin-yang school formulations, they are omitted in full.

5 · The True Meaning of “Vague and Indistinct”

5 · The True Meaning of “Vague and Indistinct” — the breath of Prior Heaven arises, the breath of Posterior Heaven receives it

About This Chapter

The Daode jing says, “Vague and indistinct, within it there is a thing,” and the alchemical books speak everywhere of “the vague and indistinct” (huanghu 恍惚) — but what, after all, is it? The author says: what one cultivates in cultivating the elixir is this vague and indistinct; what one learns in learning the Dao is this vague and indistinct. His teacher once gave him a poem, and only after reading it did he understand: the vague and indistinct is the stirring of true yang — that is, “the metal within water,” by which the Dao is attained and the elixir obtained. Cui Xifan’s Mirror for Compounding the Medicine says, “The breath of Prior Heaven, the breath of Posterior Heaven — those who obtain them are always as if drunk”; being drunk is the vague and indistinct, and the vague and indistinct is being drunk.

But only when Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven embrace is it the true vague and indistinct: the breath of Prior Heaven has no form or image and does not arise except at utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness; the breath of Posterior Heaven has form and image, and cannot be used unless it reaches the point of formlessness and imagelessness. The vague and indistinct arises only when the breath of Prior Heaven is born, the breath of Posterior Heaven receives it, and the two breaths revolve together. The going and stopping of the breath of Prior Heaven are entirely at the command of Posterior Heaven. And this breath of Posterior Heaven is not the respiration of mouth and nose — mouth and nose are too far from the Cavity of Breath; how could they cling to it and never part? The True Breath is not in the mouth and nose but in the Cavity of Breath; every breath returning to the root means breathing from the Cavity of Breath.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

恍恍惚惚，其中有物。无物，恍惚者谁也？修丹者修此恍惚，学道者学此恍惚。昔日师赐余诗一首曰：“知君夙植英灵骨，所以教君心恍惚。含光殿上水晶宫，分明指出神仙窟。”余得此诗之后，始知恍惚者真阳发动也。第三句分明指出真阳之气，即水中金之一物，成道由此，得丹由此，非神仙窟而何？

“Vague and indistinct, within it there is a thing.” If there were no thing, who would it be that is vague and indistinct? What one cultivates in cultivating the elixir is this vague and indistinct; what one learns in learning the Dao is this vague and indistinct. Once my teacher bestowed on me a poem: “Knowing that in a past life you planted bones of heroic spirit, I teach you to let your mind be vague and indistinct. The crystal palace above the Hall of Contained Light clearly points out the grotto of the immortals.” Only after I received this poem did I know that the vague and indistinct is the stirring of true yang. The third line clearly points out the breath of true yang, which is that one thing, “the metal within water”; by it the Dao is attained, by it the elixir is obtained — what is this, if not the grotto of the immortals?

§2
Original (Chinese)

君不见崔公所云“先天气，后天气，得之者常似醉”之言乎，醉即恍惚，恍惚即醉，是醉与恍惚是一非二。言醉亦可，言恍惚亦可。但先天不能与后天相抱，虽恍惚总非恍惚；果能先后打成一片，虽不欲其恍惚亦不可也。观此始知道是自然。惟识神不去，总难入得大静。

Have you not seen the words of Master Cui: “The breath of Prior Heaven, the breath of Posterior Heaven — those who obtain them are always as if drunk”? Being drunk is the vague and indistinct, and the vague and indistinct is being drunk; drunkenness and the vague and indistinct are one, not two. One may call it drunkenness, or one may call it the vague and indistinct. But if Prior Heaven cannot embrace Posterior Heaven, then even if there is vagueness and indistinctness, it is never the true vague and indistinct; if one can truly fuse Prior and Posterior Heaven into one piece, then even if one did not want to be vague and indistinct, one could not help it. From this one first knows that the Dao is natural. Only, if the cognitive spirit does not depart, it is always hard to enter great stillness.

§3
Original (Chinese)

君果知二炁之所用否？夫无中生有者先天也，息息归根者后天也。盖先天之炁原无形象，非虚极静笃不生。后天之炁本有形象，然非至于无形无象则不能用。向言恍惚，非真恍惚也。夫恍惚者，乃先天炁生，后天气接，二气旋转，始生恍惚。先天炁非后天气温暖不生，亦非后天气相恋不住，故先天炁之行止，惟听命于后天焉。若以口鼻呼吸为后天气，试问口鼻与炁穴相隔甚远，何以能相恋而不相离也？惟真息不在口鼻，而在炁穴，息息归根，即是由炁穴呼吸，而不着于口鼻也。

Do you truly know the uses of the two breaths? What produces something out of nothing is Prior Heaven; what returns breath after breath to the root is Posterior Heaven. The breath of Prior Heaven originally has no form or image and does not arise except at utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness. The breath of Posterior Heaven originally has form and image, but unless it reaches the point of formlessness and imagelessness, it cannot be used. What (people) used to call the vague and indistinct is not the true vague and indistinct. The vague and indistinct is this: the breath of Prior Heaven arises, the breath of Posterior Heaven receives it, the two breaths revolve together, and only then does the vague and indistinct arise. Without the warmth of the breath of Posterior Heaven, the breath of Prior Heaven does not arise; without the breath of Posterior Heaven clinging to it, it does not stay. So the going and stopping of the breath of Prior Heaven are entirely at the command of Posterior Heaven. If one takes the respiration of mouth and nose as the breath of Posterior Heaven, then may I ask: mouth and nose are so far from the Cavity of Breath — how could they cling to it and never part? Only the True Breath is not in the mouth and nose but in the Cavity of Breath; every breath returning to the root means breathing from the Cavity of Breath, and not being attached to mouth and nose.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

恍惚一词出自《道德经》二十一章，原文为：“道之为物，惟恍惟惚。惚兮恍兮，其中有象；恍兮惚兮，其中有物。窈兮冥兮，其中有精；其精甚真，其中有信。”此段文为历代气功家所引用。

“先天炁，后天炁，得之者常似醉”为汉朝崔希范所著《入药镜》首句，据说吕洞宾初闻道而未甚精明，及见此句而后深悟成道。

The term “vague and indistinct” comes from chapter 21 of the Daode jing, which reads: “The Dao, as a thing, is only vague, only indistinct. Indistinct and vague, within it there is an image; vague and indistinct, within it there is a thing. Dim and dark, within it there is an essence; this essence is utterly true, within it there is something trustworthy.” This passage has been quoted by qigong masters through the ages.

“The breath of Prior Heaven, the breath of Posterior Heaven — those who obtain them are always as if drunk” is the first line of the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine by Cui Xifan. It is said that when Lü Dongbin first heard the Dao he did not yet understand it well, and only on seeing this line did he awaken deeply and attain the Dao.

Key Terms

  • Vague and indistinct (huanghu 恍惚): from chapter 21 of the Daode jing; this part defines it as the state when “the breath of Prior Heaven arises, the breath of Posterior Heaven receives it, and the two breaths revolve together,” that is, the stirring of true yang.
  • The crystal palace above the Hall of Contained Light: a line from the teacher’s poem, which the author explains as the “grotto of the immortals” where the breath of true yang resides.
  • The metal within water: that is, the breath of true yang, the One Breath of Prior Heaven.
  • Being drunk is the vague and indistinct: the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine, “those who obtain them are always as if drunk.”
  • Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven: what produces something out of nothing is Prior Heaven; what returns breath after breath to the root is Posterior Heaven.
  • Cognitive spirit: the discriminating mind; see cognitive spirit.
  • The True Breath is not in the mouth and nose but in the Cavity of Breath: see True Breath and ordinary breath.

Putting It into Practice

  1. What the vague and indistinct feels like: at the height of stillness, a spark of life stirs deep in the lower abdomen, while the breathing becomes extremely fine and revolves with it; one has a slightly tipsy feeling, as if drunk and yet not drunk — this is the vague and indistinct this part speaks of.
  2. Its precondition is stillness: “the breath of Prior Heaven originally has no form or image and does not arise except at utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness” — do not try to manufacture the feeling; the vague and indistinct comes out of stillness.
  3. Posterior Heaven receives Prior Heaven: as soon as the breath of Prior Heaven stirs, the ordinary breathing should “receive” it — the breath gently following it, in the lower abdomen, not in the nose.
  4. If the cognitive spirit does not depart, it is hard to enter great stillness: being vague and indistinct while thinking “is this the vague and indistinct?” means the cognitive spirit has not departed.

Translator's Notes

  1. “What (people) used to call the vague and indistinct is not the true vague and indistinct”: meaning that what people generally called the vague and indistinct in the past is not the true thing. “People” is supplied, following the vernacular translation.
  2. Qiu Ling’s note calls Cui Xifan a man of the “Han dynasty”; he is generally thought to have lived in the late Tang and Five Dynasties period, as explained in earlier parts, and the translation omits the dynasty.
  3. “In a past life you planted” (suzhi 夙植): planted in a former life, a common Buddhist and Daoist expression, meaning one has roots from past lives, a natural gift.

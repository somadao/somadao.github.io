7 · The Method of the Celestial Circuit

7 · The Method of the Celestial Circuit — following the mechanism; stopping the fire; the Great Medicine passing the gates

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

Safety note (translator): The second half of this section, on “the Great Medicine passing the gates,” describes practices of ancient adepts such as tightly contracting the anus, pressing the perineum with a “ritual implement,” and clamping the nose with a “wooden clip”; Qiu Ling’s note adds that the ancients, when practicing this stage, “had to have a Daoist companion keeping watch beside them.” These are records from ancient texts, and must never be imitated on your own. The first half of this section, on the principle that the Lesser Celestial Circuit “follows the mechanism and must not be forcibly transported,” is among the plainest and most worthwhile reading in the whole book.

About This Chapter

This is the longest section of the whole text, in two halves.

First half: the Lesser Celestial Circuit (Qiu Ling’s note: that is, the work of laying the foundation in a hundred days). The author’s position is consistent throughout — follow the mechanism, do not force:

  • The Lesser Celestial Circuit is carried out once the true seed has entered the furnace and the yang fire arises: when there is a mechanism of moving upward, guide it upward; when there is a mechanism of settled stillness, let it be settled and still; “to guide it when it does not move is of no benefit; to settle it when it is not still does harm.”
  • Guide with the spirit, but do not cling; blow with the breath, but do not linger. The breath’s role is only “to give a little attention to the mechanism of inhaling, and let exhaling be natural.”
  • He explicitly criticizes the alchemical scriptures’ talk of counting 360 breaths, 36, 24 and the like: one round of the circuit is the full count, just as one round of the clock’s long hand is one hour — why count minutes and seconds as well? Advancing the yang fire, withdrawing the yin tally, changing hexagrams and drawing lines are all figurative comparisons.
  • The Lesser Celestial Circuit is the heaven of deliberate action; the Greater Celestial Circuit is the heaven of non-action. When gathering the Lesser Medicine one does not really feel that there is medicine; it only seems, in intention, that there is; only after the medicine enters the furnace is the fire raised. Inhalation and exhalation each have their duty: in rising, inhalation leads; in descending, exhalation leads; the other one is the ablution.
  • Many people, as soon as they meet a stirring, go and make the circuit rise and descend; some, as soon as the lower body feels slightly warm, force the circuit, and take swallowing saliva for obtaining the elixir — “is that not utterly laughable!” When a faint yang arises, one can only take it back with the spirit, against the current, to its original place and nourish it; when the yang breath is sufficient, the signs of the medicine’s production appear by themselves, and then one gathers it back into the furnace. “If the medicine has not returned to the furnace, by no means transport it first.”
  • Stopping the fire: when the elixir is complete, the fire must be stopped at the right time, the time being “the yang light appearing three times”; stop from the second appearance, and after the third gather the Great Medicine. Stopping the fire does not mean not using fire; it only means no longer pressing on with the fire of respiration.

Second half: the Heavenly Mechanism of Seven Days and the Great Medicine passing the gates (Qiu Ling’s note: that is, the work of the Greater Celestial Circuit, which the alchemical books liken to “five dragons bearing up the sage”). After stopping the fire, one quietly contemplates the middle field, day and night without slackening, and only then is the Great Medicine born, with six signs appearing first (fire blazing in the Cinnabar Field, the two kidneys seething like boiling liquid, wind arising behind the ears, the body surging and the nose twitching, an eagle’s cry behind the head, and so on). The Great Medicine stirs only in the Cavity of Breath, and the Cavity of Breath has four branching paths (the heart opening, the yang gate, the Caudal Funnel, the grain path); only the grain path is the most dangerous. Passing the gates depends entirely on “when the mechanism moves, guide; when the mechanism rests, guard,” from the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass, the Jade Pillow, the summit and the Hall of Impressions down into the Storied Tower, taking the upper and lower fields as one realm, as the foundation for ten months of warm nourishing. The author also unlocks three famous metaphors: “five dragons bearing up the sage,” “reed shoots piercing the knee,” and “crossing the river on a broken reed.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

小周天行于真种入炉之后，阳火发生之时，顺其生发之机，以神而引，以息而吹，火受神息引吹，自然上升。但不必论其爻象，限其时刻，有上行之机，则引上行，有定静之机，则听其定静，总是动而后引，静而后定。不动引之无益，不静定之有损。无论动静，均须顺其机而为之，方合乎自然也。虽用神引，亦不可着；虽以息吹，亦不可滞。当升机发动之时，元神仍凝玄关，稍分其神以引上升之阳火。息之吹逼，非息能随阳火上升也，不过稍着意于吸机，呼则听其自然。息机虽向下，而阳火受真息压迫，反转而上升也。用神用息不过如是，余此听其自升自降而已。因此阳火发于玄窍，止于玄窍，经过一周，故曰周天。如日行一周经十二时，故丹道又以刻漏误之，真可惜也。

The Lesser Celestial Circuit is carried out after the true seed has entered the furnace, at the time when the yang fire arises: following its mechanism of arising, guide with the spirit and blow with the breath; the fire, guided and blown by spirit and breath, naturally rises. But there is no need to discuss line images or to set limits of time: when there is a mechanism of moving upward, guide it upward; when there is a mechanism of settled stillness, let it be settled and still. Always guide after it moves, settle after it is still. To guide it when it does not move is of no benefit; to settle it when it is not still does harm. Whether in movement or stillness, one must act by following its mechanism; only then does it accord with nature. Though one guides with the spirit, one must not cling; though one blows with the breath, one must not linger. When the mechanism of rising stirs, the Original Spirit remains concentrated in the Mysterious Pass, dividing off only a little of the spirit to guide the rising yang fire. As for the blowing and pressing of the breath, it is not that the breath can rise along with the yang fire; it is only giving a little attention to the mechanism of inhaling, while exhaling is left to be natural. Though the mechanism of the breath goes downward, the yang fire, pressed by the True Breath, turns back and rises instead. Using spirit and using breath are no more than this; for the rest, simply let it rise and descend by itself. Because this yang fire issues from the mysterious opening, stops at the mysterious opening, and passes through one round, it is called the Celestial Circuit. Just as the sun passes through twelve double-hours in one round, so followers of the alchemical Way have misunderstood it in terms of the water clock — truly a pity.

§2
Original (Chinese)

丹经每谓周天必数三百六十息，方足一周之数。余谓但行一周，即足三百六十之数。譬如此钟大针能统一周，即是十二点钟，何必再数分秒，徒劳神思乎！至中间三十六、二十四等说，均是设辞，并非令学者遇阳升时真数三十六，遇阴降时，真数二十四也。气自尾闾上升绝顶，因其升故曰阳；自绝顶下降坤腹，因其降故曰阴，其实只一物耳。至换卦抽爻，亦是此义，不过虚比，安有爻象之可言哉！

The alchemical scriptures often say that the Celestial Circuit requires counting 360 breaths to complete the number of one round. I say that one has only to make one round, and the number 360 is complete. Just as when the long hand of this clock goes all the way round, it is one hour — why count the minutes and seconds as well, wearying the spirit in vain! As for the talk of 36 and 24 in between, these are all hypothetical expressions; they are not telling students really to count 36 when the yang rises, and really to count 24 when the yin descends. The breath rises from the Caudal Funnel to the summit; because it rises, it is called yang. It descends from the summit to the Kun belly; because it descends, it is called yin. In fact it is only one thing. Changing hexagrams and drawing lines have the same meaning too; they are only figurative comparisons — what line images could there be to speak of!

§3
Original (Chinese)

由是而升，即为进阳火，进至乾顶，转而下降，即为退阴符，降至本穴，即为归根，又名送入土釜。封炉者即归土釜。恐其走失，以意而守，以目而照，先存后忘，合乎自然，即封炉之要诀也。由是药生则采，入炉则炼，归根则封，以至生无可生，采无可采，而内阴自缩，又应停运以止火矣。止火有时，不到其时先止之，则漏尽未证。至时而不止，则火燥丹老，仍难成就。

Rising from here is advancing the yang fire; advancing to the Qian summit (the top of the head) and turning to descend is withdrawing the yin tally; descending to the original cavity is returning to the root, also called sending it into the earthen pot. Sealing the furnace means returning it to the earthen pot. Fearing it might escape, guard it with the intention, illuminate it with the eyes, first holding and then forgetting, in accord with nature — this is the essential formula for sealing the furnace. From then on, when the medicine is born, gather it; when it enters the furnace, refine it; when it returns to the root, seal it — until arising, there is nothing more to arise, gathering, there is nothing more to gather, and the “inner yin” contracts of itself; then one should again stop the circulation to stop the fire. There is a time for stopping the fire: stop it before the time, and “exhausting the outflows” is not attained; do not stop it when the time comes, and the fire becomes parching and the elixir old, and it is still hard to succeed.

§4
Original (Chinese)

丹经云：采取以升降，由督脉上升乾顶，从任脉下降坤腹。然督脉在何处？在丹田后弦，上通三关，进阳火之道路也。任脉在何处？在丹田前弦，上通乾顶，与督脉会合，此乃退阴符之道路也。小周天乃有为之天，大周天乃无为之天，此所以有大小之异耳。采药不问大小，均无别径。惟采小药时，实不觉有药，而意似有药，非如采大药时有形有象。但采小药后，非起火不能溶化阴质。火在何处？药中有火焉。药在何处？火中有药焉。起火于何时？药入炉后。运火于何处？尾闾穴间。至此自有真机，顺此机而行，神守丹田，意驭真气上升，虽藉呼吸之力，亦不可著于呼吸。此时务要著力先天之气，后天之气稍为关照而已。但先天气上升时，本有行住之机，动机现可以行，静机现可以住，行时以呼吸之数规则之，一吸一升，是知非一吸可升至乾顶。一呼一降，亦知非一呼可以降至坤腹。况上升时有沐浴之说，此时不可起火，必须静俟机动而后行，方不背乎自然。况呼吸均有责任，上升时吸为主，呼不过从之而已。下降时呼为主，吸不过从之而已。不但升降有沐浴，呼吸之间亦有沐浴。吸用事时呼从之，呼即为沐浴。呼用事时吸从之，则吸亦为之沐浴。至于采取升降，真种一发生，即一升降，亦名抽坎填离。至抽尽坎阳，填满离阴，精尽化气，实有止火景焉。

The alchemical scriptures say: gathering is done by rising and descending — rising by the Governing Vessel to the Qian summit, descending by the Conception Vessel to the Kun belly. Then where is the Governing Vessel? It is on the rear arc of the Cinnabar Field, passing upward through the Three Passes — the road of advancing the yang fire. Where is the Conception Vessel? It is on the front arc of the Cinnabar Field, reaching up to the Qian summit and meeting the Governing Vessel — the road of withdrawing the yin tally. The Lesser Celestial Circuit is the heaven of deliberate action; the Greater Celestial Circuit is the heaven of non-action; this is why there is a distinction between lesser and greater. In gathering the medicine, lesser or greater, there is no other route. Only, when gathering the Lesser Medicine one does not really feel that there is medicine; it only seems, in intention, that there is — unlike gathering the Great Medicine, which has form and image. But after gathering the Lesser Medicine, without raising the fire one cannot melt away the yin substance. Where is the fire? There is fire in the medicine. Where is the medicine? There is medicine in the fire. When is the fire raised? After the medicine enters the furnace. Where is the fire circulated? At the Caudal Funnel point. Here there is naturally a true mechanism; follow this mechanism: the spirit guards the Cinnabar Field, the intention drives the true breath upward, and though one relies on the strength of respiration, one must not be attached to the respiration. At this time one must put effort into the breath of Prior Heaven, giving only slight attention to the breath of Posterior Heaven. But when the breath of Prior Heaven rises, it has by nature mechanisms of going and stopping: when a mechanism of movement appears, one may go; when a mechanism of stillness appears, one may stop. While going, regulate it by the count of respiration: one inhalation, one rising — from which one knows that a single inhalation cannot rise all the way to the Qian summit; one exhalation, one descending — from which one also knows that a single exhalation cannot descend all the way to the Kun belly. Moreover, during the rising there is the matter of “ablution”: at this time one must not raise the fire, but must quietly wait for the mechanism to move before going on; only then does one not go against nature. Moreover, inhalation and exhalation each have their duty: in rising, inhalation leads and exhalation merely follows; in descending, exhalation leads and inhalation merely follows. Not only is there ablution in rising and descending, there is also ablution between inhalation and exhalation: when inhalation is in charge and exhalation follows, the exhalation is the ablution; when exhalation is in charge and inhalation follows, the inhalation too is the ablution. As for gathering by rising and descending: each time the true seed arises, there is one rising and descending, which is also called “drawing from Kan to fill Li.” When the yang of Kan has been entirely drawn out, the yin of Li entirely filled, and the essence entirely transformed into breath, there truly are signs for stopping the fire.

§5
Original (Chinese)

初下手凝神于气穴，神凝则气生，气生则机发，此又静中生动时也。要知此动乃微阳耳。《易》曰：“初九潜龙勿用”。即此义也。学人不明此理，往往一遇动机，便去升降周天，徒戕真机，与道无益，此犹是稍明道理者。如子并不知机，稍觉下部发热，便去强行周天，以咽津为得丹，宁不大可笑乎！当微阳发生，只可以神逆归原处，以涵养之。时来时凝，迨至阳气充足，自然有药产之景，如光透帘帏、火珠上驰、耳际风声、暖溢四肢，此皆药产之景象也。见此景象，正好将药采归玉炉。采即以神息归根，药亦自返本穴。药归炉后，移时炉中火发，此乃升降之真机耳。药不归炉，万勿先行搬运。此间不容毫发，非有心人不能明晰也。六祖曰：接引来机。《易》曰：见机而作。此皆提醒学人，临时不可忙乱，必顺其机而行，如冬至阳气不令升而自升，此亦自然而然，非人力所能助也。我能升降一任天机，方为法天也。若天法自然，而人妄自搬运，乃违天也。

At the very start, concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath; when the spirit concentrates, the breath is born; when the breath is born, the mechanism stirs — this again is the time when movement arises within stillness. Know that this movement is only a faint yang. The Changes says: “Nine at the beginning: the hidden dragon; do not act.” That is the meaning. Students who do not understand this principle often, as soon as they meet a stirring, go and make the circuit rise and descend, harming the true mechanism in vain, of no benefit to the Dao — and these are still the ones who understand the principle a little. Someone like you, who does not know the mechanism at all, as soon as you feel the lower body slightly warm, goes and forces the circuit, taking swallowing saliva for obtaining the elixir — is that not utterly laughable! When the faint yang arises, one can only take it back with the spirit, against the current, to its original place and nourish it there. Each time it comes, concentrate it; when the yang breath has become sufficient, the signs of the medicine’s production naturally appear, such as light coming through the curtains, a fiery pearl racing upward, the sound of wind at the ears, warmth overflowing into the four limbs — these are all signs of the medicine’s production. On seeing these signs, it is just the time to gather the medicine back into the jade furnace. Gathering means returning spirit and breath to the root, and the medicine too returns of itself to its original cavity. After the medicine has returned to the furnace, after a while the fire in the furnace breaks out — only this is the true mechanism of rising and descending. If the medicine has not returned to the furnace, by no means transport it first. Here there is not room for a hair’s breadth; only those who apply their minds can make it clear. The Sixth Patriarch said: “Receive the mechanism that comes.” The Changes says: “See the mechanism and act.” All these remind students not to be flustered at the moment, but always to act by following the mechanism. Just as the yang breath at the winter solstice rises by itself without being made to rise — this too is so of itself, not something human effort can assist. Only if I let rising and descending be entirely up to the mechanism of Heaven am I modeling myself on Heaven. If Heaven models itself on nature while a person recklessly transports things by himself, that is going against Heaven.

§6
Original (Chinese)

何谓止火？止火者是大丹已成，不行周天火候也。老子曰：知止不殆。盖言丹成当应时止火，以免伤丹之虞。但止火亦有时，阳光三现即其时也。当从阳光二现时止，至三现后宜采大药，又有七日天机生焉。

What is stopping the fire? Stopping the fire means that the great elixir is already complete and the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit is no longer carried out. Laozi said: “Knowing when to stop, one is not endangered.” This means that when the elixir is complete, one should stop the fire at the right time, to avoid the danger of harming the elixir. But stopping the fire also has its time: the yang light appearing three times is that time. One should stop from the second appearance of the yang light; after the third appearance it is fitting to gather the Great Medicine, and then the Heavenly Mechanism of Seven Days arises.

§7
Original (Chinese)

何以又必得止火？气足则精尽化气，向之所以用火者，炼此精耳，精尽化气，则是无精矣。无精可炼，则火亦无所施矣，是以有止火之事。然止火者，非不行火，不过不行呼吸之火。若此时再用呼吸，再鼓橐籥，再行周天，丹已成熟，再加烹炼，未有不伤丹者。古云：还丹未炼急须炼，炼了还须知止足。盖此时阳关已闭，无精可炼，即当停止呼吸，以待天机。然停息非不呼吸也，惟不以呼吸催逼，再行周天而已。止火之后，自应寂然定静，呼吸似有而似无，神炁似守而非守。古云：定里见丹成。至言也。但此时不加以温养，则大药不生，是以采大药尚有天机也。

Why must one stop the fire? When the breath is sufficient, the essence is entirely transformed into breath; the reason fire was used before was to refine this essence; when the essence is entirely transformed into breath, there is no more essence. With no essence to refine, the fire has nowhere to be applied, and so there is the matter of stopping the fire. Yet stopping the fire is not ceasing to use fire; it is only ceasing to use the fire of respiration. If at this time one again uses respiration, again works the bellows, again runs the circuit — the elixir already ripe, and further cooked and refined — it is never without harm to the elixir. The ancients said: “When the Returned Elixir is not yet refined, refine it urgently; once refined, one must know when enough is enough.” For at this time the yang gate has already closed, there is no essence to refine, and one should stop (using) respiration and wait for the mechanism of Heaven. Yet “stopping the breath” does not mean not breathing; it only means not pressing on with respiration to run the circuit again. After stopping the fire, one should naturally be silent, settled, and still, the breathing seeming to be and seeming not to be, spirit and breath seeming guarded and not guarded. The ancients said: “Within absorption one sees the elixir complete.” These are words of the highest truth. But if at this time one does not add warm nourishing, the Great Medicine will not be born; so the gathering of the Great Medicine still has its mechanism of Heaven.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§7
Original note (Chinese)

以上言小周天功法，本乎自然，升降俟机而行，切不可强行搬运。至谈及不论爻象，不限时刻，则一破古来惯例。阳光三现，乃止火之景，若到四现，丹已老而不中用。此段即百日筑基之功。

The above speaks of the method of the Lesser Celestial Circuit, which is rooted in nature: rising and descending proceed only when the mechanism comes, and one must never forcibly transport. When it goes on to say that one need not discuss line images or set limits of time, it breaks at a stroke with the custom of the ages. The yang light appearing three times is the sign for stopping the fire; if it reaches a fourth appearance, the elixir is already old and of no use. This passage is the work of laying the foundation in a hundred days.

§8
Original (Chinese)

何谓七日天机？此乃止火后采大药之事。盖以大药既成，必须服食而后能点化阴质，成全纯阳之体。虽以七日限之，身体健、功夫纯者，或二、三日，或四五日，得之者亦有之，不必七日也。惟药已成形，犹隐而不露，必以目光静观中田，昼夜不怠，大药始生焉。吾有一偈，录之可也。诗曰：“大药原来容易求，静观两间莫停留。精神团结成丹后，千道金光射斗牛。”观此可以知采大药惟在静观中田耳。大药生时，六景先现，如丹田火炽，两肾汤煎，耳后生风，身涌鼻搐，脑后鹫鸣皆是。

What is the Heavenly Mechanism of Seven Days? It is the matter of gathering the Great Medicine after stopping the fire. For once the Great Medicine is formed, it must be ingested before it can transform the yin substance and complete a body of pure yang. Though seven days is set as the limit, there are those of sound body and pure work who obtain it in two or three days, or four or five days; it need not be seven days. Only, though the medicine has taken form, it is still hidden and does not show itself; one must quietly contemplate the middle field with the light of the eyes, day and night without slackening, and only then is the Great Medicine born. I have a gatha, which may be recorded here. The poem says: “The Great Medicine is in truth easy to seek: quietly contemplate between the two, and do not linger. Once essence and spirit have bound together into the elixir, a thousand rays of golden light shoot to the Ox and the Dipper.” From this one can know that gathering the Great Medicine lies only in quietly contemplating the middle field. When the Great Medicine is born, six signs appear first, such as fire blazing in the Cinnabar Field, the two kidneys seething like boiling liquid, wind arising behind the ears, the body surging and the nose twitching, an eagle’s cry behind the head — all of these.

§9
Original (Chinese)

然大药发生，只动于气穴。惟气穴间有四条歧路，上通心窍，前彻阳关，后通尾闾，下连谷道。四窍之中，惟谷道危险。大道由气穴发出，上冲于心不受，前至阳关不受，后至尾闾不通，即欲下奔谷道。此时当紧撮谷道，勿令外泄，势必转向尾闾，仍不能过，不必强引，但以一意静守，俟其动而后引，自然冲开尾闾，升至夹脊，又不动矣，仍须待其自动之机而后引之，自然冲过夹脊，而升至玉枕，又不动矣，亦如上法引过玉枕，升至绝顶，下至印堂，又不动矣，须待其自动之机而后引之，自然冲过印堂，下入重楼，点化阴质，而以上下田为一境界，作十月温养之基矣！但过关时，上而鼻孔，下而谷道，均极危险。谷道既有紧撮内中提之诀，印堂须用夹鼻牵牛之法，脱过此险。不但此也，大药过关之时，更须以神主宰于中，以意引药上升。丹经所谓两相知之微意者此也。尤恐其不周密，更当谨闭六根，使无外驰之虞，庶几其可矣！何谓六根，眼、耳、鼻、舌、身、心是也。内视返听，则眼耳无漏。夹鼻舐腭，则鼻舌无漏，撮谷道却念虑，则身心无漏。六根既固，大药无漏。机动则引而行，机息则守其静。既不着于意，亦不驰于意，自然而然，无为而为，过关服食之道毕矣！

Yet when the Great Medicine arises, it stirs only in the Cavity of Breath. Only, from the Cavity of Breath there are four branching paths: upward it reaches the heart opening, forward it goes through to the yang gate, backward it reaches the Caudal Funnel, downward it connects with the grain path. Of the four openings, only the grain path is dangerous. The Great Medicine issues from the Cavity of Breath; it rushes up to the heart, and the heart does not receive it; it goes forward to the yang gate, and the yang gate does not receive it; it goes back to the Caudal Funnel, and the Caudal Funnel is not open; then it wants to rush down to the grain path. At this time one should tightly contract the grain path and not let it leak out; it will then necessarily turn toward the Caudal Funnel. If it still cannot pass, there is no need to force it; simply guard quietly with single-minded intention, wait for it to move, and then guide it; naturally it bursts open the Caudal Funnel and rises to the Spine-Straddling Pass, and then stops moving again. Again one must wait for its own mechanism of movement and then guide it; naturally it bursts through the Spine-Straddling Pass and rises to the Jade Pillow, and then stops moving again. Guide it past the Jade Pillow in the same way, and it rises to the summit and descends to the Hall of Impressions, and then stops moving again. One must wait for its own mechanism of movement and then guide it; naturally it bursts through the Hall of Impressions and descends into the Storied Tower, transforming the yin substance, and the upper and lower fields are taken as one realm, as the foundation for ten months of warm nourishing! But in passing the gates, the nostrils above and the grain path below are both extremely dangerous. For the grain path there is the formula of “tightly contracting and lifting from within”; for the Hall of Impressions one must use the method of “clamping the nose and leading the ox,” to escape this danger. Not only this: when the Great Medicine passes the gates, one must also let the spirit preside in the middle and let the intention guide the medicine upward — this is what the alchemical scriptures call “the subtle intention of the two knowing each other.” Still fearing that this is not thorough enough, one should carefully close the six roots, so that there is no worry of racing outward; then it may be nearly right! What are the six roots? Eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind. With inner vision and turned-back hearing, eye and ear do not leak; with the nose clamped and the tongue licking the palate, nose and tongue do not leak; with the grain path contracted and thoughts and worries dismissed, body and mind do not leak. When the six roots are secure, the Great Medicine does not leak. When the mechanism moves, guide it on; when the mechanism rests, guard its stillness. Neither attached to intention nor racing with intention, so of itself, acting through non-action — the Way of passing the gates and ingesting is complete!

§10
Original (Chinese)

大药过关乃自古不传秘密天机。虽有五龙捧圣之喻言，芦芽穿膝之巧比，折芦过江之假托，均未敲破骨髓。余不惜苦口，为君等再言之。如五龙捧圣者，五即土数，龙即真意，圣即大药也。概言以真意而采取，捧过三关九窍，至乾顶而后降中田，正所谓乾坤交媾罢，一点落黄庭也。从此守中下二田为一，存养十月，身外始有身矣。然大药上升时，虽用意领，勿着于意，顺其自然之机，机动则随之动，机静则随之静，以定静守，以温照俟，自有当动当静之机来。此机一来，则随其机而已，不沾不脱，斯为真诀。如芦芽穿膝，芦芽即喻大药，穿膝即穿过三关之意。千比万喻，总是述明过关服食之正功。过江者亦采药过关之义。须知芦上达摩即真意，芦即大药，江喻三关。采药用真意，过关亦用真意，但踏芦过江而不沉溺者，亦正不沾不脱之妙用也。沾则沉，脱则离。既无舱舵，又鲜橹浆，一芦浮水，能不任其行动自如乎。故芦行吾行，芦住吾住。然芦之行住，究谁使之行，谁使之住耶？抑其行也不能自行，必待水势流动而后顺之行，亦不能自住，必待水势平静而后随之住。亦如大药不能自行，必待真意宰之，呼吸吹之，大药始能过得三关。惟过关时有两处危险，上下鹊桥是也。盖以药至尾闾，欲从便道而出，吾当以法器抵之。药至明堂，欲从双孔而泄，吾则以木夹备之。防危至当，药无走失，始不负心苦一场也。

The Great Medicine passing the gates is a secret mechanism of Heaven not transmitted since antiquity. Though there is the metaphor of “five dragons bearing up the sage,” the clever comparison of “reed shoots piercing the knee,” and the allegory of “crossing the river on a broken reed,” none of them has struck through to the marrow. I do not spare my words, and will speak of it again for you. Take “five dragons bearing up the sage”: five is the number of earth, the dragon is the True Intention, and the sage is the Great Medicine. In short, it means gathering with the True Intention, bearing it up through the Three Passes and Nine Openings, to the Qian summit and then down to the middle field — exactly what is meant by “When Qian and Kun have finished their union, one drop falls into the Yellow Court.” From then on, guard the middle and lower fields as one, preserve and nourish for ten months, and only then is there a body beyond the body. Yet when the Great Medicine rises, though one leads it with the intention, do not be attached to the intention; follow its natural mechanism: when the mechanism moves, move with it; when the mechanism is still, be still with it; guard with settled stillness, wait with warm illumination, and the mechanism of when to move and when to be still will come of itself. When this mechanism comes, simply follow it, neither sticking nor slipping away — this is the true formula. Take “reed shoots piercing the knee”: the reed shoots are a metaphor for the Great Medicine, and piercing the knee means passing through the Three Passes. A thousand comparisons and ten thousand metaphors all explain the correct work of passing the gates and ingesting. “Crossing the river” too means gathering the medicine and passing the gates. Know that Bodhidharma on the reed is the True Intention, the reed is the Great Medicine, and the river is a metaphor for the Three Passes. Gathering the medicine uses the True Intention, and passing the gates also uses the True Intention; and to cross the river standing on a reed without sinking is precisely the wondrous use of “neither sticking nor slipping away”: stick, and one sinks; slip away, and one is separated from it. With neither cabin nor rudder, nor yet scull or oar, a single reed floating on the water — can one do other than let it move freely as it will? So when the reed goes, I go; when the reed stops, I stop. Yet as for the reed’s going and stopping, who, after all, makes it go, and who makes it stop? When it goes, it cannot go by itself; it must wait for the current of the water to flow and go along with it; nor can it stop by itself; it must wait for the water to become calm and stop with it. Just so the Great Medicine cannot go by itself; it must wait for the True Intention to preside over it and the respiration to blow it, and only then can the Great Medicine pass the Three Passes. Only, in passing the gates there are two dangerous places: the upper and lower Magpie Bridges. For when the medicine reaches the Caudal Funnel, it wants to go out by the convenient path (the grain path), and I must block it with the “ritual implement”; when the medicine reaches the Bright Hall, it wants to leak out through the two nostrils, and I guard against it with the “wooden clip.” Guarding against danger in just the right way, so that the medicine does not escape — only then is all one’s toil not in vain.

Qiu Ling's noteon §8–§10
Original note (Chinese)

静观七日（俗称坐关）再俟来复之机，即大周天功夫，丹书喻为五龙捧圣。古人习练此段功法时，须有道侣在旁守护，以防外扰及练功者一旦昏睡而及时唤醒之。

中丹田当在两乳之间膻中穴深入一寸三分之绛宫处。静观中田为此段功法要诀。黄庭在脐上心下，即下丹田上。

上鹊桥在眉间至鼻孔之间的鼻棱骨内。下鹊桥在肛门至尾椎骨之间。

Quietly contemplating for seven days (popularly called “sitting in the pass”) and then awaiting the mechanism of return is the work of the Greater Celestial Circuit, which the alchemical books liken to “five dragons bearing up the sage.” When the ancients practiced this stage, they had to have a Daoist companion keeping watch beside them, to guard against outside disturbance and to wake the practitioner promptly should he fall into a stupor.

The middle Cinnabar Field should be at the Crimson Palace, one cun three fen deep inward from the Danzhong point between the two breasts. Quietly contemplating the middle field is the essential formula of this stage. The Yellow Court is above the navel and below the heart, that is, above the lower Cinnabar Field.

The upper Magpie Bridge is inside the bridge of the nose, between the eyebrows and the nostrils; the lower Magpie Bridge is between the anus and the coccyx.

Key Terms

  • Celestial Circuit: as defined in this part — “because this yang fire issues from the mysterious opening, stops at the mysterious opening, and passes through one round, it is called the Celestial Circuit.”
  • Lesser and Greater Celestial Circuits: “the Lesser Celestial Circuit is the heaven of deliberate action; the Greater Celestial Circuit is the heaven of non-action.” Qiu Ling’s note: the Lesser Celestial Circuit is the hundred-day foundation; the Greater Celestial Circuit (the Heavenly Mechanism of Seven Days, the Great Medicine passing the gates) is likened in the alchemical books to five dragons bearing up the sage.
  • Guide with the spirit, blow with the breath: the spirit guides the yang fire and the breath blows it; “give a little attention to the mechanism of inhaling, and let exhaling be natural.”
  • Line images, the water clock: the alchemical scriptures’ ways of prescribing the fire phasing by hexagram lines and hours, which the author considers figurative comparisons.
  • 360 breaths, 36, 24: the author says plainly that “these are all hypothetical expressions,” not to be literally counted.
  • Advancing the yang fire, withdrawing the yin tally: rising is yang, descending is yin, “in fact it is only one thing”; see fire phasing.
  • Earthen pot / sealing the furnace: descending to the original cavity, returning to the root; guarding with the intention, illuminating with the eyes, first holding and then forgetting.
  • The inner yin contracting of itself: one of the signs for stopping the fire.
  • Stopping the fire: the great elixir complete, the fire of respiration no longer used; the time is “the yang light appearing three times.”
  • Rear and front arcs of the Cinnabar Field: the Governing Vessel behind the Cinnabar Field, the Conception Vessel in front of it; see Conception and Governing Vessels.
  • Lesser Medicine, Great Medicine: in gathering the Lesser Medicine, “one does not really feel that there is medicine; it only seems, in intention, that there is”; the Great Medicine “has form and image”; see Medicine.
  • Inhalation leads, exhalation leads: in rising, inhalation leads; in descending, exhalation leads; the other one is the ablution. See ablution.
  • Drawing from Kan to fill Li: see the conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Faint yang / the hidden dragon, do not act: the weak yang breath at its first stirring, to be nourished only, not raised and lowered.
  • Taking swallowing saliva for obtaining the elixir: a misunderstanding the author criticizes.
  • Signs of the medicine’s production: light coming through the curtains, a fiery pearl racing upward, the sound of wind at the ears, warmth overflowing into the four limbs.
  • “Receive the mechanism that comes,” “See the mechanism and act”: words of the Sixth Patriarch and the Book of Changes.
  • Heavenly Mechanism of Seven Days: after stopping the fire, quietly contemplating the middle field to gather the Great Medicine, usually in seven days, for the robust perhaps two or three, or four or five.
  • Between the two (liangjian 两间): between the middle and lower fields (compare Section 8).
  • Six signs: fire blazing in the Cinnabar Field, the two kidneys seething like boiling liquid, wind arising behind the ears, the body surging, the nose twitching, an eagle’s cry behind the head.
  • Four branching paths: the heart opening, the yang gate, the Caudal Funnel, the grain path.
  • Grain path: the anus. Yang gate: the genitals.
  • Tightly contracting the grain path, clamping the nose and leading the ox, ritual implement, wooden clip: ancient practices for preventing leakage while passing the gates.
  • The subtle intention of the two knowing each other: the subtle intent of spirit and intention knowing each other.
  • Six roots: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, mind; with inner vision and turned-back hearing, the nose clamped and the tongue licking the palate, the grain path contracted and thoughts dismissed, the six roots do not leak.
  • Five dragons bearing up the sage: five is the number of earth, the dragon is the True Intention, the sage is the Great Medicine.
  • Reed shoots piercing the knee: the reed shoots are a metaphor for the Great Medicine, piercing the knee for passing the Three Passes.
  • Crossing the river on a broken reed: Bodhidharma stands for the True Intention, the reed for the Great Medicine, the river for the Three Passes; “stick, and one sinks; slip away, and one is separated.”
  • “When Qian and Kun have finished their union, one drop falls into the Yellow Court”: see Part 3, Section 4.
  • Magpie Bridge: Qiu Ling’s note: the upper Magpie Bridge is inside the bridge of the nose between the eyebrows and the nostrils; the lower Magpie Bridge is between the anus and the coccyx.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The first half of this section is a “code of conduct for the Celestial Circuit” anyone can read:
    • To guide it when it does not move is of no benefit; to settle it when it is not still does harm — guide only when it moves; settle when it is still.
    • No need to count breaths, no need to set limits of time: “one has only to make one round, and the number 360 is complete.”
    • A faint yang at its first stirring is only to be nourished, not raised and lowered; to force the circuit as soon as the lower body feels slightly warm, and to take swallowing saliva for obtaining the elixir — “is that not utterly laughable.”
    • “If the medicine has not returned to the furnace, by no means transport it first.”
  2. The right measure in using the breath: only when the mechanism of rising comes, give a little attention to the inhalation, and leave the exhalation completely natural; and moreover “one must not be attached to the respiration.”
  3. The principle of stopping the fire: when the work has arrived, know when to stop — “once refined, one must know when enough is enough.” For ordinary practitioners this can be understood as: once results appear, do not add more force; return to a warm nourishing that seems to be and seems not to be.
  4. The “Great Medicine passing the gates” in the second half is for understanding only: tightly contracting the grain path, the ritual implement, the wooden clip, and clamping the nose are all ancient practices, and the ancients also required a Daoist companion keeping watch beside them. Please do not imitate them.
  5. The plain conclusion of the three metaphors: whether five dragons bearing up the sage, reed shoots piercing the knee, or crossing the river on a broken reed, they all say one thing — guide with the True Intention, but “neither sticking nor slipping away,” going when the water goes and stopping when the water stops.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Someone like you, who does not know the mechanism at all” (ru zi bing bu zhi ji 如子并不知机): zi is a form of address to the listener. This book consists of Shi Conglong’s instructions to his disciples, hence the tone.
  2. “So followers of the alchemical Way have misunderstood it in terms of the water clock”: meaning that people of the alchemical Way have also misunderstood the Celestial Circuit in terms of the water clock (hours and quarters).
  3. “Quietly contemplate between the two”: in light of Section 8’s “though the spirit dwells in the middle field, it must unite with the lower field,” “the two” is understood as the middle and lower fields.
  4. “The Great Way (dadao 大道) issues from the Cavity of Breath”: here 大道 is probably an error for 大药, “the Great Medicine”; the context speaks of where the Great Medicine goes once it stirs, and the translation follows “the Great Medicine.”
  5. “The body surging and the nose twitching, an eagle’s cry behind the head”: the specific names of the six signs vary among authors; this section lists only five or six and says “all of these”; the translation follows the original.
  6. “Ritual implement,” “wooden clip”: the original does not describe their form; they are kept as in the original without speculation.
  7. Qiu Ling divides the original text of this section into two passages; for ease of comparison and reading aloud, it has been subdivided by content into §1–§10.

1 · Stillness and Movement Alternating and Serving Each Other — “in movement contemplate the opening, in stillness contemplate the wonder”

About This Chapter

Qiu Ling says these passages can serve as an overview of the whole book. The author comes straight to the point: the Dao is originally nothing much, only the two words “movement and stillness,” and in fact only the one word “stillness” — in stillness there is stillness, and in movement there is also stillness. It is like the Pole Star holding the center while the host of stars circles around it: what moves moves, and what is still remains still. Movement is born out of stillness; movement without stillness is not true movement. Only a sudden movement at the point of utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness is true movement — and this is exactly when the Mysterious Pass opens; taking this opportunity to concentrate the spirit within, the moving breath naturally returns to its root and unites with the spirit. The last two sentences are the guiding principle of the whole book: in movement contemplate the opening; in stillness contemplate the wonder. The author also criticizes those who know only “meditation” (chan) and not the “mechanism” (ji), and so fall into dead emptiness: when meditation is settled the mechanism comes; when the mechanism subsides meditation becomes still again; from the first step to completion, it is always these two words alternating in use.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

夫道原无甚么，不过动静二字，其实只一静字。静时固静，动时亦静也。如北极坐镇中央，众星环拱，动者固动，静者仍自静耳。近年来所不能形神俱妙与道合真者，直不能守此一静，而听其自动。经云：“静为动之基。”盖以动自静里生出，不静不动也。动非常动，有时仍归于静。动静静动，循环不已，如二气往来天地之间，自成造化。

The Dao is originally nothing much, only the two words “movement and stillness,” and in fact only the one word “stillness.” In stillness there is of course stillness, and in movement there is also stillness. It is like the Pole Star holding the center while the host of stars circles around it: what moves of course moves, and what is still remains still. Those in recent years who have not been able to make form and spirit both wondrous and unite with the Dao in reality have simply been unable to keep this one stillness and let it move by itself. A scripture says: “Stillness is the foundation of movement.” For movement is born out of stillness; without stillness there is no movement. Movement does not move forever; at times it returns to stillness again. Movement and stillness, stillness and movement, alternate without end, like the two breaths going back and forth between Heaven and Earth, naturally accomplishing creation.

§2
Original (Chinese)

夫动静知宗祖，无事更寻谁。此中妙义，颇能识乎？然静乃动之基，不静不能动也。不静而动，便非真动。因其水源不清，不足以结丹，故动亦不用。必也虚极静笃时忽然一动，方是真动也。要识动静原是一物，静时为体，动时为用，无有体安有用也。是以静以待动，正玄关辟发时也。乘此辟机，凝神于内，动气亦自返回本根，与神合成一体，方为着落也。不从动中认其宗祖，静时无声无色，何以辨别？动既静中得来，乃知动是宗祖，静亦宗祖也。何谓宗祖？因此动乃生人生物之本根，故云。然而动时固知宗祖所在，而静时一片真空，虽欲强动亦不可也。无事更寻谁，乃对有事而言。动时凝神于气根，乃有事也。然而有事几同无事，设惑于有事，妄自搬运，反碍活泼之机，非清静无为之大道也。有事若行无事，无事但观其妙，更有何物之可寻哉！此二句包括天机，慎勿轻视。总而言之，动时可觅宗祖，静时无物可寻，但守其静，以待再动可也。动则观窍，静则观妙，金丹之全旨备矣！

“Movement and stillness — know the ancestor; when there is nothing to do, whom else would you seek?” Can you grasp the wonderful meaning of this? Stillness is the foundation of movement; without stillness there can be no movement. Movement without stillness is not true movement; because its source is not clear, it is not enough to form the elixir, so such movement is of no use. Only a sudden movement at the point of utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness is true movement. Know that movement and stillness are originally one thing: in stillness it is the substance, in movement it is the function — without substance, how could there be function? So to wait for movement with stillness is exactly the moment when the Mysterious Pass opens. Taking this opening, concentrate the spirit within, and the moving breath will naturally return to its root and unite with the spirit as one body — only then does it find its resting place. If you do not recognize its ancestor in movement, then in stillness there is no sound and no color — how would you distinguish it? Since movement comes from stillness, one knows that movement is the ancestor, and stillness too is the ancestor. Why call it the ancestor? Because this movement is the root that gives birth to people and things — hence the name. Yet although in movement one knows where the ancestor is, in stillness there is a sheet of true emptiness, and even if you wanted to force it to move, you could not. “When there is nothing to do, whom else would you seek?” is said in contrast to “having something to do.” In movement, concentrating the spirit at the root of breath is “having something to do.” Yet having something to do is almost the same as having nothing to do; if one is misled by “having something to do” and recklessly transports the breath, one obstructs the lively mechanism instead, and that is not the Great Way of purity, stillness and non-action. When there is something to do, act as if there were nothing to do; when there is nothing to do, simply contemplate the wonder — what else is there to seek! These two lines contain the mechanism of Heaven; do not take them lightly. In sum: in movement one may seek the ancestor; in stillness there is nothing to seek — simply keep the stillness and wait for the next movement. In movement contemplate the opening, in stillness contemplate the wonder — the whole purport of the Golden Elixir is here!

§3
Original (Chinese)

禅乃无可言者，以其无形无色，无声无臭，只空空洞洞一太虚之境界，故不易形诸笔墨。及静极生动，又曰机矣。今之佛徒只信习禅，不信有机，是以流入顽空，仍不免生老病死之患。然禅机二字，有密切关系，禅定机来，机息禅静。《道德经》谓有无相生，丹经谓动静互用。自初功以至结果，总是二字往来为用，无他道也。

“Meditation” (chan) cannot be put into words, because it has no form and no color, no sound and no smell; it is only an empty, open realm of the Great Void, so it is not easy to describe with brush and ink. When stillness reaches its extreme and gives rise to movement, that is called the “mechanism” (ji). Today’s Buddhists believe only in practicing meditation and do not believe in the mechanism, so they flow into dead emptiness and still cannot escape the sufferings of birth, old age, illness and death. Yet the two words chan and ji are closely related: when meditation is settled, the mechanism comes; when the mechanism subsides, meditation becomes still again. The Daodejing says “being and non-being give birth to each other”; the alchemical scriptures say “movement and stillness serve each other.” From the first work to the final result, it is always these two words alternating in use; there is no other way.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

此几段可作为本书概论，盖气功总是动静二字循环互用。老子《道德经》十六章说：“致极虚，守静笃。万物并作，吾以观复。夫物芸芸，各复归其根。归根曰静，是谓复命。复命曰常，知常曰明。不知常，妄作凶。”即说明静极生动，动极复静的循环往复规律。万物蓬勃生长即是动，返回本根就是静。

These few passages can serve as an overview of this book, for qigong is always the two words movement and stillness alternating and serving each other. Chapter 16 of Laozi’s Daodejing says: “Attain the utmost emptiness, keep steadfast stillness. The ten thousand things arise together, and I watch their return. Things flourish in profusion, and each returns to its root. Returning to the root is called stillness; stillness is called returning to one’s destiny. Returning to one’s destiny is called the constant; knowing the constant is called illumination. Not knowing the constant, one acts recklessly and meets misfortune.” This explains the law of cyclical return: stillness at its extreme gives rise to movement, and movement at its extreme returns to stillness. The vigorous growth of all things is movement; returning to the root is stillness.

Key Terms

  • Movement and stillness: the guiding principle of this part; “in stillness it is the substance, in movement it is the function.”
  • The Pole Star holding the center while the host of stars circles around it: the mind is like the unmoving Pole Star, the breath like the revolving stars. Compare Part One, Chapter 12: “the mind is the pivot of the Dipper; the breath is the host of stars.”
  • Stillness is the foundation of movement: movement is born out of stillness.
  • True movement: a sudden movement at the point of utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness; movement without stillness “is not true movement.”
  • Ancestor (zongzu 宗祖): this movement is “the root that gives birth to people and things.”
  • Root of breath: the root place where breath is produced, that is, the lower Cinnabar Field (see Sections 2 and 3).
  • Transporting (banyun 搬运): forcibly guiding the breath with the intention, which the author opposes.
  • In movement contemplate the opening, in stillness contemplate the wonder: an echo of Laozi, chapter 1, “always without desire, one contemplates its wonder; always with desire, one contemplates its boundaries.”
  • Mysterious Pass: the opening that opens when movement arises at the extreme of stillness; see Section 6.
  • Meditation (chan), mechanism (ji): chan is the realm of empty stillness, ji the movement arising at the extreme of stillness; “when meditation is settled, the mechanism comes; when the mechanism subsides, meditation becomes still again.”
  • Dead emptiness (wankong 顽空): emptiness that is only empty, without awareness and without the spark of life; see realm of yin.
  • “Being and non-being give birth to each other”: from the Laozi, chapter 2.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The whole work is only the one word “stillness”: there is no need to pursue “movement.” When stillness is reached, movement comes by itself; if stillness is not reached, any movement that comes is not genuine.
  2. When movement comes: gently concentrate the spirit (the attention) on the place of movement and let it return to its root and unite with the spirit. Do not “transport” it.
  3. When the movement has passed: “simply keep the stillness and wait for the next movement” — do not linger, do not chase it; return to stillness and wait for the next time.
  4. Two sentences to remember: in movement contemplate the opening, in stillness contemplate the wonder. When there is something to do, act as if there were nothing to do; when there is nothing to do, simply contemplate the wonder.
  5. Do not seek only emptiness: emptiness without the mechanism falls into dead emptiness; the mechanism without emptiness becomes reckless movement. The two are two sides of one thing.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Movement and stillness — know the ancestor; when there is nothing to do, whom else would you seek?”: these two lines are an old alchemical verse, which the author explains line by line in what follows. The translation first renders them literally, in quotation marks to mark the quotation.
  2. “Those in recent years who have not been able to …” (jin nian lai suo bu neng … zhe 近年来所不能……者): “those in recent years who have not been able to …”
  3. The Daodejing, chapter 16, quoted in Qiu Ling’s note reads 致极虚 in the base text, where the received text has 致虚极; the translation follows the sense of the received text.

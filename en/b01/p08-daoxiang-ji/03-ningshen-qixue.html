3 · Concentrate the Spirit in the Cavity of Breath, Every Breath Returning to the Root

3 · Concentrate the Spirit in the Cavity of Breath, Every Breath Returning to the Root — the word “clear,” and what is “before your eyes”

About This Chapter

This section is made up of five passages, built around the eight characters “concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root” (ningshen qixue, xixi guigen 凝神炁穴，息息归根):

  1. Word by word: ning is to gather; shen, spirit, is the right thought in the mind; qi is the true primordial breath of Prior Heaven; xue, cavity, is the root that produces the primordial breath of Prior Heaven; xi is respiration; xixi, “breath after breath,” means continuous and unbroken; gui is to return; gen is the root. Concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath means gathering the right thought of the mind in the Cavity of Breath and not letting the spirit run outward — when the spirit concentrates, the fire gathers; when the fire gathers below, the water rises of itself. Every breath returning to the root means that the breathing does not leave the root that produces breath, as if present, as if forgotten, seeming to be and seeming not to be — what the ancients called embryonic breathing. Qiu Ling’s note: embryonic breathing is the main sign that concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath is working; if there is never any embryonic breathing, the spirit has gone outward and has not entered the Cavity of Breath.
  2. Stopping and contemplating: stopping is resting the True Intention on “the place of the highest good”; contemplating is warming “the place of the highest good” with the light of the eyes — both are the lower Cinnabar Field.
  3. The one word “clear”: there is no need to rush in concentrating the spirit; simply let the light of the eyes shine clearly on the lower field. When it is clear, the spirit is lucid and does not sink into sleep; when it is clear, the breath is fresh and does not fall into dullness — like a bright moon in a clear sky, which is clear when no cloud covers it. And one should sit when one has slept enough and one’s spirit is full; otherwise “the host of demons takes over,” and one “hands the demons the hilt of the sword.”
  4. Mind and breath relying on each other: the spirit is settled by relying on the breath, and the breath is at peace by relying on the spirit. When spirit and breath cling to each other, the belly becomes warm of itself; nourished by this warmth, the ancestral primordial breath of Prior Heaven arises; as soon as the mechanism moves, the spirit is aware of it; seeking out the place of movement with the Original Spirit, one finds the “root of breath,” and from then on takes it as one’s “nest of peace and joy.”
  5. What “the Dao is before your eyes” means: Reverting the Light is only the work of beginning, not the Dao itself; the light of which Lü Chunyang spoke — “the lead cauldron warm and warm, light coming through the curtains” — is what is “before your eyes”; as soon as one sees this light, one knows that the true seed has come out of the furnace.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

我今从“凝神炁穴，息息归根”八字详为解释。夫凝者聚也。神者心中之正念也。炁者先天之真炁也，无形无象，生于虚极静笃之时。穴即生先天炁之本根。所以凝神入炁穴者，即将我心中之正念，凝聚于炁穴，不令神往外驰。神凝则火聚，火聚于下，水自上升，此水火调济之要诀，亦凝神炁穴之妙谛也。息者呼吸也。息息者，绵绵不断之义也。归者返回也。根者基本也。息息归根，即呼吸不离生炁根本，若存若忘，似有似无，昔所谓胎息者是也。

Now I shall explain in detail the eight characters “concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root.” Ning means to gather. Shen, the spirit, is the right thought in the mind. Qi is the true primordial breath of Prior Heaven, without form and without image, born at the time of utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness. Xue, the cavity, is the root that produces the primordial breath of Prior Heaven. So “concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath” means gathering the right thought of my mind in the Cavity of Breath and not letting the spirit run outward. When the spirit concentrates, the fire gathers; when the fire gathers below, the water rises of itself — this is the essential formula for balancing water and fire, and also the wonderful truth of concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath. Xi is respiration. Xixi, “breath after breath,” means continuous and unbroken. Gui means to return. Gen means the root. “Every breath returning to the root” means that the breathing does not leave the root that produces breath, as if present, as if forgotten, seeming to be and seeming not to be — this is what the ancients called embryonic breathing.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

炁穴即下丹田。息息归根即胎息，而胎息则为凝神入炁穴得效的主要标志。若终无胎息发生，则神必已外驰而未入于炁穴。

The Cavity of Breath is the lower Cinnabar Field. Every breath returning to the root is embryonic breathing, and embryonic breathing is the main sign that concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath is working. If no embryonic breathing ever arises, then the spirit must have gone outward and not entered the Cavity of Breath.

§2
Original (Chinese)

初学坐以定息为要，以止观为主。止者何？将我真意止于至善地之义也。观者何？将我目光温煦至善地之义也。目光真意常常止于斯，自然真息得定，自然身心得静。由此静中动而觉者，方是活泼天机。能将此机用真意逆归造化炉中，使神炁合抱，积累日久，真种始产。此入手要诀，君其记之。

For beginners in sitting, settling the breath is essential, and stopping and contemplating is the main thing. What is stopping? It means resting my True Intention on the place of the highest good. What is contemplating? It means warming the place of the highest good with the light of my eyes. When the light of the eyes and the True Intention constantly rest there, the True Breath naturally becomes settled, and body and mind naturally become still. What moves out of this stillness and is noticed — only that is the lively mechanism of Heaven. If one can use the True Intention to return this mechanism, against the current, into the furnace of creation, letting spirit and primordial breath unite and embrace, and accumulate this over a long time, only then is the true seed produced. This is the essential formula for beginning; remember it.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

至善地、造化炉均指下丹田。

“The place of the highest good” and “the furnace of creation” both mean the lower Cinnabar Field.

§3
Original (Chinese)

如凝神一事，不必太急，但将目光朗照下田，心亦随之俱到，要紧识得这个朗字。朗则神清，而不至流于沉睡。朗则气爽，而不至渐入昏耗。如晴空明月，无云翳遮蔽则朗。凝神亦应如是。学人不识朗字之妙，一入蒲团，神即驰去。初坐禅床，念竟纷来，此无他，失朗照力也。苟目光朗彻下田，性光亦随之凝聚于气海，如宝镜高悬，一切阴魔，无不顿化于无有。然亦须俟精神充足时去坐，方为有益，否则，群魔用事，慧剑无所用其力，足以授魔一柄矣！故高士哲人每遇睡足之际，神清之候，正襟危坐，先令神蛰于内，不使光泄于外，自然一片清虚之气，氤氲于两间。如此凝神，自得其妙。若目光未能凝入，虽日坐数次亦无益也。是以凝神须识窍。能识得窍自得其妙。老氏曰：“玄之又玄，众妙之门”。一言道破天机。

As for concentrating the spirit, there is no need to rush; simply let the light of the eyes shine clearly on the lower field, and the mind goes there with it. What matters is to recognize this word “clear.” When it is clear, the spirit is lucid and does not drift into deep sleep. When it is clear, the breath is fresh and does not gradually fall into dullness and exhaustion. Like a bright moon in a clear sky: when no cloud covers it, it is clear. Concentrating the spirit should be like this. Students who do not recognize the wonder of the word “clear” — as soon as they sit on the cushion, the spirit runs off; when they first sit on the meditation bench, thoughts come in droves. This is for no other reason than that they have lost the power of clear illumination. If the light of the eyes shines clearly and penetratingly on the lower field, the light of nature too gathers in the Sea of Breath, like a precious mirror hung high, and all yin demons are at once dissolved into nothing. But one must also sit when one’s spirit is full for it to be beneficial; otherwise the host of demons takes over, the sword of wisdom has no way to use its strength, and one has handed the demons the hilt of the sword! So the lofty and the wise, whenever they have slept enough and their spirit is clear, sit upright with their robes straight, first letting the spirit hibernate within and not letting the light leak outward; naturally a sheet of clear, empty breath spreads densely between Heaven and Earth. Concentrating the spirit like this, one naturally obtains its wonder. If the light of the eyes cannot concentrate inward, even sitting several times a day is of no benefit. So in concentrating the spirit one must recognize the opening; recognizing the opening, one naturally obtains its wonder. Laozi said: “Mystery upon mystery, the gate of all wonders.” With one sentence he revealed the mechanism of Heaven.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

反复言一“朗”字，学者自易体会凝神之义。

By repeatedly speaking of the one word “clear,” the text makes it easy for students to grasp the meaning of concentrating the spirit.

§4
Original (Chinese)

在常人不知静观，未能凝神，故此气仍从口鼻出入。知道之士静观窍妙，神亦随之定于此处。神为一身之主宰，神定于此，息亦随之定于此，所谓心息相依者此也。神不依息必外驰，息不依神难自伏，是以神依息而定，息依神而安，互相依附，始归大定。但能静观凝神，即能得此效验，决非别有奇法也。神息依恋，腹自温暖。先天祖炁受此煦育而后始发生焉。此气一生，气机先动。机一动，神即觉。丹经所谓时至神知，即此时也。此机既动，气亦随之外涌，我以元神寻其动处，即得着气根矣！既得着气根，便以此处为安乐窝可也。神既以此为家，息亦相伴不去。神息既相合，元气受息之吸收，亦自返回本根，二气相接，常自氤氲恍惚，故《入药镜》曰：“先天炁，后天炁，得之者，常似醉，”即指此也。神者火也，火生于木。气者水也，水生于金。意者土也，神为之体，意为之用，其实神与意一也。三家相见，即是五行攒簇，如孙悟空压到五行山下，即攒簇五行之义也。金受火制，不得外驰，即是悟空被山所压。五行既合而为一，真息亦相依而不相离，此即伏气之真功。丹经云息息归根，亦即藏伏之义。功夫到此，自然如是，决非勉强可知也，《胎息经》云：“胎从伏气中结”。气不伏而欲结胎，未之有也。

Ordinary people do not know quiet contemplation and cannot concentrate the spirit, so this breath still goes in and out through mouth and nose. Those who know the Dao quietly contemplate the wonder of the opening, and the spirit settles there along with it. The spirit is the master of the whole body: when the spirit settles there, the breath settles there with it — this is what is called “mind and breath relying on each other.” If the spirit does not rely on the breath, it will surely run outward; if the breath does not rely on the spirit, it can hardly lie down by itself. So the spirit is settled by relying on the breath, and the breath is at peace by relying on the spirit; relying on each other, they return to great absorption. If only one can contemplate quietly and concentrate the spirit, one can obtain this effect; there is certainly no other special method. When spirit and breath cling to each other, the belly becomes warm of itself. Only after receiving this warm nourishment does the ancestral primordial breath of Prior Heaven arise. As soon as this breath arises, the mechanism of breath moves first; as soon as the mechanism moves, the spirit is aware of it. What the alchemical scriptures call “when the time comes, the spirit knows” is this moment. When this mechanism has moved, the breath also wells outward with it; I seek out the place of movement with the Original Spirit, and then I have found the root of breath! Having found the root of breath, one can take this place as one’s nest of peace and joy. Since the spirit takes it as its home, the breath also keeps it company and does not leave. When spirit and breath have united, the original breath, absorbed by the respiration, also returns by itself to its root; the two breaths join and are constantly dense and vague. So the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine says: “The breath of Prior Heaven, the breath of Posterior Heaven — those who obtain them are always as if drunk.” This is what it refers to. The spirit is fire, and fire is born of wood. The breath is water, and water is born of metal. The intention is earth. The spirit is the substance and the intention the function; in fact spirit and intention are one. When the three families meet, that is the gathering of the five phases — like Sun Wukong pressed under the Five Phases Mountain, which means gathering the five phases: metal restrained by fire, unable to run outward, is Wukong pressed under the mountain. Once the five phases have united as one, the True Breath too relies on them and does not leave — this is the true work of letting the breath lie down. The alchemical scriptures’ “every breath returning to the root” also means hiding and lying down. When the work reaches this point, it is naturally so; it certainly cannot be achieved by forcing. The Scripture of Embryonic Breathing says: “The embryo is formed within the lying-down of the breath.” To want to form the embryo without the breath lying down — there has never been such a thing.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

气机一动，我以元神寻其动处，得着气机便以此为安乐窝，真息亦相依而不离，此是凝神炁穴息息归根之要诀。

As soon as the mechanism of breath moves, I seek out the place of movement with the Original Spirit; having found the mechanism of breath, I take this place as my nest of peace and joy, and the True Breath relies on it and does not leave — this is the essential formula of “concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, every breath returning to the root.”

§5
Original (Chinese)

人人都说道在眼前，究竟眼前是个什么？回光返照是下手功夫，不是道。而道在眼前，回光返照在气穴，气穴在眼下，非在目前。要知在眼前的是个什么，即纯阳所说“温温铅鼎，光透帘帏”，此光非在眼前乎！故修道之士一遇此光发现，即知是真种出炉。当此时吾之元神凝归气穴，神返气自回，再加以息息归根，与元神元气相依相恋，呼吸既与神气合一，始为真呼吸，有此真呼吸，口鼻呼吸自断矣。此是天机，莫忽略看过。

Everyone says that the Dao is before your eyes; but what, after all, is before your eyes? Reverting the Light is the work of beginning, not the Dao. It is said that the Dao is before your eyes, but Reverting the Light shines on the Cavity of Breath, and the Cavity of Breath is below the eyes, not in front of them. To know what is before your eyes: it is what Chunyang (Lü Dongbin) spoke of — “the lead cauldron warm and warm, light coming through the curtains.” Is this light not before your eyes! So when those who cultivate the Dao see this light appear, they know that the true seed has come out of the furnace. At this time my Original Spirit concentrates and returns to the Cavity of Breath; when the spirit returns, the breath returns of itself; adding every breath returning to the root, it clings to the Original Spirit and the original breath. Only when the respiration has united with spirit and breath is it true respiration; with this true respiration, the breathing of mouth and nose naturally ceases. This is the mechanism of Heaven; do not pass over it lightly.

Qiu Ling's noteon §5
Original note (Chinese)

吕纯阳《沁园春》词云：“七还丹，在人先须炼己待时。正一阳初动，中宵漏永，温温铅鼎，光透帘帏。造化争驰，虎龙交媾，进火工夫。牛斗危。曲江上见月华莹净，有个乌飞。当时自饮刀圭，又谁信无中养就儿？辨水源清浊，木金间隔，不因师指此事难知。道要玄微，天机深远，下手速修犹太迟，蓬莱路，仗三千行满，独步云归。”温温铅鼎谓产铅之鼎、温铅之炉，铅指先天真气。光透帘帏，即内视有张幕垂帘之象，真阳之光透之。

Lü Chunyang’s lyric to the tune “Spring in the Qin Garden” (Qinyuan chun 沁园春) says: “The sevenfold Returned Elixir: in a person, one must first refine oneself and wait for the time. Just as the one yang first stirs, the water clock long in the middle of the night, the lead cauldron warm and warm, light comes through the curtains. Creation races on, tiger and dragon unite; the work of advancing the fire is at its perilous moment. On the Winding River one sees the moon’s radiance pure and clean, and a crow flies across. At that moment one drinks the spatula of elixir oneself — yet who would believe that the child is nurtured out of nothing? To distinguish clear from turbid at the water’s source, and the separation of wood and metal — without a teacher’s pointing, this matter is hard to know. The Dao is mysterious and subtle, the mechanism of Heaven deep and far; even beginning to cultivate quickly is already too late. The road to Penglai: relying on three thousand good deeds fulfilled, one returns alone on the clouds.” “The lead cauldron warm and warm” means the cauldron that produces lead, the furnace that warms lead; lead means the true breath of Prior Heaven. “Light coming through the curtains” means that in inner vision there seems to be a screen with hanging curtains, through which the light of true yang shines.

Key Terms

  • Ning, shen, qi, xue; xi, xixi, gui, gen: this section’s word-by-word explanation of the eight characters.
  • Spirit: defined in this part as “the right thought in the mind.”
  • Concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath: gathering the right thought of the mind in the Cavity of Breath.
  • When the spirit concentrates, the fire gathers; when the fire gathers below, the water rises of itself: the essential formula for balancing water and fire.
  • Every breath returning to the root: the breathing not leaving the root that produces breath, that is, embryonic breathing; see True Breath.
  • Stopping and contemplating: stopping — resting the True Intention on the place of the highest good; contemplating — warming the place of the highest good with the light of the eyes.
  • The place of the highest good, the furnace of creation: both mean the lower Cinnabar Field (Qiu Ling’s note); an echo of the Great Learning, “to rest in the highest good.”
  • Clear (lang 朗): when clear, the spirit is lucid and does not sleep; when clear, the breath is fresh and does not grow dull.
  • Light of nature: see light of Nature.
  • Handing the demons the hilt of the sword.
  • The spirit hibernating within: the spirit lying hidden inside like a hibernating animal.
  • Mind and breath relying on each other: the spirit settled by relying on the breath, the breath at peace by relying on the spirit; see regulating the breath.
  • “When the time comes, the spirit knows.”
  • Root of breath / nest of peace and joy: finding the place where the mechanism of breath moves and taking it as home.
  • Gathering the five phases: the three families meeting — spirit as fire, breath as water, intention as earth.
  • Lying down of the breath: “the breath lies down by itself”; see Section 2.
  • The Dao before your eyes: in this part, the light of “light coming through the curtains.”
  • “The lead cauldron warm and warm, light coming through the curtains”: from Lü Chunyang’s “Spring in the Qin Garden”; Qiu Ling’s note: lead means the true breath of Prior Heaven; light coming through the curtains means that in inner vision there seem to be hanging curtains through which the light of true yang shines.
  • Spatula of elixir (daogui 刀圭): a tiny measure of elixir, a metaphor for a point of true elixir.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The eight characters in plain words: gather the right thought of the mind deep in the lower abdomen and do not let it run off; the breathing continuous and unbroken, never leaving that place, seeming to be and seeming not to be.
  2. The test (Qiu Ling): is there embryonic breathing — does the breathing naturally become extremely fine, as if it had returned into the lower abdomen? If it never does, the spirit has kept running outward.
  3. The formula of the word “clear” (the essence of this section): when illuminating the lower abdomen, the mind is bright and awake, like a bright moon in a clear sky. Without clarity, one easily falls asleep; without clarity, stray thoughts multiply. When you are sleepy or tired, do not force yourself to sit — sit when you have slept enough and your spirit is clear, and the results will be good.
  4. The result of mind and breath relying on each other: the belly becomes warm of itself. This is the simplest and most reliable early sign.
  5. Finding the “nest of peace and joy”: as soon as the mechanism of breath moves, use the mind to “find” the place where it moves; having found it, settle there from then on.
  6. Distinguish method and goal: Reverting the Light is only the work of beginning; it “is not the Dao.” Do not take the method itself for the goal.

Translator's Notes

  1. “One has handed the demons the hilt of the sword” (zu yi shou mo yi bing 足以授魔一柄): from the idiom “to hand someone the hilt”; the translation follows this.
  2. In Lü Chunyang’s “Spring in the Qin Garden” quoted in Qiu Ling’s note, the base text breaks the line between 进火工夫 and 牛斗危, while the received text mostly reads 进火工夫牛斗危 as one line. The translation follows the sense of the received text, understanding 牛斗危 as the perilous moment of the fire phasing. This lyric is full of veiled language; the translation gives only the general sense, and readers should rely on Qiu Ling’s explanation of “the lead cauldron warm and warm, light coming through the curtains.”
  3. “Sun Wukong pressed under the Five Phases Mountain”: the author borrows the story from Journey to the West to illustrate “gathering the five phases,” to make it easy for students to understand: the monkey of the mind (Wukong, belonging to metal) is restrained by the five phases and cannot run outward.
  4. The Scripture of Embryonic Breathing: the Scripture of Embryonic Breathing of the Most High Jade Emperor (Gaoshang Yuhuang taixi jing 高上玉皇胎息经), which opens with “The embryo is formed within the lying-down of the breath; the breath rests within the embryo that has formed.”

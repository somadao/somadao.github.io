6 · What Is the Mysterious Pass?

6 · What Is the Mysterious Pass? — not seen in stillness, but sought in movement

About This Chapter

The “Mysterious Pass,” the most mysterious thing in the alchemical books, is explained most clearly in this section. Qiu Ling’s note also says: ordinarily this opening has no form or image; though it is a matter of “concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath,” one cannot simply take the Cavity of Breath to be the Mysterious Pass — the Cavity of Breath mostly means the lower Cinnabar Field, and only during practice, when spirit and breath unite and something arises out of nothing, does the Mysterious Pass appear.

This section has five passages:

  1. One opening for the three teachings: Confucius called it the “secret door,” the Buddhists call it the “Mysterious Pass,” the Daoists call it the “Mysterious Female”; for the first steps of the work, Confucius said “withdraw and hide in secrecy,” the Buddhists say “hide oneself in the Northern Dipper,” the Daoists say “the true person dives into the deep abyss” — all are the one matter of concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath.
  2. Before the work begins, there is no such opening: concentrate the spirit in the lower field, let the light of nature and the light of the eyes constantly illuminate it, and as soon as warmth arises, the opening opens too; it opens at the moment of movement, and to find the place where it moves is to find the mysterious opening; once found, one must constantly lie hidden within it — to leave this opening is to leave the Dao.
  3. The Mysterious Pass is not seen in stillness but sought in movement: contemplating in stillness until utmost emptiness, there is naturally a movement within stillness — this movement is the mechanism of Heaven, the breath of Prior Heaven. As soon as the breath of Prior Heaven moves it wants to burst through the pass and go out; I quietly shine the light of the eyes on the place of movement, and the breath of Posterior Heaven naturally relies on the mind and also returns to the place of movement — this is Posterior Heaven seeing Prior Heaven, vague and indistinct, as if there were a thing.
  4. The first step is to illuminate the lower field, like the moon reflected in an autumn pool: illuminating until utmost emptiness and stillness, suddenly there is a movement, vague and indistinct, as if there were an omen — this is the opening of the Mysterious Pass; to concentrate the spirit within it while it opens is to grasp the wonder of the opening. Bai Yuchan said: “Since I received the true oral formula from my immortal teacher, it only teaches to concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath.”
  5. To concentrate the spirit at the place of movement is to bring fire to water, the sun to the moon, the spirit to unite with breath. When Heaven and Earth unite, rain falls; when husband and wife unite, the household prospers; when spirit and breath unite, the true seed is produced.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

玄关为何物？《易》曰：“诚性存存，道义之门。”既知为道义之门，是知其窍也。要知三教圣人无不从此而出。孔曰：密户。释曰：玄关。道曰：玄牝。皆指此也。至于初步功夫，孔曰：退藏于密。释曰：北斗里藏身。道曰：真人潜深渊。皆凝神入炁穴之一事耳。儒不得密户，则不可以为儒。释不得玄关，则不可以为释。道不得玄牝，则不可以为道。得其门而入，安身立命有所矣！不明此窍，胡乱摸索，到底不能成功。故老氏曰：“玄之又玄，众妙之门。”

What is the Mysterious Pass? The Changes says: “Perfecting one’s nature and preserving it again and again — this is the gate of the Dao and of righteousness.” Since one knows it is the gate of the Dao and of righteousness, one knows it is an opening. Know that the sages of the three teachings all came forth from here. Confucius called it the “secret door”; the Buddhists call it the “Mysterious Pass”; the Daoists call it the “Mysterious Female” — all refer to this. As for the first steps of the work, Confucius said “withdraw and hide in secrecy”; the Buddhists say “hide oneself in the Northern Dipper”; the Daoists say “the true person dives into the deep abyss” — all are simply the one matter of concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath. A Confucian who does not find the secret door cannot be a Confucian; a Buddhist who does not find the Mysterious Pass cannot be a Buddhist; a Daoist who does not find the Mysterious Female cannot be a Daoist. Find its gate and enter, and settling one’s body and establishing one’s life have somewhere to rest! Without understanding this opening, groping about at random, one will never succeed in the end. So Laozi said: “Mystery upon mystery, the gate of all wonders.”

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

此窍平常无形无象，既为凝神入炁穴一事，亦不能理解为炁穴即玄关，炁穴多指下丹田。只有行功时神与气合，无中生有，玄关方现。

Ordinarily this opening has no form or image. Though it is said to be the one matter of “concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath,” one cannot take this to mean that the Cavity of Breath is the Mysterious Pass; the Cavity of Breath mostly means the lower Cinnabar Field. Only during practice, when spirit and breath unite and something arises out of nothing, does the Mysterious Pass appear.

§2
Original (Chinese)

未用功之先，原无此窍。必凝神于下田，使性光目光常常观照，暖气一生，此窍亦辟。但能趁其辟时凝神于内，行坐不离这个，方能成漏尽一通。不知其窍将何以还丹者哉！但此窍辟于动时，能觅其动处，即得着玄窍矣！得着而后须常潜藏于内，一时离了此窍，即是离了道矣！故《中庸》有“道不可须臾离也”之说，即指此事也。何也？一离此窍则神驰矣！神驰则火寒矣！丹经云：“玉炉常使汤温暖，金鼎莫教火微寒。”能知此义，方可言道。

Before the work begins, there is originally no such opening. One must concentrate the spirit in the lower field and let the light of nature and the light of the eyes constantly illuminate it; as soon as warmth arises, this opening opens too. Only if one can, while it opens, concentrate the spirit within it, not leaving it whether walking or sitting, can one accomplish the one penetration of “exhausting the outflows.” Without knowing this opening, by what would one return the elixir! This opening opens at the time of movement; if one can find the place where it moves, one has found the mysterious opening! Having found it, one must constantly lie hidden within it; to leave this opening even for a moment is to leave the Dao! So the Doctrine of the Mean says, “The Dao may not be left for an instant,” and this is what it refers to. Why? Because as soon as one leaves this opening, the spirit races off; when the spirit races off, the fire grows cold! The alchemical scriptures say: “In the jade furnace always keep the liquid warm; in the golden cauldron never let the fire grow faint and cold.” Only those who know this meaning can speak of the Dao.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

凝神观照下田得法，玄窍乃辟，辟即开，开即动，即静极而动、无中生有之义。正所谓“炁发则成窍，机息则渺茫。”觅其炁动之处，潜神于内不须臾离，此乃练功机微。

When concentrating the spirit and illuminating the lower Cinnabar Field is done in the right way, the mysterious opening opens. Opening is opening, and opening is movement — that is, the meaning of movement at the extreme of stillness, something arising out of nothing. As the saying goes: “When the breath issues forth, it becomes an opening; when the mechanism rests, all is vague and boundless.” Seeking out the place where the breath moves and hiding the spirit within it without leaving for an instant — this is the subtle point of practice.

§3
Original (Chinese)

要知静观到至虚至极处，自然静中一动。此一动即天机也。能知动从何处动，迹而寻之，则知玄关，不在静中见，而在动中求也。静者阴也，动者阳也。一动一静，而阴阳可以和合。惜乎今之学道者，不知静中求动，偏向头上加头，无怪始终不能得其微奥也。如“先天炁，后天炁，得之者常似醉”之句，人皆不知何以为先天炁？何以为后天炁？一味枯修瞎炼，到老未知道其中滋味。岂知先天炁，即坐到静极，忽然一动，此动即先天炁也。先天炁既动即欲拱关而出，我以目光静照动处，后天息炁自然与心相依，亦回至动处也。真息既与先天炁相接，即是后天见先天也。二炁相接，便觉恍恍惚惚，似乎有物；杳杳冥冥，似乎有信。此乃先后天二炁相吸恋，故如醉如痴，已入无何有之乡，不知有人我之相、天地之形色也。功夫到此，方为得手。试坐十日，能将心中一切尘埃扫尽，回两目之光下照丹田，不即不离，即则着于相，不能得其微妙，离则流于空，不能合夫天机，必也冲而用之，虚以待之，至冲至虚，自有真机发动。能知机动于何处，便识得佛家不二法门也。我能顺其机以合之，即是采取妙药之大道。

Know that when contemplation in stillness reaches utmost emptiness and the extreme, there is naturally a movement within stillness. This movement is the mechanism of Heaven. If one can know where this movement moves from and follow its traces to seek it, one knows that the Mysterious Pass is not seen in stillness but sought in movement. Stillness is yin, movement is yang; with one movement and one stillness, yin and yang can be harmonized and united. What a pity that those who study the Dao today do not know to seek movement within stillness, but insist on putting a head on top of a head; no wonder they never grasp its subtle mystery. Take the line “The breath of Prior Heaven, the breath of Posterior Heaven — those who obtain them are always as if drunk”: no one knows what the breath of Prior Heaven is, or what the breath of Posterior Heaven is; they practice drily and blindly and grow old without knowing its taste. How would they know that the breath of Prior Heaven is this: sitting until the extreme of stillness, suddenly there is a movement — this movement is the breath of Prior Heaven. As soon as the breath of Prior Heaven moves, it wants to burst through the pass and go out; I quietly shine the light of my eyes on the place of movement, and the breath of Posterior Heaven naturally relies on the mind and also returns to the place of movement. Since the True Breath has joined the breath of Prior Heaven, this is Posterior Heaven seeing Prior Heaven. When the two breaths join, one feels vague and indistinct, as if there were a thing; dim and dark, as if there were something trustworthy. This is the two breaths of Prior and Posterior Heaven attracting and clinging to each other; so one is as if drunk, as if dazed, having entered “the land of nothing whatsoever,” unaware of the distinction between self and others or of the forms and colors of Heaven and Earth. Only when the work reaches this point has one got hold of it. Try sitting for ten days: sweep away all the dust in the mind, turn the light of both eyes back to shine down on the Cinnabar Field, neither clinging nor separating — cling, and one becomes attached to forms and cannot obtain its subtlety; separate, and one drifts into emptiness and cannot accord with the mechanism of Heaven. One must “use it as emptiness,” await it in emptiness, and at utmost emptiness the true mechanism naturally stirs. If one can know where the mechanism moves, one recognizes the Buddhists’ “Dharma gate of non-duality.” If I can follow this mechanism and unite with it, that is the great Way of gathering the wondrous medicine.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

静极而动，乃先天炁之动，我即以后天呼吸相接，将呼吸随寂照入于动处，则真息遂生，鼻息几无，如痴似醉，是为得手。

Movement at the extreme of stillness is the movement of the breath of Prior Heaven; I then receive it with the respiration of Posterior Heaven, letting the respiration, along with still illumination, enter the place of movement. Then the True Breath arises, the breath in the nose is almost gone, and one is as if dazed, as if drunk — this is getting hold of it.

§4
Original (Chinese)

第一下手必先观照下田，如月映秋潭，虽有实无，虽无而实有。照至至虚至静，忽然一动，恍惚似有朕兆，此即玄关开辟时也。乘其开辟之际，而凝神入于其中，方为得着窍妙。况此时有无穷消息，如丹田发暖，耳后生风，恍恍惚惚，杳杳冥冥，皆一阳初动之微机也。识得此机，方可学道。不识此机，则为当面错过。故白玉蟾有云：“自得仙师真口诀，只教凝神入炁穴。”明明说破天机，惜乎学人轻轻看过。不知凝神，坐到老死，亦无丝毫之益于我。故又曰：凝神须知窍。然神即妙明真心也。凝即聚也。窍即玄关也。以神存乎窍内，即是凝神入炁穴，但学人皆不知窍，是以无从结丹。要知窍平时原无形迹，非坐到静极生动时，此窍不开。静中忽然一动，此何故也？阳气欲拱关而出也。藉此阳气外出之际，缓缓以目光下照机动处，便是得着窍矣！我之神既凝乎其中，动气为神所吸收，亦自返回本窍，神恋气，气抱神，二物絪缊，方是真阴真阳交配也。非经阴阳交合而后不能产真种，此一定之理也。如人之祖炁原不自生，必藉神光煦照，而后气机发动，同此一理，更无他说。

The very first step must be to illuminate the lower field, like the moon reflected in an autumn pool: though present, it is in truth absent; though absent, it is in truth present. Illuminating until utmost emptiness and stillness, suddenly there is a movement, vague and indistinct, as if there were an omen — this is the moment when the Mysterious Pass opens. Seizing the moment of its opening and concentrating the spirit within it — only that is grasping the wonder of the opening. Moreover, at this time there are endless tidings, such as warmth arising in the Cinnabar Field, wind arising behind the ears, vagueness and indistinctness, dimness and darkness — all of these are subtle signs of the first stirring of the one yang. Only those who recognize this mechanism can study the Dao. Not recognizing this mechanism is to miss it face to face. So Bai Yuchan said: “Since I received the true oral formula from my immortal teacher, it only teaches to concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath.” He plainly revealed the mechanism of Heaven, but alas, students pass over it lightly. Not knowing how to concentrate the spirit, one may sit until old age and death, and it will not be of the slightest benefit to me. So it is also said: to concentrate the spirit one must know the opening. Now, the spirit is the wondrously bright true mind; to concentrate is to gather; the opening is the Mysterious Pass. To keep the spirit within the opening is to concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath; but students do not know the opening, and so have no way of forming the elixir. Know that this opening ordinarily has no form or trace; unless one sits until movement arises at the extreme of stillness, this opening does not open. Within stillness there is suddenly a movement — why? Because the yang breath wants to burst through the pass and go out. Taking advantage of the moment when this yang breath goes out, slowly shine the light of the eyes down on the place where the mechanism moves, and one has found the opening! Since my spirit is concentrated within it, the moving breath is absorbed by the spirit and also returns of itself to its original opening: the spirit cleaves to the breath, the breath embraces the spirit, the two mingle and blend — only this is the union of true yin and true yang. Without the union of yin and yang, the true seed cannot be produced; this is a fixed principle. Just as a person’s ancestral breath does not arise of itself, but only when warmed and illuminated by the light of the spirit does the mechanism of breath stir — it is the same principle; there is no other explanation.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

静中一动，阳气欲出，我即凝神于动处，遂吸引阳气返回本窍，方能阴阳交合，使神气打成一片，此神返身中气自回之理，亦一机微，学者须知之。

When there is a movement within stillness and the yang breath wants to go out, I concentrate the spirit at the place of movement, thereby drawing the yang breath back to its original opening; only then can yin and yang unite, fusing spirit and breath into one piece. This is the principle of “when the spirit returns into the body, the breath returns of itself,” and it too is a subtle point that students must know.

§5
Original (Chinese)

凝神到动处，即是以火就水，以日就月，以神合气。我之元神凝于动处，动气亦自转回，与神融合一处。天地合而后雨泽降，夫妇合而后家道昌，神气合而后真种产，同是一理也。《易》谓一阴一阳之谓道，而后学闭目枯坐，不辨阴阳，安识造化？无怪徒劳精神也。常常凝神，自然命基坚固，惟恐初坐不耐清寂，终亦无所益也。不怕事繁，全在对境忘境，否则，心烦意乱，不能臻于大定，非保身延年之大道也。

To concentrate the spirit at the place of movement is to bring fire to water, the sun to the moon, the spirit to unite with breath. When my Original Spirit is concentrated at the place of movement, the moving breath also turns back of itself and merges with the spirit in one place. When Heaven and Earth unite, rain and dew fall; when husband and wife unite, the household prospers; when spirit and breath unite, the true seed is produced — it is all the same principle. The Changes says “one yin and one yang: this is called the Dao,” yet later students sit drily with eyes closed, not distinguishing yin and yang — how could they recognize creation? No wonder they waste their spirit in vain. Concentrate the spirit constantly, and naturally the foundation of life becomes firm; the only fear is that when first sitting one cannot bear the pure solitude, and in the end gains nothing. Do not fear that affairs are many; it all lies in facing circumstances and forgetting circumstances. Otherwise, with a vexed mind and confused intention, one cannot reach great absorption, and this is not the great Way of preserving the body and prolonging life.

Qiu Ling's noteon §5
Original note (Chinese)

此段举例重申阴阳和合之理，教人欲知玄关，须辨阴阳。

This passage uses examples to restate the principle of the harmonious union of yin and yang, teaching that those who wish to know the Mysterious Pass must distinguish yin and yang.

Key Terms

  • Mysterious Pass / mysterious opening / Mysterious Female / secret door: the three teachings’ names for one and the same opening.
  • “Perfecting one’s nature and preserving it again and again — this is the gate of the Dao and of righteousness”: from the Appended Statements (part I) of the Book of Changes.
  • “Withdraw and hide in secrecy”: from the Appended Statements (part I) of the Book of Changes.
  • “Hide oneself in the Northern Dipper”: a phrase from a Chan gong’an (Yunmen Wenyan).
  • “The true person dives into the deep abyss”: from the Cantong qi.
  • Penetration of exhausting the outflows: one of the six penetrations — the outflows of affliction exhausted.
  • “The Dao may not be left for an instant”: from the Doctrine of the Mean.
  • “In the jade furnace always keep the liquid warm; in the golden cauldron never let the fire grow faint and cold”: a saying from the alchemical scriptures, a metaphor for keeping the lower Cinnabar Field constantly warm.
  • “When the breath issues forth, it becomes an opening; when the mechanism rests, all is vague and boundless”: the nature of the Mysterious Pass — there is an opening only when there is movement.
  • Mechanism of Heaven: a movement within stillness.
  • Putting a head on top of a head: adding contrivance to what is already complete.
  • “As if there were a thing; as if there were something trustworthy”: echoing chapter 21 of the Daode jing.
  • The land of nothing whatsoever: from the Zhuangzi, “Free and Easy Wandering.”
  • “Use it as emptiness”: echoing chapter 4 of the Daode jing, “The Dao is empty, and in use it is never filled”; chong 冲 means empty.
  • Dharma gate of non-duality: a Buddhist expression from the Vimalakīrti Sūtra.
  • Like the moon reflected in an autumn pool, though present it is absent, though absent it is present: the right measure for illuminating the lower field.
  • Omen (zhenzhao 朕兆): a sign.
  • “Since I received the true oral formula from my immortal teacher, it only teaches to concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath”: words of Bai Yuchan.
  • Wondrously bright true mind: a Buddhist expression, the originally pure, bright, aware mind.
  • When the spirit returns into the body, the breath returns of itself: as soon as the spirit is drawn back, the breath naturally follows.
  • Bringing fire to water, the sun to the moon: the spirit (fire, sun) goes down to the breath (water, moon); see the conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Facing circumstances and forgetting circumstances: facing circumstances without being drawn along by them.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The Mysterious Pass is not a fixed acupoint: ordinarily it is not there; it appears only when movement arises at the extreme of stillness and spirit and breath unite. Do not take a book and go “looking” for the Mysterious Pass on your body.
  2. The Mysterious Pass is sought in movement: first be still; at the extreme of stillness there is a movement; at the moment of “movement,” notice where it moves from — that is the opening.
  3. The right measure for illuminating the lower field: like the moon reflected in an autumn pool — the moon is in the pool and yet not in the pool; you shine on it, and yet you do not grasp it.
  4. Once found, “constantly lie hidden within it”: “as soon as one leaves this opening, the spirit races off; when the spirit races off, the fire grows cold” — in daily life, walking, standing, sitting, or lying down, let a touch of intention remain in the lower abdomen, keeping it constantly warm.
  5. Do not fear many affairs; fear only being drawn along by circumstances: “it all lies in facing circumstances and forgetting circumstances” — however busy life is, if the mind is not carried off by outer circumstances, that is the work.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Perfecting one’s nature and preserving it again and again” (chengxing cuncun): the Appended Statements of the Book of Changes originally has 成性存存, while the base text has 诚性; the original text follows the base text, and the translation follows the sense of “perfecting one’s nature.”
  2. “Hide oneself in the Northern Dipper”: the Chan master Yunmen Wenyan, asked “What is the phrase that passes through the Dharma-body?”, answered “Hide oneself in the Northern Dipper”; the author borrows it to illustrate concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath.
  3. “Accomplish the one penetration of ‘exhausting the outflows’”: “exhausting the outflows” is one of the six penetrations in Buddhism; the author uses it to mean the accomplishment of the work.
  4. “Later students” (houxue 后学): those who came later to study the Dao.

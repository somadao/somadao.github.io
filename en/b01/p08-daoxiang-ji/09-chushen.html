9 · The Method of Sending Out the Spirit

9 · The Method of Sending Out the Spirit — take it back as soon as it goes out; have wisdom and do not use it

About This Chapter

Qiu Ling says: “The going out of the spirit is the highest ideal of Daoist qigong for nourishing life; I record it here so that readers may glimpse the whole course of the practice, and as a reference.” We translate it as it stands, as a document for understanding this system as a whole.

The content of this section closely matches the discussion from Wu Chongxu’s Common Origin of Immortals and Buddhas quoted in Qiu Ling’s note to Part 2, Chapter 9, and is even more concrete:

  • When the spirit is complete and the embryo full, it is just the time to leave the embryo; but one must first examine clearly whether the Yang Spirit is truly pure and complete — only when there is not a speck of drowsiness and not a thread of scatteredness may it move up. The Yang Spirit moving up to the Muddy Pellet does not mean lingering in the Muddy Pellet, but still-illuminating in the Muddy Pellet, transforming together with the two lower fields into one realm of empty space.
  • The sign of the spirit going out is “six-petaled flowers flying in profusion” (snowflakes). Once the spirit has gone out, take it back as soon as it goes out; brief outings, not long; near, not far. Whether one sees beautiful women or treasures, or the cognitive spirit conjures up demons, none of it may be taken as real — “with the slightest hope, one falls into demonic obstruction.”
  • “Suckling” is not real suckling; the name is only borrowed as a metaphor for the careful protection the newly formed Yang Spirit needs: attend to it at every moment, protect it with every thought, but do not fix the intention on thought, nor lose the intention from thought — hold it as if it were there, guard it as if it were not.
  • When the Yang Spirit first goes out, take it back after two or three steps; after seven days, fourteen days… forty-nine days, its spiritual powers are vast, but “at this time it is fitting to have wisdom and not use it.” Only after three years is the power of wisdom settled.
  • The last layer: nine years of returning to emptiness, merging with the Great Void — “if one fixes the intention on empty space, then there is yet another empty space.”
  • The author also tells of a detail: in leaving the embryo one cannot do without the “mother,” and the mother is the numinous nature in the heart; when the spirit goes out, the original nature is not entirely extinguished — otherwise, who is it that goes out and is taken back?

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

神全胎足，正宜脱胎，但须审明阳神是否真正纯全，而后方定上迁与否。必须无一点昏沉，无一丝散乱，阳神始纯，方宜上迁也。但阳神上迁于泥丸，非滞于泥丸，惟将一阳神寂照于泥丸，相与下二田化一虚空境界，为存养之全体，乳哺之首务。存养功纯，出神景始至矣！何为出神景？即六花纷飞也。见斯景而后即可以灵识。出神泥丸以外，随出随收，不可不慎也。或出一步而收，或数日而一出，总须少出而多存养，方合玄妙天机。惟当以太虚为超脱之境，以上田为存养之所，愈出愈熟，愈出愈远，久而阳神充足，自通变化。初出神时，但知随出随收，慎勿贪景贪玩。出时宜暂而不宜久，宜近而不宜远。由暂而久，不致迷而忘返。由近而远，不致迷而不归。或见美色，或见宝贵，或识神来魔，慎勿认作真有，以败我道也。无论见诸何等稀奇之物、可欲之事，均应一笔扫尽，稍有希冀，便着魔障，可不慎欤！

When the spirit is complete and the embryo full, it is just the time to leave the embryo; but one must examine clearly whether the Yang Spirit is truly pure and complete, and only then decide whether or not to move it up. There must be not a speck of drowsiness and not a thread of scatteredness; only then is the Yang Spirit pure, and only then is it fitting to move it up. But the Yang Spirit moving up to the Muddy Pellet does not mean lingering in the Muddy Pellet; one simply lets the Yang Spirit still-illuminate in the Muddy Pellet, transforming together with the two lower fields into one realm of empty space — this is the whole of preserving and nourishing, and the first task of suckling. When the work of preserving and nourishing is pure, the sign of the spirit going out arrives! What is the sign of the spirit going out? It is six-petaled flowers flying in profusion. After seeing this sign, one can let the spirit go out by numinous awareness. When the spirit goes out beyond the Muddy Pellet, take it back as soon as it goes out; one cannot but be careful. Either go out one step and take it back, or go out once every few days; always go out little and preserve and nourish much — only then does it accord with the mysterious, wondrous mechanism of Heaven. One should take the Great Void as the realm of transcendence and the upper field as the place of preserving and nourishing; the more it goes out, the more practiced it becomes; the more it goes out, the farther it goes; and in time, when the Yang Spirit is full, it naturally masters transformation. When the spirit first goes out, know only to take it back as soon as it goes out; be careful never to crave the scenes or crave play. In going out, it is fitting to be brief and not long, near and not far. From brief to long, one will not become lost and forget to return; from near to far, one will not become lost and fail to come back. Whether one sees beautiful women or treasures, or the cognitive spirit conjures up demons, be careful never to take them as real and so ruin my Dao. Whatever rare things or desirable matters one sees, all should be swept away at a stroke; with the slightest hope, one falls into demonic obstruction — how could one not be careful!

§2
Original (Chinese)

十月胎功，炼气化神，神足迁之上田，以尽乳养之道。夫乳养者，非真乳养，乃藉名乳养，以使神全还虚。亦如小儿乍离母胎，非善护持，不足养成强健身躯也。但神未完全，必须时时照顾，念念护持。但不可著意于念，一著于念则不能还虚矣！亦不失意于念，一经失念，神即驰散。是以善护持者，必将此一点真神寂定于泥丸，存之似有，守之似无，虚而又虚，空而又空，始臻妙道。但不知出神法，亦不能造成此道。出神收神之法，非指破玄微，不能证于化境。若不知出神有时，收神有法，亦难顿超上乘。然出神在何时？难言矣！神完自有光现，或金光，或白光。此光一现，便知神全。当此时莫教光散，须用收念之法，凝神光中，引入性中，仍归定静。阳神初出，三两步即可收回，纯养一七再出，二七再出，三七、四七亦然，必至七七四十九日，阳神始老练矣。四十九日之后，神通广大。虽然，此时宜慧而不用，始入妙化。神常宿于泥丸，寂照而双忘，定静以寂灭，如是者三年。至九年还虚，自是与太虚合体，虚空即我，我亦虚空。若著意于虚空，则是又有一虚空矣。又有一虚空，则是虚空仍为虚空，而我仍是我也，不能合成一体，将何以与虚空打成一片？

The ten months’ work of the embryo refines breath and transforms it into spirit; when the spirit is full, it is moved up to the upper field, to complete the way of suckling. Suckling is not real suckling; the name “suckling” is borrowed so as to make the spirit complete and return to emptiness. It is like a small child just separated from its mother’s womb: without careful protection, it cannot grow into a strong, healthy body. But since the spirit is not yet complete, one must attend to it at every moment and protect it with every thought. Yet one must not fix the intention on thought; once fixed on thought, one cannot return to emptiness! Nor must one lose the intention from thought; once thought is lost, the spirit races off and scatters. So those who protect well always settle this one point of true spirit silently in the Muddy Pellet, holding it as if it were there, guarding it as if it were not, empty and again empty, void and again void — only then do they reach the wondrous Way. But without knowing the method of the spirit going out, one cannot accomplish this Way either. As for the method of letting the spirit go out and taking it back, without having its mysterious subtleties pointed out, one cannot attain the realm of transformation. If one does not know that the spirit goes out at its time and is taken back by a method, it is hard to rise at once to the highest vehicle. Yet when does the spirit go out? That is hard to say! When the spirit is complete, light naturally appears, either golden or white. As soon as this light appears, one knows the spirit is complete. At this time, do not let the light scatter; one must use the method of gathering in thought, concentrate the spirit in the light, draw it into the nature, and return again to settled stillness. When the Yang Spirit first goes out, it can be taken back after two or three steps; nourish it purely for seven days and let it out again, again after fourteen days, and likewise after twenty-one and twenty-eight days; only after seven times seven, forty-nine days, is the Yang Spirit seasoned. After forty-nine days, its spiritual powers are vast. Even so, at this time it is fitting to have wisdom and not use it; only then does one enter wondrous transformation. The spirit constantly lodges in the Muddy Pellet, stillness and illumination both forgotten, settled and still to the point of silent extinction, and so for three years. At nine years one returns to emptiness and naturally merges into one body with the Great Void: empty space is me, and I too am empty space. If one fixes the intention on empty space, then there is yet another empty space. And if there is yet another empty space, then empty space is still empty space and I am still I; they cannot merge into one body — so how could one be fused into one piece with empty space?

§3
Original (Chinese)

出胎之时，有出胎之景现，白雪缤纷，此出胎期也。然出非自能出，以意引而后出也。若谓自能出胎，篮养素拘滞不得出者数月，必待钟、吕二祖点破玄微，而后得出。如人间受生必用受生婆，盖以产妇不能自剪脐带也。是以必用稳婆。吾辈道成出胎，虽无脐带之可剪，然出胎之时，亦不能离于母也。母在何处？心中之性灵也。当出神时，非本性尽灭也。不然一出一收者谁也？吾之化身，岂但化一身，千百亿化，总在一意之作用耳。要知道胎圆满，出胎之后必须由近而远，由暂化久。初出神，仅二、三步即可收回。但收神之法乃万古不传之秘，稍纵即逝，最宜留心者此也。设见顶际现出白光如轮，吾即用意稍稍引之，神即随意涌出，凝聚轮中，即应用意缓缓收之，使目观心，神亦缓缓归缩于内，再用入定之法，一定七日而后再出，由近而远，缓缓熟习；然总不可离我五尺以外。稍为迟留，仍即收回。再定七日再出，然而不能过七尺之外。稍为迟留，仍即收回。以后一定十四日一出，或一定二十一日一出，或一定四十九日一出，或一定九十八日一出。定日愈久愈妙，慎勿急急，是所切要。三年以后，慧力乃定，神亦坚固，飞腾变化，任意所为。若于三年以内矜奇好怪，任意化身，反减慧力。故慧而不用，方成大慧。

At the time of leaving the embryo, a sign of leaving the embryo appears — white snow falling in profusion; this is the period of leaving the embryo. Yet leaving is not something it can do by itself; it leaves only when guided by the intention. If one claims it can leave the embryo by itself, (how then to explain that) Lan Yangsu was held back for several months unable to leave, and had to wait for the two patriarchs Zhong and Lü to point out the mysterious subtleties before he could leave? Just as birth in the human world requires a midwife, because the mother cannot cut the umbilical cord herself — that is why a midwife must be used. When those of us whose Dao is accomplished leave the embryo, though there is no umbilical cord to cut, yet at the time of leaving the embryo one cannot be separated from the mother either. Where is the mother? It is the numinous nature in the heart. When the spirit goes out, it is not that the original nature is entirely extinguished. Otherwise, who is it that goes out and is taken back? As for my transformation bodies, not just one body but hundreds of thousands of millions of transformations — all lie in the working of a single intention. Know that once the Dao embryo is complete and has left the embryo, one must proceed from near to far, from brief to long. When the spirit first goes out, it can be taken back after only two or three steps. But the method of taking the spirit back is a secret not transmitted through the ages; the slightest slackness and it is gone — this is what most requires care. If one sees a white light like a wheel appear at the edge of the crown, I use the intention to draw it slightly, and the spirit wells out following the intention and gathers in the wheel; then one should at once use the intention to take it back slowly, letting the eyes contemplate the heart, and the spirit too slowly withdraws back inside; then use the method of entering absorption, remaining in absorption for seven days before letting it out again, from near to far, slowly becoming practiced; but never let it go beyond five feet from me. If it lingers even a little, take it back at once. Remain in absorption another seven days and let it out again, but it may not go beyond seven feet. If it lingers even a little, take it back at once. After that, let it out once after fourteen days of absorption, or once after twenty-one days, or once after forty-nine days, or once after ninety-eight days. The longer the days of absorption, the more wondrous; be careful never to hurry — this is what is most essential. After three years, the power of wisdom is settled and the spirit is firm; soaring and transforming, it does whatever it will. If within three years one shows off marvels, delights in the strange, and makes transformation bodies at will, one diminishes the power of wisdom instead. So only by having wisdom and not using it does one achieve great wisdom.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

阳神乃先天一炁。张紫阳《悟真篇》云：“三家相见结婴儿，婴儿是一含真气，十月胎圆入圣基”。婴儿、圣胎、阳神一物也。清鹤臞子《唱道真言》、刘一明《修真辩难》亦均认为阳神是一团真气结成。

请参阅《道门阖辟仙功》第八、第九章和《传统龙门养生功》第六章及其注释。

出神为道门养生气功的最高理想，录此使读者得窥功法之全程，并作为参考。

The Yang Spirit is the One Breath of Prior Heaven. Zhang Ziyang’s Awakening to Reality says: “When the three families meet, the infant is formed; the infant is the One, containing the true breath; in ten months the embryo is complete, entering the foundation of the sage.” The infant, the Holy Embryo, and the Yang Spirit are one and the same thing. Hequzi’s True Words for Chanting the Dao and Liu Yiming’s Discriminations of Difficult Points in Cultivating Reality, both of the Qing, likewise hold that the Yang Spirit is formed from a mass of true breath.

Please refer to Chapters 8 and 9 of this book’s Daoist Immortal Practice of Opening and Closing and Section 6 of the Traditional Longmen Practice for Nourishing Life, together with their notes.

The going out of the spirit is the highest ideal of Daoist qigong for nourishing life; it is recorded here so that readers can see the whole course of the practice, and as a reference.

Key Terms

  • Yang Spirit / Holy Embryo / infant: Qiu Ling’s note: one and the same thing, that is, the One Breath of Prior Heaven.
  • Moving up: the Yang Spirit moving from the central palace to the upper Cinnabar Field (the Muddy Pellet).
  • Six-petaled flowers flying in profusion / white snow falling in profusion: the sign of the spirit going out (snowflakes have six points).
  • Numinous awareness: bright, numinous awareness.
  • Suckling / nursing: a borrowed name, a metaphor for the protection the newly formed Yang Spirit needs.
  • Fixing the intention on thought, losing the intention from thought: the two deviations.
  • Holding it as if it were there, guarding it as if it were not: the right measure of protection.
  • One seven, seven sevens: seven days, forty-nine days.
  • Wisdom not used: having wisdom without using it, “only then does one achieve great wisdom.”
  • Nine years of returning to emptiness: see the three transformations.
  • Lan Yangsu: a legendary Daoist adept who was helped to leave the embryo by Zhongli Quan and Lü Dongbin.
  • The two patriarchs Zhong and Lü: Zhongli Quan and Lü Dongbin.
  • Midwife (wenpo 稳婆 / shoushengpo 受生婆): the woman who delivers babies.
  • Mother: in this section, “the numinous nature in the heart.”
  • Transformation bodies: hundreds of thousands of millions of transformations, “all lie in the working of a single intention.”
  • The cognitive spirit conjuring up demons: demonic realms produced by the discriminating mind; see cognitive spirit.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Read this section as a document for understanding the whole picture. Qiu Ling says plainly that it is “the highest ideal,” recorded for reference.
  2. The precepts that run throughout, applicable at any stage:
    • Whatever rare things or desirable matters you see, “all should be swept away at a stroke; with the slightest hope, one falls into demonic obstruction.”
    • Do not fix the intention on thought, nor lose the intention from thought — hold it as if it were there, guard it as if it were not.
    • Brief, not long; near, not far; “be careful never to hurry.”
    • Only by having wisdom and not using it does one achieve great wisdom.
  3. The reminder of the last layer: “if one fixes the intention on empty space, then there is yet another empty space” — not even “emptiness” may be clung to.
  4. Looking back over the whole text: from Section 1’s “the Dao is only the one word ‘stillness’” to “fused into one piece with empty space” here, the author’s standard is always naturalness, no forcing, no craving.

Translator's Notes

  1. “After seeing this sign, one can let the spirit go out by numinous awareness”: the sentence is terse; following the context, it is translated as letting the spirit go out by numinous awareness after seeing this sign.
  2. “If one claims it can leave the embryo by itself, Lan Yangsu was held back for several months…”: this is an argument by rhetorical question; the original omits words like “how then to explain,” which the translation supplies in parentheses. 篮养素 is usually written 蓝养素 (Lan Yangsu), said to have been a Daoist of the Song dynasty.
  3. “Hundreds of thousands of millions of transformations”: borrowing the Buddhist expression “hundreds of thousands of millions of transformation bodies.”
  4. “Section 6 of the Traditional Longmen Practice for Nourishing Life” in Qiu Ling’s note (the note says “Chapter 6”) is Part 3, Section 6 of this book, “Nurturing the Holy Embryo.”
  5. Qiu Ling gives the original text of this section as a single passage; for ease of comparison and reading aloud, it has been subdivided by content into §1–§3.

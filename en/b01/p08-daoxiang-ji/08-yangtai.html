8 · The Method of Nurturing the Embryo

8 · The Method of Nurturing the Embryo — ten months of nurturing the embryo rest entirely on still illumination

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

Safety note (translator): This section says that during the nurturing of the embryo “in the third or fourth month one can stop eating,” “in the fifth or sixth month one can stop breathing,” and “the breath ceases and the pulse stops.” These are ancient alchemists’ descriptions of an extremely advanced stage and belong to the ideals of cultivation; they must never be taken as practice instructions to attempt fasting or holding the breath. Any practice of going without food for long periods or deliberately holding the breath can seriously endanger health and even life.

About This Chapter

Qiu Ling says: the method of nurturing the embryo is the stage of refining breath to transform it into spirit. The author’s main point is very focused — ten months of nurturing the embryo rest entirely on still illumination.

  • After the Great Medicine has entered the central palace, still illumination remains the main thing: still and illuminating, illuminating and still; without stillness there is no illumination, without illumination there is no stillness. When still illumination reaches its utmost, there is naturally a heavenly fire phasing, a natural movement, needing no spirit to drive it and no breath to blow it, just as the breath of Heaven and Earth circulates of itself without ceasing.
  • In still illumination, though the spirit dwells in the middle field, it must unite with the lower field, transforming into one realm of empty space; only this is the “great round mirror.” If it lingers only in the middle field and cannot join the lower field, the two breaths cannot arise at all times to nourish the Original Spirit.
  • The progress of still illumination is described month by month: in the first three months of entering absorption, the two breaths move only in the empty realm of the navel wheel; in the fourth and fifth months, the ingesting is complete and they return to extinguished absorption; in the sixth and seventh months, the Original Spirit alone is bright and drowsiness is entirely gone; in the eighth and ninth months, the ten thousand vessels return to their source in silent great absorption; in the tenth month, the Yang Spirit is complete and wisdom springs up in abundance — but “there is wisdom and it is not used,” for using the wisdom would harm its round brightness.
  • “Ablution” is warm nourishing, and warm nourishing is still illumination rightly done. The work of the ten months is purely the fire phasing of ablution and warmly nourishing the Greater Celestial Circuit, a natural movement, unlike the Lesser Celestial Circuit’s use of respiration.
  • After the Great Medicine has been ingested, movement suddenly arises in stillness; following its mechanism of movement, guide it upward with the intention, then down from the Bright Hall to the central palace, uniting it with the Dao embryo; at the extreme of stillness two moving breaths also rise together from the Bubbling Spring… whatever is gathered in is gathered into the central palace; the golden elixir never leaves the intention from beginning to end, until the work of returning to emptiness is complete and one is fused into one piece with empty space.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

大药入中宫后，仍以寂照为主。但此时之寂照，必寂而照，照而寂，不寂不照，不照不寂。寂照至极处，自有天然之火候，自然之运动，不须神驭，不须息吹，如天地之气自行运用而不息也。惟寂照时，必神虽居中田而必合下田，化一虚空境界，始成一大圆镜矣。若仅滞于中田，不能连合下田，则二气不能随时生发，涵养我之元神，亦不合玄妙天机。故服食后胎神大定，失于寂照，则二气不畅旺。二气不畅旺则不能资养元神，是以寂照为养胎之本。

After the Great Medicine has entered the central palace, still illumination remains the main thing. But still illumination at this time must be still and illuminating, illuminating and still; without stillness there is no illumination, without illumination there is no stillness. When still illumination reaches its utmost, there is naturally a heavenly fire phasing, a natural movement, needing no spirit to drive it and no breath to blow it, just as the breath of Heaven and Earth circulates of itself without ceasing. Only, in still illumination, though the spirit dwells in the middle field it must unite with the lower field, transforming into one realm of empty space; only then does it become a great round mirror. If it lingers only in the middle field and cannot join the lower field, the two breaths cannot arise at all times to nourish my Original Spirit, and it does not accord with the mysterious, wondrous mechanism of Heaven. So after ingesting, when the spirit of the embryo is in great absorption, if still illumination is lost, the two breaths are not flowing and vigorous; if the two breaths are not flowing and vigorous, they cannot nourish the Original Spirit. Therefore still illumination is the root of nurturing the embryo.

§2
Original (Chinese)

初入定三月，二气仅动于脐轮之虚境，迨定照到四、五月，二气受元神之寂照，服食已尽，而归于灭定。元神得二气之资养，圆明不昧，而得证真空。气息既定，食性已绝，光灼灼、圆陀陀，只存一寂照之元神而已。寂照至六、七月，而元神独明，昏睡全无。寂照八、九月，万脉归源，寂然大定。寂照至十月，阳神已足，智慧丛生，六通俱见，一镜独明，惟慧而不用，始完成胎圆之果，否则，用其慧则累其圆明，非所宜也，古曰：一年沐浴防危险。

In the first three months of entering absorption, the two breaths move only in the empty realm of the navel wheel. When absorption and illumination reach the fourth and fifth months, the two breaths, receiving the still illumination of the Original Spirit, have completed the ingesting and return to extinguished absorption. The Original Spirit, nourished by the two breaths, is round and bright and not obscured, and attains true emptiness. The breath is now settled, the desire for food is already cut off; blazing with light, perfectly round, there remains only an Original Spirit of still illumination. When still illumination reaches the sixth and seventh months, the Original Spirit alone is bright, and drowsiness is entirely gone. With still illumination in the eighth and ninth months, the ten thousand vessels return to their source, in silent great absorption. When still illumination reaches the tenth month, the Yang Spirit is complete, wisdom springs up in abundance, the six penetrations all appear, a single mirror alone is bright; only if there is wisdom and it is not used is the fruit of the embryo’s completion accomplished. Otherwise, to use its wisdom would burden its round brightness, which is not fitting. The ancients said: “A year of ablution, guarding against danger.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

盖防危险者，防其十月之中沐浴有所失宜，寂照有所疏漏否。沐浴失宜，则气息难以尽灭。寂照疏漏，则昏沉不能尽绝。是以气息有丝毫未灭，不能纯其阳明之神。昏沉有丝毫未绝，不能证其胎圆之果。必须寂而又寂，照而复照，必待食脉两绝，昏散全无，而后可以脱其胞胎矣。惟沐浴二字，学者多起疑义，不知沐浴即温养之意，温养即寂照合宜也。十月养胎全凭寂照，但将昭昭之神与中下二田化一虚空境界，浑浑融融，无人无我之相，熙熙皞皞，无散乱之机，愈寂而愈妙，益定而益微，此乃十月沐浴之真功，防危虑险之妙道也。

Guarding against danger means guarding against whether, during the ten months, the ablution has been improperly done or the still illumination has had lapses. If the ablution is improper, the breath can hardly be entirely extinguished. If the still illumination has lapses, drowsiness cannot be entirely cut off. So if the breath is not extinguished by even a hair’s breadth, the spirit of yang brightness cannot be made pure. If drowsiness is not cut off by even a hair’s breadth, the fruit of the embryo’s completion cannot be attained. One must be still and again still, illuminate and again illuminate, and wait until food and pulse are both cut off and drowsiness and scatteredness are entirely gone; only then can one leave the womb. Only, many students have doubts about the word “ablution,” not knowing that ablution means warm nourishing, and warm nourishing means still illumination rightly done. Ten months of nurturing the embryo rest entirely on still illumination: simply transform the bright, clear spirit together with the middle and lower fields into one realm of empty space, blended and fused, without the distinction of self and others; serene and content, with no hint of scatteredness; the stiller, the more wondrous; the more settled, the more subtle — this is the true work of the ten months’ ablution, the wondrous way of guarding against danger and anticipating peril.

§4
Original (Chinese)

足见十月功夫，纯是沐浴温养大周天之火候，亦是自然运动，非同小周天之用呼吸也。必须文火温煦，时时刻刻不可意乱。意乱则火散，火散则丹驰，是以无为而守，似有似无，既不可着相，亦不可落空，总要活活泼泼，潇潇洒洒，始为得法。待昏沉全无，自成纯阳之体。阴气尽化，自无食魔之扰矣，但看功夫如何，如能如法行去，三、四月即可绝食。至胎息之诀，到时自明，果能功夫无错，五、六月即可停息。至十月满足，六通已证，满天飞雪，即是出神景矣！

This shows that the work of the ten months is purely the fire phasing of ablution and warmly nourishing the Greater Celestial Circuit; it too is a natural movement, unlike the Lesser Celestial Circuit’s use of respiration. One must warm it with a civil fire, never letting the intention become confused at any moment. If the intention is confused, the fire scatters; if the fire scatters, the elixir runs off. So one guards through non-action, seeming to be and seeming not to be, neither attached to forms nor falling into emptiness; one must always be lively and free and easy — only then is it done rightly. When drowsiness is entirely gone, one naturally becomes a body of pure yang. When the yin breath is entirely transformed, there is naturally no disturbance from the demon of food. It all depends on the work: if one can proceed according to the method, in the third or fourth month one can stop eating. As for the formula of embryonic breathing, it will become clear of itself when the time comes; if the work is truly without error, in the fifth or sixth month one can stop breathing. When the ten months are complete and the six penetrations have been attained, snow flying all over the sky — that is the sign of the spirit going out!

§5
Original (Chinese)

大药服食之后，静中忽而生动，只有顺其动机，用意引之上升，如大药服食状，由明堂下降中宫，与我道胎合而为一，使不外驰，此即收而藏之之法。不但此也，静到极处，尚有两道动气，从涌泉穴同时上升，至尾闾合而为一，上升至绝顶，复从明堂降下，入于中宫，亦与道胎合而为一。但收服食后之动气，须稍用呼吸顺其阖辟之机，鼓荡浑身之阴气，不可过于用意，以免伤胎之虞也。即如初脱胎时，所现之金光，本我原有之灵光，亦必须用法收回。若不收回此光，何以做到形神俱妙之境？但收回此光时，必须将我法象注于光中。我之法身定于光中，由法身将此光收入法身之内，再由本性收回法身，定于泥丸。以上所言诸条，于结胎后初次之动机，乃是炼形之本。静之又静，又有二气由丹田先后而上升，静之日久，又有二气从涌泉穴而上升，此皆万脉归源之时。见此诸境之后，必然息无脉绝，永归大定。此皆自然而然之动机，当此机之来，不过顺其机自然引归中宫，凡所谓收者皆收之中宫。又必用意而收，是以金丹始终不能离意，直到还虚功成，我与虚空打成一片，而后再由泥丸宫入于性海，养至金光万道，直冲斗牛。

After the Great Medicine has been ingested, movement suddenly arises in stillness; one can only follow its mechanism of movement and guide it upward with the intention, as when ingesting the Great Medicine, then down from the Bright Hall to the central palace, uniting it as one with my Dao embryo, so that it does not race outward — this is the method of gathering in and storing away. Not only this: at the extreme of stillness there are also two moving breaths, rising together from the Bubbling Spring point, uniting as one at the Caudal Funnel, rising to the summit, and descending again from the Bright Hall into the central palace, where they too unite as one with the Dao embryo. But in gathering in the moving breath after ingesting, one must use the respiration slightly, following its mechanism of closing and opening, to stir up the yin breath of the whole body; one must not use too much intention, lest there be the danger of harming the embryo. Just as the golden light that appears at the first leaving of the embryo is my own original numinous light, it too must be taken back by a method. If this light is not taken back, how could one reach the state where body and spirit are both wondrous? But in taking back this light, one must pour my Dharma-image into the light: my Dharma-body is settled in the light, the Dharma-body takes the light into itself, and then the original nature takes back the Dharma-body and settles it in the Muddy Pellet. All the items spoken of above, in the first stirrings after the embryo has formed, are the root of refining the body. Being still and again still, there are again two breaths rising one after the other from the Cinnabar Field; after being still for many days, there are again two breaths rising from the Bubbling Spring point — these are all times when the ten thousand vessels return to their source. After seeing these states, the breath will surely cease and the pulse stop, returning forever to great absorption. These are all mechanisms of movement that happen of themselves; when such a mechanism comes, one simply follows it and naturally guides it back to the central palace; whatever is spoken of as gathering in is gathering into the central palace. And one must gather with the intention, so the golden elixir never leaves the intention from beginning to end, until the work of returning to emptiness is complete and I am fused into one piece with empty space; then, from the Muddy Pellet Palace, one enters the sea of nature, and nourishes until ten thousand rays of golden light shoot straight to the Dipper and the Ox.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

伍冲虚于《仙佛合宗》中解释为何此段功法守中下田合而为一时说：“初行大周天之火，元神虽居中田，却连合下田二炁以为妙用。必元神寂照于中下二田，相与混融化为一虚空之大境，使二炁助神结胎，故二田皆是落处。若拘守于一田，则神有滞碍，而失大圆镜之智用矣。”

又伍冲虚在同书解释六通道：“六通者，漏尽通、天眼通、天耳通、宿命通、他心通、神境通是也。”

养胎功法即炼炁化神阶段。

Wu Chongxu, in the Common Origin of Immortals and Buddhas (Xianfo hezong 仙佛合宗), explaining why in this stage one guards the middle and lower fields united as one, says: “When one first carries out the fire of the Greater Celestial Circuit, though the Original Spirit dwells in the middle field, it joins with the two breaths of the lower field for its wondrous use. The Original Spirit must still-illuminate both the middle and lower fields, blending them together into one great realm of empty space, so that the two breaths help the spirit form the embryo; thus both fields are places where it settles. If one clings to guarding a single field, the spirit is obstructed and loses the wise function of the great round mirror.”

Wu Chongxu, in the same book, also explains the six penetrations: “The six penetrations are the penetration of exhausting the outflows, the divine eye, the divine ear, knowledge of past lives, knowledge of others’ minds, and the realm of spiritual powers.”

The method of nurturing the embryo is the stage of refining breath to transform it into spirit.

Key Terms

  • Holy Embryo / Dao embryo / spirit of the embryo: see the general glossary.
  • Still illumination: still and illuminating, illuminating and still; “still illumination is the root of nurturing the embryo.”
  • Great round mirror: the Buddhist “great round mirror wisdom,” a metaphor for the realm of empty space formed when the Original Spirit unites the middle and lower fields.
  • Middle field, lower field: the spirit dwells in the middle field but must unite with the lower field.
  • Ingesting: the Great Medicine entering the central palace, likened to eating it.
  • Extinguished absorption: the great absorption in which the breath returns to stillness and extinction.
  • The desire for food already cut off: no longer having any desire for food (an ancient description).
  • Wisdom not used: having wisdom without using it, so as not to harm its round brightness.
  • “A year of ablution, guarding against danger”: during the nurturing of the embryo one must guard against improper ablution and lapses in still illumination.
  • Ablution: as defined in this part — “ablution means warm nourishing, and warm nourishing means still illumination rightly done.”
  • Serene and content (xixi haohao 熙熙皞皞): harmonious, happy, and at ease.
  • Demon of food: the desire for food.
  • Stopping eating, stopping breathing: ancient descriptions of an extremely advanced stage; see the safety note.
  • Snow flying all over the sky: the sign of the spirit going out; see Section 9.
  • Bubbling Spring point: an acupoint on the sole of the foot.
  • Closing and opening (hepi 阖辟): closing and opening.
  • Dharma-image, Dharma-body: see natural Dharma-body.
  • Sea of nature: the original nature like a sea.
  • Six penetrations: exhausting the outflows, the divine eye, the divine ear, knowledge of past lives, knowledge of others’ minds, the realm of spiritual powers.
  • Refining breath to transform it into spirit: see the three transformations.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The most useful line: still and illuminating, illuminating and still — the mind is quiet (still) and at the same time clear (illuminating). This is the common standard for quiet sitting at every stage.
  2. Do not cling to one spot: “if one clings to guarding a single field, the spirit is obstructed” — attention need not stare fixedly at one point; it can be a warm, empty feeling over the whole middle and lower abdomen.
  3. Wisdom, not used: if inspirations, premonitions, or the like appear in stillness, do not chase them or use them.
  4. “If the intention is confused, the fire scatters; if the fire scatters, the elixir runs off”: at any stage, once the mind becomes confused, what has been accumulated scatters.
  5. A further reminder: stopping eating, stopping breathing, the breath ceasing and the pulse stopping are all descriptions of the ideals of ancient cultivation, not methods of practice; never attempt them.

Translator's Notes

  1. “When absorption and illumination reach the fourth and fifth months” (dai ding zhao dao si wu yue 迨定照到四、五月): dai means “when, by the time that.”
  2. “Serene and content” (xixi haohao 熙熙皞皞): describing a state of harmonious contentment, echoing the Mencius, “the people of a true king are content and at ease.”
  3. “Shoot straight to the Dipper and the Ox”: the Dipper and the Ox are two of the twenty-eight lunar mansions; the phrase describes radiance at its most intense.
  4. Qiu Ling gives the original text of this section as a single passage; for ease of comparison and reading aloud, it has been subdivided by content into §1–§5.

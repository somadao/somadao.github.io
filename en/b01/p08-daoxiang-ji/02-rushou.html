2 · The Work of Beginning

2 · The Work of Beginning — “this mechanism moves, yet I remain the master of stillness”; what true embryonic breathing is

About This Chapter

This is the longest and most practical section of the part; Qiu Ling calls it “the beginning work of refining essence into primordial breath in Daoist qigong, concise yet full of meaning.” Its content falls into four layers:

  1. How to begin: first let go of body and mind and become thoroughly still; the two eyes first look at the tip of the nose to take aim, then turn back to illuminate the space between the eyebrows (the Bright Hall); when the space between the eyebrows becomes slightly warm, use the intention to draw two rays of golden light from there, up to the crown, dividing left and right, around behind the ears, to the throat, down the Twelve-Storied Tower and into the Cavity of Breath (the lower Cinnabar Field). Then not a single thought arises, mind and eyes do not leave this place, neither sticking nor letting go, neither near nor apart. When stillness reaches its extreme, there is naturally a stirring — “this mechanism moves, yet I remain the master of stillness; when I am the master of stillness, it will surely move all the more.”
  2. Civil and martial fire: the martial fire is continuous and unbroken, every breath returning to the root; the civil fire is as if guarding, as if keeping, neither forgetting nor forcing. When the Medicine is produced (the outer kidney becoming aroused), “govern” it with the spirit and “draw it in” with the breath: drawing in means applying the intention slightly to the out-breath, the in-breath merely following. Once the Medicine has returned to the furnace, stop the martial fire at once (stop applying the intention to the out-breath) and switch to the civil fire to bathe it and seal the furnace.
  3. Awareness and movement: after sitting quietly for no more than half an hour, warm breath arises in the Cinnabar Field by itself — this is the “message of spring”; soon there is a sudden movement and an awareness in the mind — that is exactly the opening of the Mysterious Pass. If, when the true breath moves, you do not meet it with awareness, you miss it right in front of you; uniting awareness with movement, movement and awareness melting into one place, is water and fire in balance.
  4. What true embryonic breathing is: it is not counting breaths, nor regulating the breath at the mouth and nose, still less holding the breath. When the spirit concentrates at the root of breath, the original breath cannot go out and unites with the spirit; the respiration too is absorbed by spirit and breath, as if there were no breathing at all — the breath lies down by itself, the respiration settles by itself. “But this lying down of the breath is not the same as forcibly holding the breath. The Great Way accords with nature; as soon as there is any forcing, it is not the correct way.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

初坐时放下身心，大静一场，即以二目守两眉之间。未视眉间时，先须两目视鼻端，如木匠之标线，总要不偏不倚，而后返照两眉中间。俟眉间稍热，即用意从眉间运两道金光，上至绝顶，由绝顶双分左右，从耳后绕至喉间，下重楼，入气穴。此穴在脐下一寸三分。目照于斯，神凝于斯，古所谓心目所在者是也。当此时须一念不起，万虑俱寂，心目不离这个，行住莫忘此间，不沾不脱，始能入妙，不即不离，方克合中。静至于极，自有动机。此机虽动，我仍主静。我主于静，彼必愈动。彼愈动而我愈静。此即初步功夫，积累动气之要诀。

When you first sit, let go of body and mind and become thoroughly still; then guard the space between the eyebrows with the two eyes. Before looking at the space between the eyebrows, the two eyes must first look at the tip of the nose, like a carpenter’s chalk line, always neither leaning nor tilting; then turn back to illuminate the middle between the eyebrows. When the space between the eyebrows becomes slightly warm, use the intention to move two rays of golden light from there up to the very top of the head; from the top they divide left and right, go around behind the ears to the throat, down the Twelve-Storied Tower and into the Cavity of Breath. This cavity is one cun three fen below the navel. The eyes shine here, the spirit concentrates here — this is what the ancients called “where the mind and eyes are.” At this time not a single thought must arise, all worries must fall silent; mind and eyes do not leave this place, and walking or standing, do not forget it. Neither sticking nor letting go, only then can one enter the wonder; neither near nor apart, only then can one accord with the center. When stillness reaches its extreme, there is naturally a stirring of the mechanism. This mechanism moves, yet I remain the master of stillness. When I am the master of stillness, it will surely move all the more; the more it moves, the stiller I become. This is the first step of the work, the essential formula for accumulating moving breath.

§2
Original (Chinese)

壮者不过三、四日，弱者不过八、九日，老者不过二、三十日，自有真种发现、药产妙景。当此时，耳后风生，光透帘帏，皆药产景也。一见药产景至，即当以神运息，返此气于气穴，而后起火炼之。火起于何时？药入炉之后。火起于何处？虚危穴之间，由此缓缓上行。

For the strong it takes no more than three or four days, for the weak no more than eight or nine days, for the old no more than twenty or thirty days, and naturally the true seed appears, with the wonderful scenes of the production of the Medicine. At this time a wind arises behind the ears and light comes through the curtains — these are all scenes of the production of the Medicine. As soon as you see the scenes of the production of the Medicine arrive, move the breath with the spirit and return this breath to the Cavity of Breath, and then raise the fire to refine it. When is the fire raised? After the Medicine enters the furnace. Where is the fire raised? Between the Xuwei point (the perineum), from where it slowly travels upward.

§3
Original (Chinese)

明堂者，即两眉之间。乃下手时收念之处。此窍乃后三关之门户。坐时二目先守此窍，少时念止，即可由门户而入，轻轻下沉海底。二目既至，神已随之，片时不起妄念，自然心定息调。由此时时刻刻不离，用文火以守，用武火以炼，一到药产，自能神知。经云“气满任督自开”，良不诬也。

The Bright Hall is the space between the two eyebrows. It is where thoughts are gathered in at the beginning of practice. This opening is the gateway to the three rear passes. When sitting, the two eyes first guard this opening; soon, when thoughts stop, one can go in through this gateway and sink gently down to the Sea Bottom. Where the two eyes arrive, the spirit has already followed; if for a moment no deluded thought arises, naturally the mind settles and the breath is regulated. From then on, never leaving it at any moment, guard it with the civil fire and refine it with the martial fire; once the Medicine is produced, the spirit will naturally know. The scripture says, “when the breath is full, the Conception and Governing vessels open by themselves” — truly it does not deceive.

Original note (Chinese)

气穴即指下丹田。虚危穴、海底均指会阴穴。

The Cavity of Breath means the lower Cinnabar Field. The Xuwei point and the Sea Bottom both mean the Perineum point.

§4
Original (Chinese)

何为武火？绵绵不断，息息归根。何为文火？若守若存，勿忘勿助。非文火薰煦则药不生。非武火锻炼则精不化。文武应时而用，真造化自见。当文，武不得。当武，文不得。毫厘差错不成丹，信不诬也。

总而言之，此中只有二候：气动一候；累气产真种一候。

What is the martial fire? Continuous and unbroken, every breath returning to the root. What is the civil fire? As if guarding, as if keeping, neither forgetting nor forcing. Without the warming of the civil fire, the Medicine is not produced; without the smelting of the martial fire, essence is not transformed. When civil and martial are used at the right times, true creation appears of itself. When it is time for the civil, the martial must not be used; when it is time for the martial, the civil must not be used. “The slightest error and no elixir is formed” — truly this is not false.

In sum, there are only two phases here: the phase when the breath moves, and the phase when the accumulated breath produces the true seed.

§5
Original (Chinese)

调药是最初一步。药不调则真种不生，是以佛经有和合凝集决定成就之句。和合者合神气二者为一。但神栖于心，气寓于肾，不能自相和合，非必神入气穴，始能神气相抱而为一，而后始有真种发生，即如夫妇交媾，男精女血，结成胎基，同此一理。自真种发生而言，其间无限的景象，恍恍惚惚，外肾勃然而举，此即采药皈炉之时也。当此时以神宰之，使不外驰，以息摄之，使皈炉内。宰是以神探气动处，坐镇丹田，不昏沉，不散乱，主宰其间，炁自转而相依。摄是用呼气之法。何谓呼气之法？稍用意于呼，吸仅随之而已。当此时呼吸绵绵，以用意于呼，元炁即随呼气转入穴内，此即摄之之法，亦曰武火。如觉炁已皈炉，即当停止武火。何谓停止武火？即停止呼气耳。此时正当沐浴，不照而照，不守而守，以文火慢慢养之，亦曰封炉。达摩谓二候采牟尼者是也。少焉，又有动机，此即子时行阳火之时，当此时真意仍坐守丹田，分意于阳火，神行则气行，气住则神住，行住相依，始得其妙。

Harmonizing the Medicine is the very first step. If the Medicine is not harmonized, the true seed is not produced; that is why the Buddhist scriptures have the phrase “harmonized, united, congealed and gathered, it is surely accomplished.” “Harmonized and united” means uniting spirit and breath as one. But the spirit dwells in the heart and the breath lodges in the kidneys; they cannot harmonize and unite by themselves. Only when the spirit enters the Cavity of Breath can spirit and breath embrace and become one, and only then does the true seed arise — just as when husband and wife unite, the man’s essence and the woman’s blood form the foundation of the embryo; it is the same principle. Speaking of the arising of the true seed, there are countless scenes in between, vague and indistinct, and the outer kidney becomes vigorously aroused — this is the time for collecting the Medicine and returning it to the furnace. At this time, “govern” it with the spirit so that it does not run outward, and “draw it in” with the breath so that it returns into the furnace. “Governing” means using the spirit to seek out the place where the breath moves, sitting firmly in the Cinnabar Field, neither torpid nor scattered, being the master within, and the primordial breath naturally turns round and clings to the spirit. “Drawing in” uses the method of the out-breath. What is the method of the out-breath? Apply the intention slightly to the out-breath, the in-breath merely following it. At this time the breathing is continuous; because the intention is applied to the out-breath, the original primordial breath follows the out-breath and turns into the cavity — this is the method of drawing in, also called the martial fire. If you feel that the primordial breath has returned to the furnace, stop the martial fire at once. What is stopping the martial fire? Simply stopping (applying the intention to) the out-breath. Now is the time of ablution: illuminating without illuminating, guarding without guarding, nourish it slowly with the civil fire — this is also called sealing the furnace. This is what Bodhidharma meant by “collecting the muni in two phases.” After a while there is another stirring — this is the time of “moving the yang fire at the zi hour”; at this time the True Intention still sits guarding the Cinnabar Field, giving a part of the intention to the yang fire: when the spirit moves the breath moves, when the breath rests the spirit rests; moving and resting depend on each other, and only then is its wonder obtained.

§6
Original (Chinese)

要知目光视下田，心亦随之到下田，能朗彻其光使不昏沉，静坐不过半点钟，丹田自生暖气。要知此暖气即是春信。少时忽然一动，心中一觉，此正玄关开辟时也。乘其时凝神于气机动处，自觉恍恍惚惚，似醉如痴。真气动时不以觉应之，即为当面错过。遂动遂以觉应，方为感而遂通。能以觉合动，动觉融化一处，即是水火既济，金木交并。动而后静，有归于无，必待再动而后凝合之。凝之既久，精神备足，不第智慧丛生，而一切旧疾恍然若失，浑身舒畅，不可以言语形容，真是三十六宫都是春也。

Know that when the light of the eyes looks at the lower field, the mind follows it there too; if one can keep this light clear and penetrating so that it does not become torpid, after sitting quietly for no more than half an hour, warm breath arises in the Cinnabar Field by itself. Know that this warm breath is the “message of spring.” Soon there is a sudden movement and an awareness in the mind — this is exactly the moment when the Mysterious Pass opens. Taking this moment, concentrate the spirit on the place where the mechanism of breath moves, and one feels vague and dim, as if drunk, as if entranced. If, when the true breath moves, you do not meet it with awareness, you miss it right in front of you; following its movement and at once meeting it with awareness is “responding and thereby penetrating.” To be able to unite awareness with movement, movement and awareness melting into one place, is water and fire in balance, metal and wood joining together. After movement comes stillness again, being returns to non-being; one must wait for it to move again and then congeal and unite it. When it has been congealed for a long time, essence and spirit are fully sufficient; not only does wisdom spring up, but all old illnesses vanish as if they had never been, the whole body is at ease beyond words — truly “all thirty-six palaces are spring.”

§7
Original (Chinese)

况气发神凝之际，神气混合一处，息亦随之不动，大似口鼻无息，非无息而息归于气根矣。元气发生于气根，呼吸之气亦根于气根。当元气动时，原拟外出，神一凝于气根，元气自不能向外，仍与神相合一处。息为神气所吸收，亦同神气相依相抱，三家相见，五行攒簇，此乃结丹之正功，亦即和合凝集之要道也。古云“胎从伏气中结”，即此义也。又曰：“服气不长生，长生须伏气”。但此处伏气，非同强闭气也。大道合乎自然，一有勉强，便非正道。此处伏气乃自然而然，何也？当元气发动，本欲拱关而出，因凝神于气根，元气亦自同归本穴，神与气既相结合，是以息息归根，正所以以风吹火也。火得风吹而愈旺，故能炼此精化为气耳，纯是自然而然。何者为火？即神是也。何者为风？即息是也。以神合气，以息合神，即是火炼也。道书所谓武火者即此也。何谓文火？即气不动时寂然朗照之谓也。

Moreover, at the moment when the breath arises and the spirit concentrates, spirit and breath blend in one place, and the respiration too follows and becomes still, very much as if there were no breathing in the mouth and nose — it is not that there is no breathing, but that the breathing has returned to the root of breath. The original breath arises at the root of breath, and the breath of respiration also has the root of breath as its root. When the original breath moves, it is about to go out; but as soon as the spirit concentrates at the root of breath, the original breath naturally cannot go outward and remains united with the spirit in one place. The respiration is absorbed by spirit and breath, and it too clings to and embraces them; the three families meet, the five phases gather — this is the correct work of forming the elixir, and the essential way of “harmonizing, uniting, congealing and gathering.” The ancients said, “the embryo is formed within the lying-down of the breath” — that is the meaning. They also said: “Swallowing breath does not give long life; long life requires the breath to lie down.” But this lying down of the breath is not the same as forcibly holding the breath. The Great Way accords with nature; as soon as there is any forcing, it is not the correct way. The lying down of the breath here is entirely natural. Why? When the original breath stirs, it would like to charge through the passes and out; because the spirit is concentrated at the root of breath, the original breath returns of itself to its own cavity together with it; since spirit and breath are united, every breath returns to the root — this is precisely blowing on the fire with wind. Fire blown by wind burns ever more strongly, so it can refine this essence and transform it into breath — all entirely natural. What is the fire? It is the spirit. What is the wind? It is the respiration. Uniting the spirit with the breath, and the respiration with the spirit, is refining with fire. This is what the Daoist books call the martial fire. What is the civil fire? It is silent, clear illumination when the breath is not moving.

§8
Original (Chinese)

最要紧者自然不自然耳。何谓自然？凝神时不沾不脱，不即不离，无过不及，方合中道。如沾则著迹，非道也。脱则落空，非道也。不即不离，亦是此意。总教合乎自然，顺其机缄，更无一点强制硬迫，方是清静无为之大道也。静时固静，即动时我自凝神气穴，以待自返，是动亦静也。学人不知天机活泼，一味枯禅，是以终落空亡。

What matters most is whether it is natural or not. What is natural? When concentrating the spirit, neither sticking nor letting go, neither near nor apart, neither too much nor too little — only then does it accord with the middle way. If one sticks, one leaves traces — that is not the Dao. If one lets go, one falls into emptiness — that is not the Dao either. “Neither near nor apart” has the same meaning. In short, it must accord with nature and follow its mechanism, without the slightest compulsion or forcing — only then is it the Great Way of purity, stillness and non-action. In stillness there is of course stillness; even in movement I simply concentrate my spirit in the Cavity of Breath and wait for it to return by itself — so movement too is stillness. Students who do not understand the liveliness of the mechanism of Heaven practice nothing but dry meditation, and so in the end fall into empty nothingness.

§9
Original (Chinese)

古人有胎息之说，世之学者竟将胎息指为数息，或在口鼻间调息，岂知皆非也。胎息一节，即看小儿在胎时，即明此理。何也？当小儿在母腹中，并非从口鼻呼吸，惟脐间一带与母相通，是以母呼亦呼，母吸亦吸。学者能凝神合气，口鼻之息亦自返回气根，不从口鼻呼吸矣。故丹经谓真人之息以踵，夫踵者深之之义也。神气相合，息亦伏于气根，若无息者然，所以谓之胎息，又谓真人呼吸。世人不明息之真机，或以数息为调息，或以闭息为伏息，并不知气乃自伏，息乃自定，勉强为之，皆失自然之妙，是以终无成就也。

The ancients spoke of embryonic breathing, and students of the world even take embryonic breathing to be counting breaths, or regulating the breath at the mouth and nose — not knowing that all these are wrong. On embryonic breathing, one need only look at a child in the womb to understand the principle. Why? When the child is in the mother’s belly, it does not breathe through mouth and nose; only the area of the navel communicates with the mother, so when the mother breathes out it breathes out, and when the mother breathes in it breathes in. If students can concentrate the spirit and unite it with the breath, the breathing of mouth and nose will naturally return to the root of breath and no longer breathe through mouth and nose. That is why the alchemical scriptures say “the Perfected breathe with their heels” — “heels” means depth. When spirit and breath unite, the respiration too lies hidden at the root of breath, as if there were no breathing at all; that is why it is called embryonic breathing, also the breathing of the Perfected. People of the world do not understand the true mechanism of breathing: some take counting breaths for regulating the breath, others take holding the breath for letting the breath lie down; they do not know that the breath lies down by itself and the respiration settles by itself. Doing it by force loses the wonder of naturalness, and so in the end there is no accomplishment.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4–§9
Original note (Chinese)

此节为道家气功炼精化炁入手功夫，言简意赅，此中详细节次，见于下文。

This section is the beginning work of refining essence into primordial breath in Daoist qigong, concise yet full of meaning; its detailed steps are found in what follows.

Key Terms

  • Looking at the tip of the nose: see looking at the tip of the nose; “like a carpenter’s chalk line,” simply to take aim.
  • Bright Hall: in this part, the space between the eyebrows, “where thoughts are gathered in at the beginning of practice,” “the gateway to the three rear passes”; see Celestial Eye.
  • Two rays of golden light: two rays drawn with the intention from between the eyebrows, through the crown, behind the ears, to the throat, down the Twelve-Storied Tower into the Cavity of Breath.
  • Cavity of Breath: this section says “one cun three fen below the navel”; Qiu Ling’s note: that is, the lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Where the mind and eyes are: both mind and eyes are here.
  • Neither sticking nor letting go, neither near nor apart: the sense of measure stressed again and again throughout this part.
  • True seed: the first production of the primordial breath of Prior Heaven; see Medicine.
  • Wind arising behind the ears, light coming through the curtains: scenes of the production of the Medicine.
  • Xuwei point / Sea Bottom: Qiu Ling’s note: both are the Perineum; see Perineum.
  • Civil fire, martial fire: the martial fire — continuous and unbroken, every breath returning to the root; the civil fire — as if guarding, as if keeping, neither forgetting nor forcing. See fire phasing.
  • Two phases: the phase when the breath moves; the phase when the accumulated breath produces the true seed.
  • “Harmonized, united, congealed and gathered, it is surely accomplished”: a Buddhist phrase, which the author explains as the union of spirit and breath.
  • Governing, drawing in: govern it with the spirit (sitting firmly in the Cinnabar Field, being master within), draw it in with the breath (applying the intention slightly to the out-breath).
  • Method of the out-breath: applying the intention slightly to the out-breath, the in-breath merely following.
  • Returning to the furnace: the Medicine returning into the furnace (the lower Cinnabar Field).
  • Ablution / sealing the furnace: after stopping the martial fire, illuminating without illuminating, guarding without guarding, nourishing it with the civil fire.
  • Collecting the muni in two phases: a saying attributed to Bodhidharma; muni is a metaphor for the elixir.
  • Moving the yang fire at the zi hour: when a stirring arises again, giving a part of the intention to the yang fire.
  • Message of spring: the warm breath in the Cinnabar Field, likened to the first news of spring.
  • Responding and thereby penetrating: from the “Appended Statements” of the Book of Changes.
  • Water and fire in balance, metal and wood joining together: different metaphors for the union of spirit and breath.
  • “All thirty-six palaces are spring”: from a poem by Shao Yong, describing ease throughout the body.
  • Root of breath: the lower Cinnabar Field.
  • The three families meeting, the five phases gathering: essence, breath and spirit (or spirit, breath and respiration) uniting as one; see the five breaths returning to the origin.
  • “The embryo is formed within the lying-down of the breath”: from the Scripture of Embryonic Breathing (Taixi jing 胎息经).
  • Lying down of the breath (fuqi 伏气): the breath naturally lying hidden, “not the same as forcibly holding the breath.”
  • Embryonic breathing / the breathing of the Perfected: the breathing of mouth and nose returning to the root of breath, as if there were no breathing at all; see True Breath.
  • Dry meditation (kuchan 枯禅): sitting meditation that seeks only empty stillness, without knowing the spark of life.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The order of beginning: let go of body and mind → glance at the tip of the nose with both eyes to take aim → turn back to illuminate the space between the eyebrows → when the space between the eyebrows feels slightly warm, lead the light down with the intention to the lower abdomen (the Cavity of Breath) → mind and eyes do not leave the lower abdomen, not a single thought arising. Beginners may omit the route of the “two rays of golden light” and simply let the attention sink gently from between the eyebrows to the lower abdomen.
  2. When movement comes, I remain the master of stillness: “this mechanism moves, yet I remain the master of stillness; when I am the master of stillness, it will surely move all the more.” This is the central teaching of the section — the stiller you are, the livelier it becomes.
  3. Awareness and movement: do not miss it, and do not grab it. When it moves, the mind clearly “knows” — that is “meeting it with awareness.”
  4. The method of applying the intention to the out-breath (martial fire): only when the Medicine is produced (a distinct movement of breath, a warm current, a response in the lower body), apply a little intention to the out-breath, the in-breath merely following; when you feel it has returned to the lower abdomen, stop at once and switch to barely perceptible attention (civil fire). Do not use the martial fire at ordinary times.
  5. Recognizing true embryonic breathing: counting breaths is not embryonic breathing, and holding the breath is even less so. Embryonic breathing is when, after the spirit has concentrated in the lower abdomen, the breathing by itself becomes extremely fine, as if it had returned into the lower abdomen. “The breath lies down by itself, the respiration settles by itself” — it appears naturally and cannot be forced.
  6. Ask yourself at all times: am I natural right now? “As soon as there is any forcing, it is not the correct way.”

Translator's Notes

  1. “This cavity is one cun three fen below the navel”: this part says “below the navel,” while the preceding parts mostly say “inside (behind) the navel, one cun two fen or one cun three fen.” The accounts differ; readers may simply take the depths of the lower abdomen as the place to guard. This is the translator’s remark.
  2. “The outer kidney becomes vigorously aroused”: one of the phenomena of the production of the Medicine faithfully recorded in the old alchemical books. The text’s requirement is “govern it with the spirit so that it does not run outward,” that is, keep the mind settled and do not let thoughts arise in response.
  3. “What is stopping the martial fire? Simply stopping the out-breath”: this does not mean stopping breathing, but stopping the application of the intention to the out-breath; the translation adds this in parentheses.
  4. “Bodhidharma’s ‘collecting the muni in two phases’”: the source of this saying is not known; it is translated as given.
  5. “All thirty-six palaces are spring”: from Shao Yong’s poem “Contemplating Things” (Northern Song): “Between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon I come and go at leisure; all thirty-six palaces are spring.”
  6. Qiu Ling divided the original of this section into two passages; for ease of comparison and recitation, it is further divided by content into §1–§9.

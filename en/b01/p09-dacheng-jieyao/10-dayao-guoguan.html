The Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment, Selected and Annotated · 一·10 大药过关实验

I.10 · A True Account of the Great Medicine Passing the Gates

Records of Experience in Refining Essence into Breath, Section 10 · A True Account of the Great Medicine Passing the Gates — five dragons bearing up the sage, ingesting, and the Celestial Circuit of Mao and You

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

Note (translator): What this section describes — “the breath ceasing and the pulse stopping,” “not knowing where the body is,” and so on — are scenes of deep absorption recorded by the ancients, belonging to the records of advanced cultivation, and are not goals of practice. If in quiet sitting you experience marked palpitations, tightness in the chest, dizziness, or loss of the sense of direction, open your eyes, stop practicing, and get up and move about; seek medical care if necessary.

About This Chapter

“True account” (shiyan 实验) here means “actual verified experience.” This section describes the firsthand scenes of the Great Medicine passing the gates:

  • Passing the gates: in someone whose breath is strong, it bursts through the Three Passes in one rush and rises to the Muddy Pellet; in someone whose breath is weak, it takes three attempts to reach the crown gate. Once it reaches the top, the whole body tingles and feels numb, floating and blissful; the whole face feels as if spiders and ants were crawling over it, spreading from the Hall of Impressions to the bridge of the nose, the eye sockets, the cheekbones, and the cheeks; the mouth fills with saliva that cannot all be swallowed. Then the mouth is too lazy to open, the body too lazy to move; one enters chaos, not knowing where the body is; breathing and pulse seem to stop, and at the Hall of Impressions there is a constant light like moonlight.
  • Ingesting: the golden elixir descends from the upper field into the mouth, and on the tongue one feels a round, bright mass. At this time, close ear, eye, and mouth, swallow as you become aware of it, send it down the Storied Tower to return to the central palace; then run one Celestial Circuit of Mao and You, concentrate the spirit, enter absorption, and nourish in stillness for seven days; only then does the Holy Embryo begin to form.

Qiu Ling’s note makes four points: where the Three Passes and the central palace are; that “ingesting” is not eating, but saliva passing the Magpie Bridge and going down the Storied Tower, which the ancients called “ghee poured on the crown”; the method of the Celestial Circuit of Mao and You in the Clarifications on Nature and Life; and, most important of all — the Great Medicine passing the gates is “guiding after it moves”; one must not “guide it to make it move.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

五龙捧圣大药过关，若气壮之人一撞三关即过，升至泥丸。气弱之人须三次方到顶门。九阳之气到了九天之上，其头有颠鸾之壮，其身似麻酥之样，如凭虚御风，快乐无边。

In five dragons bearing up the sage, the Great Medicine passing the gates: in someone whose breath is strong, it bursts through the Three Passes in one rush and rises to the Muddy Pellet. In someone whose breath is weak, it takes three times to reach the crown gate. When the breath of ninefold yang has reached above the nine heavens, the head has a topsy-turvy feeling, the body seems numb and tingling, as if riding the wind upon the void, with boundless bliss.

§2
Original (Chinese)

满面如蛛走蚁行，痒痒欲搔，散之印堂，次到鼻柱、眼框、两颧、两腮。牙关口中津液充满，咽纳不尽。此时口合懒开，身沉懒动，入于混沌，化为无有，不知身在何处，自然息住脉停，真气充满。功夫到此，印堂自有月光常明，似电光灼灼。

The whole face feels as if spiders were walking and ants crawling, itching as if one wanted to scratch; it spreads to the Hall of Impressions, then to the bridge of the nose, the eye sockets, the two cheekbones, and the two cheeks. The jaws and mouth fill with saliva that cannot all be swallowed. At this time the mouth is closed and too lazy to open, the body heavy and too lazy to move; one enters chaos and is transformed into nothingness, not knowing where the body is; naturally the breath ceases and the pulse stops, and the true breath is full. When the work reaches this point, at the Hall of Impressions there is naturally a constant light like moonlight, flashing like lightning.

§3
Original (Chinese)

盖真能过关者，当服食之际，金丹从上田降落口中，自然觉得圆陀陀、光灼灼浑然一团聚在舌上。此际切记耳、目、口三关闭塞，静候还丹入口，随觉随咽，送下重楼，归宿中宫。接行卯酉周天一遍，即凝神入定，静养七日，深入混沌，而圣胎方能凝胞。

For those who can truly pass the gates, at the time of ingesting, the golden elixir descends from the upper field into the mouth, and one naturally feels a round, bright mass gathered whole on the tongue. At this time be sure to close the three passes of ear, eye, and mouth, quietly wait for the Returned Elixir to enter the mouth, swallow as you become aware of it, send it down the Storied Tower to return to rest in the central palace. Then run one Celestial Circuit of Mao and You, at once concentrate the spirit and enter absorption, nourish in stillness for seven days, going deep into chaos, and only then can the Holy Embryo begin to form.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

三关即尾闾、夹脊、玉枕。中宫为中丹田。五龙捧圣大药过关，即大河车也。

服食者非饮食之食。津液度过舌舐上颚之鹊桥而下重楼，喻曰服食。此津液被称为甘露神水，清凉恬淡，香甜可口。此津一生，浑身苏软如绵，故古人谓之为“醍醐灌顶”。

按《性命圭旨》一书所刊卯酉周天之法为：用真意眸光下照坤脐良久，徐徐从左上照乾顶，少停从右降下坤脐，是为一度。又从坤脐右升上乾顶，再从乾顶从左降下坤脐。如此左升右降三十六转是为进阳火，右升左降二十四转是为退阴符。

大药过关是动而后引，不可引而使动，有唱和之机。见其遇阻不动即一意不生，凝神不动；忽有自动冲关，即随其动机轻轻引上。古人说，此是“两相知之微意”。

The Three Passes are the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass, and the Jade Pillow. The central palace is the middle Cinnabar Field. Five dragons bearing up the sage, the Great Medicine passing the gates, is the “Great River Chariot.”

“Ingesting” is not eating food. Saliva passing over the Magpie Bridge formed by the tongue licking the upper palate and going down the Storied Tower is figuratively called ingesting. This saliva is called “Sweet Dew divine water”; it is cool and mild, fragrant and sweet to the taste. As soon as this saliva arises, the whole body becomes soft as cotton, which is why the ancients called it “ghee poured on the crown.”

According to the method of the Celestial Circuit of Mao and You printed in the book Clarifications on Nature and Life: with the True Intention and the light of the eyes, shine down on the Kun navel (the lower Cinnabar Field) for a long while, then slowly shine upward on the left to the Qian summit (the crown), pause a little, then descend on the right to the Kun navel — this is one round. Then rise from the Kun navel on the right to the Qian summit, and descend again from the Qian summit on the left to the Kun navel. Rising on the left and descending on the right thirty-six turns in this way is advancing the yang fire; rising on the right and descending on the left twenty-four turns is withdrawing the yin tally.

The Great Medicine passing the gates is guiding after it moves; one must not guide it to make it move. There is a mechanism of call and response. When one sees that it has met an obstruction and does not move, let not a single intention arise, concentrate the spirit and do not move; when suddenly it stirs of itself and rushes at the pass, follow its mechanism of movement and guide it up lightly. The ancients said this is “the subtle intention of the two knowing each other.”

Key Terms

  • Five dragons bearing up the sage, the Great Medicine passing the gates: Qiu Ling’s note — that is, the “Great River Chariot,” the beginning of the Greater Celestial Circuit.
  • Three Passes: the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass, the Jade Pillow.
  • Breath of ninefold yang, above the nine heavens: pure yang breath rising to the crown.
  • Hall of Impressions: between the two eyebrows; see Celestial Eye.
  • The breath ceasing and the pulse stopping: the scene in deep absorption in which breathing and pulse seem to stop.
  • Ingesting: Qiu Ling’s note — saliva passing the Magpie Bridge and going down the Storied Tower, figuratively called ingesting.
  • Round and bright (yuan tuotuo, guang zhuozhuo 圆陀陀、光灼灼): describing the golden elixir as perfectly round and shining.
  • Storied Tower: the throat.
  • Central palace: the middle Cinnabar Field; see Central Yellow, Yellow Court.
  • Celestial Circuit of Mao and You: a circuit turning left and right, used to gather in the inner medicine; see Group II, Section 4 for details.
  • Sweet Dew, ghee poured on the crown: beautiful names for saliva.
  • Guiding after it moves, not guiding it to make it move: Qiu Ling’s note, the fundamental principle of the Great Medicine passing the gates.
  • The subtle intention of the two knowing each other: the subtle response of call and answer between the spirit and the medicine.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “Guiding after it moves; one must not guide it to make it move.” This is the most important sentence in this section, and a general rule of the whole alchemical method: only when there is natural movement in the body does one lightly follow it; when there is no movement, one waits quietly. Forcibly “pushing the breath through the passes” with the mind is the most common source of all qigong deviations.
  2. Much saliva in the mouth is a normal response to relaxing and entering stillness. Simply swallow it naturally; there is no need deliberately to “stir the sea” or “rinse and gargle.”
  3. Sensations such as ants crawling over the face or the body tingling come or do not come. Do not judge your level by them.

Translator's Notes

  1. “The head has a topsy-turvy feeling” (其头有颠鸾之壮): 壮 should be 状, “state, appearance.” 颠鸾 literally means “the phoenixes topsy-turvy”; here it refers to a top-heavy, upside-down sensation in the head, and the translation follows this.
  2. “Eye sockets” (眼框): should be written 眼眶.
  3. “Upper palate”: Qiu Ling’s note writes 上颚, which refers to the same roof of the mouth as 上腭 earlier in the text.
  4. “Begin to form” (ningbao 凝胞): beginning to congeal into an embryo.
  5. The original text of this section has two passages; we have divided them into three short passages.

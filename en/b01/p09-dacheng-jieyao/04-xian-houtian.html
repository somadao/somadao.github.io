I.4 · Refining Distinguishes Prior and Posterior Heaven

Records of Experience in Refining Essence into Breath, Section 4 · Refining Distinguishes Prior and Posterior Heaven — the clear and the turbid source of the water

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

Safety note (translator): The “fierce cooking and urgent refining, setting the martial fire in motion” of this section is a forceful means the ancients used to deal with a “turbid source.” Qiu Ling’s position is clear: the fundamental remedy is first to quiet the mind so that the source itself becomes clear; fierce cooking and urgent refining is only a last-resort remedy. Practitioners should put their effort into “stillness” and should not rely on forceful breathing.

About This Chapter

Of the same stirring of the body in the middle of the night, the author distinguishes two kinds:

  • The zi hour of the original essence of Prior Heaven: one is already sound asleep, the mind silent and without action; this stirring comes from emptiness and dim darkness, with no thought of heaven, earth, people, or things, purely the quintessence of Prior Heaven — this is the true medicine, which should be gathered and returned to the furnace, followed by the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit to refine it into the golden elixir.
  • The zi hour of Posterior Heaven: the mind is still entangled in worldly ties; it comes from the topsy-turvy world of dreams, carrying thoughts of people, scenes, and desire — this is yin essence changed by the arousal of desire, which cannot serve as medicine. Yet neither can it be left alone: it must be fiercely cooked and urgently refined with martial fire and transformed into breath, so that it does no harm.

Qiu Ling calls this “distinguishing the clear and turbid source of the water,” and quotes Wu Chongxu’s own words to explain: whatever comes with thought, imagination, knowledge, seeing, or hearing is a turbid source. His conclusion goes to the root: to obtain a clear source, the key is the initial return to emptiness, that is, refining the self.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

盖先天元精之子时，身已向晦宴息，而寂静无为，来自虚无杳冥之中，无天地人物，空空洞洞，虚虚灵灵，冲动阳关，纯是先天之英华，而无渣滓，可谓真药物，即当采取归炉，接行周天火符，以烹以炼，结成金丹，方能超凡入圣矣。

At the zi hour of the original essence of Prior Heaven, the body has already “turned toward the dark and gone to rest,” silent and without action; this comes from emptiness and dim darkness, with no heaven, earth, people, or things, empty and hollow, void and numinous; it stirs the yang gate and is purely the quintessence of Prior Heaven, without dregs — it may be called the true medicine. One should then gather it and return it to the furnace, go on with the fire tallies of the Celestial Circuit, cooking and refining it, and form the golden elixir; only then can one transcend the ordinary and enter sagehood.

§2
Original (Chinese)

盖后天媾精之子时，心犹牵缠尘根，来自梦魂颠倒之中，有天地人物，而冲动外肾，为情欲交感所变之阴精，不堪为药物。必须用风火采归，猛烹急炼，运动武火之神功，煽开炉焰，务使化而为炁，方不为害。

At the zi hour of the conjugal essence of Posterior Heaven, the mind is still entangled in the roots of the world; this comes from the topsy-turvy world of dreams, with heaven, earth, people, and things, and stirs the outer kidney — this is yin essence changed by the arousal of desire, which is not fit to be medicine. (Yet it cannot be left to itself:) one must gather it back with wind and fire, cook it fiercely and refine it urgently, setting in motion the divine work of the martial fire and fanning open the furnace flames, and make sure it is transformed into breath; only then will it do no harm.

§3
Original (Chinese)

其烹炼之法，以呼吸之凡火，引动命宫之真火，再以性中之神火主之，使三火腾光，发焰于炉中。而丹田内一呼一吸，息息往来，归于乾顶，神存气穴，目无转睛，阴精虽顽全又焉有不化者乎。此调药炼药之真天机，而为炼精化炁之真口诀也。

The method of cooking and refining is this: with the ordinary fire of respiration, draw out the true fire of the palace of life, and let the spirit-fire of the nature preside over it, so that the three fires rise up in light and blaze in the furnace. Within the Cinnabar Field, each exhalation and inhalation, breath after breath coming and going, returns up to the Qian summit; the spirit remains in the Cavity of Breath, the eyes unblinking — stubborn though the yin essence may be, how could it fail to be transformed? This is the true heavenly mechanism of regulating and refining the medicine, and the true oral formula of refining Essence into Breath.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

此段为水源清浊之辨及对浊源的补救办法。水喻真精，源者精气之来源。清属先天，浊属后天。

伍冲虚在《仙佛合宗》中说：“凡有念虑、存想、知见、睹闻，皆属后天所谓浊源也。阳精从此浊源中生，因而采、封、炼、止纵合玄妙天机，终成幻丹，以其水源不清也。若夫无念、无虑、不识、不知、虚极静笃时，即属先天所谓清源也。阳精从此清源中生，则采、封、炼、止兼合玄妙天机，遂成真丹，以其水源不浊也。凡阳精从清源中生，即须采而炼之。倘阳精从浊源中生，弃之不可采也。”

要得源清，关键在于最初还虚，即炼己之功。盖静定中神气合一，静极而动，神气亦不外驰，则气至足而源自清。一旦精从浊源中生，弃之不采犹恐为害，则用上法猛烹急炼而化之。

This passage distinguishes the clear and turbid source of the water, and gives a remedy for a turbid source. “Water” is a metaphor for the true essence; “source” is the origin of essence and breath. The clear belongs to Prior Heaven, the turbid to Posterior Heaven.

Wu Chongxu says in the Common Origin of Immortals and Buddhas: “Whatever has thought, imagination, knowledge, seeing, or hearing belongs to Posterior Heaven and is what is called a turbid source. If the yang essence arises from this turbid source, then even if gathering, sealing, refining, and stopping all accord with the mysterious, wondrous mechanism of Heaven, it will in the end become only an illusory elixir, because its water source is not clear. When there is no thought, no worry, no recognition, no knowing, in utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, this belongs to Prior Heaven and is what is called a clear source. If the yang essence arises from this clear source, and gathering, sealing, refining, and stopping also accord with the mysterious, wondrous mechanism of Heaven, it becomes a true elixir, because its water source is not turbid. Whenever the yang essence arises from a clear source, one must gather it and refine it. If the yang essence arises from a turbid source, abandon it; it may not be gathered.”

To obtain a clear source, the key lies in the initial return to emptiness, that is, the work of refining the self. For in settled stillness spirit and breath unite as one; when movement arises at the extreme of stillness, spirit and breath do not race outward either; then, when the breath reaches fullness, the source is naturally clear. Once essence arises from a turbid source and, if abandoned and not gathered, it is feared it may still do harm, then one transforms it by fierce cooking and urgent refining as described above.

Key Terms

  • Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven: this section divides them by “whether there are thoughts” — what stirs without thought belongs to Prior Heaven, what stirs with thought belongs to Posterior Heaven.
  • Clear source, turbid source: Wu Chongxu’s terms; water is a metaphor for the true essence, source means the origin of essence and breath.
  • “Turning toward the dark and going to rest”: from the Book of Changes, hexagram Sui, “the noble one turns toward the dark and goes in to rest” — when it grows dark, one rests peacefully.
  • Yang gate: the genitals; see outer kidney.
  • Conjugal essence of Posterior Heaven: essence stirred by the arousal of desire.
  • Three fires: the ordinary fire of respiration, the true fire of the palace of life, the spirit-fire of the nature; see Samādhi True Fire.
  • Qian summit: the Muddy Pellet at the crown of the head.
  • Illusory elixir, true elixir: Wu Chongxu’s terms — a turbid source yields an illusory elixir, a clear source a true elixir.
  • Refining the self: the initial work of returning to emptiness.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The criterion is “thoughts,” not the bodily phenomenon itself. The same bodily reaction is turbid if it comes with desire and images, and clear if it appears naturally in a stillness free of all thought. This criterion is useful for ordinary practitioners too: whatever sensation appears in quiet sitting, first see whether your mind has run off after it.
  2. The fundamental remedy is to make the source clear. Qiu Ling’s conclusion is that “to obtain a clear source, the key lies in the initial return to emptiness.” Rather than laboring to “refine away” something after it has stirred, it is better to keep the mind quiet in ordinary times.
  3. Wu Chongxu’s original view is “abandon it; it may not be gathered,” whereas this book advocates transforming it by fierce cooking and urgent refining. The two schools differ; Qiu Ling reconciles them thus: the root lies in refining the self, and only if a turbid source stirs and leaving it alone is feared to do harm does one use the remedial method. Beginners should rely chiefly on the former.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Stubborn though the yin essence may be, how could it fail to be transformed?” (阴精虽顽全又焉有不化者乎): the character 全 makes no sense here and is probably an intrusion or printing error. The translation follows “stubborn though the yin essence may be, how could it fail to be transformed,” while the original text keeps the reading of the base text.
  2. “Gathering, sealing, refining, and stopping”: gathering the medicine, sealing it, refining it (running the Celestial Circuit), and stopping the fire — a shorthand for the “six timings” mentioned earlier.
  3. The original text of this section is one passage; we have divided it into three short passages.

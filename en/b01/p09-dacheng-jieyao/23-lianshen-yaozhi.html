III.1 · The Essentials of Refining Spirit to Return to Emptiness

Records of Experience in Refining Spirit to Return to Emptiness, Section 1 · The Essentials of Refining Spirit to Return to Emptiness — the general outline of the three years of suckling

Note (translator): The third group, “refining Spirit to return to Emptiness,” deals with the going out and coming in of the Yang Spirit and its regulation and nourishment, and belongs to the highest ideals of the Daoist school. In his note to this section Qiu Ling says plainly that the following passages are only “for readers to gain a general idea of this method; the editor gives no notes.” In Part 8, the Collection of Daoxiang, he also said of sending out the spirit that he “records it so that readers can glimpse the whole course of the method, and for reference.” Please read this group as a document for understanding the whole picture of the alchemical method; do not attempt to practice it, and do not take any out-of-body or visionary experience as an achievement of cultivation; if such experiences occur and affect your life, stop practicing and seek professional help.

About This Chapter

The third stage, “refining Spirit to return to Emptiness,” is called “the higher accomplishment of suckling,” that is, the three years of suckling — like nursing an infant, slowly regulating and nourishing the Yang Spirit that has been nurtured until it is seasoned.

The overall principle is a pair of contrasts between “going out” and “coming in”:

  • The Yang Spirit’s going out is governed by movement: it should be brief, not long; near, not far.
  • The Yang Spirit’s coming in is governed by stillness: long is prized, not brief; deep is prized, not shallow.

The work goes from brief to long, and the effect from near to far. The more settled the stillness, the more solid it becomes, the firmer the Yang Spirit and the brighter the light of wisdom; the more it is regulated and exercised, the more practiced it becomes, the more numinous the Yang Spirit and the more wondrous its transformations. Until, when the work of three years is complete and the nature is seasoned: when it leaves the shell, it takes the Great Void as its dwelling, and the great earth with its mountains and rivers is all a place for it to roam and rest; when it is taken back into absorption, it again takes the physical body as its bedchamber, and the central palace is its own bed, pillow, and table.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

三节炼神还虚，名曰上成乳哺。依阳光之收放，准调神之出入。然阳神之出也，主乎动。动则宜暂不宜久，宜迩不宜遐。而阳神之入也，主乎静，静则贵久不贵暂，贵遐不贵迩。其功必由暂而至久，其效必因迩而及遐。

The third stage, refining Spirit to return to Emptiness, is called “the higher accomplishment of suckling.” According to the gathering in and letting out of the yang light, one gauges the going out and coming in of the regulated spirit. The Yang Spirit’s going out is governed by movement; in movement it should be brief, not long; near, not far. The Yang Spirit’s coming in is governed by stillness; in stillness long is prized, not brief; far is prized, not near. The work must go from brief to long, and its effect must proceed from near to far.

§2
Original (Chinese)

盖愈静定斯愈笃实，而阳神愈坚，慧光愈明，亦愈调演愈纯熟，而阳神愈灵，变化愈妙。直至三年功成，性体老练，觉得调神出壳之际，直以太虚为宅舍，大地山河，尽是我游赏栖迟之所也。

For the more settled the stillness, the more solid it becomes, and the firmer the Yang Spirit, the brighter the light of wisdom; and the more it is regulated and exercised, the more practiced it becomes, and the more numinous the Yang Spirit, the more wondrous its transformations. Until, when the work of three years is complete and the nature is seasoned, one feels that at the moment of regulating the spirit to leave the shell, one simply takes the Great Void as one’s dwelling, and the great earth with its mountains and rivers is all a place for me to roam and rest.

§3
Original (Chinese)

及收回入定之时，而阳神又以色身为寝室，合内院中宫，尽是我之床枕几席，偃仰宴息之处也。此三年乳哺之功，已返到性体坚刚，神化无方之位。

And when it is taken back into absorption, the Yang Spirit again takes the physical body as its bedchamber, and the whole inner court and central palace are my bed, pillow, and table, a place to lie and rest at ease. When the work of these three years of suckling is done, one has returned to the state in which the nature is firm and strong and the spirit transforms without fixed place.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

本书所收入古人调神出壳功法大多比较具体，以下选录数则，以供读者认识此一功法的大概，编者不作注释，可参看前载《皇极阖辟仙经》八、九、十章注释和《道乡集》出神功法部分注释。

The methods of regulating the spirit to leave the shell recorded by the ancients and included in this book are mostly fairly concrete. A few passages are selected below for readers to gain a general idea of this method; the editor gives no notes. One may refer to the notes on Chapters 8, 9, and 10 of the Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate given earlier, and to the notes on the method of sending out the spirit in the Collection of Daoxiang.

Key Terms

  • Refining Spirit to return to Emptiness: the third stage of the alchemical method, also called “the higher accomplishment of suckling” or “the three years of suckling”; see the three transmutations.
  • Suckling: regulating and nourishing the Yang Spirit like nursing an infant.
  • Gathering in and letting out of the yang light: the gathering in and letting out of the yang light between the eyebrows taken as the standard for the going out and coming in of the regulated spirit.
  • Regulating the spirit to leave the shell: the Yang Spirit leaving the physical body; see sending out the spirit.
  • Brief and long, near and far.
  • Light of wisdom: see light of Nature / light of cognition.
  • Physical body (seshen 色身): the body of flesh (a Buddhist term).
  • Inner court and central palace: the central palace within the body; see Central Yellow.
  • The nature firm and strong, the spirit transforming without fixed place: the original nature firm, the transformations of the spirit having no fixed location.

Putting It into Practice

  1. This group is for understanding only. Qiu Ling adds no notes, and we offer no practice suggestions either.
  2. Only one principle can be borrowed: “going out should be brief, coming in should be long.” In everyday life: when the mind is directed outward (work, social life), know when to stop; when it is drawn inward (rest, quiet sitting), let it be sufficient and deep. Too long outward and too little inward is the most common state of modern people.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Roam and rest” (qichi 栖迟): to wander at leisure, to dwell. From the Book of Odes, “Odes of Chen,” “Hengmen”: “Beneath the cross-beam gate one may rest at leisure.”
  2. “Lie and rest at ease” (yanyang yanxi 偃仰宴息): lying down and resting.
  3. Chapters 8 to 10 of Part 2, the Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate (sending out the spirit, regulating the spirit, returning to emptiness), and Section 9 of Part 8, the Collection of Daoxiang (the method of sending out the spirit), can be read alongside this group.
  4. The original text of this section is one passage; we have divided it into three short passages.

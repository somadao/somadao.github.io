I.6 · The Lesser Celestial Circuit after the True Seed Is Produced

Records of Experience in Refining Essence into Breath, Section 6 · The Lesser Celestial Circuit after the True Seed Is Produced — advancing the yang fire, withdrawing the yin tally, and the ablutions of Mao and You

About This Chapter

Once the true seed has been produced, one runs the Lesser Celestial Circuit to cook and refine it into the elixir. This section deals with two matters:

  1. How the number of circuits changes. The opening of the Mysterious Pass (the scene of bliss) may for some open twenty or thirty times in a row, for others for ten or twenty days in a row; each person differs. When the breath arrives and the spirit knows it, one runs one circuit. The more the true breath is refined, the stronger it grows, and one may run three or five, even ten or more circuits a day; later in the refining, as the essence is gradually transformed away, the stirrings decrease day by day and fall back again to three or five a day, the yang rising and at once waning. In time comes “the horse-like retraction,” the yang light appears three times, and it is time to go on to the work of gathering the Great Medicine in seven days.
  2. How one circuit is run. At the zi hour one advances the yang fire, rising along the back (the Governing Vessel) to the si position; at the wu hour one withdraws the yin tally, descending along the front (the Conception Vessel) to the hai position. In between there is one ablution each at the mao and you hours, neither advancing nor withdrawing. In advancing the fire, the spirit rests in the lower Cinnabar Field, exhaling civil and inhaling martial; in withdrawing the tally, the spirit rests in the upper Cinnabar Field, exhaling martial and inhaling civil; in the mao ablution the spirit rests at the Spine-Straddling Pass, silently counting thirty-six inhalations; in the you ablution the spirit rests in the Yellow Court, silently counting twenty-four exhalations.

Qiu Ling’s note makes only one point, but the most important one: three hundred sixty circuits and counting three hundred sixty breaths are all figurative expressions. He cites the Old Man of Qianfeng, Zhao Bichen, and the Collection of Daoxiang as evidence, reminding students “never to be bound by these figurative comparisons.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

玄关窍开，快乐之景有一连开二、三十次而后止，有一连开一、二十日而后止。久暂原自不同。人之神气皆日主动，夜主静。气至神知，即运一周天。

When the Mysterious Pass opens, the scene of bliss for some opens twenty or thirty times in a row before stopping, for others for ten or twenty days in a row before stopping; the duration naturally differs. In people, spirit and breath are both active by day and still by night. When the breath arrives and the spirit knows it, one runs one Celestial Circuit.

§2
Original (Chinese)

真气愈炼愈旺，而动机愈勤，日夜并进，时刻不懈，一日行三、五周天，以至十余周天，则功行彻不夜而无休歇，渐渐觉得精尽化炁，其动机日日减少。昼夜之间又渐渐退至三、五周天，觉得炁机随动随消，不能充满玉茎，阳兴即衰，随兴随采。

The more the true breath is refined, the stronger it grows, and the more frequent the stirrings become; advancing day and night together, never slackening for a moment, one runs three or five circuits a day, up to ten or more; then the work continues through day and night without rest, and gradually one feels that the essence has been entirely transformed into breath, and the stirrings decrease day by day. In a day and a night it gradually falls back again to three or five circuits; one feels the mechanism of breath dissolving as soon as it stirs, no longer able to fill the jade stalk; the yang rises and at once wanes, and one gathers as it rises.

§3
Original (Chinese)

运行周天万不可神离下田，走泄神气，三宝分离而无用矣。玄关窍开一次，行周天火符一次，谓之颠倒阴阳三百六十息。久久马阴藏相，火足药灵，龟头缩回，周天数足而阳光三现，接行七日采大药之功矣。

In running the Celestial Circuit, one must on no account let the spirit leave the lower field; (if it does,) spirit and breath leak away, the three treasures separate, and it is of no use. Each time the Mysterious Pass opens, one runs the fire tallies of the Celestial Circuit once; this is called “reversing yin and yang in three hundred sixty breaths.” In time comes the horse-like retraction; the fire is sufficient and the medicine potent, the glans retracts, the count of circuits is complete and the yang light appears three times; then one goes on to the work of gathering the Great Medicine in seven days.

§4
Original (Chinese)

药既封固，当即运转周天，子时进阳火后升至巳位时止，午时退阴符前降至亥位止。进阳火中间有卯时沐浴，退阴符中间有酉时沐浴。

Once the medicine is sealed, one should at once turn the Celestial Circuit: at the zi hour advance the yang fire, rising along the back and stopping at the si position; at the wu hour withdraw the yin tally, descending along the front and stopping at the hai position. In the middle of advancing the yang fire there is the ablution of the mao hour; in the middle of withdrawing the yin tally there is the ablution of the you hour.

§5
Original (Chinese)

子至巳，其要则神住下丹田，呼用文而吸用武；午至亥，其要则神住上丹田，呼用武而吸用文。卯时沐浴之妙用，则住夹脊，呼吸无心，惟默数三十六吸。酉时沐浴之妙用，则神住黄庭，呼吸无意，惟默数二十四呼。是卯酉二时，均用无为之文火，而心定无为，有觉有照，此阳火阴符沐浴之妙用也。

From zi to si, the key is that the spirit rests in the lower Cinnabar Field, exhaling civil and inhaling martial; from wu to hai, the key is that the spirit rests in the upper Cinnabar Field, exhaling martial and inhaling civil. The wondrous use of the ablution at the mao hour is that the spirit rests at the Spine-Straddling Pass, without intent toward the breathing, only silently counting thirty-six inhalations. The wondrous use of the ablution at the you hour is that the spirit rests in the Yellow Court, without intention toward the breathing, only silently counting twenty-four exhalations. So at both the mao and you hours one uses the civil fire of non-action, the mind settled and without action, aware and illuminating — this is the wondrous use of the yang fire, the yin tally, and the ablution.

§6
Original (Chinese)

筑基之功，除此火符，别无成不漏之木。

In the work of laying the foundation, apart from these fire tallies there is no other art for achieving non-leakage.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§6
Original note (Chinese)

三百六十周天数息三百六十回，皆譬喻之辞。千峰老人赵避尘曰：“此三百六十周天，乃是一呼一吸，前后一转，为进阳退阴。”又曰：“至于卯酉沐浴，全在一升一降之法，非实有三百息，皆譬喻之辞也。”《道乡集》也说：“丹经每谓周天必数三百六十息，方足一周之数，余谓但行一周即足三百六十之数。”学人切勿为这些虚比所拘限。

Three hundred sixty circuits and counting breaths three hundred sixty times are all figurative expressions. The Old Man of Qianfeng, Zhao Bichen, said: “These three hundred sixty circuits are one exhalation and one inhalation, one turn front and back, which is advancing the yang and withdrawing the yin.” He also said: “As for the ablutions of mao and you, it lies entirely in the method of one rising and one descending; there are not really three hundred breaths — these are all figurative expressions.” The Collection of Daoxiang also says: “The alchemical scriptures often say that the Celestial Circuit requires counting 360 breaths to complete the number of one round; I say that one has only to make one round, and the number 360 is complete.” Students must never be bound by these figurative comparisons.

Key Terms

  • Scene of bliss: the bodily pleasure when the Mysterious Pass opens and the true seed is produced; see the previous section.
  • When the breath arrives, the spirit knows: a common saying of the Wu–Liu school.
  • Jade stalk: that is, the outer kidney.
  • Reversing yin and yang in three hundred sixty breaths: a name for one Celestial Circuit; Qiu Ling’s note makes clear it is figurative.
  • Horse-like retraction, glans retracting: signs that the fire is sufficient.
  • The yang light appearing three times: the signal for gathering the Great Medicine; see Section 8 and stopping the fire.
  • Advancing the yang fire, withdrawing the yin tally: the rising and descending halves of the Celestial Circuit; see fire phasing.
  • Zi, si, wu, hai, mao, you: the twelve double-hours borrowed to mark positions on the circuit: zi at the Caudal Funnel, wu at the Muddy Pellet, mao at the Spine-Straddling Pass, you at the front of the chest (around the Yellow Court); si and hai are where the rising and descending are about to stop.
  • Ablutions of Mao and You: pauses for warm nourishing in the middle of the circuit; see ablution.
  • Exhaling civil and inhaling martial, exhaling martial and inhaling civil: emphasizing inhalation in rising and exhalation in descending, the same as the Collection of Daoxiang‘s “in rising, inhalation leads; in descending, exhalation leads.”
  • Thirty-six inhalations, twenty-four exhalations: the yang number (four nines) and the yin number (four sixes); Qiu Ling’s note, citing Zhao Bichen, makes clear these are figurative.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Qiu Ling’s note is the real “oral formula” of this section: do not count. Three hundred sixty, thirty-six, twenty-four are all comparisons the ancients drew from astronomy and the calendar. If you really count, your mind is occupied by numbers. The Collection of Daoxiang puts it most bluntly: completing one round is the full count.
  2. “In running the Celestial Circuit, one must on no account let the spirit leave the lower field.” Even when attention follows the breath upward, its root remains in the Cinnabar Field. For ordinary practitioners, the safest approach is to let attention always make the lower abdomen its home, without deliberately leading the breath around a circuit.
  3. Stirrings going from many to few is a good thing. The book describes how, as the work deepens, “the stirrings decrease day by day”; this shows that the essence has transformed into breath, and there is no need to be anxious because “there is less sensation.”

Translator's Notes

  1. “The work continues through day and night without rest” (功行彻不夜而无休歇): 彻不夜 is obscure; it should perhaps read 彻夜 (“all through the night”), or mean making no distinction between day and night (“no night to speak of”). The translation follows “continuing through day and night without rest.”
  2. “No other art for achieving non-leakage” (别无成不漏之木): 木 is clearly an error for 术, “art, method,” and the translation follows 术. The original text keeps the reading of the base text.
  3. “Rising along the back,” “descending along the front”: the back means the Governing Vessel along the spine, the front the Conception Vessel along the chest and belly; see Conception and Governing vessels.
  4. The Old Man of Qianfeng, Zhao Bichen (1860–1942), founder of the Qianfeng school in Beijing, author of Clear Directions on the Methods and Formulas of Nature and Life.
  5. The original text of this section has three passages; we have divided them into six short passages.

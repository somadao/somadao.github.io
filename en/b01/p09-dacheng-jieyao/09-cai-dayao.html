The Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment, Selected and Annotated · 一·9 采大药六根震动

I.9 · Gathering the Great Medicine: the Shaking of the Six Roots

Records of Experience in Refining Essence into Breath, Section 9 · Gathering the Great Medicine — the shaking of the six roots; gathering the Great Medicine relies entirely on the light of the eyes

Note (translator): This section and the next few describe the advanced stage of “gathering the Great Medicine in seven days” after stopping the fire, drawing mainly on the ancients’ records of cultivation. The bodily scenes in them (fire blazing in the Cinnabar Field, the two kidneys seething like boiling liquid, golden light issuing from the eyes, and so on) are not goals of practice; do not deliberately pursue them.

About This Chapter

After stopping the fire, one goes on to spend about seven days “gathering the Great Medicine.” The method has two stages:

  • The first three days: concentrate the True Intention and the light of the eyes in the Cinnabar Field, where the Mysterious Pass has opened, gently in still illumination, watching over it continuously, neither existent nor nonexistent, as if present and as if gone.
  • The last three or four days: only then add intention to gather it — the mind’s strength fully roused, the gaze fixed, but the breathing left to run by itself, with no intention placed on the breathing; one must “not leave it for an instant, like a cat catching a mouse,” not a single thought arising, not a single intention scattering.

The key to the production of the Great Medicine is “the breathing coming to rest” — not holding the breath, but the breathing stopping naturally in deep absorption. Then the scenes of the “shaking of the six roots” appear: fire blazing in the Cinnabar Field, the two kidneys seething like boiling liquid, golden light issuing from the eyes, wind arising behind the ears, an eagle’s cry in the brain, the body surging and the nose twitching. But the precondition is first to “cut off the six roots,” that is, eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind do not leak outward.

Qiu Ling’s note says: seven days is an approximate figure; gathering the Great Medicine relies solely on the work of the light of the eyes — “a mass of living force lies in the two eyes”; when the light of the eyes is concentrated on the Cinnabar Field, water and fire unite, the True Intention arrives, settled stillness arises, and the True Breath becomes steady.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

行采大药之功时，初采以真意眸光凝聚丹田，玄关窍开之处，轻轻寂照，绵绵看守，过三日方可加意采取。心力要提足，目光要窥定，任呼吸自运，不可着意于呼吸。单候呼吸一住，而大药即产，呼吸不住，则大药不生。

When doing the work of gathering the Great Medicine, at the start of gathering concentrate the True Intention and the light of the eyes in the Cinnabar Field, where the Mysterious Pass has opened, gently in still illumination, watching over it continuously; only after three days may one add intention to gather it. The mind’s strength must be fully roused, the gaze must be fixed, the breathing left to run by itself; one must not place intention on the breathing. Simply wait for the breathing to come to rest, and the Great Medicine is produced; if the breathing does not come to rest, the Great Medicine is not born.

§2
Original (Chinese)

前三日不有不无，若存若亡，轻轻寂照。后三、四日瞬息不离，如猫捕鼠，一念不起，一意不散，而六根震动之景即呈现也。所谓六根者，耳、目、鼻、舌、身、意是也。必须先将六根斩断，然后方得六根震动之景。

For the first three days, neither existent nor nonexistent, as if present and as if gone, gently in still illumination. For the last three or four days, not leaving it for an instant, like a cat catching a mouse, not a single thought arising, not a single intention scattering, and the scene of the shaking of the six roots then appears. The six roots are ear, eye, nose, tongue, body, and mind. One must first cut off the six roots, and only then does one obtain the scene of the six roots shaking.

§3
Original (Chinese)

六根不漏，自然有六根震动之景呈现：丹田火炽，两肾汤煎，眼吐金光，耳后生风，脑中鹫鸣，身涌鼻搐之类。

When the six roots do not leak, the scene of the six roots shaking naturally appears: fire blazing in the Cinnabar Field, the two kidneys seething like boiling liquid, golden light issuing from the eyes, wind arising behind the ears, an eagle’s cry in the brain, the body surging and the nose twitching, and the like.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

百日筑基之后，再加七日采大药过关，七日乃大略之数，盖估计两眉间阳光三现之时，纯阳真气已凝聚到相当程度，再加采功，即成全丹大药。

采大药专靠眸光之功。语云：“一团生意在双眸。”通过眸光专视丹田，则水火交，真意至，静定生，真息定。

After the hundred days of laying the foundation, add seven more days of gathering the Great Medicine and passing the gates. Seven days is an approximate figure, for it is estimated that when the yang light appears for the third time between the two eyebrows, the true breath of pure yang has already gathered to a considerable degree; adding the work of gathering, it becomes the complete elixir — the Great Medicine.

Gathering the Great Medicine relies solely on the work of the light of the eyes. A saying goes: “A mass of living force lies in the two eyes.” By concentrating the light of the eyes on the Cinnabar Field, water and fire unite, the True Intention arrives, settled stillness arises, and the True Breath becomes steady.

Key Terms

  • Great Medicine: the true breath of Prior Heaven gathered after stopping the fire, more concentrated than the lesser medicine; see Medicine.
  • Still illumination: still and ever illuminating, awareness within stillness.
  • Like a cat catching a mouse: a common alchemical metaphor for a state of concentration that does not let go and yet is not tense.
  • Six roots: ear, eye, nose, tongue, body, mind (a Buddhist term).
  • Cutting off the six roots, the six roots not leaking: the six roots not chasing outward.
  • Shaking of the six roots: the six scenes at the gathering of the Great Medicine, called the “six signs” in the Collection of Daoxiang.
  • Eagle’s cry: a sound in the brain as if an eagle were crying.
  • A mass of living force lies in the two eyes: gathering the Great Medicine depends entirely on the light of the eyes. See Light, Reverting the Light.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The order of light first, then firmer is worth borrowing. For the first three days it is only “gently in still illumination”; only in the last few days does one “add intention.” Any practice of concentration can go this way: first watch in a relaxed way, and once it is stable, tighten a little.
  2. “The breathing left to run by itself; one must not place intention on the breathing” — “the breathing coming to rest” happens naturally; it is not forced by holding the breath. This point is very important.
  3. The eyes are the handle of the mind. Qiu Ling says that gathering the Great Medicine “relies solely on the work of the light of the eyes.” For ordinary people, the most practical application is this: in quiet sitting, let the eyes droop gently and turn inward, without glancing about, and the mind settles easily.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Cutting off the six roots”: not actually harming the senses, but the six roots not clinging to things outside.
  2. These six scenes are roughly the same as the “six signs” listed in Section 7 of the Collection of Daoxiang (where it reads “an eagle’s cry behind the head”).
  3. The original text of this section has two passages; we have divided them into three short passages.

The Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment, Selected and Annotated · 二·7 邪水邪火潮生

II.7 · The Rising Tides of Deviant Water and Deviant Fire

Records of Experience in Refining Breath into Spirit, Section 7 · The Rising Tides of Deviant Water and Deviant Fire — two deviations in nurturing the embryo and their remedies

Note (translator): For what this section describes — “the heart hot, restless and thirsty, drinking water without end,” and so on — the first step today should be to rule out physical illness (such as fever, thyroid or blood sugar problems). If persistent feverish heat, thirst, irritability, insomnia, or chills and fatigue appear during practice, please see a doctor first and do not simply attribute them all to “reactions to the practice.” The book’s “visualizing a black ball” and “visualizing true fire” are adjustment methods of the ancients, given for information only.

About This Chapter

For those nurturing the embryo in absorption, two deviations may appear in stillness, which the author calls “deviant water” and “deviant fire”:

  • Deviant water (scenes of yin demons): in stillness one sees water arising, and it cannot be made to recede. The cause is that the natural blowing and breathing has been interrupted, the Original Spirit does not keep to its dwelling, warm nourishing does not reach, the Cinnabar Field’s fire grows cold, and the elixir light does not appear. The scenes change in a thousand ways, but never depart from “water.” The remedy: gather the Samādhi True Fire to refine it — concentrate the spirit in the central palace, attend to embryonic breathing, fix the gaze like an arrow, blow and breathe with martial fire, and visualize a mass of true fire with blazing flames; the spirit-light illuminates the whole body, and the bad scenes of the yin demons vanish of themselves.
  • Deviant fire: in settled stillness fire suddenly arises, and it cannot be put out. The cause is eating too much fire-stirring food, or bathing in hot water, which stirs up the elixir fire and sends it roaming everywhere. The scenes never depart from “fire”: the heart hot, restless, thirsty, drinking water without end; if one does not know how to subdue it, there is also the danger of the elixir escaping. The remedy: visualize a black ball the size of a fist in front of you, cool as water, draw it in with the intention, and by reverting the light lead it into the central palace, three or five times, until the heat has entirely receded and the spirit is clear and the breath refreshed.

Qiu Ling’s note has only one sentence: “These two methods of dispelling deviant water and deviant fire are excellent.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

养胎入定之士静中或见水生，而不能使之退，此皆因自然吹嘘间断，元神不守乎舍，温养不到而丹田火冷，丹光不现，故有此阴魔之景。然而阴景之变象亦自多端，要皆不离乎水象，而黄庭中之丹光杳然矣！

Those nurturing the embryo in absorption sometimes see water arising in stillness and cannot make it recede. This is all because the natural blowing and breathing has been interrupted, the Original Spirit does not keep to its dwelling, warm nourishing does not reach, the Cinnabar Field’s fire grows cold, and the elixir light does not appear; hence these scenes of yin demons. The changing forms of the yin scenes are of many kinds, but none departs from the image of water, and the elixir light in the Yellow Court vanishes from sight!

§2
Original (Chinese)

法在聚我三昧真火而锻炼之，凝神于中宫，注意于胎息，以眸光射定，武火吹嘘，存心结想，则一团真火烈焰腾腾，满鼎神光照耀周身，薰蒸四大，则阴魔坏景自然消失，而中宫之丹光复明矣！

The method is to gather my Samādhi True Fire and refine it: concentrate the spirit in the central palace, attend to embryonic breathing, fix the light of the eyes like an arrow, blow and breathe with martial fire, and hold an image in the mind; then a mass of true fire blazes with leaping flames, the spirit-light filling the cauldron illuminates the whole body and steams the four elements, and the bad scenes of the yin demons vanish of themselves, and the elixir light of the central palace becomes bright again!

§3
Original (Chinese)

养胎之士静定之中，忽然火生，而不能使之灭，此患因多饮食动火之物，或因热水沐浴身体引动丹火到处流串，炎焰焚身者有之。其幻景亦多端，要皆不离乎火象。或觉心热，烦躁发渴，饮水不休。倘不知制伏，亦有走丹之患。

Those nurturing the embryo sometimes find, in settled stillness, that fire suddenly arises and cannot be put out. This trouble comes from eating and drinking too much fire-stirring food, or from bathing the body in hot water, which stirs up the elixir fire and sends it roaming everywhere, so that some have flames burning their bodies. Its illusory scenes are also of many kinds, but none departs from the image of fire. Sometimes one feels the heart hot, restless and thirsty, drinking water without end. If one does not know how to subdue it, there is also the danger of the elixir escaping.

§4
Original (Chinese)

诀曰：存心结想面前一团黑球其大如拳，即以真意凝聚而留恋之。想就此景，其凉如水，用意以吸，即回光返照引入中宫，而邪火自然消灭，心地自然清凉。其想面前黑球吸入中宫，不拘三次五次，总以热气邪火退尽为止。神清气爽，方保无虞。

The formula says: hold in the mind the image of a black ball in front of you, the size of a fist, and concentrate it with the True Intention and linger on it. When the image is formed, it is cool as water; draw it in with the intention, and by reverting the light to shine within lead it into the central palace; the deviant fire is naturally extinguished and the ground of the mind becomes naturally cool. Imagine drawing the black ball in front of you into the central palace, three times or five times without fixed number, always until the heat and the deviant fire have entirely receded. Only when the spirit is clear and the breath refreshed is one assured of safety.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

此消除邪水邪火二法甚妙。

These two methods of dispelling deviant water and deviant fire are excellent.

Key Terms

  • Deviant water, scenes of yin demons: images of water appearing when warm nourishing is insufficient and the fire grows cold; see realm of yin / realm of the aggregates.
  • Natural blowing and breathing: natural, subtle respiration.
  • The Original Spirit not keeping to its dwelling: the spirit not in the central palace; see Original Spirit.
  • Four elements: earth, water, fire, and wind; in Buddhism, the body.
  • Holding an image in the mind (cunxin jiexiang 存心结想): forming an image with the mind, that is, visualization.
  • Deviant fire: a dry fire stirred up by external causes (food and drink, hot baths).
  • The elixir escaping: the elixir breath dissipating.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The value of this section lies in its idea of “balancing cold and heat”: if you tend toward cold (drowsiness, chill, lassitude), rouse the spirit and add a little concentration (fire); if you tend toward heat (restlessness, feverishness, an agitated mind), relax, cool down, and gather downward (water). This is a principle of adjustment usable in every form of quiet practice.
  2. The author’s warnings about the external causes of deviant fire are very practical: eat less spicy, hot, and drying food, and do not bathe in very hot water before or after practice.
  3. For persistent cold or heat, thirst, or irritability, please see a doctor first. The ancients attributed everything to the practice; today we have better means of ruling out illness.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Roaming” (liuchuan 流串): the same as 流窜.
  2. “Only then is one assured of safety” (fang bao wu yu 方保无虞): only then can one be sure of no trouble.
  3. The original text of this section has two passages; we have divided them into four short passages.

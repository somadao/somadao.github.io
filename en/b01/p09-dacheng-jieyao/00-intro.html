What this book is

The Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment (Dacheng jieyao 大成捷要), in full the Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment: Secret Text of the Heavenly Mechanism, with the Mind-Seal Oral Formulas for the Dual Cultivation of Nature and Life, is an alchemical book secretly copied and passed down in Daoist temples since the Qing dynasty. It bears no author’s name and comes from a manuscript of the Chongfu Temple on Mount Song in Henan; in the early years of the Republic it reached Mount Lao near Qingdao, where the Mount Lao Daoist Zhu Wenbin had it printed. It belongs to the Wu–Liu school (the lineage of Wu Chongxu and Liu Huayang), and is said to come from Liu Huayang’s teaching.

The preceding eight parts mostly deal only with beginning the practice and laying the foundation; this part, however, covers the whole course of the alchemical method: from the hundred days of laying the foundation (refining Essence into Breath), to the ten months of nurturing the embryo (refining Breath into Spirit), and on to the three years of suckling (refining Spirit to return to Emptiness). The book describes the bodily sensations and inner scenes of every step very concretely, which is why Qiu Ling says that “its descriptions of the inner landscape are as vivid as a painting, its methods and formulas precise and detailed, the true mechanism fully revealed.”

Qiu Ling selected twenty-six sections from the whole book, divided them into three groups, and annotated them section by section.

Qiu Ling's editorial note

《大成捷要》一书，原名《大成捷要性命双修心印口诀天机秘文》，出自河南嵩山崇福宫抄本，不著编撰人姓名，民国初年传至青岛崂山，由崂山道人朱文彬刻印问世。本书为伍柳派之嫡传，据说是柳华阳所传，乃行功修炼而迄于大成之纪实。对炼精化炁即百日筑基，而至炼炁化神即中成养胎、炼神还虚即三年乳哺和炼虚合道即面壁大成均有叙述。所述内景历历如绘，法诀精详，真机毕露。现择其精要，略加注释，以飨读者。

The book Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment, originally titled Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment: Secret Text of the Heavenly Mechanism, with the Mind-Seal Oral Formulas for the Dual Cultivation of Nature and Life, comes from a manuscript of the Chongfu Temple on Mount Song in Henan, and does not bear the name of its compiler; in the early years of the Republic it reached Mount Lao near Qingdao, and was printed and published by the Mount Lao Daoist Zhu Wenbin. This book is a direct transmission of the Wu–Liu school, said to have been handed down by Liu Huayang, and is a true record of practice and cultivation from the start through to great accomplishment. It gives accounts of refining Essence into Breath, that is, the hundred days of laying the foundation; through refining Breath into Spirit, that is, the middle accomplishment of nurturing the embryo; refining Spirit to return to Emptiness, that is, the three years of suckling; and refining Emptiness to unite with the Dao, that is, the great accomplishment of facing the wall. Its descriptions of the inner landscape are as vivid as a painting, its methods and formulas precise and detailed, the true mechanism fully revealed. I have now selected its essentials and added brief notes, to offer them to the reader.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

A Wu–Liu school “true record” of cultivation written from beginning to end: first, with Reverting the Light, regulating the breath, and the civil and martial fire phasing, refine Essence into Breath (the lesser accomplishment, laying the foundation); then, guarding the center and embracing the One, refine Breath into Spirit in great absorption (the middle accomplishment, nurturing the embryo); finally, let the Yang Spirit thus nurtured go out and come in to be regulated and nourished (the higher accomplishment, suckling) — for every step it tells what to do, what scenes will appear, and where the greatest danger lies.

Reading tips

  1. The first group (refining Essence into Breath) is the most useful part of this text. Reverting the Light to shine within, the civil and martial fire phasing, clear and turbid sources, the use of the True Intention, the six faults of the fire phasing — these principles are very illuminating for today’s practitioners of quiet sitting. Qiu Ling’s notes are also concentrated in this group.
  2. This text is a men’s alchemical method; it often takes bodily phenomena such as “the outer kidney rising,” “the penis becoming erect,” and “the tortoise retracting” as markers of the fire phasing, and we translate these faithfully according to the original meaning. For women’s methods, see Part 7, The Precious Raft of the Women’s School of Dual Cultivation, and the appendices of this book.
  3. For several forceful ancient methods — tightly contracting the grain path, fiercely cooking and urgently refining with martial fire, sealing the Three Passes, and so on — we have added safety notes at the relevant places. Qiu Ling himself also points out in the last section of the first group: if the martial fire is too heavy and the breathing coarse and unregulated, there is the risk of abdominal pain and diarrhea.
  4. The second and third groups (nurturing the embryo, sending out the spirit) belong to very high stages; “the breath entirely gone, the six pulses all stopped,” “the union of sun and moon,” “the five breaths return to the origin,” “a body beyond the body,” and so on, are all states and ideals recorded by the ancients. Of several of them Qiu Ling says plainly that “whether the transformed scenes are all the same remains to be verified” or that they “await verification,” and for the third group “the editor gives no notes.” Please treat them as a reference for understanding the whole picture of the alchemical method, not as goals of practice to pursue.
  5. The hundred days, ten months, and three years are all approximate figures. Qiu Ling’s very first note says: the hundred days refer to the ancients practicing day and night without stopping; people today generally cannot do this and need not be bound by this limit.

I.12 · Guarding against Danger and Anticipating Peril

Records of Experience in Refining Essence into Breath, Section 12 · Guarding against Danger and Anticipating Peril — the dangers in the hundred days of gathering and refining and the seven days of passing the gates

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

The last section of the first group lists the places where things may go wrong in the stage of refining Essence into Breath (the hundred days of gathering and refining, the seven days of passing the gates):

DangerWhere it lies
Missing the timeThe opening of the Mysterious Pass is the time when the medicine is produced; not knowing it, one misses it face to face
Letting the moment pass“The jade caves blow as a pair” is the moment to gather the medicine; not knowing it, one does not obtain the true seed
Rising and descending in the circuitThe exact center of the Governing Vessel is the Yellow Path, the two sides the Red Path; one is at a loss as to the road to follow
Advancing the fireNot knowing that the Kun furnace (lower Cinnabar Field) is where the fire is raised and the Qian summit where it is stopped
Withdrawing the tallyNot knowing that withdrawing the tally starts from the upper field and stops at the lower field
Passing the gatesThe passes and openings not truly opened, the true breath gathering and scattering again, not following the Yellow Path but straying through the whole body
The upper Magpie BridgeAfter the Three Passes, the danger lies at the upper Magpie Bridge; in the seven days of deep hibernation after ingesting, leaving stillness rashly lets the great elixir leak away

Qiu Ling’s note expands this list with the points today’s practitioners most need to know: if the martial fire is too light, the essence stagnates and is not transformed; if the martial fire is too heavy and the breathing coarse and unregulated, cold air is drawn into the belly, with abdominal pain and diarrhea; if the fire is sufficient and not stopped, the elixir is harmed; if the medicine is obtained but cannot rise, it is spoiled. The common root of all is an error in managing the fire phasing.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

要知百日采炼，七日过关，正多危险。药产有时，玄关窍开即其时也。不知其时，当面错过，是失时之危险。采药有候，玉洞双吹即其候也。不知其候则不得真种，是候过之危险。

Know that in the hundred days of gathering and refining and the seven days of passing the gates there are indeed many dangers. The production of the medicine has its time; the opening of the Mysterious Pass is that time. Not knowing this time and missing it face to face is the danger of missing the time. Gathering the medicine has its moment; “the jade caves blow as a pair” is that moment. Not knowing this moment, one does not obtain the true seed; this is the danger of letting the moment pass.

§2
Original (Chinese)

后升前降，分赤黄二道，督脉正中即是黄道，两旁即赤道，倘茫然恍惚，不见其循由，是周天升降之危险。进火不知坤炉，坤炉为起火之处，乾顶为止火之地，是进火之危险。退符不知从上田起至下田止，是退符之危险。

Rising behind and descending in front, there are two paths, the Red and the Yellow: the exact center of the Governing Vessel is the Yellow Path, the two sides are the Red Path. If one is at a loss and confused, not seeing the road it follows, this is the danger of rising and descending in the Celestial Circuit. In advancing the fire, not knowing the Kun furnace — the Kun furnace is where the fire is raised, the Qian summit where the fire is stopped — this is the danger of advancing the fire. In withdrawing the tally, not knowing that it starts from the upper field and stops at the lower field — this is the danger of withdrawing the tally.

§3
Original (Chinese)

关窍开通不真，真炁已聚而复散，升降不由黄道，上下流窜于周身，是过关之危险。三关已过，危险在上鹊桥渡矣。服食以后，归入黄庭，接行七日大蛰之功，若不小心谨守，妄自出静，神驰于外，大丹即泄于此。步步向前，俱有危险在焉。

If the passes and openings are not truly opened, the true breath, having gathered, scatters again; rising and descending do not follow the Yellow Path but stray up and down through the whole body — this is the danger of passing the gates. Once the Three Passes have been passed, the danger lies in crossing the upper Magpie Bridge. After ingesting, when it has returned into the Yellow Court, one goes on to the work of seven days of deep hibernation; if one does not keep careful guard, leaves stillness rashly, and the spirit races outward, the great elixir leaks away right there. At every step forward there is danger.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

此节总结炼精化炁阶段之差错，所谓“危险”，乃提醒学者知所预防之意，但仍是举其要者。扩而言之，采药武火太轻，以致精停不化，有走失之患；武火太重，呼吸粗而不调，以致外吸冷气入腹，有腹痛溏泄之患；火足而不知止，有伤丹之虑；得药而不能升三关，有败药之危险等等。多出自掌握火候有差。防危虑险，学者岂可轻视哉。

This section sums up the errors of the stage of refining Essence into Breath. The so-called “dangers” are meant to remind students to know what to guard against, but still list only the most important. To expand on it: in gathering the medicine, if the martial fire is too light, the essence stagnates and is not transformed, with the risk of escaping; if the martial fire is too heavy and the breathing coarse and unregulated, cold air is drawn in from outside into the belly, with the risk of abdominal pain and loose stools and diarrhea; if the fire is sufficient and one does not know to stop, there is the worry of harming the elixir; if the medicine is obtained but cannot rise through the Three Passes, there is the danger of spoiling the medicine; and so on. These mostly come from errors in managing the fire phasing. How could students take guarding against danger and anticipating peril lightly!

Key Terms

  • Missing the time, letting the moment pass: missing the time of the medicine’s production and the moment of gathering it.
  • The jade caves blow as a pair: the breath coming out again through both nostrils; see Section 5.
  • Yellow Path, Red Path: the exact center of the Governing Vessel is the Yellow Path, its two sides the Red Path.
  • Kun furnace, Qian summit: the lower Cinnabar Field and the crown, where the fire is raised and stopped.
  • Upper Magpie Bridge: the area from between the eyebrows to the nose; see the entry Magpie Bridge.
  • Seven days of deep hibernation: after ingesting, going deep into absorption for seven days in a row, like an insect hibernating.
  • Leaving stillness: coming out of quiet sitting.
  • Loose stools and diarrhea (tangxie 溏泄): thin stools, diarrhea.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “The martial fire too heavy,” listed by Qiu Ling, is the most common deviation today. Deliberately deepening, strengthening, or speeding up the breath, or holding the breath for long periods, easily leads to discomfort such as tightness in the chest, dizziness, abdominal distension, or diarrhea. Whenever there is discomfort, the first step is to return to natural breathing.
  2. The warning against “leaving stillness rashly,” applied today, means: at the end of quiet sitting, do not get up abruptly; first open your eyes slowly, rub your hands, massage your face, move your limbs, and then get up.
  3. “These mostly come from errors in managing the fire phasing.” All the dangers come down to one thing: measure. Better too little than too much.

Translator's Notes

  1. “The danger of letting the moment pass” (houguo 候过): that is, having missed the right moment.
  2. “Once the Three Passes have been passed, the danger lies in crossing the upper Magpie Bridge”: the Magpie Bridge has an upper and a lower part; the upper Magpie Bridge is around the Hall of Impressions and the bridge of the nose, and the medicine descends through it into the mouth, hence it must be “crossed.”
  3. The original text of this section is one passage; we have divided it into three short passages.

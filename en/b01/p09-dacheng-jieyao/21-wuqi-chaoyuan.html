II.9 · The Five Breaths Return to the Origin

Records of Experience in Refining Breath into Spirit, Section 9 · The Five Breaths Return to the Origin — the true breath of the five viscera turns into five-colored clouds paying court at the crown

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

“The five breaths return to the origin” (wuqi chaoyuan 五气朝元) is a famous expression in the alchemical books. This section explains it thus: nurturing the embryo in absorption, the whole mind given to stillness, all conditions at rest, thoughts from outside cannot get in and thoughts from inside cannot get out. At this time the yin breath of the five viscera is nearly exhausted:

ViscusWorldly matter that cannot move itTurns intoPays court at
HeartThings to be enjoyedRed cloudsKunlun (the crown)
LiverThings to be glad ofBlue-green cloudsThe left of Kunlun
SpleenThings to be desiredYellow cloudsThe top of Kunlun
LungsThings to be angry atWhite cloudsThe right of Kunlun
KidneysThings to be grieved byBlack cloudsThe back of Kunlun

“Returning to the origin” needs no method; when the time comes, the five breaths rise of themselves to pay court. The author urges: by no means look at them; with the mind dead, enter absorption. This work rests entirely on utmost stillness, with non-movement as its principle.

Qiu Ling’s note says: this is a transformed scene that the Daoists believe appears when practice reaches a fairly high level; it accords with the five-phase classification and the visceral-manifestation theory of Chinese medicine, but whether it appears in practice remains to be verified. He also quotes another explanation of the five breaths returning to the origin, from Zhang Ziyang and the Clarifications on Nature and Life — not seeing five-colored clouds, but the eyes not looking, the ears not hearing, the tongue not speaking, the nose not smelling, the four limbs not moving, so that the breath of each of the five viscera rests in its place. This explanation is much plainer, and of more practical significance.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

盖养胎入定，一心主静，万缘俱息，外想不入，内想不出。此时心中阴气将尽，世有可乐之事则心不动摇，而心经真炁自然吐露，化为红色云霞，朝于昆仑。肝中阴气将尽，世有可喜之事则肝不动，肝经真炁吐露，化为青色云霞，朝于昆仑之左。

In nurturing the embryo and entering absorption, the whole mind given to stillness, all conditions at rest, thoughts from outside do not get in and thoughts from inside do not get out. At this time the yin breath in the heart is nearly exhausted; though the world has things to be enjoyed, the heart is not shaken, and the true breath of the heart meridian naturally comes forth, turns into red clouds, and pays court at Kunlun. The yin breath in the liver is nearly exhausted; though the world has things to be glad of, the liver does not move, and the true breath of the liver meridian comes forth, turns into blue-green clouds, and pays court at the left of Kunlun.

§2
Original (Chinese)

脾经阴气将尽，世有可欲之事则脾不动，脾经真炁吐露，化为黄色云霞，朝于昆仑之顶。肺中阴气将尽，世有可怒之事则肺不动，肺经真炁吐露，化为白色云霞，朝于昆仑之右。肾中阴气将尽，世有可哀之事则肾不动，肾经真炁吐露，化为黑色云霞，朝于昆仑之后。此名曰五气。

The yin breath of the spleen meridian is nearly exhausted; though the world has things to be desired, the spleen does not move, and the true breath of the spleen meridian comes forth, turns into yellow clouds, and pays court at the top of Kunlun. The yin breath in the lungs is nearly exhausted; though the world has things to be angry at, the lungs do not move, and the true breath of the lung meridian comes forth, turns into white clouds, and pays court at the right of Kunlun. The yin breath in the kidneys is nearly exhausted; though the world has things to be grieved by, the kidneys do not move, and the true breath of the kidney meridian comes forth, turns into black clouds, and pays court at the back of Kunlun. These are called the five breaths.

§3
Original (Chinese)

所谓朝元者，不须用法，依时其炁自然上朝。将见青炁出自东方，须臾南方赤炁出，西方白炁出，北方黑炁出，中央黄炁出，五炁朝于上元，氤氲盘旋。千万不可看它，死心入定。

“Returning to the origin” needs no method; when the time comes, the breaths rise of themselves to pay court. One will see blue-green breath come out of the east; in a moment red breath comes out of the south, white breath out of the west, black breath out of the north, yellow breath out of the center; the five breaths pay court at the upper origin, swirling densely and circling. By no means look at them; with the mind dead, enter absorption.

§4
Original (Chinese)

古人云：“神居窍而千智生，丹入鼎而万种化。”五炁之灵，遇天地之灵，则内真外应，外真内应，浑然混合。这段功夫，全以至静为主，不动为宗。老子云：“人能常清静，天地悉皆归”，则太虚空中自然现出一轮太阳，与我丹光上下相映，合而为一也。

The ancients said: “When the spirit dwells in the opening, a thousand kinds of wisdom arise; when the elixir enters the cauldron, ten thousand kinds of transformation occur.” When the numinous power of the five breaths meets the numinous power of Heaven and Earth, the true within answers the outside and the true outside answers within, blending wholly together. This work rests entirely on utmost stillness, with non-movement as its principle. Laozi said: “If one can be constantly pure and still, Heaven and Earth all return to one.” Then in the Great Void a full sun naturally appears, shining above and below with my elixir light and uniting with it as one.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

五气朝元之说，乃道家认为练功至较高层次时所出现的一种化景。此种化景符合于五行归类和中医脏象学说。至于练功中是否出现如上的景象，则有待验证。

张紫阳《金丹四百字自序》曰：“以眼不视而魂在肝，耳不闻而精在肾，舌不声而神在心，鼻不香而魄在肺，四肢不动而意在脾，故名五气朝元。”《性命圭旨》中也有一段关于五气朝元的叙述：“盖身不动则精固而水朝元，心不动则气固而火朝元，真性寂则魂藏而木朝元，妄情忘则魄伏而金朝元，四大安和则意定而土朝元，此谓五气朝元皆聚于顶也。”按木、火、土、金、水五行归于五脏、五向、五色、五窍、五志，其义相同。

The idea of the five breaths returning to the origin is a transformed scene that the Daoists believe appears when practice reaches a fairly high level. This transformed scene accords with the five-phase classification and the visceral-manifestation theory of Chinese medicine. As for whether scenes like those described above appear in practice, that remains to be verified.

Zhang Ziyang, in his own preface to the Four Hundred Words on the Golden Elixir, says: “When the eyes do not look, the hun rests in the liver; when the ears do not hear, the essence rests in the kidneys; when the tongue makes no sound, the spirit rests in the heart; when the nose does not smell, the po rests in the lungs; when the four limbs do not move, the intention rests in the spleen — hence it is called the five breaths returning to the origin.” The Clarifications on Nature and Life also has a passage on the five breaths returning to the origin: “When the body does not move, the essence is secure and water returns to the origin; when the mind does not move, the breath is secure and fire returns to the origin; when the true nature is still, the hun is stored and wood returns to the origin; when deluded emotions are forgotten, the po lies quiet and metal returns to the origin; when the four elements are at peace, the intention is settled and earth returns to the origin. This is called the five breaths returning to the origin, all gathering at the crown.” Note: the five phases — wood, fire, earth, metal, water — are assigned to the five viscera, the five directions, the five colors, the five orifices, and the five emotions; the principle is the same.

Key Terms

  • Five breaths returning to the origin: the true breath of the five viscera gathering at the crown; Zhang Ziyang’s explanation is that the five senses and four limbs do not move, and the breath of each of the five viscera rests in its place.
  • Kunlun: the crown of the head; see Muddy Pellet.
  • Upper origin: the upper Cinnabar Field, the crown.
  • Thoughts from outside do not get in, thoughts from inside do not get out: inside and outside sealed off, the mind not clinging to things.
  • “When the spirit dwells in the opening, a thousand kinds of wisdom arise; when the elixir enters the cauldron, ten thousand kinds of transformation occur”: an old saying often quoted in the alchemical books.
  • Elixir light: see light of Nature / light of cognition.
  • Five phases, five viscera, five directions, five colors, five orifices, five emotions: wood–liver–east–blue-green–eyes–anger, fire–heart–south–red–tongue–joy, earth–spleen–center–yellow–mouth–thought, metal–lungs–west–white–nose–sorrow, water–kidneys–north–black–ears–fear (the usual correspondences in Chinese medicine).

Putting It into Practice

  1. Read Zhang Ziyang’s explanation first. “The eyes not looking, the ears not hearing, the tongue not speaking, the nose not smelling, the four limbs not moving” — this is the state in quiet sitting of the five senses withdrawn and the body steady. The five breaths returning to the origin requires no “seeing” of clouds; it is letting each of the senses grow quiet.
  2. The author himself says, “by no means look at them; with the mind dead, enter absorption.” Even if scenes really do appear, they are only by-products.
  3. “Though the world has things to be enjoyed, glad of, desired, angry at, or grieved by, one is not moved” — this is the work of everyday cultivation of the mind: when emotions arise in the face of events, to be able to notice them and not be carried away by them. The foundation of the five breaths returning to the origin actually lies in everyday emotional steadiness.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original pairs “enjoyment–heart, gladness–liver, desire–spleen, anger–lungs, grief–kidneys,” which differs from the usual correspondences of the five emotions in Chinese medicine (joy–heart, anger–liver, thought–spleen, sorrow–lungs, fear–kidneys); it is the alchemical book’s own scheme, translated as in the original.
  2. “If one can be constantly pure and still, Heaven and Earth all return to one”: from the Scripture of Constant Purity and Stillness Spoken by the Most High Lord Lao, attributed to Lord Lao, hence the original says “Laozi said.”
  3. Zhang Ziyang is Zhang Boduan; the Four Hundred Words on the Golden Elixir is attributed to Zhang Boduan.
  4. The original text of this section is one passage; we have divided it into four short passages.

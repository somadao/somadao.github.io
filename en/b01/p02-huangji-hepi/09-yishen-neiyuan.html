Chapter 9 · Moving the Spirit to the Inner Court

Chapter 9 · Moving the Spirit to the Inner Court — from action to non-action; going out briefly and staying near

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

The first half of cultivation is “with doing and with action” — collecting the Medicine and forming the elixir, to complete the work on existence. The second half is “without doing and without action” — embracing the One and darkening the mind, to complete the work on inner nature. This chapter is about the latter: the Holy Embryo, just delivered, moves from the Central Palace to the upper Cinnabar Field (the Muddy Pellet, the so-called “Inner Court”), where it remains pure and unmixed; then, by the way of empty space, it is expanded and filled out, so that the spirit of the Celestial Valley rises into the Great Void and becomes one with it. Qiu Ling says frankly that this chapter “has much abstract theory and lacks concrete methods, and touches mostly on religious ideals; I record only these few passages to give a glimpse of it.” His note quotes a complete passage on “sending out the spirit” from Wu Chongxu’s The Common Source of Immortals and Buddhas, which is quite concrete: going out, take the Great Void as the realm; coming back, take the upper Cinnabar Field as the place; the time spent going out and coming back is short, the time spent preserving and nourishing is long; going out should be brief, not long, near, not far.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

始而有作有为者采药结丹以了命也，终而无作无为者抱一冥心以了性也。夫冥心者深居静室，端拱默默，一尘不染，万虑潜消，无思无为，任运自如，无视无听，抱神以静，体含虚极，常觉常明。此心常明，则万法归一，婴儿常居于清净之境，栖止于不动之场，则色不得而碍之，空不得而缚之，体若虚空，斯安然自在矣！阴长生曰：“无位真人居上界，空寂更无尘可碍。有为功就又无为，无为也有功夫在。”

At the beginning, “with doing and with action,” one collects the Medicine and forms the elixir to complete the work on existence; at the end, “without doing and without action,” one embraces the One and darkens the mind to complete the work on inner nature. To darken the mind is to dwell deep in the quiet room, sitting upright with hands folded, silent, untainted by a single speck of dust, all worries quietly dissolved, without thought and without action, letting things run their natural course, without looking and without listening, embracing the spirit in stillness, the body containing utmost emptiness, always aware and always bright. When this mind is always bright, the ten thousand dharmas return to the One. The infant always dwells in a realm of purity and stillness and rests in a place of no movement; then form cannot obstruct it and emptiness cannot bind it; its substance is like empty space, and so it is at peace and at ease! Yin Changsheng said: “The True Person without rank dwells in the upper realm; in empty stillness there is not a speck of dust to obstruct. When the work of action is accomplished, one turns to non-action; yet within non-action there is still work.”

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

此章既多抽象玄论，又缺具体功法，多涉宗教理想，仅录此数段以见端倪。

移神内院，即圣胎初脱，由中宫迁入上丹田，加以三年乳哺之功。神指阳神，内院指泥丸上丹田。清代鹤臞子《唱道真言》说：“阳神者，一团真气结成。”清代刘一明《修真辩难参证》说：“圣胎是法相，不是色相。法相，是相非相，盖一气从虚无中结就，又名阳神。此神聚则成形，散则成气”。他们认为，阳神即圣胎，即婴儿，即一炁而已。至于炼神还虚则又入定于泥丸，古人云：“性在泥丸，命在脐”。盖言了修命之事在脐，了修性之事在泥丸。炼神还虚纯入性功，故在泥丸修之，修到阳神由天门而出，叫做“出胎”。

This chapter has much abstract theory and lacks concrete methods, and touches mostly on religious ideals; I record only these few passages to give a glimpse of it.

“Moving the spirit to the Inner Court” means that the Holy Embryo, just delivered, moves from the Central Palace to the upper Cinnabar Field, and to this is added the work of three years of suckling. “Spirit” means the Yang Spirit; “Inner Court” means the Muddy Pellet, the upper Cinnabar Field. Hequzi of the Qing dynasty says in True Words for Chanting the Dao (Changdao zhenyan 唱道真言): “The Yang Spirit is formed from a mass of true breath.” Liu Yiming of the Qing dynasty says in Verifications of the Doubts on Cultivating Reality: “The Holy Embryo is a Dharma-image, not a form-image. A Dharma-image is an image that is not an image, because it is the one breath formed out of emptiness and non-being; it is also called the Yang Spirit. When this spirit gathers it takes form; when it disperses it becomes breath.” In their view the Yang Spirit is the Holy Embryo, is the infant, is simply one breath. As for refining Spirit and returning to Emptiness, one again enters absorption in the Muddy Pellet. The ancients said: “Inner nature is in the Muddy Pellet; existence is in the navel.” This means that the work of completing existence is at the navel, and the work of completing inner nature is in the Muddy Pellet. Refining Spirit and returning to Emptiness is purely the work on inner nature, so it is cultivated in the Muddy Pellet, until the Yang Spirit goes out through the Gate of Heaven — this is called “leaving the womb.”

§2
Original (Chinese)

所谓居上界者，盖即婴儿之栖天谷也。谓必移居天谷者，非必以地峻极于天，实以其纯一不杂，婴儿居此，自无一毫情念得起。此时须用虚空之道扩而充之，则我天谷之神升入太虚，合而为一也。再加精进，将天谷元神炼到至极至妙之地，证成道果。而功法不外神栖天谷，行夫不识不知，惟深惟寂，阳光不漏，故能愈扩愈大，弥远弥光，自然变化生神，生之又生，生之无尽；化之又化，化之无穷。《中和集》云：“成就顶门开一窍，个中别有一乾坤。”然此顶门岂易开哉？先发三昧火以透，不通，次聚太阳火以冲之，二火腾腾，攻击不已，霎时红光遍界，紫焰弥天，霹雳一声，天门开也。

“Dwelling in the upper realm” means the infant resting in the Celestial Valley (the Muddy Pellet). The reason it is said that it must move to the Celestial Valley is not that this place is so high that it reaches Heaven, but that it is pure and unmixed; when the infant dwells here, naturally not a trace of feeling or thought can arise. At this time one must use the way of empty space to expand and fill it out; then the spirit of my Celestial Valley rises into the Great Void and becomes one with it. With further diligence, the Original Spirit of the Celestial Valley is refined to the utmost and most wondrous point, and the fruit of the Dao is realized. And the method is nothing but letting the spirit rest in the Celestial Valley, in a state of not recognizing and not knowing, only deep, only still, the yang light not leaking out; therefore it can expand more and more, grow brighter the farther it reaches, and naturally transform and give birth to spirit — giving birth again and again, without end; transforming again and again, without limit. The Collection of Central Harmony (Zhonghe ji 中和集) says: “When accomplished, an opening opens at the gate of the crown; within it there is another Heaven and Earth.” But how could this gate at the crown be opened so easily? First the samādhi fire is sent forth to pierce it; if it does not go through, then the fire of the sun is gathered to charge it. The two fires blaze up and attack without ceasing; in an instant red light fills the world, purple flames fill the sky, there is a clap of thunder — and the Gate of Heaven opens.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

明代伍冲虚《仙佛合宗》中有一段论及阳神迁上田及出神之法，录此以供参考：“上丹田一名泥丸宫，阳神归伏之本宫也。归伏本宫，阳神未壮健，如婴儿幼小，必凭乳哺，故有乳哺之名焉。倘拘神于上丹田之小境，则失还丹之义旨，大悖乳哺之法矣！其法兼存养之全体、出收之大用而言者也。盖存养之功不着意于上丹田，惟一阳神寂照于上丹田，相与混融化成一虚空之大境，斯为存养之全体，乃为乳哺之首务也。存养功纯，自有出神之景，在所当知也。当其存养功纯，忽于定中见空中六出纷纷，即出神之景也。斯时也，即当调神出壳，一出天门而旋即收焉。出则以太虚为避脱之境，收则以上丹田为存养之所。须知出收之时少，而存养之时多。又出宜暂而不宜久，宜近而不宜远。始则出一步，而旋收焉，或出多步而旋收焉。久之或出一里而旋收焉，或出多里而旋收焉。乃至百千里以渐次而至，不可躐等而至也。所以然者，以婴儿幼小，迷失难归，故须出入谨慎，方能全虚空之全体于往来之中，以完乳哺之大用也。”

道教理论认为，练功至此，自有六通的特异功能。六通是：漏尽通、天眼通、天耳通、宿命通、他心通和神境通。

赵避尘曰：“胎圆足满，自有雪花飘飞之景到，速当出胎。急用’念动向大空，日月庙门开，推情合性转，二光相遇献’出胎口诀。古人隐而不露，或是怕泄露天机，也许未得此口诀，故而不传。”

本书收入之史从龙氏著《道乡集》中有出神功法一辑，可供参考。

In The Common Source of Immortals and Buddhas by Wu Chongxu of the Ming dynasty there is a passage on the Yang Spirit moving to the upper field and on the method of sending out the spirit. I record it here for reference: “The upper Cinnabar Field, also called the Palace of the Muddy Pellet, is the home palace where the Yang Spirit returns and rests. When it returns to rest in its home palace, the Yang Spirit is not yet strong, like a small infant that must depend on suckling — hence the name ‘suckling.' If one confines the spirit within the small realm of the upper Cinnabar Field, one loses the essential meaning of the Returned Elixir and greatly contradicts the method of suckling! This method speaks both of the whole of preserving and nourishing and of the great function of going out and coming back. The work of preserving and nourishing does not fix the attention on the upper Cinnabar Field; only the Yang Spirit shines silently in the upper Cinnabar Field, the two blending into each other and transforming into a great realm of empty space — this is the whole of preserving and nourishing, and the first task of suckling. When the work of preserving and nourishing is pure, the scene of sending out the spirit appears of itself; this one should know. When the work of preserving and nourishing is pure, suddenly in absorption one sees six-pointed flakes falling thick in the air — this is the scene of sending out the spirit. At this time one should regulate the spirit to leave the shell: once it has gone out through the Gate of Heaven, bring it back at once. Going out, take the Great Void as the realm of release; coming back, take the upper Cinnabar Field as the place of preserving and nourishing. Know that the time spent going out and coming back is short, and the time spent preserving and nourishing is long. Moreover, going out should be brief, not long; near, not far. At first go out one step and come back at once, or several steps and come back at once; after a long time, go out one li (about half a kilometer) and come back at once, or several li and come back at once; and so on up to a hundred or a thousand li, reached step by step, never by skipping stages. The reason is that the infant is small, and if it gets lost it will hardly find its way back; so one must go out and come in with care, in order to keep the whole of empty space intact in the coming and going and so complete the great function of suckling.”

Daoist theory holds that when practice reaches this point, one naturally has the extraordinary abilities of the “six penetrations.” The six penetrations are: the penetration of the exhaustion of outflows, the celestial eye, the celestial ear, the knowledge of past lives, the knowledge of others’ minds, and the penetration of spiritual realms.

Zhao Bichen said: “When the embryo is complete and full, a scene of falling snowflakes naturally appears; then one should quickly leave the womb. Urgently use the formula for leaving the womb: ‘The thought moves toward the great void, the temple gate of sun and moon opens; push the emotions to join with nature and turn, the two lights meet and offer themselves.' The ancients kept this hidden and did not reveal it — perhaps for fear of leaking the secrets of Heaven, or perhaps because they had not received this formula, and so did not transmit it.”

The Collection of Daoxiang by Shi Conglong, included in this book, contains a set of methods for sending out the spirit, which may be consulted.

Key Terms

  • Completing existence, completing inner nature: accomplishing the work on existence (body, breath) and the work on inner nature (mind, spirit).
  • Darkening the mind (mingxin 冥心): bringing the mind back to silent stillness without thought.
  • Embracing the One (baoyi 抱一): holding to pure unity.
  • Sitting upright with hands folded (duangong 端拱).
  • True Person without rank: originally a saying of the Chan master Linji Yixuan; borrowed here to mean the Yang Spirit.
  • Yin Changsheng: a Daoist of the Eastern Han, said to have been a disciple of Ma Mingsheng.
  • Moving the spirit to the Inner Court: the Holy Embryo moving from the Central Palace to the upper Cinnabar Field (the Muddy Pellet).
  • Yang Spirit / Holy Embryo: Liu Yiming says, “The Holy Embryo is a Dharma-image, not a form-image”; when it gathers it takes form, when it disperses it becomes breath.
  • “Inner nature is in the Muddy Pellet; existence is in the navel”: the work of completing inner nature is in the Muddy Pellet, that of completing existence at the navel.
  • Leaving the womb / sending out the spirit: the Yang Spirit going out through the Gate of Heaven (the gate at the crown).
  • Six-pointed flakes (liuchu 六出): snowflakes (six-pointed), the sign of sending out the spirit.
  • Samādhi fire, fire of the sun: names of two kinds of “fire” in inner alchemy.
  • Six penetrations: the exhaustion of outflows, the celestial eye, the celestial ear, knowledge of past lives, knowledge of others’ minds, and the penetration of spiritual realms.
  • Collection of Central Harmony: an alchemical book by Li Daochun of the Yuan dynasty.
  • True Words for Chanting the Dao: an alchemical book transmitted by Hequzi of the Qing dynasty.

Putting It into Practice

  1. This chapter is mainly for understanding the whole picture: Qiu Ling himself says that the chapter “touches mostly on religious ideals.” Readers can take it as an explanation of “where this system believes cultivation ultimately leads.”
  2. A principle of general value: “When the work of action is accomplished, one turns to non-action; yet within non-action there is still work.” In the early stages there are methods and an order of steps; later one must let go of the methods, but letting go does not mean doing nothing — within non-action there is still clear awareness.
  3. The “safety rules” for sending out the spirit can be seen as an attitude toward cultivation: the time spent going out and coming back is short, the time spent preserving and nourishing is long; brief, not long; near, not far; step by step, never skipping stages. This applies at every stage.
  4. On the “six penetrations” and similar claims: these are recorded in Daoist theory. We translate them faithfully, without promoting them, and do not recommend that readers make extraordinary abilities their goal.

Translator's Notes

  1. “True Person without rank”: originally the words of the Tang-dynasty Chan master Linji Yixuan (“On the lump of red flesh there is a True Person without rank”); the poem attributed to Yin Changsheng borrows the phrase. Yin Changsheng lived in the Eastern Han, so the poem may have been attributed to him by later authors; it is translated as given.
  2. “There is a clap of thunder — and the Gate of Heaven opens”: a symbolic description of the opening of the gate at the crown; translated literally.
  3. The Collection of Daoxiang mentioned at the end of Qiu Ling’s note is the eighth part of this book, and the two can be read together.

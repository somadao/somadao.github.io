Chapter 8 · Suckling the Infant

Chapter 8 · Suckling the Infant — three years of suckling; any feeling of delight is a demon

About This Chapter

When the fire phasing of the ten months is complete and the Holy Embryo is whole, then, just as fruit must ripen and a baby must be born, it “comes out of the womb.” Buddhists call it the Dharma Body; Daoists call it the infant. This chapter is about the “suckling” that follows: the infant is still small; it must be hidden and guarded in mystery and silence, raised with the yellow sprouts of Mother Kun, and fed with the life-giving breath of Heaven and Earth gathered together; at the same time one must guard strictly against the intrusion of the demon-bandits of the six sense objects from outside and the disturbance of the treacherous ministers of the afflictions from within. The method is to contemplate the inner opening with the eyes and listen to the inner opening with the ears. A special warning: if at this time a feeling of delight arises, one has fallen under the sway of a demon. Qiu Ling’s note explains that this is the work of “refining Spirit and returning to Emptiness (three years of suckling),” and quotes Zhao Bichen on the signs that the embryo is complete.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

前面火候已足，圣胎已圆，若果之必熟，儿之必生，经历十月，脱出其胎。释氏以此谓之法身，玄门以此谓之婴儿。盖炁穴原是长胎住息之乡，赤子安身立命之处。

The fire phasing has been completed and the Holy Embryo is whole. Just as fruit must ripen and a baby must be born, after ten months it comes out of the womb. Buddhists call this the Dharma Body; the school of mystery calls it the infant. For the Cavity of Breath was originally the place where the embryo grows and the breath rests, the place where the newborn child settles its body and establishes its life.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

此章为炼神还虚（三年乳哺）功夫，所谓“含养胞胎须十月，婴儿乳哺要千朝”是也。

This chapter is the work of refining Spirit and returning to Emptiness (three years of suckling), as in the saying: “Holding and nourishing the embryo takes ten months; suckling the infant takes a thousand mornings (about three years).”

§2
Original (Chinese)

婴儿既冥坐静室，安处道场，须藏之以玄玄，守之以默默，始借坤母黄芽以育之，继聚天地生气以哺之，此感彼应，其中自呼自吸，自阖自辟，自动自静，自由自在，若神仙逍遥于无何有之乡，如来禅定于寂灭海之场。虽到此大安乐处，仍须关元，勿令外缘六尘魔贼所侵，内结烦恼奸臣所乱，若坐若卧，常施莹净之功，时行时止，广运维持之力，方得六门不漏，一道常存，真体如如，丹基永固。朝夕如此护持，如此保固，如龙之养珠，如鹤之抱卵，而不敢顷刻之偶忘，方谓“真人潜深渊，浮游守规中”也。

Now that the infant sits in obscurity in the quiet room, settled in the place of practice, it must be hidden in mystery upon mystery and guarded in silence. First, borrow the yellow sprouts of Mother Kun to raise it; then gather the life-giving breath of Heaven and Earth to suckle it. This side stirs and that side responds; within, it breathes in and out by itself, opens and closes by itself, moves and rests by itself, free and at ease, like an immortal roaming in “the land of nothing whatever,” like the Tathāgata in meditative absorption in the sea of quiescence. Although one has reached this place of great peace and joy, one must still keep the original breath sealed and not let it be invaded by the demon-bandits of external conditions and the six sense objects, nor disturbed by the treacherous ministers of the afflictions knotted within. Whether sitting or lying, constantly do the work of making body and mind clear and pure; whether moving or still, widely apply the strength of maintaining and guarding. Only then do the six gates not leak, the one Way always remains, the true substance is unmoved in its suchness, and the foundation of the elixir is forever firm. Morning and evening, protect it like this, secure it like this, as a dragon nourishes its pearl, as a crane broods its egg, never daring to forget it for a moment — only then can it be called “the Perfected sinks into the deep abyss, floating and roaming, guarding the compass center.”

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

其法是神归大定，一毫不染，开天门以采先天，闭地户以守坤室，刻刻提防外为尘缘所侵，内贼所乱，心心谨笃，三年婴儿志成，自得升超天谷（泥丸），即由中宫超入上田。

The method is this: the spirit returns to great absorption, not tainted in the slightest; open the Gate of Heaven to collect Prior Heaven, close the Door of Earth to guard the chamber of Kun (the lower Cinnabar Field); at every moment be on guard against invasion by worldly ties from outside and disturbance by the bandits within; let every thought be careful and earnest. After three years, the infant’s resolve is accomplished, and it can naturally rise up to the Celestial Valley (the Muddy Pellet), that is, rise from the Central Palace into the upper field.

§3
Original (Chinese)

其法以眼观内窍，以耳听内窍，潜藏飞跃，总是一心，则外无声色臭味之牵，内无意必固我之累，方寸虚明，万缘澄寂，而我本来之赤子，遂怡怡然安处其中矣！老子云：“外其身而身修，忘其形而形存。”如心空无碍，则神愈炼而愈灵，身空无碍，则神愈炼而愈精，炼到形与神而相涵，身与心而为一，才是形神俱妙与道合真者。古仙云，此际婴儿渐露其形，与人无异，愈要含华隐曜，镇静心田者。若起欢心即为著魔。婴儿既长，自然脱窍，时而上升乾顶，时而出升虚际，时而顿超三界外，不在五行中。修道必经之境，古哲处之，惟循清虚湛寂焉，是为潜养圣婴之至道。

The method is to contemplate the inner opening with the eyes and listen to the inner opening with the ears; whether hiding deep or soaring high, it is always one mind. Then outside there is no pull of sounds, sights, smells and tastes, and inside there is no burden of conjecture, dogmatism, obstinacy and egoism. The Square Inch is empty and bright, all ties are clear and still, and my original newborn child rests there contentedly! Laozi says: “Put the body outside, and the body is cultivated; forget the form, and the form is preserved.” If the mind is empty and unobstructed, the spirit grows more numinous the more it is refined; if the body is empty and unobstructed, the spirit grows more refined the more it is refined. Only when refining has reached the point where form and spirit contain each other and body and mind become one is it “form and spirit both wondrous, united with the Dao in reality.” The ancient immortals said: at this time the infant gradually reveals its form, no different from a person; all the more must one hold in its radiance, conceal its brilliance, and keep the field of the mind calm. If a feeling of delight arises, one has fallen under the sway of a demon. When the infant has grown, it naturally leaves the opening: sometimes it rises to the summit of Qian, sometimes it goes out and rises to the edge of the void, sometimes in an instant it passes beyond the Three Realms and is no longer within the Five Phases. This is a state that cultivation of the Dao must pass through. When the ancient sages were in this state, they simply followed clear emptiness and limpid stillness — this is the highest way of secretly nourishing the holy infant.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

此段功夫须得真空炼形。此术是窃无涯之元炁，续我有限之形躯。即每于凌晨太阳未升之时，凝神静坐，虚以待之，自然太虚中有一点如露如电之阳勃勃然入玄关上泥丸，化为甘霖而降于身内，我即鼓动呼吸以应之，使其驱逐三关九窍之邪，扫荡五脏六腑之垢，焚身炼质，煅滓消霾，使我变为纯阳之体。

千峰老人赵避尘指出：“淫根自缩，光射目前，龙吟虎啸于顶，虚室生白，自知舍利子足。”他说，祖窍性光与脐下命门命光二光合一，由目至脐一路皆是电光，才算胎圆结果。眼前的白月光内有金光，此是养在中宫的道胎上迁泥丸上田之景到，速将此金光入于性内养之。“二光合一，唯阳神寂照于上丹田，相与混融，化为一虚空之大境，斯为存养乏全体，乃为乳哺之首务也。”

This stage of the work requires refining the form through true emptiness. This art steals the boundless original breath to sustain my limited bodily form. Every day, in the early morning before the sun has risen, concentrate the spirit and sit quietly, waiting in emptiness. Naturally, from the Great Void, a point of yang like dew, like lightning, vigorously enters the Mysterious Pass, rises to the Muddy Pellet, turns into a sweet rain and descends into the body. I then set the breathing in motion to respond to it, letting it drive out the pathogens of the three passes and nine openings and sweep away the filth of the five viscera and six bowels, burning the body and refining its substance, smelting away the dregs and dispersing the gloom, so that I am transformed into a body of pure yang.

The Old Man of a Thousand Peaks, Zhao Bichen, points out: “When the root of desire retracts of itself, light shines before the eyes, there is a dragon’s chant and a tiger’s roar at the crown, and the empty chamber produces whiteness, one knows oneself that the śarīra is sufficient.” He says that only when the light of inner nature in the ancestral opening and the light of existence at the Gate of Life below the navel unite into one, and the whole way from the eyes to the navel is lightning light, is the embryo complete and the fruit borne. Within the white moonlight before the eyes there is golden light; this is the sign that the Dao embryo nourished in the Central Palace is about to move up to the Muddy Pellet, the upper field; quickly take this golden light into “inner nature” to nourish it. “When the two lights have united into one, let only the Yang Spirit shine silently in the upper Cinnabar Field, the two blending into each other and transforming into a great realm of empty space — this is the whole of preserving and nourishing, and the first task of suckling.”

Key Terms

  • Refining Spirit and returning to Emptiness / three years of suckling: the third great stage of inner alchemy; see the three transmutations.
  • Dharma Body (Buddhist) / infant (Daoist): see Holy Embryo.
  • Yellow sprouts of Mother Kun: Kun is the mother, the earth; yellow sprouts are a metaphor for the true breath first born in the earth.
  • Six sense objects (liuchen 六尘): form, sound, smell, taste, touch and mental objects.
  • Keeping the original breath sealed (guan yuan 关元): here used as a verb, meaning to keep the passes closed and seal the original breath.
  • The six gates not leaking: the gates of the six roots do not leak outward.
  • Conjecture, dogmatism, obstinacy and egoism: from the Analects, “The Master was free of four things: conjecture, dogmatism, obstinacy and egoism.”
  • Square Inch: the mind.
  • Holding in its radiance and concealing its brilliance: keeping the light hidden and not showing it.
  • Three Realms, Five Phases: in general, everything in the world.
  • Refining the form through true emptiness: letting the body be refined in a state like empty space.
  • Light of inner nature in the ancestral opening, light of existence at the Gate of Life: the two lights spoken of by Zhao Bichen; see light of Nature.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The method at this stage is still the most basic one: contemplating the inner opening with the eyes and listening to the inner opening with the ears — that is, the “inner seeing and inner listening” of Part One. The higher the work, the simpler the method.
  2. “If a feeling of delight arises, one has fallen under the sway of a demon”: this applies to every stage. If during quiet sitting any wonderful scene or sensation appears and a feeling of delight — “I’ve done it!” — arises, one has gone astray.
  3. Two kinds of bandits to guard against: the “demon-bandits of the six sense objects” outside (the temptations of sound and sight), and the “treacherous ministers of the afflictions” inside (emotions and stray thoughts).
  4. Sitting in the early morning: Qiu Ling’s note mentions “every day, in the early morning before the sun has risen, concentrate the spirit and sit quietly, waiting in emptiness.” This agrees with Part One, Chapter 7, “in the early morning, if you can put aside all ties and sit quietly for one or two double-hours, that is best”; it is a time for practice recommended by all schools.

Translator's Notes

  1. “One must still keep the original breath sealed” (guan yuan 关元): here guanyuan is not the acupuncture point of that name but means “sealing in the original breath”; the translation follows this sense.
  2. “Laozi says: ‘Put the body outside, and the body is cultivated’”: the received text of the Laozi, chapter 7, reads “put the body outside, and the body is preserved”; the quotation here differs slightly and is reproduced as in the base text.
  3. “This is the whole of preserving and nourishing”: in the last sentence of Qiu Ling’s note the base text has 乏, an error for 之; it is silently corrected.

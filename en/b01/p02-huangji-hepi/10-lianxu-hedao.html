Chapter 10 · Refining Emptiness and Uniting with the Dao

Chapter 10 · Refining Emptiness and Uniting with the Dao — one opening for the three teachings

About This Chapter

The last chapter deals with the final stage of cultivation, “refining Emptiness and uniting with the Dao.” Its opening sentence is important: Buddhists call it the “Chan barrier,” Daoists the “Mysterious Opening,” Confucians the “Yellow Center” — the three teachings are speaking of the same opening, and once it is seen through, the treasuries of all three are opened. The method is still to cook and refine the Yang Spirit again and again, send it back to the Celestial Valley, and then withdraw it into what is hidden, as a dragon nourishes the pearl under its chin, as a crane broods the egg in its nest; then again and again to “gather it back into the ancestral opening,” “dissolve it back into the ancestral opening,” in quiescence and stillness, stillness and quiescence, the spiritual light growing vaster each time. Qiu Ling states plainly in his note that this chapter “already belongs to religious ideal or fantasy,” and suspects that the stage of “refining Emptiness and uniting with the Dao” was added by later writers. He also points out that the ancestral opening into which the spirit “withdraws” here is not the ancestral opening between the eyes, but the one behind the heart and in front of the Spine-Straddling Pass — that is, the Central Yellow.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

禅关一窍，息心体之，一旦参透，打开三家宝藏，消释万法千门。还丹至理，豁然贯通。盖释曰禅关，道曰玄窍，儒曰黄中，事之事之，方能炼虚合道，乃为圣谛第一义，即释氏最上一乘之法也。

The single opening of the Chan barrier must be experienced with a stilled mind. Once it is seen through, the treasuries of the three schools are opened, and the thousand gates and ten thousand dharmas are dissolved; the ultimate principle of the Returned Elixir suddenly becomes clear throughout. Buddhists call it the “Chan barrier,” Daoists the “Mysterious Opening,” Confucians the “Yellow Center.” Only by devoting oneself to it wholeheartedly can one refine Emptiness and unite with the Dao; this is “the first meaning of the holy truths,” the Buddhists’ method of the highest vehicle.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

此章所述，已属宗教理想或幻想。拙说道教气功本由炼精化炁、炼炁化神到炼神还虚而止，原无炼虚合道一段，所以古人说：“养得金丹圆似月，未免有圆还有缺，何如炼个太阳红，三界十方俱洞彻。”又说：“阅尽丹书万万篇，末后一句无人说。”故炼虚合道一着，疑为后人所加。

What this chapter describes already belongs to religious ideal or fantasy. It is said that Daoist qigong originally went from refining Essence into Breath and refining Breath into Spirit to refining Spirit and returning to Emptiness, and stopped there; there was originally no stage of refining Emptiness and uniting with the Dao. That is why the ancients said: “Nourish the Golden Elixir round as the moon, and it cannot avoid being now full, now waning; better to refine a red sun, which shines through the three realms and the ten directions.” And: “Read through ten thousand alchemical books, and no one tells the very last line.” So the step of refining Emptiness and uniting with the Dao may have been added by later writers.

§2
Original (Chinese)

此法无他，只是复炼阳神，以还我毗卢性海，以烹以炼，浊尽清纯，送归天谷，又将天谷之神退藏于密，如龙养颔下之珠，似鹤抱巢中之卵，即内即外，即气即心，凝成一粒，谨谨护持，无出无入，虚寂之极，变化之至，则其所谓造化者，自然而复性命，自然而复空虚。变不尽变，化不尽化，此通灵变化之至神者也。故神百炼而愈灵，金百炼而愈精，炼之又炼，则炉火焰消，虚空现若微尘，尘尘蕴具万顷冰壶世界，少焉神光满穴，阳焰腾空，自内窍达于外窍，窍窍皆大神光也，彻内彻外，透顶透足，在在皆大神光也。

There is no other method than this: refine the Yang Spirit again, so as to return to my Vairocana ocean of nature — my originally pure and vast nature. Cook it and refine it until the turbid is exhausted and it is clear and pure; send it back to the Celestial Valley; then withdraw the spirit of the Celestial Valley into what is hidden, as a dragon nourishes the pearl under its chin, as a crane broods the egg in its nest. Inside is outside, breath is mind; congeal it into one grain and guard it carefully, neither going out nor coming in, emptiness and stillness at their extreme, transformation at its utmost. Then what is called the work of creation naturally restores inner nature and existence, naturally restores emptiness. Changing without exhausting change, transforming without exhausting transformation — this is the most spiritual of numinous transformations. So the spirit becomes more numinous the more it is refined, as gold becomes purer the more it is refined. Refine and refine again, and the flames in the furnace die away; empty space appears like fine dust, and every speck of dust contains a world like ten thousand acres of ice in a jade vessel. Soon the spiritual light fills the cavity, yang flames rise into the sky, from the inner opening to the outer openings — every opening is great spiritual light; through and through, inside and out, from the crown to the feet, everywhere is great spiritual light.

§3
Original (Chinese)

再又摄归祖窍之中，一尘不染，寂灭而静定，静定而寂灭。静定既久，则红光如奔云发电，从中窍而贯于上窍，则更无论大小之窍，而神光动耀，照彻十方。

Then gather it back again into the ancestral opening, untainted by a single speck of dust, in quiescence and stillness, stillness and quiescence. When the stillness has lasted long, red light like racing clouds and flashing lightning passes from the middle opening through to the upper opening; then, no matter whether the openings are large or small, the spiritual light flickers and shines, illuminating the ten directions.

§4
Original (Chinese)

如是更加敛摄，消归祖窍之中。一尘不染，寂灭而静定，静定而寂灭。静定既久，则六龙之变化已全，而神更变为舍利之光，如赫赫日轮，从祖窍之内一涌而出，化为万万毫光。

Like this, gather it in and hold it still more, dissolving it back into the ancestral opening, untainted by a single speck of dust, in quiescence and stillness, stillness and quiescence. When the stillness has lasted long, the transformations of the six dragons are complete, and the spirit changes further into the light of the śarīra, which, like the blazing disc of the sun, surges out all at once from within the ancestral opening and turns into myriads of rays of light.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2–§4
Original note (Chinese)

炼虚合道，即炼到形神俱妙；与道合真。形指身，亦指精炁。神指阳神。还虚以后达到形神两忘境界，方是全真。所以，脱胎以后，要做到与虚空同体，必须将可以离形的阳神，复归于形骸，收入性海天谷内院，复将阳神退藏祖窍（并非两眼之间的祖窍，而是在心后夹脊关前），静定而寂灭，不出不入，忘人忘我，则神光从中窍贯于上窍，而窍窍光明。这便是道教所主张的形神一致而延长生命、留形驻世、肉身冲举等理想幻想，也就是所谓九年面壁最后还虚功法的主要内容。

Refining Emptiness and uniting with the Dao means refining until “form and spirit are both wondrous, united with the Dao in reality.” “Form” means the body, and also essence and breath; “spirit” means the Yang Spirit. Only when, after returning to Emptiness, one reaches the state in which both form and spirit are forgotten, is one “Complete in Reality.” Therefore, after leaving the womb, in order to become one body with empty space, one must bring the Yang Spirit, which can leave the form, back into the bodily frame, and gather it into the Inner Court of the Celestial Valley, the ocean of nature; then withdraw the Yang Spirit into the ancestral opening — not the ancestral opening between the eyes, but the one behind the heart and in front of the Spine-Straddling Pass — in stillness and quiescence, neither going out nor coming in, forgetting others and forgetting oneself. Then the spiritual light passes from the middle opening through to the upper opening, and every opening is bright. This is the ideal and fantasy advocated by Daoism of the unity of form and spirit and the prolonging of life, of keeping the form and remaining in the world, of the physical body ascending — and it is the main content of the final method of returning to Emptiness, the so-called “nine years facing the wall.”

Key Terms

  • Chan barrier / Mysterious Opening / Yellow Center: the Buddhist, Daoist and Confucian names for the same opening.
  • The first meaning of the holy truths: a Buddhist expression for the highest truth.
  • The highest vehicle: the highest Buddhist teaching.
  • Vairocana ocean of nature: the ocean of nature of Vairocana (the Dharma-body Buddha), a metaphor for one’s originally pure and vast nature.
  • Withdrawing into what is hidden: from the “Appended Statements” of the Book of Changes; see Qiu Ling’s note to Chapter 1.
  • Ice in a jade vessel (binghu 冰壶): a jade vessel holding ice, a metaphor for crystal clarity.
  • Ancestral opening: in this chapter, Qiu Ling’s note specifies that the ancestral opening is behind the heart and in front of the Spine-Straddling Pass (the Central Yellow), not the ancestral opening between the eyes (the Root of the Mountain).
  • Middle opening, upper opening: the middle opening is the Central Yellow, the upper opening the Muddy Pellet.
  • Six dragons: in the Book of Changes all six lines of the hexagram Qian are dragons; a metaphor for the six transformations of yang.
  • Light of the śarīra: see Chapter 7.
  • The three transmutations: refining Essence into Breath, refining Breath into Spirit, refining Spirit and returning to Emptiness (plus, in this chapter, refining Emptiness and uniting with the Dao).
  • Nine years facing the wall: the common name for the final stage of inner alchemy, borrowed from the story of Bodhidharma sitting facing a wall for nine years.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The most valuable sentence: “Buddhists call it the Chan barrier, Daoists the Mysterious Opening, Confucians the Yellow Center” — the three teachings are speaking of the same “center.” This is exactly why this site keeps a glossary that brings different names together.
  2. There are two ancestral openings: when you meet the “ancestral opening” in an alchemical book, look at the context: sometimes it means the Root of the Mountain between the eyes (the place to begin), sometimes the Central Yellow behind the heart and in front of the Spine-Straddling Pass (as in this chapter).
  3. The structure of the whole part is now complete: laying the foundation (Chapters 1–3) → refining Essence into Breath (Chapters 4–6) → refining Breath into Spirit (Chapter 7) → refining Spirit and returning to Emptiness (Chapters 8–9) → refining Emptiness and uniting with the Dao (Chapter 10). For modern learners, what truly needs repeated work is the first three chapters.
  4. How to take the content of this chapter: Qiu Ling says plainly that it is “religious ideal or fantasy.” We translate it faithfully, as a document for understanding the full picture of this system.

Translator's Notes

  1. “It is said that Daoist qigong originally …”: the base text has characters resembling 拙说 (“my humble opinion”), which do not fit the sense; they are probably an error for 据说 (“it is said”), and the translation follows that reading.
  2. “Devoting oneself to it wholeheartedly” (shi zhi shi zhi 事之事之): working at this matter again and again, with single-minded devotion; the translation follows this sense.
  3. The two poems “Nourish the Golden Elixir round as the moon …” and “Read through ten thousand alchemical books …” are introduced in Qiu Ling’s note only with “the ancients said”; their sources are not known.

Chapter 2 · Concentrating the Spirit and Entering the Cavity

Chapter 2 · Concentrating the Spirit and Entering the Cavity — sending the spirit back to the lower Cinnabar Field

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

Chapter 1 concentrated the spirit in the Spine-Straddling Pass (the Central Yellow). This chapter goes a step further: the spirit concentrated there “sinks into the Residence of Life” — it is sent down to the lower Cinnabar Field, one cun two fen behind the navel (which this book calls the Celestial Heart, the Cavity of Breath, the Furnace of Spirit, the Furnace of Kun), and sealed there. The chapter first distinguishes three kinds of mind: the human mind is the deluded mind, the Dao mind is the mind that illuminates, and the Celestial Heart is the Cavity of Breath; cultivation means “illuminating the Celestial Heart with the Dao mind.” When the work is accomplished, only a trace of breath remains in the nose, so faint that it seems absent, and the breathing takes place entirely within the opening of the Cinnabar Field — this is “the sign of having entered the cavity.” Qiu Ling points out that this chapter and the section “Hibernating in the Cavity of Breath, All Wonders Return to the Root” of the Principles of Inner Nature and Existence are largely the same, but the Principles conceals the location of the two openings, the Central Yellow and the Celestial Heart; only by comparing the two books does it suddenly become clear.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

太上云：“吾从无量劫来，观心得道，乃至虚无”。夫观心者，非观肉团之血心。若观此心，则有血热火旺之患，不可不慎也。

The Most High said: “Through countless kalpas I attained the Dao by contemplating the mind, and so reached emptiness and non-being.” Contemplating the mind does not mean contemplating the heart of flesh and blood. If you contemplate that heart, you will suffer from heated blood and flaring fire; you must be careful about this.

§2
Original (Chinese)

人有三心，曰：人心、道心、天心。人心者妄心也；道心者照心也；天心者元关祖窍，气穴是也。太上观心者，以道心而普照天心也。

A person has three minds: the human mind, the Dao mind and the Celestial Heart. The human mind is the deluded mind; the Dao mind is the illuminating mind; the Celestial Heart is the ancestral opening of the Original Pass, that is, the Cavity of Breath. When the Most High speaks of contemplating the mind, he means using the Dao mind to illuminate the Celestial Heart everywhere.

§3
Original (Chinese)

又曰：入窍观心之法，凝神，定息，清虚自然，六根大定，百脉平和，将向来夹脊双关所凝，潜入命府，谓送归大冶牢封固。命门一窍，即脐后一寸二分，天心是也。一名神炉，又名坤炉。而息之起，息之止，在此一穴。按自气穴起息，状如炉烟，随吾呼吸，仍不外乎黄庭为之主张者，盖有元神在也。调处之法，乃以道心而照天心，则此灵谷之中气机虽繁，有神以主，亦仍如如不动，本体常存，神与气合，紧紧不离，是名外炼，而不失夫胎息。盖如凝神于炁穴，时时收视返听，照顾不已，则此气穴亦自寂寂惺惺，永无昏沉，而睡魔自遣，且能应抽应添，运用自如矣！《楞严经》云：“一根既返元，六根自解脱。”盖无六根，则无六识，无六识，则无轮迴种子，既无种子，则我一点真心，独立无倚，空空荡荡，光光净净，斯万劫而常存也。

It also says: the method of entering the cavity and contemplating the mind is this. Concentrate the spirit, settle the breath, be clear, empty and natural; let the six roots be in great stillness and all the vessels at peace. Then let what you have been concentrating in the Spine-Straddling Pass sink into the Residence of Life; this is called “sending it back to the great smelter and sealing it firmly.” The single opening of the Gate of Life is one cun two fen behind the navel; it is the Celestial Heart, also called the Furnace of Spirit or the Furnace of Kun. The rising of the breath and the stopping of the breath both take place in this one cavity. Note: the breath that rises from the Cavity of Breath looks like smoke rising from a furnace and follows my breathing; yet it is still the Yellow Court that is its master, because the Original Spirit is there. The way to regulate it is to illuminate the Celestial Heart with the Dao mind. Then, although the movements of breath in this Numinous Valley are many, the spirit is their master, and they remain unmoved in their suchness, the original substance always present; spirit and breath are joined, tightly and inseparably. This is called “outer refining,” and the embryonic breathing is not lost. If you concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, constantly withdraw your sight and turn your hearing inward, and watch over it without stopping, this Cavity of Breath will itself become still yet awake, forever free of torpor; the demon of sleep will leave of its own accord, and you will be able to withdraw and add as needed, using it freely! The Śūraṅgama Sūtra says: “When one root has returned to the source, all six roots are freed.” For without the six roots there are no six consciousnesses; without the six consciousnesses there are no seeds of rebirth; and without seeds, my single point of true mind stands alone without support, empty and open, bright and pure, and so it lasts through ten thousand kalpas.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

此章内容与《性命圭旨》一书第三节口诀《蛰藏气穴，众妙归根》大同小异，惟《性命圭旨》对中黄和天心二窍具体位置隐而不言，秘而不宣，令学者读后无从学处，两书比对，才使人豁然而悟。

凝神入窍，即凝神入气穴，亦即将在夹脊双关（中黄）所凝之神，送归天心（土釜）封固，天心即气穴也。此乃以道心而照天心，即所谓“当灭动心，莫灭照心”。《性命圭旨》称天心为窍中之窍，为中央黄晕之间的真人呼吸处，为阴阳之源、神气之宅、胎息之根、呼吸之祖，凝神入此窍中应“如龟之藏，如蛇之蛰，如蚌之含光，如蟾之纳息，绵绵续续，勿忘勿助，若存而非存，若无而非无”，则天地元始祖气得以相通而入，神息归根，性命合一，而大药孕于其中矣！

The content of this chapter is largely the same as the third formula of the Principles of Inner Nature and Existence, “Hibernating in the Cavity of Breath, All Wonders Return to the Root.” But the Principles conceals the exact location of the two openings, the Central Yellow and the Celestial Heart, and keeps it secret, so that students who read it have nowhere to begin; only when the two books are compared does it suddenly become clear.

Concentrating the spirit and entering the cavity is concentrating the spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath: the spirit concentrated in the Spine-Straddling Pass (the Central Yellow) is sent back to the Celestial Heart (the earthen crucible) and sealed there; the Celestial Heart is the Cavity of Breath. This is illuminating the Celestial Heart with the Dao mind, which is what is meant by “extinguish the moving mind, but do not extinguish the illuminating mind.” The Principles of Inner Nature and Existence calls the Celestial Heart “the opening within the opening,” the place where the Perfected breathes amid the yellow halo in the center, the source of yin and yang, the dwelling of spirit and breath, the root of embryonic breathing, the ancestor of respiration. When concentrating the spirit and entering this opening, one should be “like a tortoise withdrawing, like a snake hibernating, like a mussel holding its light, like a toad taking in breath; continuous and unbroken, neither forgetting nor forcing, as if present yet not present, as if absent yet not absent.” Then the primordial ancestral breath of Heaven and Earth can communicate and enter, spirit and breath return to the root, inner nature and existence unite as one, and the Great Medicine is conceived within!

§4
Original (Chinese)

每见专务顶门之性为宗者，是不知命也；专务坤炉修命为宗者，是不知性也。纯阳曰：“修命不修性，此是修行第一病，只修祖性不修丹，万劫阴灵难入圣。”若此凝神入窍之法，乃性命双修之诀。盖得中央黄晕所结之神以宰之耳！

I often see people who make the cultivation of inner nature at the crown of the head their sole aim — they do not understand existence; and others who make the cultivation of existence in the Furnace of Kun (the lower Cinnabar Field) their sole aim — they do not understand inner nature. Lü Chunyang said: “To cultivate existence and not inner nature is the first sickness of practice; to cultivate only the ancestral nature and not the elixir, one remains a yin spirit for ten thousand kalpas and can hardly enter sagehood.” A method like this one, concentrating the spirit and entering the cavity, is the formula for the dual cultivation of inner nature and existence, because it obtains the spirit congealed in the yellow halo of the center to be its master!

§5
Original (Chinese)

人若识于此处而迎吾一点元神，入于元始祖窍天心气穴之中，绵绵续续，勿助勿忘，引而亲之，一若升于无何有之乡，则少焉呼吸相含，神气相抱，结为丹母，镇在下田。待时至时，则摄吾身先天灵物，上引三才真一，油然下入，合我身中铅汞，即成无上英华，融而化之，有如北辰居所，众星皆拱之验，是皆元神潜入气穴所致。故尔诸气归根，万神听令。然而古哲谓之黄叶，非真金也。必须久久行之，先天性命真正合一，如汞投铅而相制伏，而大丹真孕其中矣。盖以此段功夫，神既入窍，则呼吸一在窍内，而吾鼻中呼吸只有一点而微若无，方为入窍之验。验验不失，乃得真金焉。

If one recognizes this place and welcomes my single point of Original Spirit into the primordial ancestral opening, the Cavity of Breath of the Celestial Heart, continuously and unbrokenly, neither forcing nor forgetting, drawing it in and staying close to it, as if rising into “the land of nothing whatever,” then before long exhalation and inhalation contain each other, spirit and breath embrace each other and form the “mother of the elixir,” which settles in the lower field. When the time comes, it draws in the numinous substance of Prior Heaven within my body and leads down from above the True One of the Three Powers (Heaven, Earth and Humanity), which descends abundantly and joins with the Lead and Mercury within my body. At once they become the supreme flower and essence, melting and transforming, with the effect described as “the Pole Star stays in its place and all the stars turn around it” — all of this is brought about by the Original Spirit sinking into the Cavity of Breath. Thus all the breaths return to the root and ten thousand spirits obey the command. Yet the ancient sages called this “yellow leaves,” not real gold (like yellow leaves shown to a crying child as if they were gold: only a provisional symbol of a stage). One must practice it for a long, long time, until the inner nature and existence of Prior Heaven are truly united as one, like mercury cast into lead and each subduing the other; only then is the Great Elixir truly conceived within. For in this stage of the work, once the spirit has entered the cavity, the breathing takes place entirely within the cavity, and the breathing in my nose is only a trace, so faint it seems absent — that is the sign of having entered the cavity. Only when this sign is never lost does one obtain real gold.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4–§5
Original note (Chinese)

本章功夫的证验是胎息的出现。盖我之元神（即真意）凝于中黄炁穴时，天心气穴（下丹田）早受影响，即自寂寂惺惺，一旦神自然而然入于天心一窍，则呼吸之天地正气即可与我身真气，我身之之铅、汞（精、神）融化成无上英华，内通而外亦通，于是呼吸相含，神气相抱，最后呼吸移入天心窍内，形成了腹式呼吸，而我鼻中呼吸则愈来愈微弱而近于无，此为内呼吸，即胎息也。这是性命合一、神气相交、铅汞相投的进一步表现。性命即神气，神气即铅汞，也就是神已入窍的证验。

The proof of this chapter’s work is the appearance of embryonic breathing. When my Original Spirit (that is, the True Intention) is concentrated in the opening of the Central Yellow, the Cavity of Breath of the Celestial Heart (the lower Cinnabar Field) has already been influenced and naturally becomes still yet awake. Once the spirit enters the opening of the Celestial Heart quite naturally, the correct breath of Heaven and Earth taken in by breathing can melt together with the true breath of my body and with the lead and mercury (essence and spirit) of my body into the supreme flower and essence; it is open within and open without. So exhalation and inhalation contain each other, spirit and breath embrace each other, and finally the breathing moves inside the opening of the Celestial Heart, forming abdominal breathing, while the breathing in my nose grows weaker and weaker until it is almost nothing. This is inner breathing, that is, embryonic breathing (True Breath). It is a further expression of the union of inner nature and existence, the intercourse of spirit and breath, the meeting of lead and mercury. Inner nature and existence are spirit and breath; spirit and breath are lead and mercury; and this is the proof that the spirit has entered the cavity.

Key Terms

  • Three minds: the human mind (the deluded mind), the Dao mind (the illuminating mind) and the Celestial Heart (the Cavity of Breath).
  • Contemplating the mind: not contemplating the heart of flesh and blood, but illuminating the Celestial Heart everywhere with the Dao mind.
  • Residence of Life / Gate of Life / Furnace of Spirit / Furnace of Kun / Numinous Valley / earthen crucible: in this chapter all refer to the lower Cinnabar Field, one cun two fen behind the navel. Note: the “Gate of Life” (mingmen 命门) of Chinese medicine usually means the area between the two kidneys, which is different.
  • Great smelter (daye 大冶): a great furnace, a metaphor for the lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Outer refining: this chapter’s term — the spirit is master of the movements of breath in the Cavity of Breath, spirit and breath tightly inseparable.
  • Embryonic breathing (taixi 胎息): the breath in the nose so faint it seems absent, the breathing taking place entirely within the opening of the Cinnabar Field; see True Breath.
  • Still yet awake (jiji xingxing 寂寂惺惺): quiet and at the same time alert; compare “alertness” in Part One, Chapter 5.
  • Six roots, six consciousnesses: eye, ear, nose, tongue, body and mind, and the cognitive functions they give rise to.
  • Mother of the elixir: the matrix of the elixir formed when spirit and breath embrace.
  • Lead and Mercury: in this chapter’s note, lead and mercury are essence and spirit, are spirit and breath, are inner nature and existence.
  • Yellow leaves: a Chan Buddhist metaphor — giving a crying child yellow leaves as if they were gold to stop its tears; an expedient, not the ultimate.
  • “Extinguish the moving mind, but do not extinguish the illuminating mind”: stop the agitated thoughts, but do not put out the mind that is aware.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Contemplating the mind is not staring at your chest: the chapter warns at the outset that contemplating the heart of flesh and blood leads to “heated blood and flaring fire” — the same warning as Qiu Ling’s note in Part One, Chapter 2, about “mistaking the cognitive mind for the mind and pressing on it, which only feeds the fire.”
  2. Which mind to use: the “illuminating mind” — clear knowing, not the “deluded mind” that thinks and controls.
  3. From the Central Yellow to the lower Cinnabar Field: Chapter 1 concentrated the spirit in the middle between chest and back (the Spine-Straddling Pass, the Central Yellow). Once that is familiar, gently “send” this concentration down to the lower Cinnabar Field, one cun two fen behind the navel, “like a tortoise withdrawing, like a snake hibernating,” as if present yet not present.
  4. Signs: the breathing in the abdomen becomes more and more evident and natural, and the breathing in the nose finer and finer, until it seems absent — this happens by itself; it is not produced by holding the breath.
  5. Inner nature and existence in balance: guarding only the crown of the head (cultivating inner nature) or only the lower abdomen (cultivating existence) is one-sided; this chapter’s method lets the spirit of the Central Yellow be master of the breath of the lower Cinnabar Field — dual cultivation of inner nature and existence.

Translator's Notes

  1. “This is called ‘sending it back to the great smelter and sealing it firmly’”: the character wei 谓 (“is called”) may also be read as wei 为 (“is, acts as”); the translation follows the reading “this is called.”
  2. “Yellow leaves”: from the Buddhist scriptures, where the Buddha stops a child’s crying with yellow leaves — a metaphor for expedient teaching. The meaning of the metaphor is added in parentheses in the translation.
  3. In the Chinese of Qiu Ling’s note, the phrase “the lead and mercury of my body” (我身之之铅、汞) contains a doubled character; it is reproduced as printed, and the translation reads it normally.

The Sixteen-Character Formula for Preserving Life

The Sixteen-Character Formula for Preserving Life — with each inhalation, lift; every breath returns to the navel; with each lift, swallow; water and fire meet

About This Chapter

The whole practice is sixteen characters: with each inhalation, lift; every breath returns to the navel; with each lift, swallow; water and fire meet. The text explains it line by line. The back of the human body is yang (the Governing vessel) and the chest is yin (the Conception vessel); the Governing vessel has three passes — the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Muddy Pellet — and the Conception vessel is divided into an upper and a lower section, both of which have the navel as their destination. The breathing is left to nature; the whole work lies in the two words “lift.” On the inhalation, with a little intention and eye-power, lift the breath from the root of the genitals and take it along the lower half of the Conception vessel into the navel; on the exhalation, with a little intention and eye-power, lift the breath into the Governing vessel, from the Caudal Funnel along the backbone up to the crown. The Muddy Pellet is the hardest to open; with long practice it opens by itself, and when it opens, “the joy is beyond words.” Once it has opened, the breath falls from the crown to the throat, is swallowed lightly and returns to the navel. When both exhalation and inhalation return to the navel and yin and yang are firmly joined, that is “water and fire meet.” When the Conception and Governing vessels are open and water and fire complete each other, every breath irrigates the whole body — from where could illness arise? The “shortcut formula” of Xuesuo Weng quoted by Qiu Ling at the end is a simplified version of this practice, and even easier to do.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

“一吸便提，气气归脐。一提便咽，水火相见”，乃保命十六字诀。以其珍贵，故名十六锭金。是言一吸一呼，通任督二脉而归于脐也。人身负阳抱阴，督脉在背属阳，任脉在胸属阴。督有三关，曰：尾闾；曰：夹脊；曰：泥丸。任有二截：下自阴根至脐；上自顶下至脐。人之呼吸，任乎自然，功夫全在两提字。

“With each inhalation, lift; every breath returns to the navel. With each lift, swallow; water and fire meet.” This is the sixteen-character formula for preserving life. Because it is so precious, it is called the “Sixteen Gold Ingots.” It means that each inhalation and exhalation opens the Conception and Governing vessels and returns to the navel. The human body carries yang on its back and embraces yin in front: the Governing vessel is in the back and belongs to yang; the Conception vessel is in the chest and abdomen and belongs to yin. The Governing vessel has three passes: the first is the Caudal Funnel, the second the Spine-Straddling Pass, the third the Muddy Pellet. The Conception vessel has two sections: the lower runs from the root of the genitals to the navel; the upper runs down from the crown to the navel. A person’s breathing is left to nature; the whole work lies in the two words “lift.”

§2
Original (Chinese)

静中略用意与目力为提。然太著意则反害之矣！“一吸”入腹，略用意与目力，从阴根提起，纳之于脐。“便提”者，提一吸之气，通任脉下半截而纳于脐。所谓“一吸便提，气气归脐”也。

In stillness, using a little intention and eye-power is “lifting.” But too much deliberate intention does harm! “With each inhalation”: as the breath is drawn into the belly, with a little intention and eye-power, lift it from the root of the genitals and take it into the navel. “Lift” means lifting the breath of this one inhalation, passing through the lower half of the Conception vessel and taking it into the navel. This is what is meant by “with each inhalation, lift; every breath returns to the navel.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

“一提”即一呼，于一呼之中，略用意与目力，提入督脉，从尾闾通背骨，直至顶门。常人不通督脉，故尾闾亦谓之关。提入督脉，则尾闾便通腰间，夹脊亦一关也。尾闾、夹脊皆易通，而顶门一关最难通，故谓之泥丸。用功久之，则泥丸自通。通时乐不可言，昔人比之天上醍醐，又比之交媾。

“With each lift” means with each exhalation: during an exhalation, with a little intention and eye-power, lift the breath into the Governing vessel, from the Caudal Funnel through the backbone straight to the gate at the crown. In ordinary people the Governing vessel is not open, so the Caudal Funnel too is called a “pass.” Once the breath is lifted into the Governing vessel, the Caudal Funnel opens through to the waist; the Spine-Straddling Pass is also a pass. The Caudal Funnel and the Spine-Straddling Pass are both easy to open, but the pass at the crown is the hardest of all, so it is called the “Muddy Pellet.” With long practice, the Muddy Pellet opens by itself. When it opens, the joy is beyond words; people of old likened it to heavenly ghee, and also to sexual union.

§4
Original (Chinese)

泥丸既通，则咽入任脉之上半截，而纳于脐。“便咽”之咽，非咽精，亦非咽气。气从顶门落下喉间，略一纳便归脐矣。泥丸未通，全在两提字用功，“便咽”，则通关以后事也。一阴一阳之谓道，一呼一吸之谓息。呼吸皆归于脐，阴阳固济，所谓“水火相见”也。脐内一寸三分为丹田，“归脐”，则归丹田矣！未生时胎息于此，道家炼丹亦于此，实为气之橐籥，一身之太极也。任督脉通，水火相济，每一呼吸，周身灌输，病何自而生哉？

Once the Muddy Pellet has opened, it is swallowed into the upper half of the Conception vessel and taken into the navel. The “swallow” of “with each lift, swallow” is neither swallowing essence nor swallowing air: the breath falls from the gate at the crown to the throat, is taken in lightly, and returns to the navel. Before the Muddy Pellet opens, all the work is in the two words “lift”; “swallow” comes after the passes have opened. One yin and one yang is called the Dao; one exhalation and one inhalation is called a breath. When both exhalation and inhalation return to the navel and yin and yang are firmly joined, that is what is called “water and fire meet.” One cun three fen inward from the navel is the Cinnabar Field; “returning to the navel” is returning to the Cinnabar Field! Before birth, embryonic breathing takes place here; the Daoists refine the elixir here too. It is truly the bellows of the breath, the Great Ultimate of the whole body. When the Conception and Governing vessels are open and water and fire complete each other, every breath irrigates the whole body — from where could illness arise?

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

督脉有三关：尾闾在尻尾向上数第三节下；夹脊在背脊二十四节上下之正中，即与两乳之间的膻中穴相对处；泥丸在头顶百会穴内，百会在两耳廓尖连线之中点。以上穴位位置按《寥阳殿问答编》一书所定，与通常所称督脉三关为尾闾、夹脊，玉枕稍有不同。

行本功时仍须入静，坐、立均可，注意要自然放松，不要太执著。第一个提字，志在先通任脉，第二个提字，志在后通督脉，盖督脉较任脉难通。泥丸一通，一咽便方有津液降下，此津液香似醍醐，甜如甘露，即所谓醍醐灌顶、坎离交媾，然后守胎息以终。

The Governing vessel has three passes: the Caudal Funnel is below the third segment counting up from the tailbone; the Spine-Straddling Pass is in the exact middle of the twenty-four vertebrae of the back, opposite the Chest Center point between the nipples; the Muddy Pellet is inside the Hundred Meetings point at the crown, which is at the midpoint of the line joining the tips of the two ears. These locations follow those set out in the Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall, and differ slightly from the usual three passes of the Governing vessel (the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Jade Pillow).

When practicing this method one must still enter stillness; it can be done sitting or standing. Take care to relax naturally and not to cling too much. The first “lift” aims at opening the Conception vessel first; the second “lift” aims at opening the Governing vessel afterward, because the Governing vessel is harder to open than the Conception vessel. Once the Muddy Pellet opens, only then, with a swallow, does saliva descend, fragrant as ghee and sweet as dew — this is what is called “ghee poured on the crown,” the conjunction of Kan and Li. Then guard the embryonic breathing to the end.

§5Old commentary · Xuesuo Weng's note
Original (Chinese)

但事养生，别有捷诀。盖以放下万缘为养阴，聚火开关为助阳。诀则以吸吸自尾闾，以呼呼自泥丸。方其吸升，统背内外，阳气雍雍而普升也。方其呼降，统额而胸内外，油然下胸腹腰际，内而五脏六腑，畅适无赛。以意渐收，统归下极，神注二十四息，总以造至若存若忘，悠然住手。如是日行无间，不惟却病，亦可延年也。

(Xuesuo Weng’s note:) If one’s only concern is nourishing life, there is another, shortcut formula. Letting go of all ties is nourishing yin; gathering the fire and opening the passes is assisting yang. The formula is: inhale from the Caudal Funnel; exhale from the Muddy Pellet. As the inhalation rises, the whole back, inside and out, is filled with gentle yang breath rising everywhere. As the exhalation descends, from the forehead down through the chest, inside and out, it flows down easily to the chest, belly and waist, and within to the five viscera and six bowels, with incomparable comfort. Then gradually gather it in with the intention and bring it all back to the Lower Pole (the lower Cinnabar Field); let the spirit dwell there for twenty-four breaths, always until it is as if present and as if forgotten, and then calmly stop. If one practices like this every day without interruption, it not only dispels illness but can also prolong life.

Qiu Ling's noteon §5
Original note (Chinese)

此篇后原有雪蓑翁注文曰：（注文见上。）按此捷诀，亦十六锭金功所简化，更加易行。

Originally this section was followed by a note by Xuesuo Weng, the passage given above. Note: this shortcut formula is also a simplification of the Sixteen Gold Ingots practice, and is even easier to do.

Key Terms

  • Sixteen Gold Ingots / Sixteen-Character Formula for Preserving Life: with each inhalation, lift; every breath returns to the navel; with each lift, swallow; water and fire meet.
  • Carrying yang and embracing yin: an echo of the Laozi, “the ten thousand things carry yin on their backs and embrace yang in front”; this part says the back is yang (the Governing vessel) and the chest yin (the Conception vessel).
  • Conception and Governing vessels / Conception vessel: this part divides it into two sections — the lower from the root of the genitals to the navel, the upper from the crown down to the navel.
  • The three passes of the Governing vessel: this part (the Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall) gives them as the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Muddy Pellet, slightly different from the usual Caudal Funnel, Spine-Straddling Pass and Jade Pillow; see Three Passes.
  • Lifting (ti 提): using a little intention and eye-power in stillness; “too much deliberate intention does harm.”
  • Swallowing (yan 咽): neither swallowing essence nor swallowing air, but the breath falling from the gate at the crown to the throat and being taken in lightly.
  • Water and fire meet: when exhalation and inhalation both return to the navel and yin and yang are firmly joined; that is, the conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Bellows (tuoyue 橐籥): the ancient bellows for blowing a fire; from the Laozi, “is not the space between Heaven and Earth like a bellows?”
  • Cinnabar Field: this part says “one cun three fen inward from the navel.”
  • Nourishing yin, assisting yang: Xuesuo Weng says that letting go of all ties is nourishing yin, and gathering the fire and opening the passes is assisting yang.
  • Lower Pole (xiaji 下极): the lower Cinnabar Field (some say the perineum).
  • Twenty-four breaths: the spirit dwelling in the lower Cinnabar Field for twenty-four breaths.

Putting It into Practice

  1. There are only two actions: on the inhalation, let the intention lightly carry the breath up from below (the perineum) to the navel; on the exhalation, let the intention lightly carry it up from the Caudal Funnel along the spine. The breathing itself is “left to nature”; do not change its length.
  2. “A little” is the whole key: a little intention, a little eye-power (the eyes lightly following). Too much deliberate intention does harm — forcefully lifting the anus or holding the breath is not the “lifting” meant here.
  3. Do not force the crown: the Muddy Pellet “opens by itself with long practice”; it is not a matter of a day or two. Until then, do only the two “lifts.”
  4. “Swallowing” once it has opened: when you feel the breath falling from the crown to the throat and the saliva in the mouth clear and sweet, swallow lightly and send it with the intention to the navel (the Cinnabar Field).
  5. An even simpler version (Xuesuo Weng’s shortcut formula): first let go of all ties; on the inhalation feel the breath rising from the Caudal Funnel up the back, on the exhalation feel it descending from the forehead through the chest and belly; finally bring the intention back to the lower Cinnabar Field and rest there for twenty-four breaths, as if present and as if forgotten, then stop. This version is well suited to beginners for daily practice.
  6. Do not mix the two versions: the text says “rise along the Governing vessel on the exhalation”; Xuesuo Weng says “rise along the Governing vessel on the inhalation.” Choose one.

Translator's Notes

  1. “With each lift” means with each exhalation: this practice pairs the exhalation with the rising along the Governing vessel, exactly the opposite of most Celestial Circuit methods (including Xuesuo Weng’s shortcut formula at the end), which pair the inhalation with the rising. The text says so and is translated as it stands, with a reminder in the introduction and in “Putting It into Practice.”
  2. “Only then, with a swallow, does saliva descend” (yi yan bian fang you jinye jiangxia 一咽便方有津液降下): bian fang together means “only then”; the translation renders it so.
  3. “Incomparable comfort” (changshi wu sai 畅适无赛): so comfortable that nothing compares.
  4. “Lower Pole”: generally the area of the lower Cinnabar Field, though some take it as the perineum; the translation renders it “the Lower Pole (the lower Cinnabar Field).”
  5. Qiu Ling did not divide the original text of this part into paragraphs; for ease of comparison, it is divided by content into §1–§4.

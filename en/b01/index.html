Selected Ancient Chinese Qigong Texts, Annotated

中国古代气功选注 · Edited and annotated by Qiu Ling · Guangdong Science and Technology Press · 1988

From the many classics of Daoist inner alchemy, Qiu Ling selected nine texts (one of them an appendix) and annotated them passage by passage: The Secret of the Golden Flower, the Immortal Scripture of Opening and Closing, the Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall, the Heart Talks of the Two Idlers, Further Fragmentary Words, The Precious Raft of the Women’s School of Dual Cultivation, the Collection of Daoxiang, the Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment, and Sun Bu’er’s poems on women’s inner alchemy with Chen Yingning’s commentary. The whole course of the practice is here — from Reverting the Light, gathering the spirit and regulating the breath, through the Lesser Celestial Circuit, to nurturing the embryo and sending out the spirit. He especially recommends the Collection of Daoxiang, which speaks plainly and uses few metaphors, and the Concise Essentials, which gathers the practical experience of the ancients.

How to read

  1. Every chapter follows the same order: About This Chapter → Original Text and Translation → Key Terms → Putting It into Practice → Translator’s Notes. The classical Chinese original is folded away; you can read the translation alone and follow everything.
  2. Qiu Ling’s notes come right after the passage they explain (in the appendix, Chen Yingning’s notes). The Chinese of each note can be unfolded; the full translation is shown below it.
  3. Underlined words are terms from the glossary: hover over one (or tap it on a phone) to see its English name and the Chinese term. Different names that different books give to the same thing are grouped together in the glossary.
  4. Passages marked “Safety note” record vigorous methods or very advanced stages described by the ancients. Read them for understanding only; do not try them on your own.

I · Reverting the Light (Golden Flower)12 chapters

Secrets of Reverting the Light — Selections from The Secret of the Golden Flower

Take back the gaze and the attention that normally run outward, and shine them on yourself — this is called "Reverting the Light." All twelve chapters describe how this single practice deepens step by step: where to begin (between the eyes, at the point between the eyebrows); how to pair it with the breath (listening to the breath); the common deviations (torpor, distraction, falling into dead stillness); the signs of progress (seeing light, a light body, warmth); and the deeper levels (the conjunction of Kan and Li, the Living Zi Hour, the Celestial Circuit).

II · Opening and Closing10 chapters

The Daoist Immortal Practice of Opening and Closing — Essentials of Perfected Yin’s Immortal Scripture of Opening and Closing

Starting from the foundation of "adding oil to prolong life," and passing through concentrating the spirit and entering the cavity, spirit and breath relying on each other, opening the passes, collecting the Medicine, the mao-you Celestial Circuit, nurturing the Holy Embryo, suckling the infant and moving the spirit up to the upper field, all the way to refining emptiness and uniting with the Dao — this is a complete road map of inner alchemical cultivation.

III · Longmen Nourishing Life (Secret Teachings)6 chapters

The Traditional Longmen Practice for Nourishing Life — Perfected Yin’s Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall: The Secret Teachings

First settle the mind, then regulate the breath; Revert the Light and illuminate within, from the Root of the Mountain to the Chamber of Spirit; look into the navel, until cool fluid and warm breath meet (the Lesser Celestial Circuit); when the three treasures are full, they break through the passes by themselves (the Greater Celestial Circuit); gather in the inner Medicine with the mao-you Celestial Circuit; and finally nurture the Holy Embryo.

IV · Inner Illumination (Two Idlers)3 chapters

The Practice of Inner Illumination — Selections from the Heart Talks of the Two Idlers, Annotated

With the gaze (the light of awareness), illuminate downward from the crown, station by station — the Muddy Pellet → the Crimson Palace → the Central Yellow → the lower Cinnabar Field — until each is "pure white"; then let the light go around from the Sea Bottom (the perineum) to the back, through the Caudal Funnel and straight up to the crown, above and below reflecting each other, until finally "I no longer feel that I have a body."

V · The Sixteen Gold Ingots1 chapter

The Sixteen Gold Ingots Practice — Section Two of Perfected Yin’s Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall

Coordinate the breathing with the Conception and Governing vessels: on the inhalation, with a little intention and eye-power, lift the breath from below and take it along the lower half of the Conception vessel into the navel; on the exhalation, with a little intention and eye-power, lift the breath into the Governing vessel, from the Caudal Funnel straight up the backbone to the crown; once the Muddy Pellet has opened, the breath falls from the crown to the throat, is swallowed lightly and returns to the navel — when both exhalation and inhalation return to the navel, that is "water and fire meet."

VI · The Four Seasons (Further Fragmentary Words)8 chapters

Living Formulas for Practice in the Four Seasons — Further Fragmentary Words, Annotated

Cultivation has only three general rules: keep the body upright, keep the mind empty, keep the intention genuine. On top of these three, adjust rising and descending, releasing and gathering flexibly according to the season, the hour and the state of the body — in winter and spring emphasize "passing through behind" (the breath rising along the back), in summer and autumn "opening in front" (the breath descending along the front).

VII · The Precious Raft of the Women’s School3 chapters

Selections from The Precious Raft of the Women’s School of Dual Cultivation, Annotated

Women take blood as their foundation; their nature tends toward yin and likes coolness. So they begin by stopping thoughts and regulating the mind, then add massage (attending to the Breast Stream and circling massage of the breasts) to free the movement of breath, so as not to sink into pure yin; then they guide the movement of breath in a circuit between the chest (the Southern Ocean), the waist, the navel wheel, the Caudal Funnel and the Muddy Pellet. The whole is to be done with concentration, gentleness and stillness as the main points.

VIII · The Collection of Daoxiang9 chapters

The Collection of Daoxiang, Edited and Annotated

The Dao is only the two words "movement and stillness," in fact only the one word "stillness": when stillness reaches its extreme, there is naturally a movement, and this movement is the primordial breath of Prior Heaven, the opening of the Mysterious Pass, the production of the Medicine; at that moment concentrate the spirit on the place of movement and let the breathing follow it back to the root, so that the three families — spirit, breath and respiration — unite as one. This is the whole of the work; from the beginning through nurturing the embryo to sending out the spirit, it is all this one principle.

IX · Essentials of Great Accomplishment26 chapters

The Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment, Selected and Annotated

A Wu–Liu school "true record" of cultivation written from beginning to end: first, with Reverting the Light, regulating the breath, and the civil and martial fire phasing, refine Essence into Breath (the lesser accomplishment, laying the foundation); then, guarding the center and embracing the One, refine Breath into Spirit in great absorption (the middle accomplishment, nurturing the embryo); finally, let the Yang Spirit thus nurtured go out and come in to be regulated and nourished (the higher accomplishment, suckling) — for every step it tells what to do, what scenes will appear, and where the greatest danger lies.

Appendix · Sun Bu’er’s Poems14 chapters

Appendix: Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy, with Commentary

Fourteen poems, one stage per poem: the first three — gathering in the mind, nourishing the breath, practicing the work — are broadly the same for men and women; the fourth and fifth, "Beheading the Dragon" and "Nourishing the Elixir," are beginning practices unique to women; from the sixth on — embryonic breathing, the fire tallies, receiving the medicine, refining the spirit, up to ingesting, abstaining from grain, facing the wall, sending out the spirit, and soaring aloft — is the ideal sequence from forming the elixir to becoming an immortal.

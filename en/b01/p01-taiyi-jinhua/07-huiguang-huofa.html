Chapter 7 · Reverting the Light as a Living Method

Chapter 7 · Reverting the Light as a Living Method — bringing the practice into daily life

About This Chapter

Reverting the Light is not only something you do on a cushion. This chapter tells us not to abandon our proper work and life: act with right mindfulness, and whenever a situation arises, bring the light back in that very moment — this is called “Reverting the Light without form.” It is best to set aside some time each morning for quiet sitting; for the rest of the day, in dealing with affairs and people, use just this one method of “turning back.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

回光循循然行去，不要废弃正业。以正念治事，即光不为物转，当境即回，此时时无相之回光也。

Practice Reverting the Light step by step, but do not neglect your proper occupation. Handle your affairs with right mindfulness, and the light will not be turned about by things; whatever situation you meet, bring it back at once. This is Reverting the Light at every moment, without form.

§2
Original (Chinese)

日用间能刻刻随事返照，不著一毫人我相，便是随地回光，此第一妙用。清晨能遣尽诸缘，静坐一、二时最妙。凡应事接物，只用返法。

If in daily life you can turn the illumination back at every moment, along with whatever you are doing, without clinging to the slightest distinction between “others” and “me,” this is Reverting the Light wherever you are — the finest use of all. In the early morning, if you can set aside all your business and sit quietly for one or two double-hours, that is best. In all dealings with affairs and people, use only the method of turning back.

§3Old commentary · *Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate*
Original (Chinese)

但行、住、坐、卧常操此心藏于夹脊之窍，则天地真气随鼻呼吸，以扯以进，自与己之混元真精凝结丹田，而为吾养生之益。

(The Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate says:) Just keep this mind stored in the opening of the twin passes of the spine at all times — walking, standing, sitting and lying down — and the true breath of Heaven and Earth will follow the breathing of the nose, drawn in with each breath, and naturally congeal with your own primordial true essence in the Cinnabar Field, to the benefit of your health.

Qiu Ling's noteon the whole chapter
Original note (Chinese)

回光活法即把回光与日常生活结合起来，行、住、坐、卧不离这个，除正规练功外，时刻随事返照，即收敛两眼神光内视。

《皇极阖辟仙经》曰：（引文见上。）此教人常存意于中黄也。

“Reverting the Light as a living method” means combining Reverting the Light with daily life: walking, standing, sitting and lying down, never leaving it. Apart from formal practice, at every moment turn the illumination back along with whatever you are doing — that is, gather in the spiritual light of the two eyes and look inward.

Qiu Ling also quotes the passage above from the Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate, and says that it teaches people to keep their intention constantly on the Central Yellow.

Key Terms

  • Proper occupation (zhengye 正业): one’s legitimate work and duties.
  • Right mindfulness (zhengnian 正念): awareness that is neither scattered nor biased.
  • Reverting the Light without form: Reverting the Light that is not bound to the form of seated meditation.
  • The distinction between “others” and “me” (renwo xiang 人我相): a Buddhist term for clinging to the separation between self and others.
  • The method of turning back (fanfa 返法): the method of Reverting the Light and turning the illumination back.
  • One or two double-hours: one or two traditional shichen, about two to four hours.
  • Twin passes of the spine (jiaji): Qiu Ling takes this passage as teaching people to keep their intention constantly on the Central Yellow.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Do not neglect your proper work: cultivation is no excuse for escaping life. Work and family go on as usual, and Reverting the Light is practiced within them.
  2. Reverting the Light in daily life: when an event, a person or a remark stirs your emotions — “whatever situation you meet, bring it back at once”: the moment you notice, bring your attention back to the breath and to the point between the eyes.
  3. A fixed morning sitting: the text recommends sitting quietly in the morning for “one or two double-hours” (about two to four hours). For people today, start with a fixed period each morning; what matters is not interrupting it.
  4. Do not cling to “others” and “me”: in your work, think a little less about “showing what I can do” or “what will they think of me,” and the mind will be less easily carried away.

Translator's Notes

  1. “One or two double-hours” is about two to four hours by the traditional reckoning; people today can start with shorter periods according to their circumstances. This is the translator’s practical suggestion, not part of the original text.

Chapter 1 · The Celestial Heart

Chapter 1 · The Celestial Heart — the Mysterious Opening everyone has, and the ∴ formula for getting started

About This Chapter

The chapter begins with the goal of the practice — the “Celestial Heart” (tianxin 天心), which alchemical books usually call the Mysterious Opening (xuanqiao 玄窍). Everyone has it; all one can do is quietly keep watch and wait for it to open by itself. Then the text gives a very concrete formula for getting started: use the shape of the three dots “∴” to connect the two eyes with the point between the eyebrows, so that the spiritual light of both eyes converges there. Finally, it compares the body to a ruler and his country to explain why, if you simply devote yourself to Reverting the Light (huiguang 回光), all the breath in the body will submit to it.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

天心者，三才同禀之心，丹书所谓玄窍是也。人人具有，贤哲启之，愚迷闭之。启则长生，闭则短折，委之命数者，凡夫之见也。

The “Celestial Heart” is the heart that Heaven, Earth and Humanity — the Three Powers — all receive in common; it is what the alchemical books call the Mysterious Opening. Everyone has it. The wise and discerning open it; the foolish and deluded keep it shut. Open it, and you live long; keep it shut, and your life is cut short. To blame the length of one’s life entirely on fate is the view of ordinary people.

§2
Original (Chinese)

然天心一窍，不在身中，不在身外，不可摸索而开，只可默存以俟。功法在于存诚两字。

Yet this one opening of the Celestial Heart is neither inside the body nor outside it. It cannot be forced open by groping for it; one can only keep it silently in mind and wait for it to open of itself. The whole method lies in two words: “preserving sincerity” (cuncheng 存诚) — keeping the mind sincere, single and undivided.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§2
Original note (Chinese)

古人说：“人身为三才（天、地、人）合一之身”，盖人身为一小天地。故天心为天、地、人三才合一之心，属玄窍。玄窍者指至玄至妙之机关，未开时无处可寻；只有开启时无中生有，才显现出来。要得玄关窍开，也就是说要得天心开启，只有诚诚恳恳地去练功，功到自然开。

同出于金盖山传本之《尹真人东华正脉皇极阖辟证道仙经》一书则说天心一窍在脐后一寸二分处。该书云：婴儿“裂胞而出，剪断脐带，囫地一声，一点元阳落于脐轮之后，号曰天心，虚灵一点是也。”此处天心指下丹田，与上文所指属于玄窍之天心有别。

The ancients said: “The human body unites the Three Powers — Heaven, Earth and Humanity.” In other words, the human body is a small cosmos. The Celestial Heart is therefore the heart in which the Three Powers are united, and it belongs to the Mysterious Opening. “Mysterious Opening” means the most mysterious and subtle mechanism of all: before it opens, it is nowhere to be found; only when it opens, producing something out of nothing, does it appear. If you want the Mysterious Pass to open — that is, if you want the Celestial Heart to open — the only way is to practice sincerely and earnestly; when the work is ripe, it opens by itself.

Another book transmitted from Mount Jingai, the Immortal Scripture for Realizing the Dao through the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate, of the True Eastern Florescence Lineage, by the Perfected Yin (Yin zhenren Donghua zhengmai Huangji hepi zhengdao xianjing 尹真人东华正脉皇极阖辟证道仙经), says instead that the opening of the Celestial Heart lies 1.2 cun (Chinese inches; one cun is roughly 3 cm) behind the navel. That book says: when the baby “breaks out of the womb, the umbilical cord is cut, and with the first cry a single point of Original Yang settles behind the navel. This is called the Celestial Heart — that one point of empty spiritual awareness.” There, “Celestial Heart” means the lower Cinnabar Field; it is not the same as the Celestial Heart of our chapter, which belongs to the Mysterious Opening.

§3
Original (Chinese)

存诚妙用，尚有诀中捷诀：乃于万缘放下之时，惟用∴字，以字之中点存诸眉心，以左点存左目，右点存右目，则人两目神光，自得会眉心，眉心即天目，乃为三光会归出入之总户。人能用三目如∴字然，微以意运如磨镜，三光立聚眉心，光耀如日现前。既即以意引临心后关前。此一处也，按即玄牝之门。以意引之，光立随临，而毋忘若如二字玄义，天心必自洞启。

To make wonderful use of “preserving sincerity,” there is a shortcut within the formula. When you have set down all your entanglements, use only the figure “∴”: place its upper middle dot at the point between the eyebrows, its left dot at the left eye, and its right dot at the right eye. Then the spiritual light of your two eyes will naturally meet at the point between the eyebrows. That point is the Celestial Eye (tianmu 天目), the main gateway through which the Three Lights gather, return, go out and come in. If you can use your three eyes like the figure “∴” and move them ever so slightly with your intention, the way one polishes a mirror, the Three Lights will at once gather at the point between the eyebrows, shining before you like the sun. Then, with your intention, lead this light to the place “behind the heart and in front of the Pass.” This place is what is called the “Gate of the Mysterious Female.” Lead it with your intention, and the light immediately follows. And never forget the deep meaning of the two words “as if” (ruoru 若如) — seeming to be there and not to be there, letting things take their own course. Then the Celestial Heart will surely open wide by itself.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

此回光返照功夫的入手要诀。古称神人皆具三目，即左、右目与眉心，人知修炼眉心而开启之，即开天目。三光者在天为日、月、星，在人乃耳、目、口。

《周易参同契》云：“耳目口三宝，闭塞勿发通。”回者，止也。光者观也。闭塞耳、目、口三窍神光，合乎此旨，可使三光立聚眉心天目。

存∴字于三目，微以意运如磨镜，此为回光打开天目不传之秘法，然此章乃概略言之，未尽其意。盖∴字之下二点中尚有两眼之间山根一窍（又称祖窍），位于中岳之巅，为人身之性户，上达泥丸，中达黄中（位在夹脊双关前、心之后），下通脐后。若欲聚光眉心，则须系念此窍。本书回光守中章有云：“初学但于两目中间齐平处系念便了。光是活泼泼的东西，系念眼之齐平处，光自然透入。”两目中间齐平处即山根是也。《尹真人皇极阖辟仙经》亦有“穷想山根”之语。则知系念山根是乃不易之法。

以意引光临心后关前，乃下一步功法，本章顺带言及，详见下文。“若如”二字乃求其自然之意。

This is the key to getting started in the practice of Reverting the Light. The ancients said that divine beings all have three eyes — the left eye, the right eye, and the point between the eyebrows. When a person knows how to cultivate the point between the eyebrows and open it, this is called “opening the Celestial Eye.” The “Three Lights” are, in Heaven, the sun, the moon and the stars; in a person, the ears, the eyes and the mouth.

The Token for the Agreement of the Three According to the Book of Changes (Zhouyi cantong qi 周易参同契) says: “The ears, the eyes and the mouth are the three treasures: close them off and let nothing leak out.” “Reverting” (hui 回) means stopping; “light” (guang 光) means looking. Closing off the spiritual light that goes out through the three openings of the ears, eyes and mouth agrees with this principle, and lets the Three Lights gather at once at the Celestial Eye between the eyebrows.

Holding the figure “∴” over the three eyes and moving it ever so slightly with the intention, like polishing a mirror, is the secret, never-transmitted method for opening the Celestial Eye through Reverting the Light. But this chapter only gives an outline and does not say everything. Between the two lower dots of the “∴” there is another opening, the Root of the Mountain (shangen 山根) between the two eyes, also called the Ancestral Opening (zuqiao 祖窍). It sits at the top of the “Central Peak” — in face reading, the nose is called the Central Peak — and it is the “door of inner nature” in the human body: upward it reaches the Muddy Pellet (niwan 泥丸), in the middle it reaches the Central Yellow (huangzhong, located in front of the twin passes of the spine and behind the heart), and downward it connects with the place behind the navel. If you want to gather the light at the point between the eyebrows, you must fasten your attention on this opening. Chapter 3 of this book, “Reverting the Light and Guarding the Center,” says: “Beginners need only fasten their attention on the point level with and midway between the two eyes. The light is something alive and lively; when you fasten your attention on the point level with the eyes, the light naturally penetrates there.” The point level with and midway between the two eyes is the Root of the Mountain. The Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate by the Perfected Yin also speaks of “thinking intently of the Root of the Mountain.” From this we know that fastening one’s attention on the Root of the Mountain is an unchanging method.

Leading the light with the intention to the place “behind the heart and in front of the Pass” is the next stage of the practice; this chapter only mentions it in passing, and it is explained in detail later. The two words “as if” mean seeking what is natural.

§4
Original (Chinese)

盖身犹国土，而一乃主君。光即是主君心意，又如主君敕旨。故一回光，则周身之气皆上朝，如圣王定都立极，执玉帛者万国；又如主佐同心，臣庶自然奉命，各司其事。只去专一回光，便是无上妙谛。回之既久，此光凝结，即成自然法身，廓而充之，吾宗所谓鄞鄂，西教所谓法王城是也。主君得辅，精气日生，而神愈旺，一旦身心融化，岂仅天外有天，身外有身已哉。

The body is like a country, and the “One” is its ruler. The light is the ruler’s will — or, again, the ruler’s decree. So as soon as you revert the light, all the breath of the body comes to pay homage, just as when a sage king establishes his capital and sets up the supreme standard, ten thousand states come bearing jade and silk as tribute; or as when ruler and ministers are of one mind, the officials and the common people naturally obey orders and each attends to their own duties. Simply devote yourself to Reverting the Light: this is the supreme and wondrous truth. When you have reverted it for a long time, the light congeals and becomes the spontaneous Dharma Body. Expanded and filled out, it is what our school calls the “embryo and its outline” (yin’e 鄞鄂), and what the Western teaching — Buddhism — calls the “City of the Dharma King.” When the ruler has support, essence and breath grow day by day and the spirit becomes ever more vigorous. Once body and mind melt into one, it is far more than merely “a heaven beyond heaven, a body beyond the body”!

§5
Original (Chinese)

然则金华即金丹，神明变化，各师于心。此中妙诀，虽不差毫末，然而甚活，全要聪明，又须沉静。非极聪明人行不得，非极沉静人守不得。

So, then, the Golden Flower (jinhua 金华) is the Golden Elixir (jindan 金丹). Its divine transformations must be learned by each person taking their own heart as teacher. The wondrous formula within it does not deviate by a hair, yet it is extremely lively: it depends entirely on intelligence and also requires deep calm. Without great intelligence one cannot practice it; without great calm one cannot keep to it.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4–§5
Original note (Chinese)

此节设喻言回光之重要，学者专一回光，便是无上妙谛。回之既久，天目渐开，眼前便显示出慧光（又名性光、丹光），养足名蟾光，此乃先天真炁所形成，古人以○代之，慧光如月光，蟾光如金光。由于光的凝结，成为“自然法身”。何谓“自然法身”？即本人“色身”以外的“法身”，亦即光所凝成的这个○。随着功夫的深入，这个○越来越圆明。本节所谓“身外有身”的另一说法是：炼此色身，内外贞白，是身非身，非身是身，功举则身无，功停则身有。

此功为上乘功法，专为接引上根（即资质极佳善于学功者）而设，故云：“非极聪明人行不得，非极沉静人守不得。”高血压或阳亢患者，更不宜练此功。

This section uses a parable to show how important Reverting the Light is: if the student simply devotes himself to Reverting the Light, that is the supreme and wondrous truth. After reverting the light for a long time, the Celestial Eye gradually opens and the “light of wisdom” (huiguang 慧光, also called the light of inner nature or the light of the elixir) appears before the eyes; when it has been fully nourished it is called “moonlight” (changuang 蟾光, literally “toad light”). It is formed by the true breath of Prior Heaven, and the ancients represented it with a circle, “○”. The light of wisdom is like moonlight; the toad light is like golden light. As the light congeals, it becomes the “spontaneous Dharma Body.” What is the spontaneous Dharma Body? It is the “Dharma Body” beyond one’s physical body — what Buddhists call the “form body” — that is, this “○” congealed from light. As the practice deepens, the “○” becomes ever rounder and brighter. There is another way of understanding the “body beyond the body” of this section: refine the physical body until it is pure and white inside and out; it is a body and yet not a body, not a body and yet a body; while you practice, the body seems to disappear; when you stop, it is there again.

This is an advanced method, designed specifically to guide people of “superior roots” — that is, people of excellent aptitude who learn the practice well. That is why the text says, “Without great intelligence one cannot practice it; without great calm one cannot keep to it.” People with high blood pressure or “rising yang” should all the more avoid this practice.

Key Terms

  • Celestial Heart (tianxin): in this chapter, the Mysterious Opening, the place where spirit and breath meet. Note that the “Celestial Heart” of the Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate, 1.2 cun behind the navel, means the lower Cinnabar Field — a different thing.
  • Mysterious Opening (xuanqiao): nowhere to be found before it opens; it appears only when it opens. The locations given in the text are “places to rest one’s attention at the start,” not anatomical points one could point to.
  • the ∴ figure: a sign made of three dots, an old way of writing the Indian letter i. The middle dot corresponds to the point between the eyebrows, the left and right dots to the two eyes.
  • Celestial Eye (tianmu): the space between the eyebrows, the main gateway through which the Three Lights go out and come in.
  • Three Lights (sanguang): according to Qiu Ling, the sun, moon and stars in Heaven, and the ears, eyes and mouth in a person; in this chapter, concretely, the light of the two eyes and of the point between the eyebrows.
  • behind the heart, in front of the Pass: behind the heart and in front of the twin passes of the spine — the “Central Yellow,” which this text calls the Gate of the Mysterious Female.
  • embryo and its outline (yin’e): a term from the Cantong qi, meaning the foundation and contour of the elixir.
  • City of the Dharma King: a Buddhist expression for the dwelling of the Dharma Body; the “Western teaching” is Buddhism.
  • spontaneous Dharma Body / a body beyond the body: the “body outside the body” that forms when the light congeals.

Putting It into Practice

  1. First let go, then use the formula. “When you have set down all your entanglements” is the precondition: first let body and mind become quiet, then do what follows.
  2. How to use the ∴ formula: gently imagine a triangle of three dots — the top dot at the point between the eyebrows, the two lower dots at the two eyes. Feel the “light” of the eyes — your attention — converging toward the middle and slightly upward.
  3. “Move ever so slightly with your intention”: use only a little intention. “Like polishing a mirror” describes the intention turning gently and the mirror growing brighter the more it is polished; do not take it to mean rolling the eyeballs.
  4. Two steps: first gather the light at the point between the eyebrows (and at the Root of the Mountain between the eyes); once the light has gathered, gently lead it with your intention to the place “behind the heart and in front of the Pass.” Beginners should practice only the first step.
  5. The two words “as if” set the measure for the whole chapter: seeming to be there and not to be there, without effort. Effort means “fixing the intention,” which leads to a swollen head and “rising fire.”
  6. Precautions (from Qiu Ling’s notes): this is an advanced method; people with high blood pressure or rising yang (easily overheated, a swollen feeling in the head, a flushed face) should all the more avoid it.

Translator's Notes

  1. “This place is what is called (an ji 按即) the Gate of the Mysterious Female”: the word an 按 looks like an editor’s remark inserted into the text (“Note: this is the Gate of the Mysterious Female”); the translation follows this reading.
  2. “The deep meaning of the two words ‘as if’”: Qiu Ling takes ruoru 若如 to mean seeking what is natural; Min Yide’s lineage usually explains it as “as if present, as if absent; just so, just so.” We translate it as “seeming to be there and not to be there, letting things take their own course.”
  3. On the chapter titles: Qiu Ling’s twelve chapters are his own regrouping of the text, so they do not match the chapter divisions of Wilhelm’s or Cleary’s translations. In Cleary’s version (based on the Daozang xubian), the material of this chapter belongs to the first chapter, on the Celestial Heart.

Chapter 11 · The Conjunction of Kan and Li

Chapter 11 · The Conjunction of Kan and Li — a string of code words that come down to a single act

About This Chapter

“The conjunction of Kan and Li” is one of the most common expressions in alchemical scriptures, and one of the most mysterious-sounding. This chapter sees through it in a few words: everything that leaks outward is Li; everything that gathers inward is Kan. Kan and Li are yin and yang; yin and yang are inner nature and life; inner nature and life are body and mind; body and mind are spirit and breath. Simply gather in the breath and do not let the spirit flow along with outer circumstances — that is the true conjunction. This chapter is a key to reading all the code words of the alchemical scriptures.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

凡漏泄精神，动而交物者，皆离也。凡收转神识，静而中涵者，皆坎也。七窍之外，走者为离；七窍之内，返者为坎。一阴主于逐声随声；一阳主于返闻收见。

Whatever lets the vital spirit leak out and moves to deal with outer things is Li ☲. Whatever turns spirit and consciousness back and is still, holding within, is Kan ☵. Outside the seven openings, what runs off is Li; inside the seven openings, what returns is Kan. The single yin (in the middle of Li) is in charge of chasing after sounds and following them; the single yang (in the middle of Kan) is in charge of turning the hearing back and gathering in what is seen.

§2
Original (Chinese)

坎离即阴阳，阴阳即性命，性命即身心，身心即神炁。一自敛息，精神不为境缘流转，即是真交。而沉默趺坐时，又无论矣。

Kan and Li are yin and yang; yin and yang are inner nature and life; inner nature and life are body and mind; body and mind are spirit and breath. As soon as you gather in the breath and the vital spirit is no longer carried along by outer circumstances, that is the true conjunction. And when you sit cross-legged in silence, it goes without saying.

Qiu Ling's noteon the whole chapter
Original note (Chinese)

凝神敛息即是坎离交媾，此言最精。凝神入气穴，息息归根，亦即此意。敛息即收敛口鼻呼吸之气，藏伏于丹田，变成丹田呼吸，亦即胎息。不限时间与方所，果能常常如此，则真交媾如立竿见影。

“Concentrating the spirit and gathering in the breath is the conjunction of Kan and Li” — this is the most precise statement of all. “Concentrating the spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath” and “every breath returning to the root” mean the same thing. Gathering in the breath means drawing in the breath of the mouth and nose and letting it lie hidden in the Cinnabar Field, becoming Cinnabar-Field breathing — that is, embryonic breathing. It is not limited to any time or place. If you can truly do this constantly, the true conjunction will appear as surely as a pole set upright casts a shadow.

Key Terms

  • Li ☲: yang outside and yin inside; it stands for fire, the heart and the spirit. This chapter defines it as “letting the vital spirit leak out and moving to deal with outer things.”
  • Kan ☵: yin outside and yang inside; it stands for water, the kidneys and the breath. This chapter defines it as “turning spirit and consciousness back, still and holding within.”
  • The seven openings: the two eyes, two ears, two nostrils and the mouth.
  • Chasing after sounds / turning the hearing back: the first is chasing outward; the second is listening back inward (close to the Śūraṅgama Sūtra‘s “turn the hearing back to hear one’s own nature”).
  • Conjunction of Kan and Li, inner nature and life, spirit and breath: the chain of equations given in this chapter — Kan and Li = yin and yang = inner nature and life = body and mind = spirit and breath.
  • Gathering in the breath: drawing in the breath and letting it lie hidden in the Cinnabar Field.
  • Sitting cross-legged (fuzuo 趺坐): the lotus or cross-legged posture.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The one-sentence version: concentrating the spirit and gathering in the breath = the conjunction of Kan and Li.
  2. How to gather in the breath: continuing the listening to the breath of Chapter 4: the breath becomes finer and finer, as if it were stored and hidden in the lower abdomen (the Cinnabar Field), becoming a barely perceptible opening and closing there (what Qiu Ling calls “Cinnabar-Field breathing,” that is, embryonic breathing). There is no need to push the breath down by force.
  3. Not only while sitting: “not limited to any time or place” — while walking or working, as long as the spirit is not carried off by outer circumstances and the breath is calm and gathered, the conjunction is taking place.
  4. A key for reading the alchemical scriptures: from now on, when you read about dragon and tiger, lead and mercury, crow and hare, infant and maiden, water and fire in balance… first try to “translate” them with this chain of equations: most often they are speaking about the relationship between spirit and breath, mind and body.

Translator's Notes

  1. “The single yin is in charge of chasing after sounds; the single yang is in charge of turning the hearing back”: the single yin is the middle line of the trigram Li ☲, the single yang the middle line of the trigram Kan ☵.

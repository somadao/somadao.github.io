Chapter 5 · Errors in Reverting the Light

Chapter 5 · Errors in Reverting the Light — several ways of going astray

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

This chapter is short, but it is a “guide to the pitfalls” that everyone who practices quiet sitting should know by heart. It lists four ways of going astray: sitting like an empty shell that does nothing (the “do-nothing case”); making too much effort to “take hold” of the practice; sitting into a cold, dead stillness (falling into the realm of yin); and being led along by thoughts that rush in one after another, while even finding it pleasant (“the master made a slave”). The right state lies in between: letting go yet clear, clear yet letting go.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

《宗旨》将行之际，预作方便，勿多用心，放教活泼泼地，令气和心适，然后入静。静时正要得机得窍，不可坐在无事甲里，万缘放下之中，惺惺自若也。又不可著意承当，惺惺不昧之中，放下自若也。

When you are about to begin practicing according to the Secret, prepare yourself beforehand. Do not use the mind too much; let body and mind be relaxed and lively, so that the breath is harmonious and the mind at ease, and only then enter stillness. In stillness, what matters is to catch the right moment and the right opening. You must not sit inside a “do-nothing case,” an empty shell; rather, while setting down all entanglements, remain wide awake and at ease. Nor should you deliberately take hold and exert yourself; rather, while wide awake and undimmed, remain relaxed and at ease.

§2
Original (Chinese)

又不可堕于蕴界，所谓蕴界者乃五阴魔用事，如一般入定，而槁木死灰之意多，大地阳春之意少，此则落阴界，其气冷，其息沉，且有许多寒衰现象，久之便堕木石。

Nor should you fall into the realm of the aggregates. The realm of the aggregates is where the “demons of the five aggregates” are in charge. It looks like ordinary meditative absorption, but there is more of “dead wood and cold ashes” in it than of “spring returning to the great earth.” This is falling into the realm of yin: the breath is cold, the breathing heavy, and there are many signs of cold and decline. In time you sink into being like wood or stone.

§3
Original (Chinese)

又不可随于万缘，如一入静，而无端众绪忽至，欲却之不能，随之反觉顺适，此名主为奴役，久之落于色欲界。此数者皆差路也。

Nor should you follow along with all your entanglements. For example, as soon as you enter stillness, all sorts of thoughts suddenly rush in for no reason; you try to push them away and cannot, so you go along with them — and it even feels smooth and pleasant. This is called “the master made a slave of his servants.” In time you fall into the realm of form and desire. All of these are wrong roads.

Qiu Ling's noteon the whole chapter
Original note (Chinese)

回光差谬乃入静不得其法其窍。入静不可太无意亦不可太著意。太无意为顽空死虚，即什么不想，无窍无用（坐在无事甲里）；太著意为执着，不能自然放松。只有处于若有若无、有意无意之间，才会得到真消息。至于入静时阴气沉沉，或为杂念所左右，则更是差路。

Errors in Reverting the Light come from not having grasped the method and the knack of entering stillness. In entering stillness, you must neither have too little intention nor too much. Too little intention is “dull emptiness,” a dead void — thinking of nothing at all, with no knack and no effect; this is “sitting inside a do-nothing case.” Too much intention is clinging; you cannot relax naturally. Only between being and non-being, between intending and not intending, will you receive the true news. As for heavy, sinking yin breath during stillness, or being pushed around by scattered thoughts, those are wrong roads all the more.

Key Terms

  • Preparation (fangbian 方便): a Buddhist term (“skillful means”); here, preparing oneself and arranging things skillfully before entering stillness.
  • Catching the moment and the opening: seizing the right timing and the right key point.
  • “Do-nothing case” (wushi jia 无事甲): a Chan expression for sitting in an empty shell, empty but without awareness.
  • Wide awake (xingxing 惺惺): clear and lucid.
  • Deliberately taking hold: forcefully, intentionally trying to carry or control the practice.
  • Realm of the aggregates / demons of the five aggregates: the five aggregates (skandhas) are form, sensation, perception, mental formations and consciousness; the Śūraṅgama Sūtra lists the demonic states associated with each.
  • Realm of yin: the deviation into cold, dead stillness during sitting.
  • Dead wood and cold ashes: from Zhuangzi, “The Adjustment of Controversies”; here used negatively, meaning a lifeless stillness.
  • “The master made a slave”: the master ordered about by his servants — a figure for the Original Spirit being led along by scattered thoughts.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Relax before you sit: “Do not use the mind too much; let body and mind be relaxed and lively, so that the breath is harmonious and the mind at ease, and only then enter stillness.” Do not take your seat full of tension, goals and a sense of duty.
  2. Two symmetrical errors:
    • Letting go without clarity → the “do-nothing case,” dull emptiness (Qiu Ling: too little intention, thinking of nothing, no knack and no effect).
    • Clarity without letting go → deliberately taking hold, clinging (Qiu Ling: too much intention, unable to relax naturally).
    • Correct: wide awake within letting go, letting go within being wide awake.
  3. Check yourself for the realm of yin: feeling cold after sitting, heavy breathing, low mood, growing more listless the longer you sit — these mean you have drifted toward dead stillness. Return to the feeling of “spring returning to the great earth”: warm, gentle and alive.
  4. Check whether thoughts are carrying you off: sitting down and thinking about this and that, and even finding it “pleasant thinking” — this is “the master made a slave.” When you notice it, return to listening to the breath and Reverting the Light.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Let body and mind be relaxed and lively” (fang jiao huopopo di 放教活泼泼地): jiao 教 here means “to let, to make.”
  2. “Dead wood and cold ashes” (gaomu sihui 槁木死灰): in the Zhuangzi, this phrase describes a sage in deep meditation and is not negative; here the text uses it negatively, for a stillness without life.

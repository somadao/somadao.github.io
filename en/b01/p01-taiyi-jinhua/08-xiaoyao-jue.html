Chapter 8 · The Formula of Free Wandering

Chapter 8 · The Formula of Free Wandering — one poem that sums up the whole practice; the three stages of Reverting the Light

About This Chapter

This is the chapter that reaches the deepest level of practice in the whole part. It opens with a seven-character poem, the “Formula of Free Wandering,” which is said to contain “the whole mystery in one poem”; the text explains it line by line itself. It speaks of the conjunction of Kan and Li, of “taking from Kan to fill Li,” and of the eyes as the pivot. Then it describes a deeper level of Reverting the Light: from “controlling the inside from the outside” to “governing the outside from the center.” The two eyes shine inward on the lower Cinnabar Field (the Palace of Kan), true yang comes out to answer, spirit and breath knot together, until the true conjunction in which “the pulse stops and the breath halts,” and then the Living Zi Hour when “the Celestial Heart stirs.” Finally it explains how to respond when the Living Zi Hour arrives, and the three stages of Reverting the Light: adding oil to prolong life, settling the spirit in the Ancestral Opening, and all wonders returning to the root.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

「玉清留下逍遥诀，四字凝神入气穴。六月俄看白雪飞，三更又见日轮赫。水中吹起藉巽风，天上游归食坤德。更有一句玄中玄，无何有乡是真宅。」诗一首，玄奥已尽。大道之要，不外无为而为四字。惟无为，故不滞方所形象。惟无为而为，故不堕顽空死虚。作用不外一中，而枢机全在二目。二目者斗柄也，斡旋造化，转运阴阳。其大药，则始终一水中金而已。

“The Lord of Jade Purity left behind the Formula of Free Wandering; / its key words are: concentrate the spirit and enter the Cavity of Breath. / In the sixth month, suddenly, white snow is seen flying; / at the third watch of the night, the sun’s disk blazes again. / Blowing up from within the water, it relies on the Wind of Xun; / returning from wandering in heaven, it feeds on the virtue of Kun. / And there is one more line, the mystery within the mystery: / the Land of Nothing-at-All is the true home.”

This one poem already says all there is of the mystery. The essence of the great Dao comes down to four words: “acting through non-action.” Because it is non-action, it does not get stuck in any place, location, form or image. Because it is non-action that nonetheless acts, it does not fall into dull emptiness or dead void. Its working is nothing other than the “center,” and its pivot lies entirely in the two eyes. The two eyes are the Handle of the Dipper: they turn creation and set yin and yang in motion. As for the Great Medicine, from beginning to end it is nothing but “the metal within the water” — that is, the lead within the water, meaning the One Breath of Prior Heaven.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

（“其大药，则始终一水中金”句下）编者按：即水中铅，指先天一炁。

相传吕洞宾曾作诗三章，题曰《至教宗旨》，此《逍遥诀》即其次章。

Qiu Ling notes: “the metal within the water” is the lead within the water, meaning the One Breath of Prior Heaven.

According to tradition, Lü Dongbin wrote three poems under the title The Principle of the Supreme Teaching; this “Formula of Free Wandering” is the second of them.

§2The text's own explanation (originally later in this chapter; moved here by Qiu Ling)
Original (Chinese)

诗首二句，全括金华作用。次二句，是日月互体意。六月即离火也，白雪飞即离中真阴将返乎坤也。三更即坎水，日轮即坎中一阳，将赫然而返乎乾也。取坎填离即在此中。次二句，说斗柄作用，升降全机。水中非坎乎，目为巽风，目光照入坎宫，摄召太阳之精是也。天上即乾宫，游归食坤德，即神入炁中，天入地中，养火也。末二句是指出诀中诀。诀中之诀，始终离不得，所谓洗心涤虑为沐浴也。

The first two lines of the poem sum up the whole working of the Golden Flower. The next two lines express the idea that sun and moon contain each other. The “sixth month” is the fire of Li; “white snow flying” is the true yin in the middle of Li about to return to Kun. The “third watch” is the water of Kan; the “sun’s disk” is the single yang in the middle of Kan, about to blaze its way back to Qian. “Taking from Kan to fill Li” lies right here. The next two lines describe the working of the Handle of the Dipper, the whole mechanism of rising and descending. Is not “within the water” Kan? The eyes are the Wind of Xun: the light of the eyes shines into the Palace of Kan and draws forth the essence of great yang. “Heaven” is the Palace of Qian; “returning from wandering, it feeds on the virtue of Kun” means the spirit entering the breath, Heaven entering Earth — this is nourishing the fire. The last two lines point out the formula within the formula. This formula within the formula can never be left aside from beginning to end: it is “cleansing the mind and washing away thoughts,” which is called ablution.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

本章中有解释此诀段落，特移前刊于此：（见上）此诀之意，已概括一部全功。

Later in this chapter there was originally a passage explaining the formula; Qiu Ling deliberately moved it forward and printed it after the poem — the passage above. In his view, the meaning of this formula already sums up a complete course of practice.

§3
Original (Chinese)

夫回光其总名耳。功夫进一层，则光华盛一番，回法更妙一番。前者由外制内，今则居中御外；前者即辅相主，今则奉主宣猷，面目一大颠倒矣！法子欲入静，先调摄身心，自在安和，放下万缘，一丝不挂，天心正位乎中。然后两目垂帘，如奉圣旨以召大臣，孰敢不至。次以两目内照坎宫，光华所到，真阳即出而应之。离外阳而内阴，乾体也。一阴入内而为主，随物生心，顺出流转。今回光内照，不随物主，阴气即住，而光华注照，则纯阳也。同类必亲，故坎阳上腾。非坎阳也，仍是乾阳应乾阳耳。二物一遇，便纽结不散，絪缊活动，倏来倏往，倏浮倏沉，自己元宫中，恍如太虚无量，遍身轻妙欲腾，所谓云满千山也。次则来往无踪，浮沉无辨，脉住气停，此则真交媾矣！所谓月涵万水也。俟其杳冥中忽然天心一动，此则一阳来复，活子时也。

“Reverting the Light” is only a general name. With each level the practice advances, the radiance grows stronger and the method of reverting becomes more subtle. Before, it was controlling the inside from the outside; now it is governing the outside from the center. Before, the light was like a chief minister assisting the ruler; now it carries out the ruler’s commands and proclaims his policies — the whole picture has been turned upside down! The method is this. When you want to enter stillness, first adjust and gather body and mind so that they are at ease, settled and calm; set down all entanglements, with not a thread clinging, and let the Celestial Heart take its proper place in the center. Then lower the curtains of the two eyes, as if summoning the ministers by imperial decree — who would dare not come? Next, let the two eyes shine inward on the Palace of Kan (the lower Cinnabar Field); wherever the radiance reaches, true yang comes out to answer it. Li is yang outside and yin inside; its original substance is Qian. Only because a single yin entered within and became master does the mind arise in response to things and flow outward along with them. Now that the light is reverted to shine within and no longer takes things as its master, the yin breath stops, and as the radiance pours in and shines, it becomes pure yang. Like is drawn to like, so the yang of Kan rises up. Yet it is not really the yang of Kan; it is still the yang of Qian answering the yang of Qian. As soon as the two meet, they knot together and do not separate; they swirl and stir, suddenly coming and going, suddenly floating and sinking. Within your own Original Palace it is as if there were boundless empty space, and the whole body feels so light and wonderful it seems about to soar — this is what is called “clouds filling a thousand mountains.” Next, the coming and going leave no trace, floating and sinking can no longer be told apart, the pulse seems to stop and the breath to halt — this is the true conjunction! This is what is called “the moon contained in ten thousand waters.” Wait until, in that dark and dim depth, the Celestial Heart suddenly stirs: this is “the return of the single yang,” the Living Zi Hour.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

此节介绍更深一层功法。前者意守山根，光聚天目，乃第一步功也；以意引光临心后关前，即意守中黄一窍，乃第二步功也。这两步功是由外制内。现在则以两目载离光内照坎宫，实行坎离交媾，为第三步功，此步功居中而御外。坎宫当是下丹田，即脐内一寸二分处，一名神炉，又名坤炉。功夫已到者，意守中黄一窍到一定程度，则有一股热流，自中黄自然接通神炉。《皇极阖辟仙经·神息相依章》曰：“天谷（即泥丸）之神，湛然寂然，真性也。神炉之中，真气氤氲而不息者，真命也。他两个才是真水火、真乌兔、真夫妇、真性命。使二者纽结一团，混合一处，炼在一炉，二六时中神不离气，气不离神，性不离命，命不离性。二者则二而一，一而二者也。”而此中斡旋造化，运转阴阳，枢机全在二目。

This section introduces a deeper level of practice. Before, keeping the intention on the Root of the Mountain and gathering the light at the Celestial Eye was the first step; leading the light with the intention to the place “behind the heart and in front of the Pass,” that is, keeping the intention on the opening of the Central Yellow, was the second step. Both steps are “controlling the inside from the outside.” Now the two eyes carry the light of Li and shine inward on the Palace of Kan, carrying out the conjunction of Kan and Li; this is the third step, and this step “governs the outside from the center.” The Palace of Kan should be the lower Cinnabar Field, 1.2 cun inward from the navel; it is also called the Spirit Furnace or the Furnace of Kun. For those whose practice is ripe, once the intention has rested on the opening of the Central Yellow long enough, a current of warmth naturally connects the Central Yellow with the Spirit Furnace.

The chapter “Spirit and Breath Relying on Each Other” of the Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate says: “The spirit in the Celestial Valley — that is, the Muddy Pellet — clear and still, is true inner nature. In the Spirit Furnace, the true breath that swirls without ceasing is true life. These two are the true water and fire, the true crow and hare (sun and moon), the true husband and wife, the true inner nature and life. Let the two knot together into one mass, merge in one place, be refined in one furnace; throughout the twelve double-hours of the day, let spirit not leave breath and breath not leave spirit, inner nature not leave life and life not leave inner nature. The two are two yet one, one yet two.” And the pivot that turns creation and sets yin and yang in motion in all this lies entirely in the two eyes.

§4
Original (Chinese)

天心镇静，动违其时，则失之嫩；天心已动，而后动以应之，则失之老。天心一动，即以真意上升乾宫而神光视顶为导引焉，此动而应时者也。天君既升乾顶，游扬自得，忽而欲寂，急以真意引入黄庭，而目光视中黄神室焉，既而欲寂者一念不生矣，视内者忽忘其视矣。尔时身心便当一场大放，万缘泯迹，即我之神室鼎炉亦不知在何所，欲觅己身了不可得。此为天入地中，众妙归根之时也。即此便是凝神入气穴。夫一回光也，始而散者欲敛，六用不行，此为涵养本原，添油接命也；既而敛者，自然优游，不费纤毫之力，此为安神祖窍，翕聚先天也；既而影响俱灭，寂然大定，此为蛰藏气穴，众妙归根也。

If the Celestial Heart is still at rest and you move out of season, the mistake is being “too tender”; if the Celestial Heart has already stirred and only then do you move to answer it, the mistake is being “too old.” The moment the Celestial Heart stirs, use the True Intention to rise to the Palace of Qian (the Muddy Pellet at the crown), letting the spiritual light look up toward the top of the head as a guide — this is moving in time with it. Once the Heavenly Ruler has risen to the summit of Qian, it roams there at ease. When it suddenly wants to become still, quickly use the True Intention to lead it into the Yellow Court, with the light of the eyes looking at the Chamber of the Spirit in the Central Yellow. Soon what wanted to be still no longer gives rise to a single thought, and what was looking inward suddenly forgets that it is looking. At that moment body and mind should let go completely: all entanglements vanish without a trace; even my Chamber of the Spirit, my tripod and furnace, I no longer know where they are; I look for my own body and cannot find it. This is the time of “Heaven entering Earth, all wonders returning to the root.” This is precisely concentrating the spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath. One cycle of Reverting the Light goes like this. At first, what is scattered wants to gather, and the six functions (of eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body and mind) cease to operate: this is nourishing the root and source, “adding oil to prolong life.” Next, what has gathered roams freely by itself, without the slightest effort: this is settling the spirit in the Ancestral Opening and gathering in Prior Heaven. Next, even shadow and echo are extinguished, in still and great absorption: this is hibernating in the Cavity of Breath, “all wonders returning to the root.”

Qiu Ling's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

此节言阳生活子时和复命归根功法。我凝神入气穴，即凝神入于下丹田，绵绵续续，勿忘勿助，呼吸相含，神气相抱，既而呼吸一在窍内，则我鼻中呼吸微而若无，胎息渐现，于是虚极静笃，静极生动，动者炁机动也，即真阳（先天一炁）忽然发动，无中生有。此时丹田发暖，四肢百骸融融然，竟体愉快，大地皆春，心花怒放，正是活子时到来。活子时即汉代崔希范《入药镜》所说“一日内，十二时，意所到，皆可为。”此时境界亦即“先天气，后天气，得之者，常似醉”。天心已动，我即动而应之，不早不迟行以真意上升乾宫而神光视顶之法。古人称此为“一觉而动”，这一觉即正觉，即真意，不由感附，自然而生，有如电火石火，当前则是，转眼即非，我须临机把持得住。然后动极生静，我急以真意引入黄庭，而目光视中黄神室，既而静之又静，万缘俱泯，则为复命归根。

This section describes the birth of yang at the Living Zi Hour and the practice of returning to life and going back to the root. When I concentrate the spirit and enter the Cavity of Breath, I concentrate the spirit and sink it into the lower Cinnabar Field, fine and continuous, neither forgetting nor forcing; inhalation and exhalation contain each other, spirit and breath embrace each other. Then the breathing takes place entirely within the opening, and the breath in my nose becomes so faint it seems absent; embryonic breathing gradually appears. Then emptiness reaches its limit and stillness becomes complete, and at the extreme of stillness movement is born. What moves is the mechanism of breath: true yang (the One Breath of Prior Heaven) suddenly stirs, being is born out of non-being. At this time the Cinnabar Field grows warm, the four limbs and hundred bones feel melting and mellow, the whole body is filled with delight, as if the whole earth were in spring, and the heart bursts into bloom — this is precisely the arrival of the Living Zi Hour.

The Living Zi Hour is what Cui Xifan’s Mirror for Compounding the Medicine (Ruyao jing 入药镜) describes: “Within one day, at any of the twelve double-hours, whenever the intention arrives, it can be done.” The state at this time is also what the Mirror describes: “The breath of Prior Heaven, the breath of Posterior Heaven — those who obtain them always seem drunk.” When the Celestial Heart has stirred, I immediately move to answer it, neither too early nor too late, applying the method of “using the True Intention to rise to the Palace of Qian, the spiritual light looking up toward the top of the head.” The ancients called this “moving at the first awareness.” This awareness is right awareness, the True Intention; it does not come from being stirred by or attaching to outer things, but arises naturally, like a spark from flint or a flash of lightning — present in this instant, gone in the next. I must hold onto it at the critical moment. Then at the extreme of movement stillness is born, and I quickly use the True Intention to lead it into the Yellow Court, with the light of the eyes looking at the Chamber of the Spirit in the Central Yellow. Then, stiller and stiller, all entanglements vanish — this is returning to life and going back to the root.

Key Terms

  • Jade Purity: the Celestial Worthy of Primordial Beginning (Yuanshi tianzun) of the Palace of Jade Purity, one of the Three Pure Ones of Daoism.
  • Concentrating the spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath: the key words of the formula; see §4, “This is precisely concentrating the spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath.”
  • The sixth month / white snow flying: the sixth month is the fire of Li; white snow flying stands for the true yin in Li returning to Kun.
  • The third watch / the sun’s disk blazing: the third watch is the water of Kan; the sun’s disk stands for the yang in Kan returning to Qian.
  • Taking from Kan to fill Li: using the yang within Kan to fill the yin within Li, so that Li becomes Qian again.
  • Wind of Xun: the text explains, “the eyes are the Wind of Xun.”
  • The virtue of Kun: Kun is Earth; “returning from wandering in heaven, it feeds on the virtue of Kun” means the spirit entering the breath, Heaven entering Earth — nourishing the fire.
  • The Land of Nothing-at-All: from Zhuangzi, “Free and Easy Wandering”; the realm of emptiness.
  • Acting through non-action: non-action, so it does not get stuck in any location or image; acting, so it does not fall into dull emptiness or dead void.
  • Handle of the Dipper: the two eyes as the pivot that turns creation.
  • Metal within the water / lead within the water: according to Qiu Ling, the One Breath of Prior Heaven.
  • Ablution: cleansing the mind and washing away thoughts.
  • Controlling the inside from the outside / governing the outside from the center: two levels of Reverting the Light.
  • Carrying out the ruler’s commands and proclaiming his policies: the light changing from an assistant to one who acts on the ruler’s orders.
  • Palace of Kan: according to Qiu Ling, the lower Cinnabar Field, 1.2 cun inward from the navel; also called the Spirit Furnace or Furnace of Kun.
  • Qian and Kun, conjunction of Kan and Li: see the general glossary.
  • “Clouds filling a thousand mountains,” “the moon contained in ten thousand waters”: images for the two levels before and at the true conjunction.
  • Living Zi Hour / the return of the single yang: the moment when, at the extreme of stillness, the Celestial Heart stirs by itself.
  • Too tender / too old: responding too early is “too tender,” too late is “too old.”
  • Heavenly Ruler: the Original Spirit.
  • The six functions cease to operate: the activity of the six senses is suspended.
  • Adding oil to prolong life, settling the spirit in the Ancestral Opening, hibernating in the Cavity of Breath: the three stages of Reverting the Light.
  • All wonders returning to the root: echoing the Laozi‘s “the gate of all wonders” and “returning to the root is called stillness.”

Putting It into Practice

  1. The whole poem comes down to one act: “concentrate the spirit and enter the Cavity of Breath” — gather the spirit and let it sink into the lower Cinnabar Field. The rest — snow in the sixth month, the sun at the third watch, the Wind of Xun, the virtue of Kun — describes how spirit and breath meet within this one act.
  2. The three steps of this chapter (following Qiu Ling): first, keep the intention on the Root of the Mountain and gather the light at the Celestial Eye (controlling the inside from the outside); second, lead the light with the intention to the place behind the heart and in front of the Pass, keeping the intention on the Central Yellow (still controlling the inside from the outside); third, let the two eyes carry the light of Li and shine inward on the Palace of Kan — the lower Cinnabar Field — carrying out the conjunction of Kan and Li (governing the outside from the center).
  3. Preparation before entering stillness: adjust and gather body and mind, at ease, settled and calm; set down all entanglements, with not a thread clinging. This is the precondition every time.
  4. Responding to the Living Zi Hour (advanced; for information): when the Celestial Heart stirs, use the True Intention to lead the spiritual light to look up toward the crown; when it wants to become still, use the True Intention to lead it into the Yellow Court, with the eyes looking at the Central Yellow; finally forget even the “looking” and let go completely. The key is “neither too early nor too late,” and at every step following the direction it wants to go, rather than pulling it by force.
  5. The three stages of Reverting the Light, for checking yourself: what is scattered gradually gathers (adding oil to prolong life) → it is gathered without effort, roaming freely (settling the spirit in the Ancestral Opening) → still and great absorption (all wonders returning to the root).
  6. On “the pulse stops and the breath halts”: this describes a subjective experience in deep absorption; it does not mean you should hold your breath or try to slow your heartbeat. If you feel unwell, stop practicing and consult a doctor.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Its key words are: concentrate the spirit and enter the Cavity of Breath”: the original says “four characters” (sizi 四字), but “concentrate the spirit and enter the Cavity of Breath” (ningshen ru qixue 凝神入气穴) has five. It is usually understood as a four-character formula, ningshen ru xue 凝神入穴 (“concentrate the spirit and enter the cavity”), with qi 气 specifying which cavity. We translate it as “its key words are.”
  2. §2 was originally the Secret‘s own explanation of the poem, found later in this chapter; Qiu Ling moved it to follow the poem as a commentary. We still treat it as part of the original text and translate it as such.
  3. “The method is this” (fazi 法子): fazi means method or way of doing.
  4. After “it is nothing but the metal within the water,” the book has an editor’s note: “that is, the lead within the water, meaning the One Breath of Prior Heaven.” It has been worked into the translation.
  5. In his note on §4, Qiu Ling calls Cui Xifan a man of the Han dynasty; Cui Xifan is generally thought to have lived at the end of the Tang and during the Five Dynasties. We have left out the dynasty in the translation and note it here.
  6. Translations of the poem: the poem is well known, and other translators (Wilhelm, Cleary) have rendered it in their own ways. The version given here is our own, made to follow the text’s own line-by-line explanation in §2.

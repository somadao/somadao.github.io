Chapter 6 · Signs of Reverting the Light

Chapter 6 · Signs of Reverting the Light — what happens when you practice correctly

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

What do you experience when you practice correctly? The chapter has two parts. First it describes three levels of the Golden Flower: first budding, full bloom and great congealing. Then it lists two phenomena you can verify right away — a white brightness before the eyes (“the empty chamber produces whiteness”) and the body feeling soft and floating upward (the spirit returning to the top of the head). Finally it reminds us that the signs differ from person to person: as when one drinks water, only the drinker knows whether it is cold or warm.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

证验亦多。静中绵绵无间，神情悦豫，如醉如浴，此为遍体阳和，金华乍吐也。既而万类俱寂，皓月中天，觉大地俱是光明境界，此为心体开明，金华正放也。既而遍体充实，不畏风霜，人当之兴味索然者，我遇之精神更旺，红血为乳，七尺肉团无非金宝，此为金华大凝也。

There are many signs. In stillness the practice continues unbroken, without gaps, and you feel joyful and at ease, as if drunk or freshly bathed: this is the yang of the whole body becoming warm and harmonious, the Golden Flower beginning to bud. Then all things fall silent, as if a bright moon stood in the middle of the sky, and you feel the whole earth to be a realm of light: this is the substance of the mind opening and brightening, the Golden Flower in full bloom. Then the whole body becomes full and solid and fears neither wind nor frost; what others find dull and tiresome only makes your spirit more vigorous; red blood turns into milk, and the seven-foot lump of flesh is nothing but gold and jewels: this is the great congealing of the Golden Flower.

§2
Original (Chinese)

现在可考证者，一则静中目光腾腾满前，皆白如在云中开眼，觅身无从觅视，此为虚室生白，内外通明；一则静中肉身絪缊，如绵如玉，坐中若留不住，而腾腾上浮，此为神归顶天，久之上升可以立待。

There are two signs you can verify right now. One is that in stillness the light of the eyes rises and fills the whole field of vision, all white, as if opening your eyes inside a cloud; you look for your body and cannot find it. This is “the empty chamber produces whiteness”: inside and outside are clear and bright. The other is that in stillness the physical body feels suffused and mellow, soft as cotton, smooth as jade; while sitting it seems it cannot be held down and floats upward. This is the spirit returning to the top of the head; given time, its ascent can be expected any day.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

（“久之上升可以立待”句下）编者按：上升言神升天谷即泥丸，不可误认为升天。

Editor’s note by Qiu Ling: “ascent” here means the spirit rising to the Celestial Valley, that is, the Muddy Pellet (niwan); it must not be mistaken for the body ascending to heaven.

§3
Original (Chinese)

然亦说不尽，随人根器，各现殊胜。此事如人饮水，冷暖自知。须自己信得过方真。先天一炁即在现前，证验中自讨。一炁若得，丹亦立成，一粒真黍也。

But the signs can never all be described; according to each person’s capacities, different remarkable signs appear. This is like drinking water: only the drinker knows whether it is cold or warm. Only what you yourself can trust is real. The One Breath of Prior Heaven is right before you; seek it yourself within the signs. Once this One Breath is obtained, the elixir is formed at once — that single true grain of millet.

Qiu Ling's noteon the whole chapter
Original note (Chinese)

此言练功效应及自我感觉。金华者金丹也，首段述说了先天一炁凝结成丹过程的自我感觉及体现。但由于练功者体质条件、练功努力程度以及所谓“根器”的不同，证验是各有差别的，本节不过举例而言。

This chapter describes the effects of practice and how they feel. The Golden Flower is the Golden Elixir. The first paragraph describes what one feels and what appears as the One Breath of Prior Heaven congeals into the elixir. But because practitioners differ in constitution, in how hard they practice, and in their so-called “capacities,” the signs differ too; this chapter only gives examples.

Key Terms

  • Golden Flower budding / in full bloom / greatly congealed: three images for the Golden Elixir, from sprouting to full bloom to congealing.
  • Red blood turning into milk: a common alchemical expression for essence and blood being transformed into pure essence; it should not be taken literally as a physiological change.
  • The empty chamber produces whiteness: from Zhuangzi, “In the World of Men” — when the mind is empty, light arises.
  • Suffused and mellow (yinyun 絪缊): the look of breath blending, spreading and flowing harmoniously.
  • The spirit returning to the top of the head / ascent: the spirit returning to the Muddy Pellet at the crown. Qiu Ling notes: “ascent” means the spirit rising to the Celestial Valley, the Muddy Pellet; it must not be mistaken for ascending to heaven.
  • Capacities (genqi 根器): a Buddhist term for a person’s aptitude and endowment.
  • One Breath of Prior Heaven, grain-sized pearl (the “single true grain of millet”): the substance of the elixir itself.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The first-level signs are the most common: unbroken continuity in stillness, a happy mood, a comfortable and warm body (“as if drunk or freshly bathed”). These show you are heading in the right direction.
  2. Seeing white light, the body feeling light and floating: both may occur, but “according to each person’s capacities, different remarkable signs appear” — if they do not appear, it does not mean you are practicing wrongly.
  3. Do not chase the signs: Chapter 4 says “do not seek results, and results will come by themselves”; Chapter 10 says not to cling to the light once it appears. Signs are signposts, not the destination.
  4. “Only what you yourself can trust is real”: do not measure yourself against other people’s descriptions, and do not imagine things in order to match the descriptions in books.

Translator's Notes

  1. “The seven-foot lump of flesh is nothing but gold and jewels”: a description of the whole body being full of essence and breath. (The “seven-foot” body uses the old Chinese foot, much shorter than the English foot.)
  2. After “its ascent can be expected any day,” Qiu Ling’s book has an editor’s note: “ascent means the spirit rising to the Celestial Valley, the Muddy Pellet; it must not be mistaken for ascending to heaven.” The translation follows this.

Introduction to Part One

What this book is

The Secret of the Golden Flower of the Great One, by the Patriarch Lü, from Prior-Heaven Emptiness (Lüzu xiantian xuwu Taiyi jinhua zongzhi 吕祖先天虚无太乙金华宗旨), usually shortened to Taiyi jinhua zongzhi or Jinhua zongzhi, is attributed to the Tang-dynasty immortal Lü Dongbin. It emerged in 1688 (the wuchen year of the Kangxi reign) from the Longjiao Hermitage lineage on Mount Jingai in Wuxing, Zhejiang, and was later edited and published by Min Yide, eleventh-generation master of the Longmen (Dragon Gate) lineage on Mount Jingai. It is one of the few alchemical texts devoted entirely to the practice of Reverting the Light (huiguang fanzhao 回光返照). In 1929 the German sinologist Richard Wilhelm translated it into German with a commentary by the psychologist C. G. Jung; from then on it became widely known in the West as The Secret of the Golden Flower.

Readers who want the complete text can consult the existing translations: Wilhelm’s German version (1929), rendered into English by Cary F. Baynes (1931) and published in French together with Jung’s commentary (Commentaire sur le mystère de la Fleur d’or, Albin Michel); Thomas Cleary’s English translation made directly from the Chinese (1991); and Fabrizio Pregadio’s selections in Taoist Internal Alchemy: An Anthology of Neidan Texts. Note that these translations are based on different Chinese editions, so their chapter divisions and wording differ from the version used here.

Qiu Ling's selection

Qiu Ling did not include the whole book. He chose the passages that teach the practice most concretely, regrouped them into twelve chapters with titles of his own, and added notes. On this site, every passage of the original is followed first by a translation; Qiu Ling’s note on that passage comes right after it — the original Chinese of the note (which can be folded) and then a full translation. Where his notes quote the old commentary of Min Yide or the Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate (Huangji hepi xianjing 皇极阖辟仙经), those quotations appear as separate “old commentary” passages, again with the original and a translation.

Editor's preface by Qiu Ling

《吕祖先天虚无太乙金华宗旨》一书出于清代康熙戊辰岁，浙江吴兴县金盖山龙峤山房所传，由金盖山人龙门第十一代闵一得校订。此书为丹书中少见的介绍回光返照功法的专著，颇有实用价值，故节录其精要，并略加注释于此。

The Secret of the Golden Flower of the Great One, by the Patriarch Lü, from Prior-Heaven Emptiness appeared in the wuchen year of the Kangxi reign of the Qing dynasty (1688). It was transmitted by the Longjiao Hermitage on Mount Jingai in Wuxing county, Zhejiang, and edited by Min Yide, a man of Mount Jingai and eleventh-generation master of the Longmen lineage. Among alchemical books it is a rare work devoted specifically to the practice of Reverting the Light, and it has real practical value. I have therefore excerpted its essentials and added brief notes.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

Take back the gaze and the attention that normally run outward, and shine them on yourself — this is called “Reverting the Light.” All twelve chapters describe how this single practice deepens step by step: where to begin (between the eyes, at the point between the eyebrows); how to pair it with the breath (listening to the breath); the common deviations (torpor, distraction, falling into dead stillness); the signs of progress (seeing light, a light body, warmth); and the deeper levels (the conjunction of Kan and Li, the Living Zi Hour, the Celestial Circuit).

Reading tips

  1. The text itself says, “Without great intelligence one cannot practice it; without great calm one cannot keep to it,” and Qiu Ling notes that this is an advanced method. Beginners can start by reading Chapters 3, 4 and 5 (how to begin, regulating the breath, avoiding deviations) until they know them well.
  2. Keep any focus on the head (the point between the eyebrows, the space between the eyes) very light. Qiu Ling specifically warns that people with high blood pressure or “rising yang” (easily overheated, with a swollen feeling in the head or a flushed face) should not practice this method.
  3. The book is full of symbolic words — Qian and Kun, Kan and Li, sun and moon, the Handle of the Dipper, the Golden Flower, the grain-sized pearl. Read them together with the “Key Terms” section of each chapter and the general glossary. The most important key to the whole part is in Chapter 11: “Kan and Li are yin and yang; yin and yang are inner nature and life; inner nature and life are body and mind; body and mind are spirit and breath.”

The twelve chapters

  1. The Celestial Heart — the “Mysterious Opening” everyone has, and the ∴ formula for getting started
  2. Original Spirit and Cognitive Spirit — who should be in charge: the original spirit or the cognitive spirit, the hun soul or the po soul
  3. Reverting the Light and Guarding the Center — why Reverting the Light is a “method of reversal”; what “looking at the tip of the nose” really means; cessation and contemplation
  4. Reverting the Light and Regulating the Breath — mind and breath relying on each other, listening to the breath; curing torpor and distraction
  5. Errors in Reverting the Light — several ways of going astray
  6. Signs of Reverting the Light — what happens when you practice correctly
  7. Reverting the Light as a Living Method — bringing the practice into daily life
  8. The Formula of Free Wandering — one poem that sums up the whole practice; the three stages of Reverting the Light; the Living Zi Hour
  9. Laying the Foundation in a Hundred Days — the first hundred days
  10. The Light of Nature and the Light of Cognition — what to do once the light appears
  11. The Conjunction of Kan and Li — a string of code words that come down to a single act: concentrating the spirit and gathering the breath
  12. The Celestial Circuit — the circuit is led by the mind, not by guiding the breath; a summary of the whole part

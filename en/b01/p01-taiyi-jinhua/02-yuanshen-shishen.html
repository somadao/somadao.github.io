Chapter 2 · Original Spirit and Cognitive Spirit — who should be in charge

About This Chapter

There are two “hearts” in a person. The Original Spirit (yuanshen 元神) lives in the “Square Inch” inside the head; it is quiet and does nothing. The cognitive spirit (shishen 识神) lives in the heart of flesh and blood in the chest; it loves movement and is full of emotion. Ordinarily the cognitive spirit has usurped power and the Original Spirit has been pushed aside. Reverting the Light means putting the Original Spirit back on the throne. The chapter also presents the “three treasures” of alchemy — the Water of Essence, the Fire of Spirit and the Earth of Intention — and the difference between the hun and po souls. Its conclusion: Reverting the Light is the one and only formula for “dissolving the po and making the hun whole,” and for returning to pure yang.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

凡人投胎时，元神居方寸，而识神则居下心。下面血肉心，形如大桃，有肺以覆翼之，肝以佐之，大小肠承之。假如一日不食，心上便大不自在，以致闻惊则跳，闻怒则闷，见死亡则悲，见美色则眩。头上何尝微微有些儿动，方寸中之真意如何能动？到动时便不妙，然亦最妙。凡人死时方动，此为不妙；最妙者，光已凝结为法身，渐渐灵通欲动矣！此千古不传之秘也。

When a person enters the womb, the Original Spirit takes up residence in the Square Inch (fangcun 方寸) inside the head, while the cognitive spirit takes up residence in the heart below. This heart of flesh and blood below is shaped like a large peach; the lungs cover it like wings, the liver assists it, and the large and small intestines support it from beneath. If you go a single day without eating, this heart becomes very uncomfortable: it jumps at a fright, feels oppressed at an angry word, grieves at the sight of death, and is dazzled at the sight of beauty. But the one in the head — when has it ever moved in the slightest? How could the True Intention (zhenyi 真意) within the Square Inch move? When it does move, things are bad — and yet this can also be the best of all. For ordinary people it moves only at the moment of death: that is what is bad. The best case is when the light has already congealed into the Dharma Body, gradually becomes spiritually responsive, and is about to move! This is a secret that has not been passed on for a thousand ages.

§2
Original (Chinese)

下识心如强藩悍将，欺天君孤立，便尔遥执纪纲，久之太阿倒置矣！今拟光照元宫，如英明主有伊周佐之，日日回光，如左右臣工，尽心辅弼，内政既肃，自然一切奸邪，无不倒戈乞命矣！

The cognitive heart below is like a powerful regional warlord or an unruly general. Taking advantage of the Heavenly Ruler’s isolation, it seizes the reins of government from afar, and over time it becomes “holding the Tai’e sword by the blade” — the hilt is in someone else’s hand, and master and servant have changed places. Now, to make the light shine back into the Original Palace is like an enlightened ruler gaining the help of worthy ministers such as Yi Yin and the Duke of Zhou. Reverting the Light every day is like the ministers on the left and right giving their whole hearts to assisting him. Once the internal government is put in order, all the treacherous and wicked will naturally turn their weapons around and beg for mercy!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§2
Original note (Chinese)

千古不传之秘，岂仅光凝法身并渐渐灵通能动一节，尚有教人懂得元神居方寸，识神居下心之论。方寸亦喻心，但有别于血肉之下识心。识神触景动情，元神若有动摇便为不妙。学者有误认识心为心而加以运注者，便着有为，以致助火。盖此血肉心体乃识神所依，属阴火，惟宜致寂致虚而致无者。下识心作祟之患，惟回光可以制之。

The “secret not passed on for a thousand ages” is not only the point that “the light congeals into the Dharma Body, gradually becomes spiritually responsive and can move.” It also lies in teaching people that “the Original Spirit lives in the Square Inch and the cognitive spirit lives in the heart below.” The Square Inch is also a figure for the heart, but it is different from the cognitive heart below, in the body of flesh and blood. The cognitive spirit is stirred to emotion by whatever it meets; if the Original Spirit is shaken by it, things go badly. Some students mistake the cognitive heart for “the heart” and use their intention to work on it and pour attention into it. They then fall into “deliberate action” and end up feeding the fire. For this heart of flesh and blood is what the cognitive spirit relies on; it belongs to yin fire and should only be brought to stillness, emptiness and nothingness. Only Reverting the Light can subdue the trouble caused by the cognitive heart below.

§3
Original (Chinese)

丹道以精水、神火、意土三者为无上之宝。精水云何？乃先天真一之炁；神火即光也；意土即中宫天心也。以神火为用，意土为体，精水为基。盖身有魄焉，魄附识而用，识依魄而生。魄阴也，识之体也。惟有魂者，神之所藏也。故回光即所以炼魂，即所以保神，即所以制魄，即所以断识。古人炼尽阴滓以返纯乾，不过消魄全魂耳。回光者，消阴制魄之诀也。无返乾之功，祇有回光之诀。光即乾也。回之即返之也。只守此法，自然精足，神火发生，意土凝定，而圣胎可结矣！

The way of the elixir regards three things as supreme treasures: the Water of Essence, the Fire of Spirit and the Earth of Intention. What is the Water of Essence? It is the One Breath of Prior Heaven, the true and single breath. The Fire of Spirit is the light. The Earth of Intention is the Celestial Heart in the Central Palace. The Fire of Spirit is the function, the Earth of Intention is the substance, and the Water of Essence is the foundation. The body has a po soul; the po attaches itself to cognition in order to act, and cognition relies on the po in order to live. The po is yin; it is the substance of cognition. Only the hun soul is where the spirit is stored. So Reverting the Light is the way to refine the hun, to preserve the spirit, to subdue the po, and to cut off cognition. When the ancients refined away all the yin dregs to return to pure Qian, they were doing nothing more than “dissolving the po and making the hun whole.” Reverting the Light is the formula for dissolving yin and subduing the po. There is no other practice for returning to Qian; there is only the formula of Reverting the Light. The light is Qian; to revert it is to return to Qian. If you simply keep to this method, essence will naturally become abundant, the Fire of Spirit will arise, the Earth of Intention will settle and congeal, and the Holy Embryo (shengtai 圣胎) can be formed!

§4
Original (Chinese)

一灵真性既落乾宫，便分魂魄。魂在天心，阳也，轻清之气也。此自太虚得来，与元始同形。魄阴也，沉浊之气也，附于有形之凡体。魂好生，魄望死。一切好色动气，皆魄之所为，即识神也。学人炼尽阴魄，即为纯阳。

As soon as the single spiritual point of true nature falls into the Palace of Qian (the head), it divides into hun and po. The hun is in the Celestial Heart; it is yang, a light and clear breath. It comes from the Great Void and has the same form as the primordial origin of the cosmos. The po is yin, a heavy and turbid breath, attached to the ordinary physical body. The hun loves life; the po longs for death. All lust and anger are the doing of the po — that is, of the cognitive spirit. When students refine away the yin po completely, they become pure yang.

§5Old commentary · Min Yide's note
Original (Chinese)

按此功法，究其入手，以回光聚天池，是由泥丸外宫悬于天目，有如日然。以意引由绛阙，存照中黄，透入玄窍，乃达神室。既则牵降识神，下达下田。其时必有津液护识护神而就冶炼者也。此是回光光聚泥丸以后功法，大忌躁妄，又忌散漫与昏沉。法惟万虑皆空，一念不扰，待得天心一开，则自油然照入。是时也，不独一身百窍，窍窍放光，大地天元、三才三宝，皆可悠然感至者。故我斯时，总以不采采之，其妙更无穷焉。盖神室毗连绛阙。绛阙一地，纯以无作无为为事。如是寂体寂照，绛阙乃凉，识神有制，始自随神下降下田，受烹受炼，而无逆违之验。其妙在于一念虚寂，则六贼六根自无驻足处，中宫始泰，元神得以临莅，而胎元有兆矣。

(Note by Min Yide, eleventh-generation master of the Longmen lineage on Mount Jingai:) As for where this practice begins: through Reverting the Light, gather the light at the Celestial Pool, that is, the Muddy Pellet. From the outer palace of the Muddy Pellet the light hangs over the Celestial Eye like a sun. Then, with the intention, lead the light through the Crimson Gate-Tower (jiangque 绛阙, the same as the Crimson Palace, 1.3 cun inward from the shanzhong point between the breasts), hold it shining on the Central Yellow (behind the heart and in front of the twin passes of the spine), let it penetrate the Mysterious Opening, and so reach the Chamber of the Spirit. Next, draw the cognitive spirit down to the lower Cinnabar Field. At this time there will certainly be saliva, which protects the cognitive spirit and the Original Spirit as they go together to be smelted. This is the practice that follows after the light has been gathered at the Muddy Pellet. Above all, avoid impatience and rash action; also avoid laxity and torpor. The only method is to let all thoughts become empty, with not a single thought disturbing you, and wait until the Celestial Heart opens; then the light will flow in of its own accord. At that time, not only do the hundred openings of the body each give off light, but the great earth, the origin of Heaven, the Three Powers (Heaven, Earth and Humanity) and the three treasures (essence, breath and spirit) can all be felt arriving, at ease. So at this time I always “gather without gathering” — not trying to gather, and yet gathering naturally — and its wonders are all the more endless. For the Chamber of the Spirit adjoins the Crimson Gate-Tower, and the Crimson Gate-Tower deals purely in non-doing and non-contrivance. With the substance still and its illumination still, the Crimson Gate-Tower becomes cool; the cognitive spirit is restrained and only then willingly follows the Original Spirit down to the lower Cinnabar Field to be cooked and refined, without any sign of resistance. The wonder lies in a single thought of empty stillness: then the “six thieves,” the six roots — eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body and mind — have nowhere to set foot; only then is the Central Palace at peace, the Original Spirit can come to preside, and the first signs of the embryo appear.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3–§5
Original note (Chinese)

金盖山人龙门十一代闵一得对此节有注云：（注文见上。注中“天池”下夹注：即泥丸；“绛阙”下编者按：即绛宫，在两乳之间的膻中穴内深入一寸三分；“中黄”下夹注：位于心后关前，关即夹脊双关，在人背脊24节上下之正中，即脊椎第七、八胸椎之间，直对膻中穴处。）

此段注，为天心章诀中捷诀一节功法中“既即以意引临心后关前”一句注脚及详解，亦即消阴制魄之法。

On the Water of Essence, Fire of Spirit and Earth of Intention, and on the hun and po of §3 and §4, Qiu Ling does not add his own explanation; instead he quotes the note by Min Yide of Mount Jingai, eleventh-generation master of the Longmen lineage — the passage given above. Qiu Ling added small notes on several locations in it: the Celestial Pool is the Muddy Pellet; the Crimson Gate-Tower is the Crimson Palace, 1.3 cun deep inward from the shanzhong point between the breasts; the Central Yellow lies behind the heart and in front of the Pass, the “Pass” being the twin passes of the spine, located in the middle of the twenty-four vertebrae of the back, between the seventh and eighth thoracic vertebrae, directly opposite the shanzhong point.

Qiu Ling says: this note is a footnote and detailed explanation of the sentence “then, with your intention, lead this light to the place behind the heart and in front of the Pass” in the “shortcut within the formula” of the chapter on the Celestial Heart; it is also the method for dissolving yin and subduing the po.

Key Terms

  • Original Spirit (yuanshen): lives in the Square Inch in the head; quiet and non-acting, it is the “Heavenly Ruler,” the “sovereign.”
  • cognitive spirit (shishen) / the heart below: attached to the heart of flesh and blood in the chest; it loves movement and is full of emotion.
  • Square Inch (fangcun): in this chapter, the place of the Original Spirit in the head — hence the later line “when has the one in the head ever moved in the slightest?”
  • Original Palace: the palace of the Original Spirit, where the Square Inch and the Celestial Heart are.
  • “Holding the Tai’e sword by the blade”: Tai’e was a famous ancient sword; holding it by the blade puts the hilt in someone else’s hand — a figure for losing power to others.
  • Yi Yin and the Duke of Zhou: Yi Yin assisted King Tang of Shang, the Duke of Zhou assisted King Cheng of Zhou; both are models of the worthy minister.
  • Water of Essence, Fire of Spirit, Earth of Intention: this book’s version of the three treasures of alchemy. The Water of Essence is the One Breath of Prior Heaven (see Essence), the Fire of Spirit is the light (see Spirit), the Earth of Intention is the Celestial Heart in the Central Palace (see True Intention).
  • hun and po: the hun is yang, light and clear, and stores the spirit; the po is yin, heavy and turbid, and attaches to cognition.
  • pure Qian / returning to Qian: returning to pure yang. The light is Qian; to revert the light is to return to Qian.
  • Holy Embryo: a metaphor for the middle stage of alchemical cultivation, when spirit and breath congeal like an embryo.
  • Muddy Pellet (Celestial Pool), Crimson Palace (Crimson Gate-Tower), Central Yellow, the Chamber of the Spirit, the lower field (lower Cinnabar Field): the route of the light in Min Yide’s note.
  • The six thieves, the six roots: a Buddhist expression. The six roots — eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body and mind — lead a person to chase after outer things, and so are called the six thieves.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Recognize the two hearts: the emotions, plans and judgments that pop up during sitting are the cognitive spirit; the clear awareness that watches all of this without being carried away is close to the Original Spirit.
  2. Do not press on the chest: the cognitive spirit is attached to the heart of flesh and blood in the chest. Qiu Ling warns that if you mistake the cognitive heart for the “heart” you should concentrate on, and press your attention on it, you fall into deliberate action and “feed the fire,” which easily causes a tight chest and restless heat. Here only emptiness and stillness are appropriate.
  3. What Reverting the Light means: each time you bring your attention back, the ruler is “holding court.” There is no need to fight the cognitive spirit; once the master is present, it submits of its own accord.
  4. The route given by Min Yide (advanced): gather the light at the Muddy Pellet → pass through the Crimson Palace → hold it shining on the Central Yellow → let it penetrate the Mysterious Opening → lead the cognitive spirit down to the lower Cinnabar Field. The key is “gathering without gathering”: not impatient, not lax, not drowsy, all thoughts empty, waiting for the Celestial Heart to open by itself. Beginners need not deliberately follow this route.

Translator's Notes

  1. “As soon as the single spiritual point of true nature falls into the Palace of Qian”: Qian stands for the head and for Heaven, so this is usually understood to mean that true nature descends into the head of the body; some read it simply as “falls into the human body.” We follow the first reading, which matches “the Original Spirit lives in the Square Inch” and “the one in the head” in this chapter.
  2. “Has the same form as the primordial origin” (yuanshi 元始): yuanshi means the primordial origin of the cosmos (it can also refer to the deity Yuanshi tianzun, the Celestial Worthy of Primordial Beginning); we translate it as “the primordial origin of the cosmos.”
  3. “Regards three things as supreme treasures”: another edition reads “supreme formulas,” and adds the glosses “spirit,” “heart” and “body” under the three items. We follow Qiu Ling’s base text.
  4. “There is no other practice for returning to Qian; there is only the formula of Reverting the Light”: the original line is very terse (literally “no practice of returning to Qian, only the formula of Reverting the Light”); it means that to return to pure Qian there is no other practice to use — only this one formula.
  5. The small notes on locations in Min Yide’s commentary (the Celestial Pool is the Muddy Pellet; the Crimson Gate-Tower is the Crimson Palace, 1.3 cun inward from shanzhong; the Central Yellow is behind the heart and in front of the twin passes of the spine, roughly between the seventh and eighth thoracic vertebrae, opposite shanzhong) are interlinear notes in Qiu Ling’s book; the one on the Crimson Palace is explicitly marked as an editor’s note.

Chapter 10 · The Light of Nature and the Light of Cognition

Chapter 10 · The Light of Nature and the Light of Cognition — what to do once the light appears

About This Chapter

When the practice reaches a certain point, you may “see light” in stillness. This chapter answers one question: is this light true or false? The answer is subtle: the light itself is not the problem — the problem is whether you “cling” to it. When ordinary people look at something, the first glance, before they have had time to discriminate, is the light of nature; as soon as discrimination begins, it becomes the light of cognition. In the same way, if you see light in stillness and grab at it with the mind, you fall into ordinary consciousness; only letting go is the light of nature. The conclusion of the chapter: if cognition is not cut off, the spirit is not born; if the mind is not empty, the elixir does not form.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

回光法原通行、住、坐、卧，只要自得机窍，虚室生白，光非白耶。但有一说，初未见光时，此为效验；若见为光，而有意著之，即落意识，非性光也。不管他有光无光，只要无念生念。何谓无念？千休千处得。何谓生念？一念一生持。此念乃正念，与平日念不同。今心为念，念者现在心也，此心即光即药。

The method of Reverting the Light was always meant for walking, standing, sitting and lying down; you need only find the knack yourself. “The empty chamber produces whiteness” — when the mind is empty, brightness arises within; and is not the light white? But there is one point to note. At first, before you have seen the light, its appearance is a sign of progress. But if, once you see the light, you deliberately cling to it, you fall into ordinary consciousness, and it is no longer the light of nature. Whether there is light or not, just practice “no-thought” and “arising thought.” What is no-thought? “Let go in a thousand places, and gain in a thousand places.” What is arising thought? “One thought, held for a whole life.” This thought is right thought, different from the thoughts of ordinary days. The character for “thought,” nian 念, is made of jin 今 (“now”) and xin 心 (“mind”): thought is the present mind. This present mind is the light; it is the Medicine.

§2
Original (Chinese)

凡人视物，任眼一照去，不及分别，此为性光，如镜之无心而照也，如水之无心而鉴也。少顷，即为识光。以其分别也，镜有影已无镜矣，水有像已非水矣。光有识，尚何光哉？

When ordinary people look at things, the eyes shine on them at a glance, before there is time to discriminate: this is the light of nature — like a mirror reflecting without intention, like water mirroring without intention. A moment later it is the light of cognition, because discrimination has begun: once there is an image in the mirror, it is no longer a (pure) mirror; once there is a reflection on the water, it is no longer (pure) water. When cognition is mixed into the light, what light is left?

§3
Original (Chinese)

用心即为识光，放下乃为性光，毫厘千里，不可不辨。识不断，则神不生。心不空，则丹不结。

Grasping with the mind is the light of cognition; letting go is the light of nature. A hair’s breadth of difference here leads a thousand miles astray; you must tell them apart. If the cognitive spirit is not cut off, the Original Spirit is not born; if the mind is not empty, the elixir does not form.

Qiu Ling's noteon the whole chapter
Original note (Chinese)

此教人常见性光，而勿为识光所迷。见光而勿著意勿用心，但求空灵放下，便得之矣！

This chapter teaches people to keep seeing the light of nature and not to be deluded by the light of cognition. When you see the light, do not fix your intention on it, do not use your mind on it; seek only to be empty, open and letting go — and you have it!

Key Terms

  • Light of nature: the light as it is, before any discrimination; the function of the Original Spirit.
  • Light of cognition: as soon as there is discrimination and clinging, it becomes the light of cognition; the function of the cognitive spirit.
  • The empty chamber produces whiteness: see Chapter 6.
  • No-thought: “let go in a thousand places, and gain in a thousand places” — letting go everywhere.
  • Arising thought / right thought: “one thought, held for a whole life” — one right thought, held for a long time. Note that “arising thought” here does not mean scattered thoughts arising, but giving rise to and holding a right thought.
  • Present mind: a breakdown of the character nian 念 (“thought”) = jin 今 (“now”) + xin 心 (“mind”).
  • Medicine: this chapter says “this present mind is the light; it is the Medicine,” which echoes Chapter 12, “the Medicine… is the light of nature.”

Putting It into Practice

  1. When you see light, do not chase it: if a sense of light appears in stillness, just know it; do not stare at it or wonder “what is this?” or “how can I make it brighter?” As soon as you chase it, it becomes the light of cognition.
  2. When you do not see light, do not worry: “whether there is light or not” — whether you see light is not the measure of your practice.
  3. Practice the “first glance”: in daily life you can taste it: the clarity of the very first instant of looking at something, before you put a label on it — that is the flavor of the light of nature.
  4. Grasping and letting go: the whole chapter turns on one sentence: “Grasping with the mind is the light of cognition; letting go is the light of nature.”

Translator's Notes

  1. “Walking, standing, sitting and lying down”: the base text has xing zhi zuo wo 行止坐卧; following the common editions and the sense, zhi 止 should be zhu 住 (“standing”), and the text has been corrected accordingly.
  2. “Let go in a thousand places, and gain in a thousand places; one thought, held for a whole life”: the two lines form a parallel formula — the first about letting go (no-thought), the second about holding (right thought).

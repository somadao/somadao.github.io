Chapter 3 · Reverting the Light and Guarding the Center

Chapter 3 · Reverting the Light and Guarding the Center — reversal, the real meaning of “looking at the tip of the nose,” cessation and contemplation

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

This is the central and most practical chapter of the whole part. It covers four things. First, why Reverting the Light is a “method of reversal”: the eyes are the easiest exit through which the spirit leaks outward, so they must be turned back. Second, where “turning the illumination back” (fanzhao 返照) actually returns to — to the moment before a thought arises, before form and spirit show any sign. Third, the real meaning of “looking at the tip of the nose”: it only gives the eyes a reference point; once that is set, you let it go. Fourth, Reverting the Light is the same as the Buddhist practice of cessation and contemplation (zhiguan 止观): cessation is the “reverting,” contemplation is the “light.” Two old commentaries by Min Yide add an advanced method of “sending out the light” and the sequence “first gather the light at the Celestial Eye, then let it flow down into the Central Yellow.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

回光，则天地阴阳之气无不凝，所谓精思者此也，纯气者此也，纯想者此也。初行之诀，是有中似无，久之功成，身外有身，乃无中生有。百日专功光才真，方为神火。百日后，光自然聚，一点真阳，忽生黍珠，如夫妇交合有胎，便当静以待之。光之回，即火候也。

Revert the light, and the yin and yang breaths of Heaven and Earth all congeal. This is what the ancients meant by “refined thought,” by “pure breath,” and by “pure thought.” The formula when you first practice is “within being, as if non-being”: acting, yet acting as if not acting. After long practice, when the work is accomplished and there is a body beyond the body, that is “being born out of non-being.” Only after a hundred days of dedicated work is the light true and worthy of the name “Fire of Spirit.” After a hundred days the light gathers by itself; a point of true yang suddenly produces a grain-sized pearl, like a husband and wife conceiving a child. At that point you should wait for it quietly. The reverting of the light is itself the fire phasing (huohou 火候).

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

久之功成，身外有身，即自然法身之显现，已如上述。至一点真阳忽生黍珠，即结丹也，此为回光的最重要成就之一。回光既久，虚室生白，眼前荧光屏幕忽生一小白点，起先游移不定，渐大渐定，变成亮晶晶的一个○，即慧光也。《性命圭旨》一书描写为“圆陀陀，活泼泼，如露如电，非雾非烟，辉煌闪烁，光照昆仑。放则迸开天地窍，归兮隐入翠微宫。”该书又有诗云：“一颗金丹何赫赤，大似弹丸黄似橘。人人分上本圆明，夜夜灵光照神室。”即此之谓也。此丹光一现，今后变化由此而生。

“After long practice, when the work is accomplished and there is a body beyond the body” refers to the appearance of the spontaneous Dharma Body, already discussed above. As for “a point of true yang suddenly produces a grain-sized pearl,” this is the forming of the elixir, one of the most important accomplishments of Reverting the Light. After reverting the light for a long time, “the empty chamber produces whiteness”: on what seems like a glowing screen before the eyes, a small white dot suddenly appears. At first it drifts about, then it gradually grows larger and steadier until it becomes a sparkling circle, “○” — this is the light of wisdom. The Book of Inner Nature and Life (Xingming guizhi 性命圭旨) describes it like this: “Perfectly round, lively and alive, like dew, like lightning; not mist, not smoke; brilliant and flashing, its light shines on Kunlun (the top of the head). Sent out, it bursts open the openings of Heaven and Earth; called back, it hides in the Palace of Azure Subtlety.” The same book has a poem: “A single grain of Golden Elixir — how red it glows! / Big as a pellet, yellow as an orange. / In every person it is originally round and bright; / night after night its spiritual light shines in the Chamber of the Spirit.” That is what is meant here. Once this light of the elixir appears, all later transformations arise from it.

§2Old commentary · Min Yide's note
Original (Chinese)

回光之益之妙，本文详矣。回光得聚之诀亦备矣。然犹有欲取先与玄妙一诀，可引而伸之。其诀乃放光以引耳。放光妙用，在知廓其气机。欲廓气机，在知气透九霄。欲行上透，须知下达。下达作用，须先将目光聚于乾宫，光足则下达中下，穿闾后透，透顶而上。透愈高，现愈广。觉广，仍以事回耳。此未传之秘也。

(Note by Min Yide:) The benefits and wonders of Reverting the Light have been described in detail in the main text, and the formula for gathering the reverted light is complete. But there is still one more subtle formula — “to take, first give” — which can be drawn out and developed. This formula is “sending out the light in order to draw it in.” The wonderful use of sending out the light lies in knowing how to expand one’s mechanism of breath (qiji 气机). To expand the mechanism of breath, one must know how to let the breath pass through to the ninth heaven. To pass through upward, one must first know how to reach downward. The way to reach downward is this: first gather the light of the eyes in the Palace of Qian (the Muddy Pellet). When the light is abundant, let it reach down to the middle and lower parts of the body, pass through the Coccyx, rise up the back, go through the top of the head and continue upward. The higher it passes, the wider it shows. When you feel it has become wide, bring it back again. This is a secret never before transmitted.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

闵一得氏对此节有一注，虽未涉及结丹事，持论亦颇精对，乃经验之谈。（注文见上。注中“穿闾”下按：即尾闾，从脊椎下往上数，第三椎与第四椎之间是也。）

此功法欲取先与，引而伸之，实欲进入人天合一境界，为回光正功的重要补充。放光妙用须先将目光聚于泥丸，待光足用意念引光下达身体中下部，穿尾闾从背后透上顶，更用意念扩大气机，气透九霄，与周围大自然之气之光融成一片，则透愈高现愈广，最后再用意念收回放出之光，神返身中气自回，免致消耗元气，有损身体气机，此至关紧要者也。此功可使人通体生白，似消失于大自然光芒之中。

Min Yide has a note on this section — the one given above. Although it does not touch on forming the elixir, its view is quite precise and comes from experience. For the “Coccyx” (lü 闾) in his note, Qiu Ling adds: this is the Caudal Funnel (weilü 尾闾), between the third and fourth vertebrae counting upward from the bottom of the spine.

This method — “to take, first give,” drawn out and developed — actually aims at entering the state in which humanity and Heaven become one; it is an important supplement to the main practice of Reverting the Light. To make wonderful use of sending out the light, first gather the light of the eyes in the Muddy Pellet. When the light is abundant, use the intention to lead it down to the middle and lower parts of the body, through the Caudal Funnel, and up the back through the top of the head. Then use the intention to expand the mechanism of breath, letting the breath pass through to the ninth heaven and merge with the breath and light of the surrounding natural world; thus “the higher it passes, the wider it shows.” Finally — and this is the most important point — you must use the intention to bring back the light you sent out. When the spirit returns into the body, the breath returns by itself; this avoids wasting the original breath and harming the body’s mechanism of breath. This practice can make the whole body “produce whiteness,” as if dissolving into the radiance of nature.

§3
Original (Chinese)

夫元化之中，有阳光为主宰。有形者为日，在人为目，走漏神识，莫此甚顺。故光华之道，全用逆法。回光者，非回一身之精华，直回造化之真气；非止一时之妄想，直空千劫之轮迴。凡人自囫囫地一声之后，逐境顺生，至老未尝逆视，阳气衰灭，便是九幽之界。故《楞严经》云：「纯想即飞，纯情即堕」。学人想少情多，沉沦下道，惟谛观息静，便成正觉，用逆法也。《阴符经》云：「机在目」。《黄帝素问》云：人身精华，皆上注于空窍是也。得此一节，长生者在兹。此贯彻三教功夫也。

In the great transformation of the cosmos, a yang light is the master. In visible form, it is the sun in the sky and the eyes in a person. There is no easier exit through which the spirit and consciousness leak out than the eyes. Therefore the way of the light relies entirely on the method of reversal. Reverting the Light does not merely turn back the essence of one’s own body; it turns back the true breath of creation itself. It does not merely stop a moment’s deluded thoughts; it empties out the cycle of rebirth of a thousand kalpas. From the first cry at birth, ordinary people follow outer circumstances and grow outward; until old age they never once look back inward, and their yang breath declines and is extinguished — that is the realm of the Nine Darknesses. So the Śūraṅgama Sūtra says: “Pure thought, and one flies upward; pure passion, and one falls.” Students who have little thought and much passion sink into the lower paths. Only by attentive contemplation and stilling the breath does one attain right awakening — this is using the method of reversal. The Scripture of the Hidden Accordance (Yinfu jing 阴符经) says: “The trigger is in the eyes.” The Basic Questions of the Yellow Emperor (Huangdi suwen 黄帝素问) says that the essence of the human body all flows upward into the empty openings. Grasp this point, and long life lies here. This is a practice that runs through all three teachings — Confucianism, Buddhism and Daoism.

§4
Original (Chinese)

光不在身中，亦不在身外。山河日月大地无非此光，故不独在身中。聪明智慧，一切运转，亦无非此光，所以亦不在身外。天地之光华布满大千，一身之光华亦自漫天盖地，所以一回光，天地山河一切皆回矣！人之精华，上注于目，此人身之大关键也。一日不静坐，此光流转，何所底止？若一刻静坐，万劫千生从此了彻。万法归于静，真不可思议，此妙谛也。然功夫下手，由浅入深，由粗入细，总以不间断为妙。功夫始终则一，但其间冷暖自知。要归于天空海阔，万法如如，方为得手。圣圣相传，不离返照。孔云知止，释号观心，老云内观，皆此法也。但返照二字，人人能言，不能得手，未识二字之义耳。返者，自知觉之心返乎形神未兆之初，即吾六尺之中，返求个天地未生之体。今人但一二时闲坐，反顾其私，便云返照，安得到头？

The light is neither inside the body nor outside it. Mountains and rivers, sun and moon, the great earth — all are nothing but this light, so it is not only inside the body. Intelligence, wisdom and all our activity are also nothing but this light, so it is not outside the body either. The radiance of Heaven and Earth fills the great chiliocosm, and the radiance of a single body likewise covers heaven and earth. So as soon as you revert the light, Heaven and Earth, mountains and rivers — everything returns with it! A person’s essence flows upward into the eyes: this is the great hinge of the human body. If you do not sit in stillness for a single day, this light keeps flowing outward — where will it ever stop? If you sit in stillness for even a moment, the affairs of ten thousand kalpas and a thousand lives can be settled from that moment on. All things return to stillness: this is truly inconceivable, a wondrous truth. But in beginning the practice, go from shallow to deep, from coarse to fine; above all, it is best not to interrupt it. From beginning to end the practice is one and the same, but how it feels along the way — cold or warm — only you will know. Only when your mind becomes as open as the sky and as wide as the sea, and all things are simply as they are, have you truly got hold of it. The sages have handed it down from one to the next, and it never departs from “turning the illumination back.” Confucius spoke of “knowing where to stop,” the Buddhists call it “contemplating the mind,” Laozi spoke of “inner contemplation” — all are this method. Yet although everyone can say the words “turning the illumination back,” few can actually do it, because they do not understand what the words mean. “Turning back” means that the mind that knows and perceives returns to the very beginning, before form and spirit show any sign; that is, within this six-foot body of mine, seeking back for the substance that existed before Heaven and Earth were born. People today sit idly for an hour or two, look back over their own private affairs, and call this “turning the illumination back.” How could they ever reach the end of the road?

Qiu Ling's noteon §3–§4
Original note (Chinese)

此节论目之作用及回光返照全用逆法。人身精华皆上注于目者，即《灵枢·邪气脏腑病形篇》中所说：“十二经脉，三百六十五络，其血气皆上于面而走空窍，其精阳气上走于目而为睛。”盖弩之发动在机，心之缘引在目，机不动则弩住，目不动则心住，故曰机在于目。人若顺视，迷于色欲，逐物流转，则走漏神识属甚。回光，逆视也；返照，乃返乎形神未兆之初，所谓制于外所以养其中，合乎返还之道。

This section discusses the function of the eyes and why Reverting the Light relies entirely on the method of reversal. “The essence of the human body all flows upward into the eyes” is what the Divine Pivot (Lingshu 灵枢), in the chapter “Pathogenic Breath, the Organs and the Forms of Disease,” says: “The blood and breath of the twelve channels and three hundred and sixty-five collaterals all rise to the face and run into the openings; their essential yang breath rises to the eyes and becomes the eyeballs.” A crossbow fires by its trigger; the mind is drawn outward by the eyes. When the trigger does not move, the crossbow stays still; when the eyes do not move, the mind stays still. That is why it is said “the trigger is in the eyes.” If a person looks outward along the usual direction, infatuated with sensual desire and chasing after things, the leakage of spirit and consciousness is at its worst. Reverting the Light is looking back in the reverse direction; “turning the illumination back” is returning to the beginning, before form and spirit show any sign. This is what the ancients called “restraining the outside in order to nourish the inside,” in accord with the way of return.

§5
Original (Chinese)

佛道二祖教人看鼻尖者，非谓著念于鼻端也，亦非谓眼观鼻端而念又注中黄也。眼之所至，心亦至焉，心之所至，气亦至焉，何能一上而一下也？又何能忽上而忽下也？此皆认指为月。毕竟如何？曰鼻端二字最妙，只是借鼻以为眼之准耳。初不在鼻上，盖以太开眼，则视远而不见鼻矣；太闭眼，则眼合而不见鼻矣。太开失之外走，易于散乱；太闭失之内驰，易于昏沉。惟垂帘得中，恰好望见鼻端，故以为准。只是垂帘恰好去处，彼光自然透入，不劳你注射与不注射也。

When the patriarchs of Buddhism and Daoism teach people to look at the tip of the nose, they do not mean that you should fix your thought on the tip of the nose, nor that your eyes should look at the tip of the nose while your thought is placed on the Central Yellow. Where the eyes go, the mind goes too; where the mind goes, the breath goes too. How could one be up and the other down? Or now up, now down? All this is mistaking the finger for the moon. So what is it really? The answer: the words “tip of the nose” are most subtle — the nose is simply borrowed as a reference point for the eyes; the point was never the nose itself. If you open your eyes too wide, your gaze goes far away and you no longer see the nose; if you close them too tightly, the lids shut and you do not see the nose either. Opening too wide errs by running outward and easily leads to distraction; closing too tightly errs by rushing inward and easily leads to torpor. Only when the eyelids are lowered like curtains just right can you faintly see the tip of the nose — so it is taken as the reference point. Just lower the curtains to the right position, and the light will penetrate by itself; you need not trouble to project it, nor to refrain from projecting it.

§6
Original (Chinese)

看鼻端只在最初入静处，举眼一视，定个准则，便放下，如泥水匠人用线一般，彼只起手一挂，便依了做去，不只管把线看也。

Looking at the tip of the nose is only for the very beginning, as you enter stillness: raise your eyes, take one look, set the reference, and then let it go. It is like a mason using a plumb line: he hangs the line once before he starts and then works along it; he does not keep staring at the line.

§7Old commentary · Min Yide's note
Original (Chinese)

天心章言以意引临心后关前，是示功夫已到之人，统说回光之全功也。此言不必念又注中黄，是教初学凝神一处以聚光，不可分心两处也。待得光透入之后，则鼻尖是指，中黄是月。看鼻尖者，用以为眼之准，使无外走内驰之弊，惟垂帘为得中。然意初不在鼻而在天目，所以聚光于此。光既得聚，则又须引光下注中黄。盖中黄在人身地天之正中，即《易》之黄中，释氏所谓缘中，吾宗名曰玄牝之门，乃是生天、生地、生人、生物之玄窍，修真成道之基著基于此者，初学如何便得注此，故须假鼻尖以为准，始得光聚天目。天目为三光之都会，而山根为人身之性户，上达泥丸，中达中黄，下通脐后者，故须聚光于此处，由此而下注，是乃不易之功法。然忌太著意，又忌无意，兼忌躐等而进，其理如此，必须循序而行，尤须无滞无脱，密密绵绵，一任自然，总以光聚中黄为得也。

(Note by Min Yide:) When the chapter on the Celestial Heart says “with your intention, lead this light to the place behind the heart and in front of the Pass,” it is speaking to people whose practice is already ripe, and it describes the whole practice of Reverting the Light at once. When this chapter says your thought need not also be placed on the Central Yellow, it is teaching beginners to concentrate the spirit on one place in order to gather the light, and not to divide the mind between two places. Once the light has penetrated, the tip of the nose is the finger and the Central Yellow is the moon. Looking at the tip of the nose serves as a reference for the eyes, so that they neither run outward nor rush inward; only lowering the curtains strikes the right balance. But from the start the intention is not on the nose — it is on the Celestial Eye, so as to gather the light there. Once the light has gathered, you must then lead it down to flow into the Central Yellow. For the Central Yellow is at the exact center of Heaven and Earth within the human body. It is the “yellow center” of the Book of Changes, what the Buddhists call the “center of conditions” (yuanzhong 缘中), and what our school calls the Gate of the Mysterious Female. It is the Mysterious Opening that gives birth to Heaven, Earth, humanity and all things, and the foundation for cultivating reality and attaining the Dao is built here. How could a beginner pour the light into it straight away? So the tip of the nose must be borrowed as a reference, so that the light can first gather at the Celestial Eye. The Celestial Eye is the capital where the Three Lights meet, and the Root of the Mountain is the “door of inner nature” in the human body, reaching up to the Muddy Pellet, through the middle to the Central Yellow, and down to the place behind the navel. Therefore the light must first be gathered here and then poured down from here — this is an unchanging method. But avoid too much intention, and also avoid no intention; and avoid skipping stages. That is the principle. You must proceed in order, and above all neither cling nor let go, but keep it fine and continuous, leaving everything to nature. Success is always measured by the light gathering in the Central Yellow.

闵一得氏对此节有注云：（注文见上。注中“中黄是月”下夹注：此用“直指见月”法，以鼻尖为准则，内视中黄似皎明如月。）

§8Old commentary · *Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate*
Original (Chinese)

盖常人呼吸，皆从咽喉而下，至中脘而回，不能与祖气相通，所谓众人之息以喉也。若至人呼吸，直贯明堂而上，盖切切然以意守夹脊双关，自然通于天心一窍，得与元始祖气相连，如磁吸铁，而同类相亲，即庄子所谓真人之息以踵也。踵者深也。

欲通此窍，先要穷想山根，则呼吸之气方渐次而通夹脊，透泥丸，以达于天心祖窍，而子母会合，破镜重圆，渐渐扩充，则根本完固，救住命宝，始可言修炼功夫。行之既久，一呼一吸入于气穴，乃自然而然之妙也。

(The Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate by the Perfected Yin says:) The breathing of ordinary people goes down from the throat to the zhongwan — four cun above the navel — and turns back there; it cannot connect with the Ancestral Breath. This is what Zhuangzi calls “the breathing of ordinary people is from the throat.” But the breathing of an accomplished person passes straight through the Hall of Light (mingtang 明堂, 1.2 cun inward from the exact center between the eyebrows) and upward, because he earnestly and attentively guards the twin passes of the spine (jiaji 夹脊) with his intention. Naturally it connects with the opening of the Celestial Heart and links up with the primordial Ancestral Breath, like a magnet attracting iron, like drawn to like. This is what Zhuangzi calls “the breathing of the true person is from the heels.” “Heels” means deep.

(It also says:) To open this opening — the twin passes of the spine — you must first think intently of the Root of the Mountain. Only then does the breath gradually pass through the spine, through the Muddy Pellet, and reach the Ancestral Opening of the Celestial Heart. Then mother and child meet, like a broken mirror made whole again; gradually this expands, the root becomes firm and complete, the treasure of life is saved — and only then can one speak of the work of cultivation. After long practice, every inhalation and exhalation enters the Cavity of Breath (qixue 气穴): this is the wonder of what happens of itself.

Qiu Ling's noteon §8
Original note (Chinese)

闵氏对中黄一窍之作用，犹未尽言。《尹真人皇极阖辟仙经》云：（引文见上。“中脘”下夹注：脐上四寸处；“明堂”下夹注：在两眉之正中深入一寸二分处；“此窍”下夹注：夹脊双关。）

夹脊双关即中黄，位于心后关前，非后天呼吸所经之处。行、住、坐、卧凝神此窍，守而不离，则里面皎明如月在水，自然散其邪火，消其杂虑，降其动心，止其妄念，妄念止则真息自现，而性存命固。

Qiu Ling considers that Min Yide has not said everything about the function of the Central Yellow, so he quotes the two passages above from the Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate by the Perfected Yin to complete it. He adds small notes on the locations: the zhongwan is four cun above the navel; the Hall of Light is 1.2 cun deep inward from the exact center between the eyebrows; “this opening” refers to the twin passes of the spine.

Qiu Ling says: the twin passes of the spine are the Central Yellow, located behind the heart and in front of the Pass; this is not a place through which ordinary (Posterior-Heaven) breathing passes. Whether walking, standing, sitting or lying down, concentrate the spirit on this opening and guard it without leaving it; then inside it becomes clear and bright, like the moon reflected in water. Naturally this disperses pathogenic fire, dissolves scattered thoughts, calms the agitated mind and stops deluded thoughts. When deluded thoughts stop, the True Breath appears by itself, inner nature is preserved and life is made firm.

§9
Original (Chinese)

止观是佛法，原不秘的。以两目谛观鼻端，正身安坐，系心缘中，道言中黄，佛言缘中，一也。初学但于两目中间齐平处系念便了。光是活泼泼的东西，系念眼之平齐处，光自然透入，不必著一念于中黄也。此数语已括尽要旨。

Cessation and contemplation is a Buddhist method, and it was never secret. With both eyes look attentively at the tip of the nose, sit upright and at ease, and fasten the mind on the “center of conditions.” The Daoists say “Central Yellow,” the Buddhists say “center of conditions”: they are the same. Beginners need only fasten their attention on the point midway between the two eyes, level with them. The light is something alive and lively; when you fasten your attention on the point level with the eyes, the light penetrates by itself, and there is no need to place a further thought on the Central Yellow. These few sentences already contain the whole essence.

§10
Original (Chinese)

止观二字，原离不得，即定慧也。以后凡念起时，不要仍旧兀坐，当究此念在何处，从何起，从何灭，反复推穷，了不可得。即见此念起处也，不要又讨过起处。所谓觅心了不可得，吾与汝安心竟。此是正观，反此者名为邪观。如是不可得已，即仍旧绵绵去。止而继之以观，观而继之以止，是定慧双修法。此为回光。回者止也，光者观也。止而不观，名为有回无光；观而不止，名为有光无回。志之。

The two words “cessation” and “contemplation” cannot be separated; they are concentration and wisdom. From now on, whenever a thought arises, do not just keep sitting there blankly. Investigate: where is this thought? Where does it arise from? Where does it cease? Search this through again and again, and in the end it cannot be found at all. That is seeing where the thought arises; do not go looking for some origin beyond that. This is the Chan saying “I searched for the mind and could not find it” — to which Bodhidharma replied, “Then I have already put your mind at rest.” This is right contemplation; the opposite is called wrong contemplation. Once you find it cannot be found, carry on sitting, fine and continuous as before. Cessation followed by contemplation, contemplation followed by cessation: this is the method of cultivating concentration and wisdom together. This is Reverting the Light. “Reverting” is cessation; “light” is contemplation. Cessation without contemplation is called “reverting without light”; contemplation without cessation is called “light without reverting.” Remember this!

Qiu Ling's noteon §9–§10
Original note (Chinese)

此节言佛家止观法门。修止的第一步是“系缘止”，即系心鼻端或中黄；修止的第二步为“制心止”，即看清心上杂念起处，随时制止它；第三步为“体真止”，即仔细体会心里所想的事物，追寻杂念的源头和去处，这样反复推穷，所有杂念便不必有意去制止它，自然会止息，这就叫做觅心了不可得，终于安下心来（安心竟）。本节所述主要是“系缘止”和“体真止”的功夫。修止时决不能离开观，观时也不能离开止。回光即是观止。

This section presents the Buddhist method of cessation and contemplation. The first step in cultivating cessation is called “cessation by fastening to an object”: fastening the mind on the tip of the nose or the Central Yellow. The second step is “cessation by controlling the mind”: seeing clearly where scattered thoughts arise in the mind and stopping them as they come. The third step is “cessation by realizing the truth”: carefully examining what the mind is thinking about, tracing scattered thoughts back to where they come from and where they go. Searching this through again and again, you no longer need to stop scattered thoughts deliberately; they cease by themselves. This is called “searching for the mind and not finding it,” and finally the mind is at rest. This section mainly describes “cessation by fastening to an object” and “cessation by realizing the truth.” When cultivating cessation, never abandon contemplation; when contemplating, never abandon cessation. Reverting the Light is contemplation and cessation.

Key Terms

  • Refined thought, pure breath, pure thought: “refined thought” means concentrated thinking; “pure breath” comes from Zhuangzi, “Mastering Life” (“this is guarding the pure breath”); “pure thought” comes from the Śūraṅgama Sūtra (“pure thought, and one flies upward”). All three refer to the unified state brought about by Reverting the Light.
  • “Within being, as if non-being” / “being born out of non-being”: the first describes how to apply effort at the start; the second, the result once the work is accomplished.
  • grain-sized pearl: the first congealing of a point of true yang, as small as a grain of millet — the beginning of the elixir.
  • fire phasing (huohou): “The reverting of the light is itself the fire phasing.”
  • “To take, first give” / “sending out the light to draw it in”: an advanced method added by Min Yide — first send out, then take back, so as to expand the mechanism of breath.
  • The Nine Darknesses: the underworld, the hells; a figure for the state in which yang is exhausted and only yin remains.
  • reversal (nifa): the opposite of ordinary people flowing along with outer circumstances — taking the spiritual light back.
  • lowering the curtains, looking at the tip of the nose: the eyelids half lowered; the tip of the nose gives the eyes a reference point, and once it is set it is let go.
  • Mistaking the finger for the moon: a Buddhist image — taking the finger that points at the moon for the moon itself, that is, clinging to the method itself.
  • Central Yellow / center of conditions / yellow center / Gate of the Mysterious Female: one and the same place, at the center of the body, behind the heart and in front of the spine.
  • Root of the Mountain: the point midway between the eyes, level with them — where attention is fastened at the start.
  • cessation and contemplation, concentration and wisdom: Buddhist practices; cessation is the “reverting,” contemplation the “light.”
  • “Searching for the mind and not finding it”: Huike, later the second patriarch of Chan, asked Bodhidharma to put his mind at rest. Bodhidharma said, “Bring me your mind and I will put it at rest.” Huike said, “I have searched for the mind and cannot find it.” Bodhidharma said, “Then I have already put your mind at rest.”
  • Zhongwan, Hall of Light, twin passes of the spine, Muddy Pellet, the Ancestral Opening of the Celestial Heart, Cavity of Breath: the route along which, according to the Opening and Closing scripture, the breath passes through.
  • “The breathing of ordinary people is from the throat; the breathing of the true person is from the heels”: from Zhuangzi, “The Great and Venerable Teacher.”

Putting It into Practice

  1. The simplest way in (which the text itself says “already contains the whole essence”): sit upright and at ease → lower the eyelids and glance once at the tip of the nose to set the reference → let go of the nose → lightly fasten your attention on the point between the eyes, level with them (the Root of the Mountain). The light is alive; it will penetrate by itself. There is no need to think of the Central Yellow as well.
  2. One place, not two: eyes on the tip of the nose and mind on the Central Yellow is a common mistake. Beginners guard only one place.
  3. What to do when thoughts come: do not sit there forcing them down. Gently ask where they come from and where they go — when you cannot find them, rest in that “cannot find,” and go on sitting, fine and continuous.
  4. Both reverting and light: just gathering the mind without any clear awareness leads to sitting in a fog (reverting without light); just observing everywhere without gathering in leads to distraction (light without reverting). Alternate them, using each to support the other.
  5. Continuity matters most: “In beginning the practice, go from shallow to deep, from coarse to fine; above all, it is best not to interrupt it.” Sitting every day matters more than sitting for a long time once in a while.
  6. The advanced method (sending out the light) is for information only: in Min Yide’s “sending out the light to draw it in,” the last step — “bring it back again” — is the crucial one: the light sent out must always be brought back. Qiu Ling stresses that this is to avoid wasting the original breath and harming the mechanism of breath.

Translator's Notes

  1. “From the first cry at birth”: the original has huhu di yisheng 囫囫地一声; Chapter 1 and the Opening and Closing scripture write hudi yisheng 囫地一声. Both describe a baby’s first cry.
  2. “An hour or two”: the original says “one or two shi”; one traditional double-hour (shichen) is about two hours. Here it simply means “sitting idly for a while.”
  3. “That is seeing where the thought arises; do not go looking for some origin beyond that”: the very fact that it “cannot be found at all” is the seeing of where the thought arises; there is no other origin to look for beyond this.
  4. “The foundation for cultivating reality and attaining the Dao is built here”: the Chinese sentence seems to contain a superfluous character; we translate according to its general sense.
  5. “To open this opening”: according to Qiu Ling’s small note, “this opening” refers to the twin passes of the spine.
  6. In a small note on Min Yide’s commentary, Qiu Ling says that “the tip of the nose is the finger and the Central Yellow is the moon” uses the method of “pointing directly to see the moon”: taking the tip of the nose as the reference, one looks inward at the Central Yellow, which appears clear and bright like the moon.
  7. In his note on §8 Qiu Ling says “the twin passes of the spine are the Central Yellow,” while Min Yide’s note and Chapter 1 both place the Central Yellow “behind the heart and in front of the Pass,” that is, in front of the twin passes of the spine. Literally the two statements do not agree. One way to understand them: the Central Yellow and the spine face each other front to back, on the same level between chest and back; in practice the attention rests at the center between chest and back, not pressed against the spine. This is the translator’s understanding, offered for reference.

Appendix: Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy, with Commentary · 第八首 接药

Poem 8 · Receiving the Medicine

Poem 8 · Receiving the Medicine — the same for men and women

About This Chapter

一半玄机悟，丹头如露凝。虽云能固命，安得炼成形。鼻观纯阳接，神铅透体灵。哺含须慎重，完满即飞腾。

The eighth poem is about “receiving the medicine,” and is the same for men and women.

  • By this point half of the whole work has been attained; the inner elixir has formed, congealing like dew;
  • Though forming the elixir can secure one’s life (anciently called a “human immortal”), it cannot yet refine and transform the bodily form;
  • One must use the work of “receiving pure yang by the nose’s contemplation” and the divine lead penetrating the body — this is the real work of transcending the ordinary and entering sagehood, of bringing out the Yang Spirit;
  • Warm nourishing must be done with care; when the breath is sufficient and the medicine potent, there will be the scene of the Great Medicine rushing through the passes.

Chen Yingning says of the third couplet that “in the present age… even those who can understand this principle are rarely met”; not daring to invent an interpretation of his own, he quotes the method of “refining the body in true emptiness” handed down from antiquity to explain it. The key points of refining the body in true emptiness are: before sunrise in the early morning, concentrate the spirit and sit quietly; within, give up thoughts; without, give up all conditions; suddenly forget heaven and earth and shatter the bodily frame — but he explains specifically that “shattering” only means forgetting things and forgetting form, and must not be taken literally. “Though it is called action, it is in fact non-action; though it is called refining the body, it is in fact refining the spirit.” He also says that the subsequent refining of the spirit, ingesting, abstaining from grain, facing the wall, and sending out the spirit all lie within the scope of this working, differing only in the depth of the work.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

一半玄机悟，丹头如露凝。

Half the mysterious mechanism is understood; the head of the elixir congeals like dew.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

全部工夫，到此已得一半，因内丹已结也。

“露”乃地面之水因热化气，腾散于空中，至夜遇冷，遂附著于最易散热之物体，而凝结成露。丹道亦同此理，可以神悟，难以言传。

Of the whole work, half has been attained at this point, because the inner elixir has already formed.

“Dew” is the water on the ground that turns into vapor through heat and rises and spreads into the air; at night, meeting cold, it attaches to the objects that most easily lose heat and congeals into dew. The alchemical Way follows the same principle; it can be understood by the spirit but is hard to convey in words.

§2
Original (Chinese)

虽云能固命，安得炼成形。

Though it is said it can secure one’s life, how can one refine it into form?

Chen Yingning's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

既已结丹，则一身精气神皆完全坚固，决定可以长生，此时古称为“人仙”。

Once the elixir has formed, the essence, breath, and spirit of the whole body are entirely secure and firm, and one can certainly live long; at this point the ancients called one a “human immortal.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

鼻观纯阳接，神铅透体灵。

By the nose’s contemplation receive pure yang; the divine lead penetrates the body and is numinous.

Chen Yingning's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

此二句乃言超凡入圣之实功，不由此道，不能出阳神。当今之世，除一二修炼专家而外，非但无人能行此功，即能悟此理者，亦罕遇之。余若自出心裁，勉为注释，恐人不能解，反嗤为妄，故引自古相传之“真空炼形”丹法，以释其玄奥之义。

真空炼形法云："夫人未生之先，一呼一吸，气通于母；既生之后，一呼一吸，气通于天，天人一气，联属流通，相吞相吐，如扯锯焉。天与之，我能取之，得其气，气盛而生也；天与之，天复取之，失其气，气绝而死也。

故圣人观天之道，执天之行，每于羲驭未升旸谷之时，凝神静坐，虚以待之，内舍意念，外舍万缘，顿忘天地，粉碎形骸（道家常有粉碎虚空、粉碎形骸等语，不过忘物形之意耳，不可拘泥粉碎二字。）自然太虚中有一点如露如电之阳，勃勃然入于玄门，透长谷而上泥丸，化为甘霖而降于五内。我即鼓动巽风以应之，使其驱逐三关九窍之邪，扫荡五脏六腑之垢，焚身炼质，煅滓销霾，抽尽秽浊之躯，变换纯阳之体。累积长久，化形而仙。"

《破迷正道歌》曰：“果然百日防危险，血化为膏体似银；果然百日无亏失，玉膏流润生光明。”

《翠虚篇》曰：“透体金光骨髓香，金筋玉骨尽纯阳；炼教赤血流为白，阴气消磨身自康。”

邱长春曰：“但能息息长相顾，换尽形骸玉液流。”

张紫琼曰：“天人一气本来同，为有形骸碍不通；炼到形神冥合处，方知色相即真空。”

"炼形之法，总有六门：其一曰玉液炼形，其二曰金液炼形，其三曰太阴炼形，其四曰太阳炼形，其五曰内观炼形，若此者总非虚无大道，终不能与太虚同体；惟此一诀，乃曰真空炼形，虽曰有作，其实无为，虽曰炼形，其实炼神，是修外而兼修内也。

依法炼之百日，则七魄亡形，三尸绝迹，六贼潜藏，十魔远遁；炼之千日，则四大一身，俨如水晶塔子，表里玲珑，内外洞彻，心华灿然，灵光显现。故《生神经》曰：‘身神并一，则为真身，身与神合，形随道通。隐则形固于神，显则神合于气，所以蹈水火而无害，对日月而无影。'"

按真空炼形一段工夫，所包甚广，不仅为此首诗作注脚，虽以后炼神、服食、辟谷、面壁、出神等法，亦不出此运用之外，不过依功程之浅深而分阶级耳。

These two lines speak of the real work of transcending the ordinary and entering sagehood; without following this path, one cannot bring out the Yang Spirit. In the present age, apart from one or two specialists in cultivation, not only is there no one who can do this work, but even those who can understand this principle are rarely met. If I were to invent an interpretation of my own and force a commentary, I fear people would not understand it and would instead mock it as presumptuous; so I quote the alchemical method of “refining the body in true emptiness” handed down from antiquity to explain its mysterious meaning.

The method of refining the body in true emptiness says: "Before a person is born, with each exhalation and inhalation the breath communicates with the mother; after birth, with each exhalation and inhalation the breath communicates with Heaven. Heaven and the human are one breath, connected and flowing, swallowing and expelling each other, back and forth like a saw being pulled. When Heaven gives and I can take, I obtain its breath, and when the breath is abundant I live; when Heaven gives and Heaven takes it back again, I lose its breath, and when the breath is cut off I die.

“So the sage observes the Way of Heaven and holds to the movement of Heaven: each time, before Xi’s chariot has risen from the Valley of Sunrise, he concentrates the spirit and sits quietly, waiting in emptiness; within he gives up thoughts, without he gives up all conditions; suddenly he forgets heaven and earth and shatters the bodily frame (Daoists often use expressions such as ‘shattering empty space’ and ‘shattering the bodily frame,' which only mean forgetting things and form; the word ‘shattering’ must not be taken literally); naturally, from the Great Void, a point of yang like dew, like lightning, bursts vigorously into the mysterious gate, passes through the long valley up to the Muddy Pellet, and turns into sweet rain descending into the five viscera. I then stir up the Wind of Xun to answer it, letting it drive out the evils of the Three Passes and Nine Openings and sweep away the filth of the five viscera and six bowels, burning the body and refining its substance, forging away the dregs and dispelling the haze, drawing out all of this impure body and exchanging it for a body of pure yang. Accumulated over a long time, the form is transformed and one becomes an immortal.”

The Song of the Correct Way that Dispels Delusion says: “If indeed for a hundred days one guards against danger, the blood turns to cream and the body is like silver; if indeed for a hundred days there is no loss, jade cream flows and moistens and gives off light.”

The Writings of Cuixu says: “Golden light penetrates the body, the marrow is fragrant; golden sinews and jade bones are all pure yang; refine until the red blood flows white; the yin breath worn away, the body is naturally healthy.”

Qiu Changchun said: “Only let breath after breath ever attend to each other, and the bodily frame is wholly exchanged, jade liquor flowing.”

Zhang Ziqiong said: “Heaven and the human are one breath, originally the same; because there is a bodily frame, it is obstructed and does not get through; refine until form and spirit merge in darkness, and only then does one know that form is true emptiness.”

"The methods of refining the body are six gates in all: the first is called refining the body with jade liquor, the second refining the body with golden liquor, the third refining the body with the Great Yin, the fourth refining the body with the Great Yang, the fifth refining the body by inner contemplation; none of these is the great Way of emptiness, and in the end they cannot become one body with the Great Void. Only this one formula, called refining the body in true emptiness — though it is called action, it is in fact non-action; though it is called refining the body, it is in fact refining the spirit — cultivates the outer while also cultivating the inner.

“Refine according to this method for a hundred days, and the seven po lose their form, the three corpses vanish without trace, the six thieves go into hiding, the ten demons flee far away; refine for a thousand days, and the whole body of four elements becomes like a crystal pagoda, transparent inside and out, clear through and through, the flower of the mind brilliant, the numinous light manifest. So the Scripture of Generating the Spirits says: ‘When body and spirit are merged into one, it is the true body; when body unites with spirit, the form passes through with the Dao. When hidden, the form is secured in the spirit; when manifest, the spirit unites with the breath; so one can tread on water and fire without harm, and stand facing sun and moon without casting a shadow.'”

Note: this work of refining the body in true emptiness covers a great deal; it is not only a footnote to this poem; even the later methods of refining the spirit, ingesting, abstaining from grain, facing the wall, sending out the spirit, and so on do not go beyond this working, but are only divided into stages according to the depth of the work.

§4
Original (Chinese)

哺含须慎重，完满即飞腾。

Nursing and holding it must be done with care; when complete and full, it soars aloft.

Chen Yingning's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

“哺含”即温养之意。“完满”者，气已足，药已灵也。“飞腾”者，似指大药冲关之象。

“Nursing and holding” means warm nourishing. “Complete and full” means the breath is already sufficient and the medicine already potent. “Soaring aloft” seems to refer to the scene of the Great Medicine rushing through the passes.

Key Terms

  • Head of the elixir: the inner elixir newly formed; see Golden Flower.
  • Human immortal: the stage after forming the elixir in which essence, breath, and spirit are entirely secure and firm; see the three transmutations.
  • Receiving pure yang by the nose’s contemplation: drawing in the breath of pure yang by contemplation through the nose.
  • Divine lead: the lead of Prior Heaven in which spirit and breath are united as one; see Lead and Mercury.
  • Refining the body in true emptiness: the core of this poem’s commentary; “though it is called action, it is in fact non-action; though it is called refining the body, it is in fact refining the spirit.”
  • Before Xi’s chariot has risen from the Valley of Sunrise: before the sun has risen in the east, that is, in the early morning before sunrise.
  • Shattering the bodily frame: only meaning forgetting things and form; the word “shattering” must not be taken literally.
  • Mysterious gate, long valley: the Mysterious Pass and the nose (by another explanation, the channel in the spine); here, the path by which the yang breath enters the body.
  • Sweet rain: see Sweet Dew.
  • Six gates of refining the body: jade liquor, golden liquor, the Great Yin, the Great Yang, inner contemplation, and refining the body in true emptiness.
  • Seven po, three corpses, six thieves, ten demons: yin and evil things in the body and obstacles, as spoken of in Daoism; the six thieves are the eye, ear, nose, tongue, body, and mind.
  • Nursing and holding: warm nourishing. Soaring aloft: the Great Medicine rushing through the passes; see the Great Medicine passing the gates.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “Shattering the bodily frame” does not mean harming the body; it means forgetting the body. Chen Yingning added this parenthetical note precisely for fear that people would take the words literally.
  2. The beginning of refining the body in true emptiness is very plain: sit quietly in the early morning, wait in emptiness, within give up thoughts, without give up all conditions. What can be borrowed is “waiting in emptiness” — not seeking anything, simply letting yourself become empty.
  3. “Though it is called refining the body, it is in fact refining the spirit.” All talk of “refining the body” ultimately comes down to the mind.
  4. “Nursing and holding it must be done with care”: once there are results, warm nourishing is more important than adding more.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Zhang Ziqiong”: probably an error for “Zhang Ziyang” (Zhang Boduan) or “Bai Ziqing” (Bai Yuchan); kept as in the original.
  2. The Writings of Cuixu: by Chen Nan (styled Cuixu) of the Southern Song.
  3. Qiu Changchun: Qiu Chuji, one of the Seven Masters of Quanzhen.
  4. The Scripture of Generating the Spirits: the Scripture of the Stanzas of the Natural Generation of the Spirits of the Nine Heavens of the Cavern Mystery Lingbao.
  5. “The blood turns to cream and the body is like silver,” “refine until the red blood flows white,” “stand facing sun and moon without casting a shadow,” and the like are idealized descriptions of the ancient alchemical books that modern science cannot confirm.
  6. The “method of refining the body in true emptiness” quoted by Chen Yingning is almost identical in wording to the second half of Group II, Section 8, “The Ancestral Breath of Pure Yang Helps Complete the Embryo,” in the previous part, the Concise Essentials of Great Accomplishment, where Qiu Ling also calls it “the art of refining the body in true emptiness”; the two can be read together.

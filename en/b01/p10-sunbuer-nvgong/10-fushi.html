Poem 10 · Ingesting

Poem 10 · Ingesting — the same for men and women

About This Chapter

大冶成山泽，中含造化情。朝迎日乌气，夜吸月蟾精。时候丹能采，年华体自轻。元神来往处，万窍发光明。

The tenth poem is about “ingesting,” and is the same for men and women. “Ingesting” here does not mean eating anything, but gathering the pure, numinous breath between Heaven and Earth into the body and refining and nourishing it together with one’s own spirit; in time spirit and breath fuse into one piece, and only then is the great elixir formed.

  • Heaven and Earth are like a great furnace that casts the mountains and marshes, and the mountains and marshes in turn hold the vital force of creation;
  • In the morning welcome the breath of the sun; at night draw in the essence of the moon;
  • Only by knowing the times of the waxing and waning of yin and yang can one gather the elixir; only after three years and nine years can one transform the body;
  • Where the Original Spirit comes and goes, all the pores of the body give forth light.

Chen Yingning says, “the reason the work of the latter half is best done living in the mountains is that the pure, numinous breath in the mountains is better than in the cities” — a very simple reason. His explanation of the body’s emitting light in terms of “electricity” and “radium” reflects the ideas of his time; it is enough for the reader to be aware of it.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

大冶成山泽，中含造化情。

The great smelter forms the mountains and marshes; within them is held the feeling of creation.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

“大冶”本意为熔铸五金，今以之喻造化之伟功。乾坤为炉鼎，阴阳为水火，万象从兹而铸成，是万物其有一太极也。“山”与“泽”，乃万物中之一物，而山泽中又有造化，是一物各得一太极也。山泽通气，震兑相交，而造化之情见矣。

修道者，贵在收积虚空中清灵之气于身中，然后将吾人之神与此气配合而炼养之，为时既久，则神气打成一片，而大丹始成。后半部工夫所以宜居山者，因山中清灵之气较城市为优耳。

“The great smelter” originally means melting and casting the five metals; here it is used as a metaphor for the mighty work of creation. Qian and Kun are the furnace and cauldron, yin and yang the water and fire; the myriad images are cast from here — this is the myriad things together having one Great Ultimate. “Mountains” and “marshes” are each one thing among the myriad things, and within the mountains and marshes there is again creation — this is each thing having its own Great Ultimate. Mountains and marshes communicate their breath, Zhen and Dui join, and the feeling of creation appears.

What those who cultivate the Dao value is gathering the pure, numinous breath of empty space into the body, then combining our spirit with this breath and refining and nourishing it; when this has gone on long enough, spirit and breath fuse into one piece, and only then is the great elixir formed. The reason the work of the latter half is best done living in the mountains is simply that the pure, numinous breath in the mountains is better than in the cities.

§2
Original (Chinese)

朝迎日乌气，夜吸月蟾精。

In the morning welcome the breath of the sun-crow; at night draw in the essence of the moon-toad.

Chen Yingning's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

蚌受月华而结珠胎，土得日精而产金玉，人知采取日月精华，则可以结丹，变化凡体。至其所以采取之法，到此地步，自能领悟，不必执著迹象，致碍圆通。

The oyster receives the moon’s radiance and forms the pearl; the soil obtains the sun’s essence and produces gold and jade; if people know how to gather the essence of sun and moon, they can form the elixir and transform the ordinary body. As for the method of gathering, when one reaches this stage one will naturally understand it; there is no need to cling to traces and appearances, which would hinder perfect understanding.

§3
Original (Chinese)

时候丹能采，年华体自轻。

When the time comes, the elixir can be gathered; as the years go by, the body grows light of itself.

Chen Yingning's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

采天地之灵气以结丹，须识阴阳盛衰之候；夺造化之玄机而换体，必经三年九载之功。

To gather the numinous breath of Heaven and Earth and form the elixir, one must know the times of the waxing and waning of yin and yang; to seize the mysterious mechanism of creation and transform the body, one must pass through the work of three years and nine years.

§4
Original (Chinese)

元神来往处，万窍发光明。

Where the Original Spirit comes and goes, ten thousand openings give forth light.

Chen Yingning's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

此言周身毛窍皆有光明发现，丹经云：“一朝功满人不知，四面皆成夜光阙。”亦同此意，其所以有光者，或者因身中电力充足之故。世上雷锭能有发光，经过长久时期，而本体不减毫厘。彼无知之物质，且灵异若此，又何疑乎人体？

This says that light appears from all the pores of the body. An alchemical scripture says: “One morning, when the work is complete, unknown to others, on all four sides it becomes a palace of night-shining light.” This has the same meaning. The reason there is light is perhaps that the electricity in the body is abundant. In the world, radium can emit light for a very long time without its substance diminishing by a hair. If even that unknowing matter is so marvelous, why doubt it of the human body?

Key Terms

  • Ingesting: in this poem, gathering the pure, numinous breath of Heaven and Earth into the body and refining and nourishing it together with the spirit; not eating and drinking.
  • Great smelter: the great furnace that melts and casts the five metals, a metaphor for creation.
  • The myriad things together having one Great Ultimate, each thing having its own Great Ultimate: an expression of Song Neo-Confucianism.
  • Mountains and marshes communicate their breath: from the Book of Changes, “Discussion of the Trigrams.”
  • Sun-crow, moon-toad: names for the sun and the moon.
  • Three years and nine years: the three years of suckling and the nine years of facing the wall; see the three transmutations.
  • Palace of night-shining light: describing the whole body shining like a palace lit at night.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “The pure, numinous breath in the mountains is better than in the cities” — simple and practical: when you have the opportunity, sit quietly somewhere with good air and quiet, and the effect really is different.
  2. “There is no need to cling to traces and appearances, which would hinder perfect understanding.” Do not take phrases like “gathering the essence of sun and moon” literally and perform some action facing the sun or moon.
  3. Descriptions such as the body emitting light belong to ancient ideals; there is no need to take them seriously, still less to pursue them.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Zhen and Dui join”: according to the Book of Changes, the mountain is Gen and the marsh is Dui, so “mountains and marshes communicate their breath” should be “Gen and Dui join”; the original note has “Zhen and Dui,” probably a slip of the pen, kept as in the original.
  2. “Radium” (leiding 雷锭): an old Chinese transliteration of radium. Chen Yingning’s explanation of the body’s emitting light in terms of “electricity” and “radium” reflects ideas of the early twentieth century and has no scientific basis. Incidentally, radium is strongly radioactive and by no means harmless.
  3. “One morning, when the work is complete, unknown to others, on all four sides it becomes a palace of night-shining light”: a saying from an alchemical scripture; its source remains to be determined.

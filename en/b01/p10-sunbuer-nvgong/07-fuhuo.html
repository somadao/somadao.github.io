Appendix: Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy, with Commentary · 第七首 符火

Poem 7 · The Fire Tallies

Poem 7 · The Fire Tallies — the fifth and sixth lines for women only

About This Chapter

胎息绵绵处，须分动静机。阳光当益进，阴魄要防飞。潭里珠含景，山头月吐辉。六时休少纵，灌溉药苗肥。

The seventh poem is about the “fire tallies” (the yang fire and the yin tally); the fifth and sixth lines are for women only.

  • When embryonic breathing is continuous, one must distinguish the movement and stillness of the mechanism of breath;
  • When the yang breath stirs, the spirit quietly helps it advance and grow ever stronger; when the mechanism of breath tends toward settled stillness, the spirit also helps it settle, preventing it from flying off restlessly;
  • The mechanism of breath in women: in stillness it is like a pearl in a pool, holding its light without showing it (the Sea of Blood); in movement it is like the moon on a mountain top, pouring out its radiance (the two breasts);
  • During the “six times” there must not be the slightest slackness, one unbroken thread to the end; only then can the sprout of the medicine be watered.

Two of Chen Yingning’s explanations are noteworthy: first, before the elixir has formed, the breath in women often rushes up to the nipples when it stirs (in men it rushes down to the genitals), whereas after the elixir has formed the two breasts contract like a young girl’s and the movement is only internal; second, the “six times” are not hours of the day and night but six stages of silent working imagined within the body — “one must never cling rigidly to the hours of heaven, lest a living method become a dead one.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

胎息绵绵处，须分动静机。

Where embryonic breathing is continuous, one must distinguish the mechanisms of movement and stillness.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

阴符阳火，气机动静，前数段工夫已有之，不必定在胎息后也。但未到结丹地步，其气之动，常有上冲乳头之时。（男子则下冲于生殖器。）既结丹，则两乳已紧缩如童女，身内虽有动机，不能再向外发，只内动而已。动亦有时，或数日一动，或一日数动，视其用功之勤惰以为衡。凡未动之先，及既动之后，皆静也。

The yin tally and the yang fire, the movement and stillness of the mechanism of breath, are already present in the work of the preceding passages; they need not come only after embryonic breathing. But before reaching the stage of forming the elixir, the stirring of the breath often rushes up to the nipples (in men it rushes down to the genitals). Once the elixir has formed, the two breasts have contracted like a young girl’s, and though there are stirrings within the body, they can no longer issue outward; there is only movement within. Movement also has its times: perhaps once in several days, perhaps several times a day, measured by how diligent or lax one’s practice is. Before any movement and after movement, all is stillness.

§2
Original (Chinese)

阳光当益进，阴魄要防飞。

The yang light should advance all the more; the yin po must be guarded against flying.

Chen Yingning's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

动者属“阳”，静者属“阴”。阳气发动时，则元神亦随之而动；气到人身某处，神亦同到某处。阳气发动曰“进”，而暗中以神助之，愈进愈旺，故曰“益进”。

阳极则阴生，动极必归静。人之魂属阳，主上升；魄属阴，主下降。当升之时不可降；当降之时不可升。“阴魄要防飞”者，意谓气若有静定之态，则神必助之静定，以防其飞躁不宁。

What moves belongs to “yang,” what is still belongs to “yin.” When the yang breath stirs, the Original Spirit moves along with it; wherever in the body the breath goes, the spirit goes there too. The stirring of the yang breath is called “advancing”; and quietly helping it with the spirit, so that the more it advances the stronger it grows, is why it is called “advancing all the more.”

When yang reaches its extreme, yin arises; when movement reaches its extreme, it must return to stillness. A person’s hun belongs to yang and governs rising; the po belongs to yin and governs descending. When it should rise it must not descend; when it should descend it must not rise. “The yin po must be guarded against flying” means that if the breath shows signs of settled stillness, the spirit must help it to settle, to prevent it from flying off restlessly without peace.

§3
Original (Chinese)

潭里珠含景，山头月吐辉。

In the pool a pearl holds its light; on the mountain top the moon pours out its radiance.

Chen Yingning's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

“潭”在下，喻血海子宫之部位；“山”在上，喻膻中两乳之部位。“珠”之光，隐而敛；“月”之光，耀而明。曰潭里，曰含景，言其静而深藏之象；曰山头，曰吐辉，言其动而显出之机。

The “pool” is below and is a metaphor for the area of the Sea of Blood and the womb; the “mountain” is above and is a metaphor for the area of Danzhong and the two breasts. The light of the “pearl” is hidden and withdrawn; the light of the “moon” shines and is bright. Saying “in the pool” and “holds its light” speaks of the image of its stillness and deep concealment; saying “on the mountain top” and “pours out its radiance” speaks of the mechanism of its movement and manifestation.

§4
Original (Chinese)

六时休少纵，灌溉药苗肥。

During the six times allow not the slightest slackness; water the sprout of the medicine so that it grows plump.

Chen Yingning's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

“六时”者，非谓昼之六时，亦非夜之六时，乃人身虚拟默运之六时，古人又有名为六候者，切不可拘泥天时，免致活法变成死法。若问：“人身六时何似？”仍不外乎神气动静，阴阳升降之消息而已。

“休少纵”者，即谓念不可起，意不可散，一线到底，勿使中间断续不贯，俟此一段工夫行毕，方可自由动作。

The “six times” are neither six double-hours of the day nor six double-hours of the night, but six times of silent working imagined within the body, which the ancients also called the “six periods”; one must never cling rigidly to the hours of heaven, lest a living method become a dead one. If one asks, “What are the six times in the body like?” — they are still nothing but the waxing and waning of the movement and stillness of spirit and breath, the rising and descending of yin and yang.

“Allow not the slightest slackness” means that thoughts must not arise and the intention must not scatter, one unbroken thread to the end, not letting it be broken off and disconnected in the middle; only when this stretch of work has been completed may one move about freely.

Key Terms

  • Fire tallies: the yang fire and the yin tally; see fire phasing.
  • Mechanisms of movement and stillness: the movement and stillness of the mechanism of breath; before movement and after movement, all is stillness.
  • Advancing all the more: when the yang breath stirs, quietly helping it with the spirit, so that the more it advances the stronger it grows.
  • Hun and po: the hun belongs to yang and governs rising, the po belongs to yin and governs descending; see hun and po.
  • Pool, pearl: the area of the Sea of Blood and the womb, the image of stillness and deep concealment; see Sea of Blood.
  • Mountain, moon: the area of Danzhong and the two breasts, the mechanism of movement and manifestation; see Breast Stream.
  • Six times, six periods: six stages of silent working imagined within the body; one must not cling rigidly to the hours of heaven.
  • Allow not the slightest slackness: thoughts must not arise, the intention must not scatter, one unbroken thread to the end.
  • Sprout of the medicine: see Medicine.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “Before any movement and after movement, all is stillness.” Most of the time is spent in stillness; movement happens only occasionally. Do not stake the work on “movement.”
  2. Follow the tendency and help it: when it moves, the spirit follows; when it is still, the spirit helps it be still — always following, not leading.
  3. “One must never cling rigidly to the hours of heaven, lest a living method become a dead one.” This line appears once again. The hours and numbers in the alchemical books are mostly metaphors for processes in the body.
  4. In a session of quiet sitting, finish a stretch before getting up. “Only when this stretch of work has been completed may one move about freely” — do not let yourself be interrupted halfway by your phone or other matters.

Translator's Notes

  1. The explanations in this commentary concerning the nipples and the genitals are Chen Yingning’s direct description of the differences between men’s and women’s alchemical methods, translated as in the original.
  2. “The two breasts have contracted like a young girl’s”: a description of results common in women’s alchemical books, belonging to the records of the ancients.

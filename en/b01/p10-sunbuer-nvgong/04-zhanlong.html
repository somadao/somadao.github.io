Appendix: Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy, with Commentary · 第四首 斩龙

Poem 4 · Beheading the Dragon

Poem 4 · Beheading the Dragon — for women only

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

Safety note (translator): This poem is about “beheading the Red Dragon,” that is, using practice to stop menstruation. This is a central idea of ancient women’s alchemy; we translate it according to its original meaning, as a document for understanding. But it must be said: in modern medicine, cessation of menstruation outside pregnancy and menopause is a symptom requiring medical examination, and may be linked to endocrine disorders, malnutrition, excessive dieting, or excessive exercise. No practice should take the cessation of menstruation as its goal; if your menstruation changes noticeably during practice, see a doctor first, and do not on your own attribute it to “effects of the practice.”

About This Chapter

静极能生动，阴阳相与模。风中擒玉虎，月里捉金乌。著眼絪缊候，留心顺逆途。鹊桥重过处，丹炁复归炉。

The fourth poem, “Beheading the Dragon,” is a practice unique to women. The alchemical books call menstruation the “dragon” (the Red Dragon), and “beheading the dragon” means using practice to stop menstruation so that it no longer comes. Chen Yingning holds that this is where women’s cultivation differs from men’s, and also why women succeed faster than men — “men in cultivation achieve the non-leakage of semen; women in cultivation achieve the non-leakage of menses.”

The meaning of the four couplets:

  • When stillness reaches its extreme, movement arises of itself; yin and yang are each other’s root and complete each other;
  • Use the breathing (the wind) to seize the yang breath of Prior Heaven (the jade tiger); when the yang breath stirs, the spirit responds at the same time (catching the golden crow in the moon);
  • Pay attention to the moment of “dense mingling” in the body: at this time, following the current leads to conceiving a child; going against it leads to gathering the medicine and returning the elixir;
  • The elixir breath passes once around through the upper and lower Magpie Bridges and returns to the Yellow Court (the furnace).

Chen Yingning’s answer to a common question is also interesting: some think that for older women whose menstruation has already ceased, cultivation should be easier; he says it is just the opposite — older people lack vitality and must first “produce something from nothing,” which is why the ancients urged, “Add oil early; do not delay in prolonging life.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

静极能生动，阴阳相与模。

When stillness reaches its extreme, movement can arise; yin and yang serve as each other’s mold.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

“龙”者，女子之月经也。“斩龙”者，用法炼断月经，使永远不复再行也。若问：“月经何以名为龙？”则自唐朝以后，至于今日，凡丹书所写，及口诀所传，皆同此说，当有一种意义存于其间，暂可不必详解。若问：“女子修道，何故要先断月经？”此则神仙家独得之传授，无上之玄机，非世界各种宗教、各种哲学、各种生理卫生学所能比拟。女子修炼与男子不同者，即在于此；女子成功较男子更速者，亦在于此。若离开此道、别寻门路，决无成功之希望。

或者谓：“既是月经为修道之累，必须炼断，则老年妇人月经天然断绝者，岂不省却许多工夫，其成就当比少年者更易？”不知若彼童女月经未行者，果生有夙慧，悟彻玄功，成就自然更易。一到老年，月经干枯，生机缺乏，与童女有霄壤之殊，何能一概而论？

法要无中生有，使老年天癸已绝者，复有通行之象。然后再以有还无，按照少年女子修炼成规，渐渐依次而斩之，斯为更难，岂云更易？所以古德劝人“添油宜及早，接命莫教迟。”

静极则动，动极则静；阳极则阴，阴极则阳，乃理气自然之循环，无足怪者。《道德经》第十五章云：“孰能浊以静之徐清？孰能安以久动之徐生？”上句言人能静，则身中浊气渐化为清气；下句言静之既久，则身中又渐生动机矣。

《道德经》第十六章云：“致虚极，守静笃；万物并作，吾以观其复。”上二句言静极，下二句言生动。“复”即复卦之复。阴象静，阳象动，五阴之下，一阳来复，亦言“静极生动”也。

“模”者，模范，所以成物。“相与模”者，盖言阴阳互根，彼此互相成就而不可离之意。

The “dragon” is a woman’s menstruation. “Beheading the dragon” means using a method to refine away the menses, so that they never flow again. If one asks, “Why is menstruation called the dragon?” — from the Tang dynasty down to today, all that the alchemical books write and the oral formulas transmit say the same; there must be some meaning in it, which need not be explained in detail for now. If one asks, “Why must women who cultivate the Dao first stop menstruation?” — this is a transmission held only by the immortalist school, a supreme mysterious mechanism, beyond comparison with the world’s various religions, philosophies, and sciences of physiology and hygiene. This is where women’s cultivation differs from men’s; this is also why women succeed faster than men. If one departs from this path and seeks another way, there is no hope of success at all.

Some say: “Since menstruation is a burden to cultivating the Dao and must be refined away, then old women whose menstruation has ceased naturally must save a great deal of work, and their accomplishment should be easier than that of young women?” They do not know that if a girl whose menstruation has not yet begun is truly born with innate wisdom and has thoroughly understood the mysterious work, her accomplishment is naturally easier. But once one reaches old age, the menses have dried up and vitality is lacking — as different from a young girl as heaven from earth; how can they be lumped together?

The method (for older women) must produce something from nothing, causing an old woman whose tiangui has ceased to show again the signs of menstruation flowing; then return from something to nothing again, beheading it gradually and in order according to the established rules of cultivation for young women — this is harder; how can it be called easier? Hence the virtuous ancients urged: “Add oil early; do not delay in prolonging life.”

When stillness reaches its extreme, there is movement; when movement reaches its extreme, there is stillness; when yang reaches its extreme it turns to yin, when yin reaches its extreme it turns to yang — this is the natural cycle of principle and breath, nothing strange about it. Chapter 15 of the Daode jing says: “Who can, being turbid, by stillness slowly become clear? Who can, being at rest, by long movement slowly come to life?” The first line says that if a person can be still, the turbid breath in the body gradually transforms into clear breath; the second line says that when stillness has lasted long, the mechanism of movement gradually arises again in the body.

Chapter 16 of the Daode jing says: “Attain the utmost emptiness, keep steadfast stillness; the myriad things arise together, and I thereby watch their return.” The first two lines speak of the extreme of stillness, the last two of movement arising. “Return” (fu 复) is the fu of the hexagram Fu: yin stands for stillness and yang for movement; beneath five yin lines one yang line returns — this too is “movement arising at the extreme of stillness.”

“Mold” (mo 模) means a model, that by which things are formed. “Serving as each other’s mold” means that yin and yang are each other’s root, complete each other, and cannot be separated.

§2
Original (Chinese)

风中擒玉虎，月里捉金乌。

In the wind seize the jade tiger; in the moon catch the golden crow.

Chen Yingning's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

“风”者，人之呼吸也。如丹经云：“后天呼吸起微风。”又云：“吹嘘藉巽风。”皆是此意。道书常以“虎”配西方金，“龙”配东方木；凡言铅、言金、言虎，都属一物，不过比喻人身中静极而动之先天阳炁而已。

“月”有二义：若言性功者，则当一念不生时谓之月，谓其清净无瑕，孤明独照也；若言命功，则当先天阳气发动时亦谓之月，譬如晦朔弦望，轮转不忒也。“金乌”即日之代名词，日即离，离即火，火即汞，汞即神也。

当采取先天气之时，须借后天气以为枢纽，故曰“风中擒玉虎”。玉字表其温和之状。石杏林真人曰：“万籁风初起，千山月乍圆。”正是此景。

丹道有风必有火，气动神必应，故吕纯阳真人云：“铅亦生，汞亦生，生汞生铅一处烹。”铅与月，喻阳气；汞与金乌，喻阴神。阳气发生，阴神必同时而应，故曰“月里捉金乌”。

“Wind” is a person’s breathing. As the alchemical scriptures say, “The breathing of Posterior Heaven raises a gentle wind,” and also, “Blowing and breathing rely on the Wind of Xun” — both mean this. Daoist books often pair the “tiger” with metal of the west and the “dragon” with wood of the east; whatever speaks of lead, of metal, or of the tiger refers to one and the same thing, merely a metaphor for the yang breath of Prior Heaven that moves at the extreme of stillness in the human body.

“Moon” has two meanings: in speaking of the work on nature, the moment when not a single thought arises is called the moon, meaning that it is pure and flawless, shining alone in solitary brightness; in speaking of the work on life, the moment when the yang breath of Prior Heaven stirs is also called the moon, like the moon’s dark, new, quarter, and full phases, turning without error. The “golden crow” is another name for the sun; the sun is Li, Li is fire, fire is mercury, and mercury is spirit.

When gathering the breath of Prior Heaven, one must use the breath of Posterior Heaven as the pivot; hence “in the wind seize the jade tiger.” The word “jade” indicates its gentle, warm state. The Perfected Shi Xinglin said: “The ten thousand pipes of nature — the wind first rises; on a thousand mountains — the moon is just full.” This is exactly the scene.

In the alchemical Way, where there is wind there must be fire; when the breath moves, the spirit must respond. Hence the Perfected Lü Chunyang said: “Lead is born, and mercury is born; the mercury born and the lead born are cooked together in one place.” Lead and the moon are metaphors for yang breath; mercury and the golden crow are metaphors for the yin spirit. When the yang breath arises, the yin spirit must respond at the same time; hence “in the moon catch the golden crow.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

著眼絪缊候，留心顺逆途。

Fix your eyes on the moment of dense mingling; take heed of the paths of following and reversing.

Chen Yingning's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

《易》曰：“天地絪缊，万物化醇。”盖“絪缊”者，天气下交于地，地气上交于天，温和酝酿，欲雨未雨，将雷未雷，所谓“万里阴沉春气合”者是也。若雷雨既施，则非絪缊矣。

人身絪缊之候，亦同此理，但究竟是如何现象？则因有难言之隐，不便写在纸上，聪明女子，若得真传，则可及时下功，否则恐当面错过。虽说有自造机会之可能，总不若天然机会之巧妙，此时如顺其机而行人道，则可受胎生子；逆其机而行仙道，则可采药还丹。

然顺逆之意，尚不止此，生机外发为顺，生机内敛为逆；生气下行，变为月经为顺，生气上行，不使化经为逆。故道书云：“男子修成不漏精，女子修成不漏经。”

The Changes says: “Heaven and Earth mingle densely, and the myriad things are transformed and made pure.” “Dense mingling” (yinyun 絪缊) is when the breath of Heaven descends to join Earth and the breath of Earth rises to join Heaven, warm and gentle, brewing, about to rain but not yet raining, about to thunder but not yet thundering — what is called “ten thousand miles dark and heavy, the breath of spring united.” Once thunder and rain have come, it is no longer dense mingling.

The moment of dense mingling in the human body follows the same principle; but what exactly is the phenomenon? Because there are matters difficult to speak of, it is not convenient to write them on paper. An intelligent woman who receives the true transmission can apply the work in time; otherwise she may miss it face to face. Although it is said that one may create the opportunity oneself, this is never as wonderful as the natural opportunity. At this time, if one follows this mechanism and takes the human way, one can conceive and bear a child; if one goes against this mechanism and takes the immortal way, one can gather the medicine and return the elixir.

Yet the meaning of following and reversing goes further than this: the vital mechanism issuing outward is following, the vital mechanism gathered inward is reversing; the vital breath going downward and turning into menses is following, the vital breath going upward and not being allowed to turn into menses is reversing. Hence the Daoist books say: “Men in cultivation achieve the non-leakage of semen; women in cultivation achieve the non-leakage of menses.”

§4
Original (Chinese)

鹊桥重过处，丹炁复归炉。

Where the Magpie Bridge is crossed again, the elixir breath returns once more to the furnace.

Chen Yingning's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

《入药镜》云：“上鹊桥，下鹊桥；天应星，地应潮。”后世丹经言“鹊桥”者，皆本于此。凡炼丹之运用，必先由下鹊桥转上背脊，撞通玉枕，直达泥丸；再由上鹊桥转下胸前十二重楼，还归元海。“上鹊桥”在印堂、山根之里，“下鹊桥”在尾闾、会阴之间。丹炁转到上鹊桥时，自觉两眉之间有圆光闪灼，故曰“天应星”；丹炁由下鹊桥上升时，自觉血海之中有热气蒸腾，故曰“地应潮”。此言“鹊桥重过”者，兼上下言之也。

“归炉”者，归到黄庭，黄庭一名坤炉。（按上下鹊桥，另有别解，此处不具论。）

The Mirror for Compounding the Medicine says: “The upper Magpie Bridge, the lower Magpie Bridge; Heaven answers with stars, Earth answers with tides.” Later alchemical scriptures that speak of the “Magpie Bridge” all take this as their basis. In all the workings of refining the elixir, it must first turn from the lower Magpie Bridge up the spine, break through the Jade Pillow, and reach straight to the Muddy Pellet; then from the upper Magpie Bridge it turns down through the Twelve-Storied Tower in front of the chest and returns to the Original Sea. The “upper Magpie Bridge” is inside the Hall of Impressions and the Root of the Mountain; the “lower Magpie Bridge” is between the Caudal Funnel and the perineum. When the elixir breath turns to the upper Magpie Bridge, one feels a round light flickering between the two eyebrows, hence “Heaven answers with stars”; when the elixir breath rises from the lower Magpie Bridge, one feels hot breath steaming in the Sea of Blood, hence “Earth answers with tides.” “The Magpie Bridge crossed again” here refers to both the upper and lower bridges.

“Returning to the furnace” means returning to the Yellow Court; the Yellow Court is also called the Kun furnace. (Note: there are other interpretations of the upper and lower Magpie Bridges, which are not discussed here.)

Key Terms

  • Beheading the Red Dragon: using practice to stop menstruation; the “dragon” means menstruation.
  • Tiangui 天癸: in Chinese medicine, the substance that brings about reproductive function; here it means menstruation.
  • Adding oil to prolong life: “Add oil early; do not delay in prolonging life.”
  • Movement arising at the extreme of stillness, the return of the one yang: see Living Zi Hour.
  • Serving as each other’s mold: yin and yang as each other’s root, completing each other.
  • Wind: breathing; see Wind of Xun.
  • Jade tiger, golden crow: the jade tiger is a metaphor for the yang breath of Prior Heaven (lead), the golden crow for spirit (mercury); see Lead and Mercury.
  • Moon (two meanings): in the work on nature, not a single thought arising; in the work on life, the yang breath of Prior Heaven stirring.
  • Dense mingling: the close intercourse of the breaths of Heaven and Earth, the state of about to rain but not yet raining; in the body, the corresponding moment when the vital mechanism begins to stir.
  • Following and reversing: following leads to conceiving and bearing a child, turning into menses; reversing leads to gathering the medicine and returning the elixir, the non-leakage of menses. See the method of reversal.
  • Magpie Bridge: the upper Magpie Bridge is inside the Hall of Impressions and the Root of the Mountain, the lower between the Caudal Funnel and the perineum.
  • Heaven answers with stars, Earth answers with tides: when the elixir breath reaches the upper Magpie Bridge, a round light flickers between the eyebrows; when it rises from the lower Magpie Bridge, hot breath steams in the Sea of Blood.
  • Kun furnace: the Yellow Court.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Please read this poem as a document. The goal of “beheading the dragon” is, in the eyes of modern medicine, a symptom requiring examination, and should not be taken as a direction of practice.
  2. The principle of “movement at the extreme of stillness” is universal. The two lines of the Daode jing — being turbid, by stillness slowly becoming clear; being at rest, by long movement slowly coming to life — applied to daily life: first let the mind become quiet and clear, and vitality will come of itself.
  3. Chen Yingning’s two explanations of the “moon” are useful: in the work on nature, “not a single thought arising” is that bright moon — pure and flawless, shining alone in solitary brightness. This is a state of quiet sitting anyone can experience.
  4. The essence of following and reversing is “issuing outward” and “gathering inward.” For ordinary practitioners this can be understood as: spend less outward, nourish more inward.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Ten thousand miles dark and heavy, the breath of spring united”: a quotation in the alchemical books; its source remains to be determined.
  2. “Who can, being turbid, by stillness slowly become clear? Who can, being at rest, by long movement slowly come to life?”: Laozi, chapter 15.
  3. “Attain the utmost emptiness, keep steadfast stillness; the myriad things arise together, and I thereby watch their return”: Laozi, chapter 16.
  4. “The breathing of Posterior Heaven raises a gentle wind,” “Blowing and breathing rely on the Wind of Xun”: common sayings in the alchemical scriptures; for the latter, compare “in the water, blowing up relies on the Wind of Xun” in the Formula of Free and Easy Wandering quoted in Part 1, the Secret of the Golden Flower.
  5. “The upper Magpie Bridge, the lower Magpie Bridge; Heaven answers with stars, Earth answers with tides”: a line from Cui Xifan’s Mirror for Compounding the Medicine.
  6. “Because there are matters difficult to speak of, it is not convenient to write them on paper”: so in Chen Yingning’s original; we add no speculative supplement.

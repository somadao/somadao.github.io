Appendix: Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy, with Commentary · 第三首 行功

Poem 3 · Practicing the Work

Poem 3 · Practicing the Work — the last two lines for women only

About This Chapter

敛息凝神处，东方生气来。万缘都不著，一气复归台。阴象宜前降，阳光许后栽。山头并海底，雨过一声雷。

The third poem is about “practicing the work”; the first six lines are the same for men and women, the last two for women only.

  • When the breathing lies hidden and the spirit is concentrated without scattering, the living breath comes;
  • When one clings to none of the myriad conditions, the One Breath returns to the Numinous Terrace (life returns to nature);
  • The yang fire rises along the back, the yin tally descends along the chest;
  • When a woman practices the work, the breath rushes up from the “sea bottom” (the Sea of Blood of the womb) to the “mountain top” (the two breasts), like a clap of thunder after rain — this is a woman’s Living Zi Hour.

The passage in Chen Yingning’s commentary most worth remembering is his refusal to fix in writing “how the breath is gathered in, how the spirit is concentrated”: “If it were all written down on paper, it would turn a living method into a dead one; people’s temperaments differ and their constitutions vary, and learning such a dead method only leads to illness.” He also distinguishes clearly: the “thunder” spoken of in the alchemical scriptures of the various schools is the great stirring of the One Breath of Prior Heaven after long accumulation, whereas Sun Bu’er’s poem here comes before “Beheading the Dragon” and can hardly have this effect; it probably means only that breath rushes up from the Sea of Blood to the two breasts.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

敛息凝神处，东方生气来。

Where the breath is gathered in and the spirit concentrated, the living breath comes from the east.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

“敛息”者，呼吸之气蛰藏而不动也；“凝神”者，虚灵之神凝定而不散也。“东方”者，日出之位；“生气”者，对于死气而言。古之修炼家行吐纳之功者，大概于寅卯二时，面对东方，招摄空中生气，入于吾身，借其势力，而驱出身内停蓄之死气。

上乘丹法，虽不限定时间与方所，然总宜在山林清静之区，日暖风和之候，则身中效验随做随来，如立竿见影。果能常常凝神敛息，酝酿薰蒸，不久即可由造化窟中，采取先天一气。孔子云：“先天而天弗违。天且弗违，而况于人乎？况于鬼神乎？”

此段作用，乃真实工夫，非空谈，亦非理想，惟证方知。若问“息如何敛？神如何凝？处在何处？来从何来？”既非片语能明，且笔墨亦难宣达，须经多次辩论，多次实验，又要学者夙具慧根，苦心孤诣，方可入门。若一一写在纸上，反令活法变成死法，世人性情不同，体质各异，学此死法，适足致疾，非徒无益，而又害之，将何取耶？

“Gathering in the breath” means that the breath of respiration lies hidden and does not move; “concentrating the spirit” means that the empty, numinous spirit is concentrated and settled and does not scatter. “The east” is the direction of sunrise; “living breath” is said in contrast to dead breath. The cultivators of old who practiced exhaling and inhaling mostly did so in the yin and mao double-hours (about three to seven in the morning), facing east, drawing the living breath from the air into their bodies and using its force to drive out the dead breath stagnating within.

Although the highest alchemical methods do not restrict time and place, it is always best to practice in a quiet place among mountains and forests, when the sun is warm and the wind mild; then the effects in the body come as soon as one practices, like a shadow appearing as soon as a pole is set up. If one can truly concentrate the spirit and gather in the breath constantly, brewing and steaming, before long one can gather the One Breath of Prior Heaven from the cave of creation. Confucius said: “He acts before Heaven, and Heaven does not go against him. If even Heaven does not go against him, how much less will men? How much less will ghosts and spirits?”

The working of this passage is real work, neither empty talk nor an ideal; only by verifying it oneself does one know it. If one asks, “How is the breath gathered in? How is the spirit concentrated? Where is the place? Where does (the living breath) come from?” — this can neither be made clear in a few words nor easily conveyed by brush and ink; it requires many discussions and many experiments, and the student must also have innate wisdom and apply himself with painstaking effort before he can enter the gate. If it were all written down on paper item by item, it would instead turn a living method into a dead one; people’s temperaments differ and their constitutions vary, and learning such a dead method only leads to illness — not merely useless but harmful. What would be the good of that?

§2
Original (Chinese)

万缘都不著，一气复归台。

Clinging to none of the myriad conditions, the One Breath returns again to the terrace.

Chen Yingning's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

昔人云：“修道者须谢绝万缘，坚持一念，使此心寂寂如死，而后可以不死；使此气绵绵不停，而后可以长停。”“台”者何？灵台也，灵台者性也；“一气”者，命也。命来归性，即是还丹。

张紫阳真人云：“修炼至此，泥丸风生，绛宫月明，丹田火炽，谷海波澄，夹脊如车轮，四肢如山石，毛窍如浴之方起，骨脉如睡之正酣，精神如夫妇之欢合，魂魄如子母之留恋。此乃真境界，非譬喻也”。以上所云，可谓形容极致。

The ancients said: “Those who cultivate the Dao must decline all conditions and hold firmly to one thought, making this mind still as death; only then can they not die. Let this breath continue without stopping; only then can it stop for long.” What is the “terrace”? It is the Numinous Terrace, and the Numinous Terrace is nature; “the One Breath” is life. Life coming back to nature is the Returned Elixir.

The Perfected Zhang Ziyang said: “When cultivation reaches this point, wind arises in the Muddy Pellet, the moon is bright in the Crimson Palace, fire blazes in the Cinnabar Field, the waves of the valley sea are clear, the Spine-Straddling Pass turns like a cartwheel, the four limbs are as steady as mountain rock, the pores feel as if one had just come out of a bath, the bones and vessels as if one were in the deepest sleep, essence and spirit like husband and wife in joyful union, hun and po like mother and child clinging to each other. This is a true realm, not a metaphor.” What is said above may be called description at its utmost.

§3
Original (Chinese)

阴象宜前降，阳光许后栽。

The yin image should descend in front; the yang light is to be planted behind.

Chen Yingning's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

阳火阴符之运用，虽出于自然，但人工亦有默化潜移之力，不可不知。自尾闾升上泥丸，乃在背脊一路，名为“进阳火”；自泥丸降下气海，乃在胸前一路，名为“退阴符”。以升为进，以降为退。

又凡后升之时，身中自觉热气蒸腾；及至前降之时，则热气已渐归冷静。此以热气盛为“进阳火”，热气平为“退阴符”。二解虽义有不同，理则一贯，此中有许多奥妙，应当研究。

Although the working of the yang fire and the yin tally arises from nature, human effort also has a power of quiet, subtle influence that must be known. Rising from the Caudal Funnel up to the Muddy Pellet is along the path of the spine, and is called “advancing the yang fire”; descending from the Muddy Pellet down to the Sea of Breath is along the path in front of the chest, and is called “withdrawing the yin tally.” Rising is advancing, descending is withdrawing.

Also, whenever it rises behind, one feels hot breath steaming up in the body; and when it descends in front, the hot breath has gradually returned to coolness and calm. Here strong hot breath is “advancing the yang fire,” and calmed hot breath is “withdrawing the yin tally.” Although the two explanations differ in meaning, the principle is one and the same; there are many subtleties in this that should be studied.

§4
Original (Chinese)

山头并海底，雨过一声雷。

Between the mountain top and the sea bottom, after the rain, a clap of thunder.

Chen Yingning's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

吕纯阳真人《步蟾宫词》云：“地雷震动山头雨”。《百字碑》云：“阴阳生反覆，普化一声雷。”邵康节先生诗云：“忽然夜半一声雷，万户千门次第开。”

钟离真人云：“达人采得先天炁，一夜雷声不暂停。”彭鹤林先生云：“九华天上人知得，一夜风雷撼万山。”

丹经言雷者甚多，不可殚述。其源皆出于《周易》地雷复一卦，其实则喻先天一气积蓄既久，势力雄厚，应机发动之现象耳。其气之来也，周身关窍齐开，耳闻风声，脑后震动，眼中闪光，鼻中抽掣，种种景象，宜预知之，方免临时惊慌失措。

然女工修炼，欲求到此地步，必在月经断绝之后。而孙诗所云，乃在斩龙之前，恐难得此效。大约此处所谓雷者，不过言行功之时，血海中有气上冲于两乳耳。此气发生，丹家名曰活子时。

“山头”喻两乳及膻中部位；“海底”喻子宫血海部位。“雨”喻阴气；“雷”喻阳气。

The Perfected Lü Chunyang’s lyric “Stepping into the Toad Palace” says: “The earth-thunder shakes the rain on the mountain top.” The Hundred-Character Stele says: “Yin and yang give rise to reversal; a clap of thunder transforms all.” Master Shao Kangjie’s poem says: “Suddenly at midnight a clap of thunder; ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open one after another.”

The Perfected Zhongli said: “When the adept gathers the breath of Prior Heaven, the thunder does not pause all night.” Master Peng Helin said: “Those in the heaven of Nine Flowers know it: in one night wind and thunder shake ten thousand mountains.”

The alchemical scriptures that speak of thunder are very many, too many to list. Their source is the hexagram Fu, Earth over Thunder, of the Book of Changes; in reality it is a metaphor for the phenomenon of the One Breath of Prior Heaven, having accumulated for a long time and grown powerful, stirring in response to the moment. When this breath comes, all the passes and openings of the body open together, the ears hear the sound of wind, there is a jolt behind the head, flashes of light in the eyes, twitching in the nose — all kinds of scenes, which one should know in advance so as not to be alarmed and at a loss when the time comes.

But for a woman’s cultivation to reach this point, it must be after menstruation has ceased. What Sun’s poem speaks of, however, comes before Beheading the Dragon, and it is probably hard to obtain this effect. Probably the “thunder” here only means that, when practicing the work, breath rushes up from the Sea of Blood to the two breasts. When this breath arises, the alchemists call it the Living Zi Hour.

“The mountain top” is a metaphor for the two breasts and the area of Danzhong; “the sea bottom” is a metaphor for the area of the Sea of Blood of the womb. “Rain” is a metaphor for yin breath, “thunder” for yang breath.

Key Terms

  • Gathering in the breath, concentrating the spirit: the breath of respiration lying hidden and unmoving, the empty, numinous spirit concentrated, settled, and not scattering; see concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath.
  • The east, living breath: the direction of sunrise; as opposed to dead breath.
  • The yin and mao double-hours: about three to seven in the morning.
  • Numinous Terrace: that is, nature; see Original Spirit; “life coming back to nature is the Returned Elixir”; see nature and life.
  • Advancing the yang fire, withdrawing the yin tally: rising from the Caudal Funnel along the spine to the Muddy Pellet is advancing, descending from the Muddy Pellet along the chest to the Sea of Breath is withdrawing; alternatively, strong hot breath is advancing and calmed hot breath is withdrawing. See fire phasing.
  • Mountain top, sea bottom: in this poem, the two breasts and Danzhong, and the Sea of Blood of the womb, respectively. Note that this differs from what “sea bottom” (the perineum) means in men’s alchemy.
  • Thunder: the image of the accumulated One Breath of Prior Heaven stirring; in this poem Chen Yingning takes it to be only the breath of the Sea of Blood rushing up to the two breasts.
  • Earth over Thunder, Fu: the hexagram Fu (Return) of the Book of Changes, one yang returning beneath five yin lines.

Putting It into Practice

  1. A living method, not a dead one. Chen Yingning would rather not write down “how the breath is gathered in, how the spirit is concentrated” than have people practice a dead method and make themselves ill. We should read the alchemical scriptures in the same way: understand the principles, practice gently according to your own constitution and condition, and when in doubt ask a knowledgeable teacher.
  2. “Clinging to none of the myriad conditions” is the precondition of practicing the work. Before quiet sitting, first put down what you are doing and carry no concerns in your mind; only then can there be talk of concentrating the spirit and gathering in the breath.
  3. When phenomena such as heat or tremors appear in the body, “one should know them in advance so as not to be alarmed and at a loss when the time comes.” Knowing that such things exist, do not panic when they appear, and do not seek them when they do not.
  4. Note Chen Yingning’s distinction: the great scenes described by the ancients are not the same thing as the early-stage “breath of the Sea of Blood rushing up to the two breasts” in women. Do not apply advanced descriptions to early experiences.

Translator's Notes

  1. “He acts before Heaven, and Heaven does not go against him… how much less will ghosts and spirits?”: from the Book of Changes, hexagram Qian, “Commentary on the Words of the Text.”
  2. “A clap of thunder transforms all”: from the Hundred-Character Stele (attributed to Lü Dongbin).
  3. “Suddenly at midnight a clap of thunder; ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open one after another”: lines from a poem by Shao Yong (Kangjie).
  4. Peng Helin: Peng Si, a Daoist of the Southern School in the Song, disciple of Bai Yuchan.
  5. “The yang light is to be planted behind” (阳光许后栽): 栽 here means to cultivate, to make rise.

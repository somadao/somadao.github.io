Poem 1 · Gathering in the Mind

Poem 1 · Gathering in the Mind — the same for men and women

About This Chapter

吾身未有日，一气已先存。似玉磨逾润，如金炼岂昏。扫空生灭海，固守总持门。半黍虚灵处，融融火候温。

The first poem is about “gathering in the mind,” and is the same for men and women. The meaning of its four couplets:

  • Before this body existed, the One Breath of Prior Heaven already existed — cultivation is returning to it;
  • Like jade, the more it is polished the more lustrous; like gold, the more it is refined the brighter;
  • Sweep away the “sea of arising and ceasing” of thoughts; the method is to guard the “Gate of All-Holding” (the Mysterious Pass);
  • While guarding, with an utterly subtle mind, in the place of empty numinosity, let the fire phasing be warm and moderate.

Chen Yingning’s commentary contains two especially important warnings: though at the start one must remove deluded thoughts, by no means is the work done solely on thoughts — relying on nothing and thinking of nothing will surely have no effect at all; and guarding the Mysterious Pass by no means means fixing on some part of the body and rigidly guarding it — if beginners rigidly guard one spot without knowing how to adapt, they will surely develop strange illnesses later.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

吾身未有日，一气已先存。

Before the day my body existed, the One Breath was already there.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

吾人未有此身，先有此气。《谭子化书》云：“虚化神，神化气，气化血，血化形，形化婴，婴化童，童化少，少化壮，壮化老，老化死。”此言顺则成人。若达道之士，能逆而行之，使血化气，气化神，神化虚，则成道矣。

“一气”者，即先天阴阳未判之气；至于分阴分阳，两仪既立，则不得名为一气。儒家云：“其为物不二，则其生物不测。”亦指先天一气而言。老氏之得一，即得此一气也。此中有实在工夫，非空谈可以了事。

Before we have this body, we first have this breath. Master Tan’s Book of Transformations says: “Emptiness transforms into spirit, spirit transforms into breath, breath transforms into blood, blood transforms into form, form transforms into an infant, the infant into a child, the child into a youth, the youth into an adult, the adult into an old person, the old person into death.” This says that following the current one becomes a human being (and goes on to old age and death). One who has penetrated the Dao can go against the current, making blood transform into breath, breath into spirit, and spirit into emptiness, and so attains the Dao.

“The One Breath” is the breath of Prior Heaven before yin and yang have divided; once yin and yang have been divided and the two modes established, it can no longer be called the One Breath. The Confucians say: “As a thing it is not two, therefore its producing of things is unfathomable.” This too refers to the One Breath of Prior Heaven. Laozi’s “attaining the One” is attaining this One Breath. There is real work in this, not something that empty talk can settle.

§2
Original (Chinese)

似玉磨逾润，如金炼岂昏。

Like jade, the more it is polished the more lustrous; like gold, refined — how could it be dim?

Chen Yingning's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

丹家常有玉池、金鼎、玉兔、金乌，玉液、金液种种名目。大凡言阴、言神、言文火者，则以“玉”拟之；言阳、言气、言武火者，则以“金”拟之。意谓玉有温和之德，金有坚刚之象也。然亦偶有例外。

The alchemists often have all kinds of names such as jade pool, golden cauldron, jade hare, golden crow, jade liquor, golden liquor. In general, whatever speaks of yin, of spirit, or of civil fire is likened to “jade”; whatever speaks of yang, of breath, or of martial fire is likened to “gold.” The idea is that jade has the virtue of gentle warmth and gold the image of firm strength. But there are occasional exceptions.

§3
Original (Chinese)

扫空生灭海，固守总持门。

Sweep away the sea of arising and ceasing; firmly guard the Gate of All-Holding.

Chen Yingning's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

“生灭海”即吾人之念头。刹那之间，杂念无端而至，忽起忽灭，莫能定止。念起为生，念灭为死，一日之内，万死万生，轮迴即在目前，何须待证于身后？然欲扫空此念，谈何容易！惟有用法使念头一耳。其法如何？即固守总持门也。

“总持门”者，老子名为玄牝之门，即后世道家所谓玄关一窍。张紫阳云：“此窍非凡窍，乾坤共合成，名为神气穴，内有坎离精。”质而言之，不过一阴一阳、一神一气而已。能使阴阳相合，神气相搏，则玄关之体已立。

虽说初下手要除妄念，然决不是专在念头上做工夫，若一切不依，一切不想，其弊必至毫无效果，令人失望灰心，是宜熟思而明辨也。（紫阳此诗，另有一解，不在本篇范围之内。）

“The sea of arising and ceasing” is our thoughts. In an instant, distracting thoughts come for no reason, suddenly arising and suddenly ceasing, and cannot be made to stop. A thought arising is birth, a thought ceasing is death; within a single day, ten thousand deaths and ten thousand births — transmigration is right before our eyes; why wait to verify it after death? Yet to sweep these thoughts away — that is easier said than done! The only way is to use a method to make thought one. What is the method? It is firmly guarding the Gate of All-Holding.

“The Gate of All-Holding” is what Laozi called the gate of the Mysterious Female, that is, what later Daoists call the one opening of the Mysterious Pass. Zhang Ziyang said: “This opening is no ordinary opening; Qian and Kun together formed it; it is named the cavity of spirit and breath, and within it is the essence of Kan and Li.” To put it plainly, it is nothing but one yin and one yang, one spirit and one breath. If one can make yin and yang unite and spirit and breath grapple together, the substance of the Mysterious Pass is already established.

Although it is said that at the start one must remove deluded thoughts, by no means is the work done solely on thoughts. If one relies on nothing and thinks of nothing, the fault will surely be that there is no effect at all, leaving one disappointed and disheartened. This must be carefully considered and clearly distinguished. (Ziyang’s poem has another interpretation, which is outside the scope of this part.)

§4
Original (Chinese)

半黍虚灵处，融融火候温。

In the place of empty numinosity half a millet grain in size, the fire phasing is melting and warm.

Chen Yingning's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

“半黍”者，言凝神入气穴时，神在气中，气包神外，退藏于密，其用至微至细，故以半黍喻之。“虚”者，不滞于迹象。“灵”者，不堕于昏沉。杂念不可起，念起则火燥；真意不可散，意散则火寒。必如老子所云：“绵绵若存，用之不勤。”方合乎中道。“融融”者，调和适宜。“温”者，不寒不燥也。

此诗二句言守玄关时之真实下手工夫，维妙维肖。然决不是执著人身某一处部位而死守之，切勿误会。若初学者死守一处，不知变通，将来必得怪病。

“Half a millet grain” means that when concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, the spirit is within the breath and the breath envelops the spirit, withdrawn and hidden in secrecy; its working is utterly minute and subtle, hence the metaphor of half a millet grain. “Empty” means not lingering on traces and appearances; “numinous” means not falling into drowsiness. Distracting thoughts must not arise — when thoughts arise, the fire is parching; the True Intention must not scatter — when the intention scatters, the fire is cold. Only when it is as Laozi said, “continuous, as if present; used without exertion,” does it accord with the middle way. “Melting” means harmonious and well adjusted; “warm” means neither cold nor parching.

These two lines of the poem describe the real work of beginning when guarding the Mysterious Pass, to the life. But by no means is it fixing on a certain part of the body and rigidly guarding it; be sure not to misunderstand. If beginners rigidly guard one spot without knowing how to adapt, they will surely develop strange illnesses later.

Key Terms

  • One Breath of Prior Heaven: “the breath of Prior Heaven before yin and yang have divided.”
  • Following the current one becomes human, going against it one attains the Dao: see the method of reversal.
  • Jade, gold: yin, spirit, and civil fire are likened to jade; yang, breath, and martial fire to gold.
  • Sea of arising and ceasing: the sea of thoughts that arise and cease without stopping. “Arising and ceasing” is a Buddhist term.
  • Gate of All-Holding: that is, the gate of the Mysterious Female, the one opening of the Mysterious Pass; Chen Yingning’s note quotes Zhang Ziyang, “named the cavity of spirit and breath, and within it is the essence of Kan and Li.”
  • Half a millet grain: describing the utterly subtle use of the mind when concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath.
  • Empty, numinous: not lingering on traces and appearances, not falling into drowsiness; see drowsiness and scatteredness.
  • The fire parching, the fire cold: when thoughts arise it is parching, when the intention scatters it is cold; see fire phasing.
  • “Continuous, as if present; used without exertion”: Laozi, chapter 6.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Do not do battle with thoughts. Chen Yingning says it clearly: removing deluded thoughts “by no means is the work done solely on thoughts”; relying on nothing and thinking of nothing has no effect at all. The method is to give the mind something to rest on — guarding the Mysterious Pass, mind and breath relying on each other — so that thoughts naturally become one.
  2. Do not rigidly guard one part of the body. “If beginners rigidly guard one spot without knowing how to adapt, they will surely develop strange illnesses later.” Attention can rest lightly somewhere, but it must be alive, not stiff.
  3. The two words “empty” and “numinous” are the standard: not clinging to any scene (empty), and yet not drowsy (numinous).
  4. The fire phasing is simply neither cold nor parching. When thoughts arise it is parching; when attention scatters it is cold; return to a warm, gentle state, as if present and as if absent.

Translator's Notes

  1. Master Tan’s Book of Transformations: the Book of Transformations (Huashu 化书) by Tan Qiao of the Five Dynasties.
  2. “As a thing it is not two, therefore its producing of things is unfathomable”: from the Doctrine of the Mean, chapter 26.
  3. “Laozi’s attaining the One”: referring to Laozi, chapter 39, “Heaven attained the One and became clear, Earth attained the One and became settled…”
  4. “This opening is no ordinary opening… within it is the essence of Kan and Li”: lines from Zhang Boduan’s Four Hundred Words on the Golden Elixir.
  5. “Transmigration” (轮迴): the same as 轮回.
  6. Format of this part: each poem is divided into four short passages of one couplet each; each passage gives first the original poem and its translation, then the original text of Chen Yingning’s note and its translation.

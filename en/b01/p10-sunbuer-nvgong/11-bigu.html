Appendix: Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy, with Commentary · 第十一首 辟谷

Poem 11 · Abstaining from Grain

Poem 11 · Abstaining from Grain — the same for men and women

Safety note (translator): This poem is about “abstaining from grain,” that is, giving up cooked food. Chen Yingning says plainly that this is a matter of the numinous breath being full so that one naturally does not want to eat, and “does not mean enduring hunger on an empty stomach.” Even so, it is only a record of advanced ancient cultivation. Fasting on your own or prolonged excessive dieting carries serious health risks (low blood sugar, electrolyte imbalance, malnutrition, heart arrhythmia, and so on), and is especially unsuitable for people with chronic illness or diabetes, during pregnancy or breastfeeding, for adolescents, and for anyone with a history of eating disorders. Please do not imitate it.

About This Chapter

既得餐灵气，清冷肺腑奇。忘神无相著，合极有空离。朝食寻山芋，昏饥采泽芝。若将烟火混，体不履瑶池。

The eleventh poem is about “abstaining from grain,” and is the same for men and women.

  • Since one now dines on numinous breath, the lungs and viscera are cool and wondrously so — this is truly giving up cooked food, because the numinous breath is full and one naturally does not want to eat;
  • Forgetting the spirit (having wisdom and not using it), not attached to forms; uniting with the Ultimate, leaving emptiness when it is met — form and emptiness both forgotten, wholly in great absorption;
  • In the morning look for mountain taro; when hungry at dusk gather marsh mushrooms;
  • If one again mixes in food cooked over smoke and fire, ordinary turbid breath is mixed in, and one will not reach the Jasper Pool.

Chen Yingning’s explanation of “forgetting the spirit” is interesting: “At this time, though one has wisdom, one does not use it; if one shows off one’s cleverness, demonic obstacles easily arise.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

既得餐灵气，清冷肺腑奇。

Since one has come to dine on numinous breath, the lungs and viscera are cool and wondrous.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

此实行断绝烟火食也。所以能如此者，因灵气充满于吾身，自然不思食，非枵腹忍饥之谓也。

This is actually giving up food cooked over smoke and fire. The reason one can do this is that numinous breath fills one’s body, so that one naturally does not think of food; it does not mean enduring hunger on an empty stomach.

§2
Original (Chinese)

忘神无相著，合极有空离。

Forgetting the spirit, there is no form to cling to; uniting with the Ultimate, emptiness, when present, is left.

Chen Yingning's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

“忘神”者，此时虽有智慧而不用；若卖弄聪明，则易生魔障。“无相著”者，谓无色相之可著也。

“合极”者，合乎太极也。合乎太极者，即神气合一，阴阳相纽也；如是则不落顽空，故曰“有空离”，谓遇空即远离也。

第三句言不著于色，第四句言不著于空，色空两忘，浑然大定。

“Forgetting the spirit” means that at this time, though one has wisdom, one does not use it; if one shows off one’s cleverness, demonic obstacles easily arise. “No form to cling to” means there is no form or appearance that can be clung to.

“Uniting with the Ultimate” means uniting with the Great Ultimate. Uniting with the Great Ultimate is spirit and breath united as one, yin and yang knotted together; in this way one does not fall into dull emptiness, hence “emptiness, when present, is left,” meaning that when emptiness is met, one leaves it far behind.

The third line speaks of not clinging to form, the fourth of not clinging to emptiness; form and emptiness both forgotten, one is wholly in great absorption.

§3
Original (Chinese)

朝食寻山芋，昏饥采泽芝。

To eat in the morning, seek mountain taro; hungry at dusk, gather marsh mushrooms.

Chen Yingning's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

“芋”为普通食品，人皆知之。“芝”形如菌，上有盖，下有柄，其质坚硬而光滑，《本草》载有青、赤、黄、白、黑、紫六种，服之皆能轻身延年。若经书所标灵芝名目，多至数十百种，不可毕陈。

“Taro” is an ordinary food that everyone knows. “Zhi” (芝) is shaped like a mushroom, with a cap above and a stalk below, hard and smooth in texture; the Materia Medica records six kinds — blue-green, red, yellow, white, black, and purple — all of which, when eaten, can lighten the body and prolong life. As for the names of numinous zhi listed in the scriptures, they number in the dozens or hundreds and cannot all be set out.

§4
Original (Chinese)

若将烟火混，体不履瑶池。

If one mixes in food cooked with smoke and fire, the body will not set foot in the Jasper Pool.

Chen Yingning's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

体贵乎清灵，若不绝烟火食，则凡浊之气混入体中，安有超脱之望？“瑶池”者，女仙所居之地，《集仙传》云：“西王母宫阙，左带瑶池，右环翠水。”

The body’s value lies in being pure and numinous; if one does not give up food cooked with smoke and fire, ordinary turbid breath is mixed into the body — what hope is there of transcendence? “The Jasper Pool” is the place where female immortals dwell. The Biographies of Assembled Immortals says: “The palace of the Queen Mother of the West has the Jasper Pool encircling it on the left and the Kingfisher Waters on the right.”

Key Terms

  • Abstaining from grain: giving up cooked food; “does not mean enduring hunger on an empty stomach.”
  • Enduring hunger on an empty stomach (xiaofu renji 枵腹忍饥): bearing hunger with an empty belly.
  • Forgetting the spirit: having wisdom and not using it; showing off one’s cleverness easily gives rise to demonic obstacles.
  • No form to cling to: there being no form or appearance to cling to (not clinging to form).
  • Uniting with the Ultimate: uniting with the Great Ultimate, spirit and breath united as one, yin and yang knotted together.
  • Dull emptiness: a dead, inert emptiness; see realm of yin / realm of the aggregates.
  • Emptiness, when present, is left: leaving emptiness when it is met (not clinging to emptiness).
  • Form and emptiness both forgotten: wholly in great absorption.
  • Marsh mushrooms (zezhi 泽芝): a kind of numinous mushroom (lingzhi).
  • Jasper Pool: the dwelling of the Queen Mother of the West, the place of female immortals.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Please do not imitate abstaining from grain. This poem records what the ancients regarded as an advanced state. What today’s practitioners need is a regular, light, moderate diet.
  2. “Not clinging to form, not clinging to emptiness, form and emptiness both forgotten” is a method of mind worth borrowing: in quiet sitting, neither chase scenes (form) nor fall into a dead, inert emptiness (dull emptiness) — a little clarity is present, and that is enough.
  3. “Though one has wisdom, one does not use it; if one shows off one’s cleverness, demonic obstacles easily arise” — having gained a little understanding, do not rush to talk about it everywhere and show it off.

Translator's Notes

  1. That numinous mushrooms “when eaten can lighten the body and prolong life” is the claim of ancient materia medica; eating wild mushrooms by mistake carries a risk of poisoning, so never gather and eat them yourself.
  2. The Biographies of Assembled Immortals: a collection of biographies of immortals compiled by Zeng Zao of the Song.

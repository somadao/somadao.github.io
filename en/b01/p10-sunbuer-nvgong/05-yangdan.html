Appendix: Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy, with Commentary · 第五首 养丹

Poem 5 · Nourishing the Elixir

Poem 5 · Nourishing the Elixir — the first two lines for women only

Note (translator): The first couplet of this poem speaks of a woman “accumulating breath in the breasts.” In Part 7, The Precious Raft of the Women’s School of Dual Cultivation, there were methods of massaging the breasts, about which we noted that they are unsuitable during pregnancy, menstruation, after childbirth, while breastfeeding, and for those with breast disease. This poem speaks only of concentrating the spirit between the two breasts and involves no massage; if breast swelling, pain, lumps, or the like appear during practice, please see a doctor promptly.

About This Chapter

缚虎归真穴，牵龙渐益丹。性须澄似水，心欲静如山。调息收金鼎，安神守玉关。日能增黍米，鹤发复朱颜。

The fifth poem is about “nourishing the elixir”; the first two lines are for women only.

  • The “tiger” is breath, the “dragon” is spirit, and the “true cavity” is roughly between the two breasts: a woman cultivating immortality first accumulates breath in the breasts; then she unites spirit with breath, and the elixir gradually forms. (The dragon here is not the same as the dragon of “Beheading the Dragon.”)
  • The nature must be clear as water, the mind still as a mountain.
  • Regulate the breath with martial fire and gather it in (the golden cauldron); settle the spirit with civil fire and guard (the jade pass).
  • The elixir grows day by day like millet grains, until finally white hair turns black again and the face regains its rosy color.

The commentary on this poem is especially readable, because Chen Yingning quotes at length Zhang Sanfeng’s words on “concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath” and Guangchengzi’s words on “settling the spirit” — he says, “for the method of regulating the breath, Sanfeng is the most detailed; for the discussion of settling the spirit, Guangcheng is the most refined.” Zhang Sanfeng’s sentence, “When the mind is not yet clear, do not close the eyes rashly. First exhort and encourage yourself; when you have coaxed it back and it is cool, clear, and calm, only then gather it into the Cavity of Breath,” is very practical for anyone.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

缚虎归真穴，牵龙渐益丹。

Bind the tiger and return it to the true cavity; lead the dragon and gradually increase the elixir.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

“虎”即气，“龙”即神，“真穴”大约在两乳之间。“缚虎归真穴”者，即上阳子陈致虚所云：“女子修仙，必先积气于乳房也。”气有先天后天之分：炼后天气，即用调息凝神之法；采先天气，则俟身中有生气发动时下手。

“牵龙”者，不过凝神以合于气而已。神气合一，魂魄相拘，则丹结矣。张虚靖天师云：“元神一出便收来，神返身中气自回。如此朝朝并暮暮，自然赤子结灵胎。”此即“牵龙渐益丹”之意。

此处所谓龙，与斩龙之龙字不同。

The “tiger” is breath, the “dragon” is spirit, and the “true cavity” is roughly between the two breasts. “Bind the tiger and return it to the true cavity” is what the Master of Upper Yang, Chen Zhixu, said: “A woman cultivating immortality must first accumulate breath in the breasts.” Breath is divided into that of Prior Heaven and that of Posterior Heaven: to refine the breath of Posterior Heaven, use the method of regulating the breath and concentrating the spirit; to gather the breath of Prior Heaven, wait until the living breath stirs in the body before setting to work.

“Leading the dragon” is nothing but concentrating the spirit to unite it with the breath. When spirit and breath are united as one and hun and po hold each other fast, the elixir is formed. The Celestial Master Zhang Xujing said: “As soon as the Original Spirit goes out, gather it back; when the spirit returns into the body, the breath returns of itself. Morning after morning and evening after evening like this, the newborn child will naturally form the numinous embryo.” This is the meaning of “lead the dragon and gradually increase the elixir.”

The dragon spoken of here is not the same as the word dragon in “beheading the dragon.”

§2
Original (Chinese)

性须澄似水，心欲静如山。

The nature must be clear as water; the mind should be still as a mountain.

Chen Yingning's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

张三丰真人云：“凝神调息，调息凝神，八个字须一片做去，分层次而不断乃可。凝神者，收已清之心而入其内也。心未清时，眼勿乱闭。先要自劝自勉，劝得回来，清凉恬淡，始行收入气穴，乃曰凝神。然后如坐高山而视众山众水，如燃天灯而照九幽九昧，所谓凝神于虚者，此也。调息不难，心神一静，随息自然，我只守其自然而已。”

The Perfected Zhang Sanfeng said: “Concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, regulate the breath and concentrate the spirit — these eight characters must be done as one piece, divided into stages and yet without interruption. Concentrating the spirit is gathering a mind that has already become clear and bringing it within. When the mind is not yet clear, do not close the eyes rashly. First exhort and encourage yourself; when you have coaxed it back and it is cool, clear, and calm, only then begin to gather it into the Cavity of Breath — this is called concentrating the spirit. Then it is like sitting on a high mountain looking at all the mountains and waters, like lighting a heavenly lamp to illuminate the nine dark and obscure places — this is what is called concentrating the spirit in emptiness. Regulating the breath is not difficult: once mind and spirit are still, the breathing follows naturally, and I simply keep to this naturalness.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

调息收金鼎，安神守玉关。

Regulate the breath and gather it into the golden cauldron; settle the spirit and guard the jade pass.

Chen Yingning's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

张三丰真人云：“大凡打坐，须要将神抱住气，意系住息，在丹田中，宛转悠扬，聚而不散，则内藏之气，与外来之气，交结于丹田；日充月盛，达乎四肢，流乎百脉，撞开夹脊双关，而上游于泥丸，旋复降下绛宫，而下入于丹田，神气相守，息息相依，河车之路通矣。功夫至此筑基之效已得一半。”又云：“调息须以后天呼吸，寻真人呼吸处。然调后天呼吸，须任他自调，方能调得起先天呼吸，我惟致虚守静而已。真息一动，玄关即不远矣，照此进功，筑基可翘足而至。”

广成子云：“抱神以静，形将自正。无劳汝形，无摇汝精，乃可以长生。目无所见，耳无所闻，心无所知，汝神将守形，形乃长生。慎汝内，闭汝外，多知为败。我守其一，以处其和，故我修身千二百岁，而形未尝衰。”

按：调息之法，三丰最详，安神之论，广成最精，故引以为注。

本诗上句言武火，故曰“金鼎”；下句言文火，故曰“玉关”。

The Perfected Zhang Sanfeng said: “Whenever one sits in meditation, one must let the spirit embrace the breath and the intention tie the breath, within the Cinnabar Field, turning gently and leisurely, gathered and not scattered; then the breath stored within and the breath coming from outside join in the Cinnabar Field; growing fuller day by day and stronger month by month, it reaches the four limbs, flows through the hundred vessels, bursts open the double pass of the Spine-Straddling Pass, travels up to the Muddy Pellet, then turns and descends to the Crimson Palace and goes down into the Cinnabar Field; spirit and breath guard each other, breath after breath relying on each other, and the road of the River Chariot is open. When the work reaches this point, half the effect of laying the foundation has already been obtained.” He also said: “In regulating the breath one must use the breathing of Posterior Heaven to find the place of the breathing of the Perfected. Yet in regulating the breathing of Posterior Heaven, one must let it regulate itself; only then can one raise the breathing of Prior Heaven; I simply attain emptiness and keep to stillness. Once the True Breath stirs, the Mysterious Pass is not far; advancing in the work in this way, laying the foundation can be reached in no time.”

Guangchengzi said: “Embrace the spirit in stillness, and the form will right itself. Do not weary your form, do not stir your essence, and you may live long. When the eyes see nothing, the ears hear nothing, and the mind knows nothing, your spirit will guard your form, and the form will live long. Be careful of what is within you, shut off what is outside you; much knowledge leads to ruin. I guard the One and dwell in its harmony; therefore I have cultivated my person for twelve hundred years, and my form has never declined.”

Note: for the method of regulating the breath, Sanfeng is the most detailed; for the discussion of settling the spirit, Guangcheng is the most refined; hence I quote them as commentary.

The first line of this couplet speaks of martial fire, hence “golden cauldron”; the second speaks of civil fire, hence “jade pass.”

§4
Original (Chinese)

日能增黍米，鹤发复朱颜。

Day by day it can increase like millet grains; crane-white hair regains a rosy face.

Chen Yingning's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

《金丹四百字》云：“混沌包虚空，虚空括三界，及寻其根源，一粒如黍大。”又云：“一粒复一粒，从微而至著。”此即“日能增黍米”之意。质而言之，不过渐采渐炼，渐凝渐结而已。

《参同契》云：“金砂入五内，雾散若风雨。薰蒸达四肢，颜色悦泽好。发白皆变黑，齿落生旧所。老翁复丁壮，耆妪成姹女。改形免世厄，号之曰真人。”即此诗末句之意。

或谓：“头有白发，面似婴儿，是为鹤发复朱颜。”此言误矣。修炼家若行先天工夫，虽白发亦必变成黑发。苟发白不变，仅面容红润，此乃后天之功，或行采补之术耳。世俗所谓仙人“鹤发童颜”，乃门外语。

The Four Hundred Words on the Golden Elixir says: “Chaos contains empty space, empty space encompasses the three realms; and when one seeks their root, it is one grain the size of a millet seed.” It also says: “Grain upon grain, from the minute to the manifest.” This is the meaning of “day by day it can increase like millet grains.” To put it plainly, it is nothing but gradually gathering and gradually refining, gradually congealing and gradually forming.

The Cantong qi says: “When the golden sand enters the five viscera, it disperses like mist, like wind and rain. Steaming through to the four limbs, the complexion becomes joyful and lustrous. White hair all turns black, fallen teeth grow again in their old places. The old man becomes a strong young man again, the old woman becomes a fair maiden. Transforming the form and escaping the troubles of the world, one is called a Perfected.” This is the meaning of the last line of the poem.

Some say: “White hair on the head and a face like an infant’s — that is crane-white hair regaining a rosy face.” This is wrong. If a cultivator practices the work of Prior Heaven, even white hair will surely turn black. If the white hair does not change and only the face becomes rosy, that is the work of Posterior Heaven, or perhaps the practice of the arts of gathering and supplementing. What the world calls the immortals’ “crane-white hair and youthful face” is the talk of outsiders.

Key Terms

  • Tiger, dragon: in this poem the tiger is breath and the dragon is spirit (not the same as the dragon of “Beheading the Dragon”).
  • True cavity: roughly between the two breasts; see Breast Stream.
  • Accumulating breath in the breasts: Chen Zhixu’s words, the starting point of a woman’s cultivation of immortality.
  • Leading the dragon: concentrating the spirit to unite it with the breath.
  • Hun and po holding each other fast: see hun and po.
  • Concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath: Zhang Sanfeng’s “these eight characters must be done as one piece”; see concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath, regulating the breath.
  • Concentrating the spirit in emptiness: like sitting on a high mountain looking at all the mountains and waters.
  • Golden cauldron, jade pass: the golden cauldron refers to martial fire, the jade pass to civil fire; see fire phasing.
  • Road of the River Chariot: the Celestial Circuit; see Celestial Circuit.
  • Millet grain: a metaphor for the elixir; see Golden Flower.
  • Crane-white hair regaining a rosy face: Chen Yingning interprets it as white hair turning black and the face becoming rosy, and regards “crane-white hair and youthful face” as the talk of outsiders.
  • Arts of gathering and supplementing: a side path criticized by the alchemists.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “When the mind is not yet clear, do not close the eyes rashly.” This is the most practical line of this poem. When the mind is still agitated, do not hurry to close your eyes and enter stillness; first exhort and encourage yourself, coax the mind back, and when it is cool, clear, and calm, then gather it within.
  2. The key to regulating the breath is “letting it regulate itself.” Zhang Sanfeng says plainly: in regulating the breathing of Posterior Heaven, one must let it regulate itself; I simply attain emptiness and keep to stillness. It is not controlling the breath, but letting the mind become still, and the breathing regulates itself.
  3. Guangchengzi’s “be careful of what is within you, shut off what is outside you; much knowledge leads to ruin,” applied today: scroll through less information, cling less to things outside, and guard the peace within.
  4. “Gradually gathering and gradually refining, gradually congealing and gradually forming” — grain upon grain, from the minute to the manifest. The work accumulates day by day; there is no need to seek speed.

Translator's Notes

  1. The Master of Upper Yang, Chen Zhixu: a Daoist of the Yuan dynasty, author of the Great Essentials of the Golden Elixir of the Master of Upper Yang.
  2. Zhang Xujing: Zhang Jixian (1092–1127), the thirtieth Celestial Master.
  3. In the line “the nature must be clear as water,” Chen Yingning’s edition writes 澂, a variant of 澄, “clear.”
  4. “In no time” (qiaozu er zhi 翘足而至): arriving in the time it takes to raise a foot, meaning very quickly.
  5. Guangchengzi’s words are found in the Zhuangzi, “Letting Be”; the quotation differs slightly from the received Zhuangzi, and is translated as in the original commentary.
  6. Statements such as “white hair all turns black” belong to the records and ideals of the ancients and cannot be confirmed by modern medicine.

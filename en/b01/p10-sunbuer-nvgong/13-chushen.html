Appendix: Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy, with Commentary · 第十三首 出神

Poem 13 · Sending Out the Spirit

Poem 13 · Sending Out the Spirit — the same for men and women

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

Note (translator): This poem is an ancient record of the stage of “sending out the spirit.” Chen Yingning himself says he has not personally verified it and only excerpts the recorded sayings of predecessors. Please read it as a document for understanding; do not attempt to practice it, and do not take any out-of-body, visionary, or auditory experience as an achievement; if such experiences occur and affect your life, stop practicing and seek professional help.

About This Chapter

身外复有身，非关幻术成。圆通此灵气，活泼一元神。皓月凝金液，青莲炼玉真。烹来乌兔髓，珠皎不愁贫。

The thirteenth poem is about “sending out the spirit,” and is the same for men and women. The general meaning of the poem: beyond the body there is again a body, not produced by magic tricks, but the result of the numinous breath being perfectly penetrating and the Original Spirit lively; golden liquor, jade truth, the marrow of the crow and the hare are refined into a bright, pure pearl, and from then on one need never worry about poverty.

Chen Yingning says that if this poem were left entirely without commentary, readers would feel something was missing; but to annotate each line, he would not know where to begin; so he could only excerpt the recorded sayings of the Old Man of Qinghua. What is most noteworthy in these sayings is not the golden light and fragrance when the Yang Spirit appears, but their repeated emphasis on the ground of the mind:

  • Only if the ground of the mind is ordinarily empty and bright will the Yang Spirit that goes out “meet circumstances without being stained, see things without being moved”;
  • If the ground of the mind is ordinarily not empty and bright enough, the spirit that goes out carries impurities: “as soon as it sees something frightening, fear arises; as soon as it sees something desirable, attachment arises,” and it will fall into demonic paths;
  • The remedy is “refining emptiness”: a vast and open heart, with no self and no others;
  • Sending out the spirit too early, before the yin is exhausted, is called a “yin spirit”; to refine the yin spirit into a Yang Spirit, there is only “forgetting one’s body, emptying one’s mind, so that in the hollow emptiness not a single thing arises.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

身外复有身，非关幻术成。

Beyond the body there is again a body, not something produced by magic arts.

Chen Yingning's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

今按此首若完全不注，未免令读者意有缺憾，若每句作注，又苦于不能落笔，只得将前贤语录摘抄数条，以见出神之时，是何景象。出神之后，尚有工夫，欲知其详，请博览丹经，真参实悟，非此编所能限也。

青华老人语录曰："阳神脱胎之先兆，有光自脐轮外注，有香自鼻口中出。既脱之后，则金光四射，毛窍晶融，如日之初升于海，如珠之初出于渊，香气氤氲满室，一声霹雳，金火交流，而阳神已出于泥丸矣。

出神以后，全看平日工夫；若阳神纯是先天灵气结成，则遇境不染，见物不迁，收纵在我，去来自如。一进泥丸，此身便如火热，金光复从毛窍间出，香气亦复氤氲，顷刻反到黄庭，虽有如无，不知不觉，此真境也。

若平日心地未能虚明，所结之胎，决非圣胎，所出之神，原带几分驳杂，一见可惧则怖生，一见可欲则爱生，殆将流连忘返，堕入魔道。此身既死，不知者以为得仙坐化，谁知阳神一出而不复者，殆不堪问矣。

问曰：‘倘心地未纯，而胎神已出，为之奈何？‘师曰：‘必不得已，尚有炼虚一著，胎神虽出，要紧紧收住，留他做完了炼虚一段工夫，再放出去，则真光法界，任意逍遥，大而化之矣。炼虚全要胸怀浩荡，无我无人，何天何地，觉清空一气，混混沌沌中，是我非我，是虚非虚，造化运旋，分之无可分，合之无可合，是曰炼虚。盖以阳神之虚，合太虚之虚，而融洽无间，所谓形神俱妙，与道合真。此乃出胎以后之功，分身以前之事也。'

问：‘阳神、阴神之别如何？‘师曰：‘阴未尽而出神太早，谓之阴神。其出之时，或眼中见白光如何，则神从眼出；或耳中闻钟磬箫管之音，则神从耳出。由其阳气未壮，不能撞破天关，故旁趋别径，从其便也。既出之后，亦自逍遥快乐，但能成形，不能分形，不能飞腾变化；若盛夏太阳当空，则阴神畏而避之，是未离鬼趣。'

问：‘阴神可以炼为阳神乎？‘师曰：‘可。学道之士，不甘以小乘自居，只得于阴神既出后，再行修炼，将那阴神原形粉碎，倾下金鼎玉炉，重新起火，火候足时，自然阴尽阳纯，真人显象。'

问：‘阴神如何能使原形粉碎？‘师曰：‘忘其身，虚其心，空洞之中，一物不生，则可以换凡胎为灵胎，变俗子为真人，而事毕矣。'"

Note now: if this poem were left entirely without commentary, it would inevitably leave readers feeling something was missing; if I annotated each line, I would be at a loss where to put my brush; so I can only excerpt a few items from the recorded sayings of earlier worthies, to show what the scene is like when the spirit goes out. After the spirit goes out, there is still work to do; those who wish to know the details should read widely in the alchemical scriptures and truly investigate and realize it; it is not something this little compilation can contain.

The recorded sayings of the Old Man of Qinghua say: "The omens of the Yang Spirit leaving the embryo are that light flows outward from the navel wheel and fragrance comes out of the nose and mouth. After it has left, golden light radiates in all directions, the pores become crystalline and melt open, like the sun first rising over the sea, like a pearl first coming out of the abyss; fragrance fills the room in dense clouds; with a clap of thunder, metal and fire flow together, and the Yang Spirit has already gone out through the Muddy Pellet.

"After the spirit goes out, everything depends on one’s ordinary work. If the Yang Spirit is formed purely of the numinous breath of Prior Heaven, then it meets circumstances without being stained and sees things without being moved; gathering in and letting go are up to me, and it comes and goes freely. As soon as it enters the Muddy Pellet, this body becomes hot as fire, golden light again issues from the pores, and fragrance again fills the air in dense clouds; in an instant it returns to the Yellow Court, present as if absent, unnoticed and unfelt — this is the true realm.

"If the ground of the mind has ordinarily not been empty and bright, the embryo formed is certainly not a Holy Embryo, and the spirit that goes out carries some impurities from the start: as soon as it sees something frightening, fear arises; as soon as it sees something desirable, attachment arises; it may linger and forget to return, and fall into demonic paths. When this body dies, those who do not know think he has attained immortality and passed away in meditation; who would know that the Yang Spirit went out once and never returned? This hardly bears asking about.

“Someone asked: ‘If the ground of the mind is not yet pure and the spirit of the embryo has already gone out, what is to be done?' The master said: ‘If there is no other way, there is still the one move of refining emptiness. Though the spirit of the embryo has gone out, gather it back tightly, and keep it until it has completed the work of refining emptiness before letting it out again; then in the Dharma realm of true light it may roam at will, great and transformed. Refining emptiness lies entirely in a vast and open heart, with no self and no others — what Heaven, what Earth? — one feels only one clear, empty breath, and in the chaotic undifferentiation it is I and not I, emptiness and not emptiness; creation revolves, and it cannot be divided nor joined — this is called refining emptiness. It is uniting the emptiness of the Yang Spirit with the emptiness of the Great Void, blending harmoniously without gap — what is called ”form and spirit both wondrous, united with the Dao in truth." This is the work after leaving the embryo, the matter before dividing the body.'

"Someone asked: ‘What is the difference between the Yang Spirit and the yin spirit?' The master said: ‘Sending out the spirit too early, before the yin is exhausted, is called a yin spirit. When it goes out, perhaps one sees white light in the eyes like a river, and the spirit goes out through the eyes; or perhaps one hears the sounds of bells, chimes, flutes, and pipes in the ears, and the spirit goes out through the ears. Because its yang breath is not yet strong, it cannot break through the Pass of Heaven, and so it takes another path to the side, for convenience. Having gone out, it too roams freely and happily, but it can only take form, not divide its form, and cannot soar aloft and transform; if the sun stands high in the height of summer, the yin spirit fears it and avoids it — it has not yet left the realm of ghosts.'

"Someone asked: ‘Can the yin spirit be refined into a Yang Spirit?' The master said: ‘It can. A student of the Dao who is unwilling to settle for the Lesser Vehicle can only, after the yin spirit has gone out, cultivate further, shattering the original form of that yin spirit and pouring it back into the golden cauldron and jade furnace, raising the fire anew; when the fire phasing is sufficient, naturally yin is exhausted and yang pure, and the Perfected appears.'

“Someone asked: ‘How can the original form of the yin spirit be shattered?' The master said: ‘Forget your body, empty your mind, so that in the hollow emptiness not a single thing arises; then the ordinary embryo can be exchanged for a numinous embryo and the worldly person transformed into a Perfected, and the matter is complete.'”

§2
Original (Chinese)

圆通此灵气，活泼一元神。

Perfectly penetrating, this numinous breath; lively, the one Original Spirit.

§3
Original (Chinese)

皓月凝金液，青莲炼玉真。

The bright moon congeals golden liquor; the blue lotus refines the jade truth.

§4
Original (Chinese)

烹来乌兔髓，珠皎不愁贫。

Cook out the marrow of the crow and the hare; the pearl is bright, and one need not worry about poverty.

Key Terms

  • A body beyond the body: that is, sending out the spirit; see sending out the spirit, spontaneous Dharma Body.
  • Navel wheel: the area around the navel.
  • Meeting circumstances without being stained, seeing things without being moved: the mark of a Yang Spirit formed purely of the numinous breath of Prior Heaven.
  • Refining emptiness: a vast and open heart, with no self and no others, uniting the emptiness of the Yang Spirit with the emptiness of the Great Void; see the three transmutations, “refining emptiness to unite with the Dao.”
  • Form and spirit both wondrous, united with the Dao in truth: a common expression of the alchemists for the highest realm.
  • Yin spirit: a spirit sent out too early, before the yin is exhausted, going out by side paths such as the eyes and ears; it “has not yet left the realm of ghosts.”
  • Yang Spirit: the spirit that goes out when yin is exhausted and yang pure; see Holy Embryo.
  • Pass of Heaven: the crown of the head; see Gate of Heaven / Door of Earth.
  • Crow and hare: the sun-crow and the moon-hare, that is, sun and moon, metaphors for lead and mercury; see Lead and Mercury.

Putting It into Practice

  1. This poem is for understanding only.
  2. What is most valuable in these sayings is the phrase “the ground of the mind”: whether any realm is good or bad depends on whether the ground of the mind is ordinarily empty and bright. Feeling fear at what is frightening and attachment at what is desirable is the sign of not yet having cultivated well — something that can be tested at any ordinary moment.
  3. “Forget your body, empty your mind, so that in the hollow emptiness not a single thing arises” is the final method these sayings give, echoing from beginning to end the first poem’s “sweep away the sea of arising and ceasing.”

Translator's Notes

  1. “One sees white light in the eyes like a river” (眼中见白光如何): 如何 (“how”) makes no sense here and is probably an error for 如河 (“like a river”); the translation follows “like a river,” while the original text keeps the reading of the base text.
  2. Chen Yingning gives no commentary on the second to fourth couplets; we give only a literal translation.
  3. The recorded sayings of the Old Man of Qinghua: Chen Yingning’s commentary labels the dialogue quoted here only as “recorded sayings of the Old Man of Qinghua”; its specific source remains to be determined.

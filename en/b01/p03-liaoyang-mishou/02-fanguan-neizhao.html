2 · Reverting the Gaze and Illuminating Within — “do not fear thoughts arising, only fear being slow to notice”

About This Chapter

Once the body has become still, the first thing is to stop thoughts. The method is to revert the gaze and illuminate within: begin in the space in front of the hollow of the nose bridge between the eyes (the Root of the Mountain), imagine there a red-yellow light like a moon, hanging there and shining on the Root of the Mountain, and then let mind and breath rely on each other. This section contains two very famous formulas: “Do not fear thoughts arising, only fear being slow to notice” and “a thought arising is the sickness; not continuing it is the medicine.” It does not matter that thoughts arise; what matters is noticing them quickly; once noticed, not letting them continue is the medicine. When body and mind have settled, “lead the spirit back to the chamber” — gather the spirit into the Chamber of Spirit (the Central Yellow). Qiu Ling’s note makes the location of the Chamber of Spirit very clear and confirms it with the notes of Nanqiaozi and Min Xiaoliang on the Scripture of Opening and Closing.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

身既得静，止念为先。刻惟返观内照，起自颏前空际，存有红黄如月，悬照山根，有光无光听之。从此心息相依，念外无念，惟觉三田一贯。凡夫色相，虚空玄况，非我本来，隐现听之，不为转念，此是主脑。是即《周易》思不出位之至道。

Once the body has become still, stopping thoughts comes first. At every moment simply revert the gaze and illuminate within. Begin in the space in front of the hollow of the nose bridge (the Root of the Mountain): visualize a red-yellow light like the moon, hanging there and shining on the Root of the Mountain; whether there is light or not, let it be. From then on mind and breath rely on each other; beyond this one thought there is no other thought; one feels only that the three Cinnabar Fields, upper, middle and lower, are joined in one. All the forms and appearances of ordinary life, and the mysterious states that appear in empty space, are not my original self; whether they appear or vanish, let them be and do not let them turn your thoughts — this is the heart of the matter. This is the supreme way spoken of in the Book of Changes: “thoughts do not go beyond one’s place.”

§2
Original (Chinese)

然而人心至灵，况又初学，何能便尔。诀有之：不怕念起，只怕觉迟。又曰：念起是病，不续是药。故有但灭动心，不灭照心之义。

But the human mind is extremely lively, and moreover you are a beginner — how could you do this right away? The formula says: do not fear thoughts arising, only fear being slow to notice. It also says: a thought arising is the sickness; not continuing it is the medicine. Hence the saying, “extinguish only the moving mind, not the illuminating mind.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

而于身心既定，亦必返照有所，所谓即一以制万。初则率神归室，室即神室也。如是体照，自觉此中虚寂而湛如。得到自如之候，不假存照而得，乃为得见吾心本体。初学焉能至此，寂体久之，此况始现。若欲保此而求进，必须不忘而如忘，则自中广无际，清寂如之。

Once body and mind have settled, the turning of the light inward must also have a place: this is what is called “holding the One to master the ten thousand.” At first, lead the spirit back to the chamber — the chamber is the Chamber of Spirit. Experiencing and illuminating it like this, you will feel that within it is empty, still and limpid. When it comes of itself, without needing to visualize or illuminate, only then have you seen the original substance of my mind. How could a beginner reach this? Only after the still substance has been maintained for a long time does this state begin to appear. If you wish to preserve it and make progress, you must “not forget, yet be as if forgetting”; then within it will naturally be vast and boundless, clear and still as ever.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

返观内照从颏前空际开始，颏即两眼之间鼻梁凹处，亦即山根穴，红黄如月初乃想象之景。《吕祖金华宗旨》一书闵一得注曰：“而山根为人身之性户，上达泥丸，中达黄中，下通脐后者。故凝聚光于此处，由此而下注，是乃不易之功法。”史从龙《道乡集》曰：山根“乃后三关之门户。”玄况指化景幻景，看到种种化景幻景，千万不要为它们转移念头。

返照要有个处所，起先的处所就是神室。神室即指心后夹脊关前的夹缝，此处被称为玄窍，与两乳之间的膻中穴及深入一寸三分的绛宫相对，名曰中黄或黄中。《尹真人皇极阖辟仙经》南樵子注曰：“此夹脊双关一窍，在人背脊二十四节上下之正中，真可以夺神功，改天命。”同书闵小良则注曰：“按此窍在脊前脘后而有形无形。未开谓之玄关，既开谓之玄窍，学者行到虚极静笃时，此窍乃现，胎息息于此也。我身元神于此升降，乃谓得道道路也，谓得彻天彻地也，故此元神一入自觉此中大无外细无内也。丹书一名神室，又曰黄房，其名不一，总之结胎、养胎，造至脱胎，皆基于此处。”《尹真人阖辟仙经》说：“学者诚能凝神夹脊之窍，守而不离，久久纯熟，则里面皎皎明明，如月在水相似，自然散其邪火，消其杂虑，降其动心，止其妄念。”

Reverting the gaze and illuminating within begins “in the space in front of the ke.” The ke here is the hollow of the nose bridge between the eyes, that is, the point of the Root of the Mountain. The “red-yellow light like the moon” is at first an imagined scene. Min Yide’s commentary on The Secret of the Golden Flower of Patriarch Lü says: “The Root of the Mountain is the door of inner nature of the human body; it reaches up to the Muddy Pellet, in the middle to the Yellow Center, and down to behind the navel. So to gather and concentrate the light here and pour it downward from here is an unchanging method.” Shi Conglong’s Collection of Daoxiang says that the Root of the Mountain “is the gateway of the three rear passes.” “Mysterious states” means transformed scenes and illusory scenes; when you see such scenes, on no account let them turn your thoughts.

The turning of the light inward must have a place, and the first place is the Chamber of Spirit. The Chamber of Spirit is the gap behind the heart and in front of the Spine-Straddling Pass. This place is called the Mysterious Opening; it lies opposite the Chest Center point between the nipples and the Crimson Palace one cun three fen inward from it, and is called the Central Yellow or the Yellow Center. Nanqiaozi’s note on Perfected Yin’s Immortal Scripture of the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Ultimate says: “This single opening of the Spine-Straddling Pass, in the exact middle of the twenty-four vertebrae of the back, can truly seize the work of the spirits and change heavenly destiny.” Min Xiaoliang’s note in the same book says: “Note: this opening is in front of the spine and behind the stomach, with form and yet without form. Before it opens it is called the Mysterious Pass; once open it is called the Mysterious Opening. Only when students reach the utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness does this opening appear; embryonic breathing rests here. The Original Spirit of my body rises and descends here; this is called finding the road to the Dao, called penetrating Heaven and Earth. So as soon as the Original Spirit enters here, one feels it to be so vast it has no outside and so fine it has no inside. The alchemical books also call it the Chamber of Spirit, or the Yellow Chamber; its names vary, but in short, forming the embryo, nourishing the embryo, right up to leaving the womb, all are based on this place.” Perfected Yin’s Scripture of Opening and Closing says: “If students can truly concentrate the spirit in the opening of the Spine-Straddling Pass and guard it without leaving it, then after long practice, when it has become thoroughly familiar, within there will be a clear brightness, like the moon in water; naturally this disperses the pathogenic fire, dissolves stray thoughts, subdues the agitated mind and stops deluded thinking.”

Key Terms

  • Reverting the gaze and illuminating within: that is, Reverting the Light.
  • Space in front of the ke (ke qian kongji 颏前空际): the space in front of the hollow of the nose bridge (the Root of the Mountain); see the Translator’s Notes.
  • Root of the Mountain: the hollow of the nose bridge between the eyes, the place to begin; Shi Conglong says it “is the gateway of the three rear passes.”
  • Red-yellow light like the moon: the scene visualized in the early stage.
  • The three fields joined in one: the upper, middle and lower Cinnabar Fields connected by a single breath.
  • Mysterious states (xuankuang 玄况): the mysterious scenes that appear in empty space; Qiu Ling’s note: transformed scenes, illusory scenes.
  • Heart of the matter (zhunao 主脑): the fundamental key.
  • “Thoughts do not go beyond one’s place”: from the commentary on the image of the hexagram Gen in the Book of Changes, “the noble one does not let his thoughts go beyond his place.”
  • “Do not fear thoughts arising, only fear being slow to notice”: it does not matter that thoughts arise; what is to be feared is noticing them slowly.
  • “A thought arising is the sickness; not continuing it is the medicine.”
  • “Extinguish only the moving mind, not the illuminating mind”: see Qiu Ling’s note in Part Two, Chapter 2, “extinguish the moving mind, but do not extinguish the illuminating mind.”
  • Holding the One to master the ten thousand.
  • Chamber of Spirit: the gap behind the heart and in front of the Spine-Straddling Pass, that is, the Central Yellow; also called the Mysterious Opening, the Yellow Center, the Yellow Chamber.
  • Not forgetting, yet as if forgetting: neither losing it nor seeming to hold it, that is, “neither forgetting nor forcing.”

Putting It into Practice

  1. To begin: close your eyes and relax; in the space in front of the point between the eyes (the Root of the Mountain), lightly imagine a red-yellow moon hanging there and shining on the Root of the Mountain. Whether you can see it or not does not matter — “whether there is light or not, let it be.”
  2. Then let mind and breath rely on each other: rest the mind on the breathing, with no thought beyond this one.
  3. When thoughts come: do not fear thoughts arising, only fear being slow to notice. As soon as you notice, do not let the thought continue — “not continuing it is the medicine.” There is no need to fight your thoughts.
  4. When scenes appear: forms, lights and shadows, all kinds of “mysterious states” are not your original self; “whether they appear or vanish, let them be and do not let them turn your thoughts.”
  5. Once settled: gather the spirit into the Chamber of Spirit (the middle between chest and back) and experience the empty, still limpidity within. The secret of maintaining it: not forgetting, yet as if forgetting.

Translator's Notes

  1. “In the space in front of the ke”: ke 颏 usually means the chin, but Qiu Ling’s note says clearly that it is “the hollow of the nose bridge between the eyes.” The original character was probably e 頞 (the bridge of the nose), miswritten because of the similar shape. The translation follows Qiu Ling’s explanation.
  2. “Thoughts do not go beyond one’s place”: from the commentary on the image of the hexagram Gen in the Book of Changes; originally it means that the noble one’s thoughts do not go beyond his own position; here it is borrowed to mean that the mind stays in one place and does not run outward.
  3. In Qiu Ling’s note, in the sentence “as soon as the Original Spirit enters here, one feels …” (故此元神一入自觉此中大无外细无内也), the base text has a blank space of one character between 一入 and 自觉; the Chinese edition runs them together.

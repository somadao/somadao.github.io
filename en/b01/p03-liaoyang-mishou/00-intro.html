What this book is

Perfected Yin’s Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall (Yin zhenren Liaoyang dian wenda bian 尹真人寥阳殿问答编), like the Scripture of Opening and Closing of Part Two, comes from a manuscript copy kept at the Qingyang Temple in Chengdu; it was edited by Min Yide, eleventh-generation master of the Longmen lineage on Mount Jingai, and printed in 1834 (the jiawu year of the Daoguang reign). The whole book has six sections. The Secret Teachings (Mishou pian 秘授篇) is the sixth and serves as the summary of the whole book: in very few pages it goes through the entire course of the Longmen practice, from settling the mind before sitting all the way to nurturing the Holy Embryo. The title “The Traditional Longmen Practice for Nourishing Life” was added by Qiu Ling.

Qiu Ling's editorial note

《秘授篇》为《尹真人寥阳殿问答编》一书的第六篇。《传统龙门养生功》一题为笔者所加。此书出自青羊宫抄本，由浙江吴兴县金盖山龙门第十一代闵一得编订，于清代道光甲午年镌刻。全书分六篇，《秘授篇》则为全书的总结，介绍了道教龙门派气功功法的全过程，亦全书精华所在，言简而意赅，颇切实用。此功与前刊阖辟仙功同出一源，可互相参看。现于篇中交关处略加注释，以飨读者。

The Secret Teachings is the sixth section of Perfected Yin’s Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall. The title “The Traditional Longmen Practice for Nourishing Life” is my own (Qiu Ling’s). The book comes from a manuscript copy of the Qingyang Temple; it was edited by Min Yide, eleventh-generation master of the Longmen lineage on Mount Jingai in Wuxing county, Zhejiang, and engraved in the jiawu year of the Daoguang reign of the Qing. The whole book has six sections; The Secret Teachings is the summary of the whole, presenting the complete course of the qigong methods of the Daoist Longmen lineage. It is also the essence of the book — concise yet full of meaning, and highly practical. This practice and the immortal practice of opening and closing published above come from the same source and can be read together. I have added brief notes at the key points of the text for the reader.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

First settle the mind, then regulate the breath; Revert the Light and illuminate within, from the Root of the Mountain to the Chamber of Spirit; look into the navel, until cool fluid and warm breath meet (the Lesser Celestial Circuit); when the three treasures are full, they break through the passes by themselves (the Greater Celestial Circuit); gather in the inner Medicine with the mao-you Celestial Circuit; and finally nurture the Holy Embryo.

The six sections

Sec.TitleContent
1Settling the Mind and Regulating the BreathBefore sitting, first settle the mind, then regulate the breath; no fixed hour, no need to cross the legs, but the hands must hold the “firm grip”
2Reverting the Gaze and Illuminating WithinBegin at the Root of the Mountain, then lead the spirit back to the Chamber of Spirit (the Central Yellow)
3Looking into the NavelThe gaze looks down into the lower Cinnabar Field; cool fluid and warm breath meet — the Lesser Celestial Circuit
4Gathering the Fire and Opening the PassesThe three treasures are full and break through the three passes — the Greater Celestial Circuit
5The Mao-You Celestial CircuitTurning left and right to gather in the inner Medicine; the Bridge for Governing Life
6Nurturing the Holy EmbryoThe numinous elixir descends to the Central Palace; ten months of nurturing the embryo

Reading tips

  1. The first three sections are the most worth rereading. Section 1’s “before sitting, first settle the mind,” Section 2’s “do not fear thoughts arising, only fear being slow to notice,” and Section 3’s description of the signs of cool fluid and warm breath are all very concrete and gentle — the content of these three parts closest to everyday practice.
  2. Read it alongside Part Two. Sections 4 to 6 correspond basically to Chapters 4, 6 and 7 of the Scripture of Opening and Closing, but go into more detail: for example, the exact route of the mao-you Celestial Circuit, the location of the Bridge for Governing Life, and how to pass the Jade Pillow.
  3. Sections 4 and 5 still contain actions of guiding, lifting and contracting, although this part’s wording is much gentler than that of Part Two (“use the method of lifting and contracting the grain path slightly,” “on no account be agitated and wish to hurry,” “neither be alarmed nor delighted”). Again, practice only under the guidance of an experienced teacher.
  4. Section 2 begins at the Root of the Mountain (between the eyes): as Qiu Ling’s notes in Part One have already warned, people with high blood pressure or “excess yang” should hold the intention on the head very lightly, or not practice this at all.

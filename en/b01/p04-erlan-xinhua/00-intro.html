What this book is

The author of the Heart Talks of the Two Idlers (Erlan xinhua 二懒心话) is no longer known. The book is included in the Sequel to the Daoist Canon (Daozang xubian 道藏续编), the Mount Jingai collection printed in 1834 (the jiawu year of the Daoguang reign), and, like the preceding parts, belongs to the Longmen lineage of the Northern School of Daoism. Qiu Ling says that the Practice of Inner Illumination is also a method of “Reverting the Light”: the method is simple, but its principles are deep and quite distinctive.

Qiu Ling's editorial note

内照功选自《二懒心话》，著者不知何许人。该书为清代道光甲午年镌刻的金盖山藏本《道藏续篇》所收入。此为道教北宗龙门派功法，亦回光之一法也。功法虽简，但功理深奥，颇具特色，特略加整理并注释之。

The Practice of Inner Illumination is selected from the Heart Talks of the Two Idlers, whose author is unknown. The book is included in the Mount Jingai collection, the Sequel to the Daoist Canon, engraved in the jiawu year of the Daoguang reign of the Qing. It is a method of the Longmen lineage of the Northern School of Daoism, and also one of the methods of Reverting the Light. Although the method is simple, its principles are profound and quite distinctive, so I have arranged it a little and added notes.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

With the gaze (the light of awareness), illuminate downward from the crown, station by station — the Muddy Pellet → the Crimson Palace → the Central Yellow → the lower Cinnabar Field — until each is “pure white”; then let the light go around from the Sea Bottom (the perineum) to the back, through the Caudal Funnel and straight up to the crown, above and below reflecting each other, until finally “I no longer feel that I have a body.”

Reading tips

  1. This part belongs to the same family as Part One, The Secret of the Golden Flower, and the “Reverting the Gaze and Illuminating Within” of Part Three: all use “light” — that is, awareness — to illuminate within. The difference is that this part gives a very clear route of “illuminating downward station by station,” with “pure white” as the standard at each station.
  2. Qiu Ling’s notes are very useful: he matches each step with a term from the alchemical scriptures (washing the marrow, washing the heart, purifying the land, calming the sea …), so that readers can connect pages of veiled language with concrete actions.
  3. Qiu Ling gives a special warning: people with high blood pressure should not practice this method. It begins at the head (the Muddy Pellet), and all methods that hold the intention on the head must be done very lightly.
  4. Section 2 involves “holding the breath and visualizing”; practice it only under the guidance of an experienced teacher.

1 · Refining Values the Mean and the Right Time

1 · Refining Values the Mean and the Right Time — three rules, and “rising, descending, releasing, gathering”

About This Chapter

The opening sets out the general outline: cultivation has three vehicles, but the method of refining has only three rules — keep the body upright, keep the mind empty, keep the intention genuine. These three sentences must never be dropped, from beginning to end, not for a moment; as for how the concrete methods are increased or reduced, each person can judge for himself. Then two principles are put forward: “practice values the mean” — adjust according to your own constitution, temperament and circumstances; and “refining must fit the time” — the year has its solstices, the month its new and full moon, the day its zi and wu hours; the natural movement of breath in the body shows different scenes at different times, and the fire phasing lies hidden in them. The secret is no more than four words: “rising, descending, releasing, gathering.” Finally it says that the ancients compressed a year into a month, a month into a day, a day into a double-hour, a double-hour into a quarter-hour, and called this “the work seizing creation”; but they also said one must “follow the substance and wait for the time” — the time is the Living Zi Hour, the substance is the substance of the Dao.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

古哲有言曰：修炼有三乘，而炼法惟三则。三则为何？端直其体；空洞其心；真实其念。此三句，是双修家彻终彻始、片时片刻莫可或遗者。其功法增减，第可自审而维持之。

An ancient sage said: cultivation has three vehicles, but the method of refining has only three rules. What are the three rules? Keep your body upright and straight; keep your mind empty and open; keep your intention genuine and real. These three sentences are what practitioners of the dual cultivation of inner nature and existence must never omit, from beginning to end, at any time or moment. As for increasing or reducing the methods, one can only judge for oneself and maintain them accordingly.

§2
Original (Chinese)

丹书所载，无非培命口诀，而行贵得中，又炼须合时。盖一年有二至，一月有朔望，一日有子午，体有自然气机，现有不同景象，而火候寓焉。究其秘要，不过升降放收而已。其利弊，不过勉强、自然，通泰、塞执，与夫确遵疑间焉而已。

What the alchemical books record is nothing but formulas for cultivating existence; and practice values the mean, while refining must fit the time. For the year has its two solstices, the month its new and full moon, the day its zi and wu hours; the body has its natural movement of breath, which shows different scenes, and the fire phasing lies within them. If we look into its secret, it is no more than rising, descending, releasing and gathering. Its benefits and harms are no more than forcing or naturalness, openness or obstruction and clinging, and whether one follows it faithfully or with doubts and interruptions.

§3
Original (Chinese)

古之人，以一年缩一月，以一月缩一日，以一日缩一时，以一时缩一刻，名曰功夺造化。《入药镜》寿其诀，诸丹经承述之，行无不验，而古哲谓须循体以待时，时乃活子，体乃道体也。

The ancients compressed a year into a month, a month into a day, a day into a double-hour, and a double-hour into a quarter-hour; this they called “the work seizing creation.” The Mirror for Compounding the Medicine (Ruyao jing 入药镜) handed down this formula, the alchemical scriptures have all repeated it, and when practiced it never fails. Yet the ancient sages said that one must “follow the substance and wait for the time”: the time is the Living Zi Hour, the substance is the substance of the Dao.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

三乘者指天仙、地仙、水仙。双修家指性命双修家。行贵得中，乃炼功须根据自己之秉性明暗、体质厚薄、阴阳偏胜、境地忙闲、气机强弱等而调剂之。炼须合时，即掌握天时。盖一年有二至即夏至和冬至。一月有朔望指农历初一和十五。一日有子午，子时在23时至一时，午时在十一时至十三时。

古人求功夺造化，以缩短功成的时限。汉代崔希范著《入药镜》云：“盗天地，夺造化，攒五行，会八卦”，意即人为万物之灵，可以提挈天地，握定阴阳，攒簇五行，会合八卦，于一刻之内行一年之功，所谓“一刻功夫应一年”。古人还认为行功须用活子时，亦即《入药镜》所说：“一日内，十二时，意所到，皆可为”。此言一日二十四小时中皆可修为，每日一阳（真气）初生时皆可下手修炼。

The “three vehicles” are the celestial immortal, the earthly immortal and the water immortal. “Practitioners of dual cultivation” means practitioners of the dual cultivation of inner nature and existence. “Practice values the mean” means that practice must be adjusted according to whether one’s nature is bright or dull, one’s constitution strong or weak, whether yin or yang predominates, whether one’s situation is busy or leisurely, whether the movement of breath is strong or weak, and so on. “Refining must fit the time” means grasping the timing of Heaven. The year has “two solstices,” the summer and winter solstices; the month has “new and full moon,” the first and fifteenth days of the lunar calendar; the day has “zi and wu”: the zi hour is from 11 pm to 1 am, the wu hour from 11 am to 1 pm.

The ancients sought “the work seizing creation” in order to shorten the time needed to succeed. The Mirror for Compounding the Medicine by Cui Xifan says: “Steal from Heaven and Earth, seize creation, assemble the five phases, unite the eight trigrams.” That is, humans are the most numinous of all beings and can lift up Heaven and Earth, grasp yin and yang, assemble the five phases and unite the eight trigrams, doing a year’s work within a quarter-hour — “a quarter-hour of work corresponds to a year.” The ancients also held that practice must use the Living Zi Hour, which is what the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine means by “within one day, at any of the twelve double-hours, wherever the intention reaches, one can practice.” That is, one can cultivate at any of the twenty-four hours of the day; whenever the one yang (the true breath) is first born each day, one can begin to cultivate.

Key Terms

  • Three vehicles: Qiu Ling’s note: the celestial immortal, the earthly immortal and the water immortal.
  • The three rules of cultivation: keep the body upright, keep the mind empty, keep the intention genuine (explained in detail in Section 8).
  • Practitioners of dual cultivation: practitioners of the dual cultivation of inner nature and existence (not sexual “dual cultivation”).
  • Practice values the mean: adjusting the method according to one’s nature, constitution, predominance of yin or yang, busyness or leisure of one’s situation, strength or weakness of the movement of breath.
  • Refining must fit the time: grasping the timing of Heaven — the two solstices, the new and full moon, the zi and wu hours.
  • Rising, descending, releasing, gathering: the four secrets of fire phasing.
  • Following faithfully or with doubts and interruptions (que zun yi jian 确遵疑间).
  • The work seizing creation: completing a year’s work within a quarter-hour.
  • Mirror for Compounding the Medicine: an alchemical scripture by Cui Xifan.
  • Living Zi Hour: the moment when the one yang is first born, not tied to the clock.
  • Following the substance and waiting for the time: following the substance of the Dao and waiting for the Living Zi Hour.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Recite the three sentences silently every day: keep the body upright, keep the mind empty, keep the intention genuine. Say them once before sitting, and again when your mind wanders during sitting.
  2. “Practice values the mean” means it differs from person to person: other people’s methods, durations and sensations do not necessarily suit you. Adjust according to your own constitution, daily rhythm and emotional state.
  3. Four pairs of benefit and harm: forcing or naturalness, openness or obstruction, following faithfully or with doubts and interruptions — after each session you can check yourself with these pairs: was it forced or natural today? Open or blocked? Did I really do it, or was my mind divided?
  4. A practical understanding of the Living Zi Hour: it need not be in the middle of the night. Whenever in your day body and mind are quietest and you feel a little stirring of life, that is the time to begin.

Translator's Notes

  1. “The Mirror for Compounding the Medicine handed down this formula”: shou 寿 here means “to make last, to transmit”; the translation renders it accordingly.
  2. Qiu Ling’s note says “Cui Xifan of the Han dynasty”; Cui Xifan is generally considered to have lived at the end of the Tang and in the Five Dynasties period, as noted in Part One, Chapter 8. The translation omits the dynasty.
  3. “Practitioners of dual cultivation”: in this book this means the dual cultivation of inner nature and existence, and has nothing to do with the sexual “dual cultivation” spoken of in later times.

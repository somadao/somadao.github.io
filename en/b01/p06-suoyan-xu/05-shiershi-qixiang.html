5 · The Atmosphere of the Twelve Double-Hours

5 · The Atmosphere of the Twelve Double-Hours — the main point is “non-abiding,” the key is “not moving”

About This Chapter

This section is quite unusual: the author matches each of the twelve double-hours of the day with an “atmosphere,” from the “water and moon reflecting each other” of the zi hour to the “limpid and still” of the hai hour, forming a complete sequence of states of mind. It does not only say what happens at which hour of the day; it also says that a single session of quiet sitting can pass in order through the numinous states of a year, a month and a day compressed into a moment. The second passage describes in detail the method after the exact middle of the hai hour: the spirit pours into the lower Cinnabar Field, as if present and as if forgotten; suddenly one sees a red sun emerging from the moon at the Celestial Heart; the field of the belly is hot as if roasted; the primordial breath passes through the Caudal Funnel and reaches the Jade Pillow, is lightly led with the intention, and when it passes through, that is called “opening the Governing vessel” — “when cultivation has reached the opening of the Governing vessel, half the work is done.” It also says that cool fluid drips from empty space, extremely cool on reaching the heart and extremely hot after passing it — only that is true fluid. The key sentence of the whole section is: “Its main point is non-abiding, and its key lies entirely in the one formula of not moving.” As soon as one entertains a thought of observation or forms a deliberation, the scene changes; one should stop practicing at once and go back to the very first step to begin again.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

谨按古哲遗有四时功法，而缩行于十二时中。余今以十二时一定气象为学者言之。凡夫水月交映，得之自然者，子正有之。我无觉有，丑正有之。觉气通流，寅正也。气机洋溢，卯正也。存无守有，辰正之功。隐现莫测，巳正气象。万象罗列，午正气局。真幻无常，未正如之。念起即扫，申正功法。一灵独露，是酉正兆。闻见顿泯，觉无端倪，非戌不现。切戒惊疑，守戌正法，湛如寂如，是值亥正。

Respectfully noted: the ancient sages left us methods for the four seasons, and compressed them into the twelve double-hours. Now I shall describe to students the fixed atmosphere of each of the twelve double-hours. Water and moon reflecting each other, obtained naturally — this occurs at the exact middle of zi. I have no awareness, yet there is awareness — this occurs at the exact middle of chou. Feeling the breath flowing freely — that is the middle of yin. The movement of breath overflowing — that is the middle of mao. Keeping non-being and guarding being — that is the work of the middle of chen. Appearing and disappearing unfathomably — that is the atmosphere of the middle of si. The ten thousand images arrayed — that is the configuration of the middle of wu. The real and the illusory changing without constancy — so it is at the middle of wei. Sweeping away each thought as it arises — that is the method of the middle of shen. A single point of numinous clarity showing itself alone — that is the sign of the middle of you. Hearing and seeing suddenly vanish, one feels no trace of anything — this does not appear before xu. Beware of alarm and doubt; keep to the method of the middle of xu; limpid and still — then one has reached the middle of hai.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

古之十二时即今之二十四小时，古之一时当今之二小时。若从子时算起，子即二十三时至凌晨一时，丑即凌晨一时至三时，如此类推。

十二时虽有一定气局，惟行功时得之自然为真，学者可循而行之，或有吻合。

The ancient twelve double-hours are our present twenty-four hours; one ancient double-hour equals two of our hours. Counting from the zi hour, zi is from 11 pm to 1 am, chou from 1 am to 3 am, and so on.

Although each of the twelve double-hours has its fixed configuration, only what is obtained naturally during practice is genuine. Students can follow this in practice, and there may be some correspondence.

§2
Original (Chinese)

上所述，核即前辈缩年缩月缩时缩刻，按部行去，所现灵境有如此，然须善会。而功法咸备，其大旨在无住，而旨脑全凭不动一诀。故凡行至亥正，法惟神注下田，而功须若存若忘，忽得红日一轮，透自天心月中，初见大如豆许，切戒念动，已而月隐日彰。法惟息心以俟，得有腹田若炙，乃是日浴海底之效。故得遍体充和，又觉内炁后攻，旋复炁穿尾闾，腾腾上透，乃无弊焉。方其海浴未透时，切戒惊提，否则立有莫测，状若流火，法惟叠用忘诀乃痊。

What has been described above, when examined, is how the earlier masters compressed a year, a month, a double-hour and a quarter-hour and practiced them step by step; the numinous states that appear are like this, but one must understand them well. The methods are all complete; their main point is “non-abiding,” and their key lies entirely in the one formula of “not moving.” So whenever one reaches the exact middle of the hai hour, the only method is to let the spirit pour into the lower field, and the work must be as if present and as if forgotten. Suddenly one obtains a red sun, emerging from the moon at the Celestial Heart; at first it looks only as big as a bean — beware of stirring any thought. Soon the moon fades and the sun shines forth. The only method is to still the mind and wait; when the field of the belly becomes as if roasted, that is the effect of “the sun bathing at the Sea Bottom.” Then one obtains fullness and harmony throughout the body, and feels the primordial breath within attacking backward; soon it passes through the Caudal Funnel and rises surging upward, and then there is no harm. While the “sea bath” has not yet passed upward, beware of lifting it in alarm; otherwise there will at once be unpredictable effects, like flowing fire, which can only be calmed by repeatedly using the formula of “forgetting.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

古哲于此，微移其神，导之后透，自必破关上达。既已抵枕，乃以意引。盖此玉枕，丹书名为铁壁，在天即名罡际，非此真炁，莫能透也。得此真炁以引透之，乃为通督。修至通督，一半功矣！第行此功，惟用引字，切戒用武。一杂武功，便致有声若雷，若霹雳。学者即或失戒，致有此种，切戒惊怖。法惟益加定静，守过半晌工夫，随有一滴如泉，从空滴下，体其趣味，觉大清凉，或极甘美；体其滴下，有形亦好，无形更妙不可言。

At this point the ancient sages moved their spirit slightly and guided it to pass through behind, and it would naturally break through the passes and rise. Once it reached the Pillow, they led it with the intention. For this Jade Pillow is called the “Iron Wall” in the alchemical books, and in the heavens it is called the “Edge of the Dipper”; without this true primordial breath, nothing can pass through it. To lead it through with this true primordial breath is called “opening the Governing vessel.” When cultivation has reached the opening of the Governing vessel, half the work is done! But in doing this work, use only the word “lead”; beware of using force. As soon as forceful effort is mixed in, there will be a sound like thunder, like a thunderclap. Even if a student slips and this happens, beware of being frightened. The only method is to become still more settled and quiet; after keeping this for a good while, a drop like a spring will drip down from empty space. Experience its taste: it feels greatly cool, or extremely sweet. Experience its dripping: if it has form, that is good; if it has no form, it is wonderful beyond words.

§4
Original (Chinese)

古哲谓此一滴，直落绛宫，是名真阴。其降景象，缓亦好，捷亦好，惟能滴下无阻，点点到心，更为难得。又云，有形而速名玉液，无形而速名金液。有形而速，无形而缓，总属妙有。法当体认得真者，盖以此点到心极凉，过心极热。热若汤泼，斯乃真液。若点无上说，尚属后天，不过得润气机而已，不可视为仙品。然于当时，切戒拟议念起，法惟循次行去，亦为有益功验。

The ancient sages said that this drop falls straight to the Crimson Palace and is called “true yin.” Whether it descends slowly or quickly, both are good; but if it can drip down without obstruction, drop by drop reaching the heart, that is even rarer. They also said: what has form and is quick is called “jade liquid”; what has no form and is quick is called “golden liquid.” With form and quick, without form and slow — all belong to wondrous being. The method is to recognize whether it is genuine: when this drop reaches the heart it is extremely cool, and after passing the heart it is extremely hot, hot as if broth were poured — only that is true fluid. If the drop is not as described above, it still belongs to Posterior Heaven and merely moistens the movement of breath; it cannot be regarded as an immortal substance. Yet at that moment, beware of deliberating or letting thoughts arise; the only method is to continue in order, and that too is a beneficial result of practice.

§5
Original (Chinese)

诚以古哲功法，有参天时之四季，有参岁日之二六，其法至活而至玄。如前所议之四时，与夫十二时之灵境，乃缩得片刻之间，其情其景，确可逐步绘示，然须学者自问，可果能步步如戒，中无或著，或有中著否耳。若行无或著，自无中变。倘有或著，便有中变。所现情景，便自大异。夫岂古哲示有未真者哉！学者于中，著一察念，起一拟议，落一意识，来情来景，变更莫测，便与古议大殊，急宜停功勿事。少顷，念静气平，仍事本步，不如退行本步前程，总以得合入手初步灵境，体无二焉，方许顺循下行，否则宁将前功尽舍，另起入手为妙。

Truly, the methods of the ancient sages sometimes take the four seasons of Heaven as their reference, sometimes the twelve double-hours of the day; their method is extremely lively and extremely mysterious. The four seasons discussed above and the numinous states of the twelve double-hours are compressed into a moment; their feelings and scenes can indeed be depicted step by step. But students must ask themselves: can I really keep the precepts at every step, without any clinging in between — or is there clinging in between? If one practices without clinging, there will naturally be no change midway. If there is clinging, there will be a change midway, and the feelings and scenes that appear will naturally be very different. How could this mean that what the ancient sages taught was untrue! If, in the midst of it, the student entertains a single thought of observation, forms a single deliberation, falls into a single act of consciousness, the feelings and scenes that come will change unpredictably and be very different from what the ancients described; one should stop practicing at once. After a while, when thoughts are still and breath is calm, one may resume the same step — but better still to go back to the stage before that step; only when one obtains the numinous state that matches the very first step of entry, and experiences it as no different, may one continue onward in order. Otherwise it is better to give up all the previous work and begin again from the entry.

§6
Original (Chinese)

古哲所谓行贵得中，而天时须合者，乃缩一年一月一日一时之灵境，彙而按次，统现于片刻之中，而具有四时十二月，月月有朔望三十日，日日有十二时，时时有自然光景，其法至玄而至真。然惟能息心体认，循行不惑，方能步步合古说法。噫，要知既缩岁景于一刻者，法惟活其气机，寂其心意以行，自合古法，能行炷香已夺百年造化。古哲故名曰功夺造化，是难而易者，学者勉之。

What the ancient sages meant by “practice values the mean, and the timing of Heaven must be matched” is this: the numinous states of a year, a month, a day and a double-hour are compressed, gathered and arranged in order, and all appear within a moment, containing the four seasons and twelve months, each month with its new and full moon and thirty days, each day with its twelve double-hours, each double-hour with its natural scenery. This method is extremely mysterious and extremely true. But only by stilling the mind to experience it and following it without doubt can one match the ancients’ teaching step by step. Ah! Know that since the scenery of a year is compressed into a quarter-hour, the only method is to keep the movement of breath lively and the mind and intention still; then one naturally matches the ancient method, and practicing for the time it takes an incense stick to burn already seizes a hundred years of creation. That is why the ancient sages called it “the work seizing creation.” It is hard and yet easy; students, strive for it.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2–§6
Original note (Chinese)

此段功法大旨在“无住”二字，而湛如寂如即是“无住”，无住即不动。

亥时在二十一时至二十三时之间，行功至亥正，神注下田，忽得红日一轮，透自天心月中，此时学者当是身无其身，此月似在下极海底（会阴）透出，古人认为，下极乃坎水，坎水波澄，故得现水月，光华上射，乾气凝之而得日辉，乃有日月同宫之妙。但证之体验，不过是眼前性光变化，静极时将眼前性光照于下田，皎白一片，有如水月，随之化为红日，此乃月吐日辉之由。日未升时尚潜海底，日升则宜直循督透。

通督以后，有一滴凉液如泉从空降下，此乃在益加静定中得。此液直落绛宫，滴下甚捷，到心极凉，过心极热，方是真液。

The main point of this method lies in the two words “non-abiding,” and being limpid and still is “non-abiding”; non-abiding is not moving.

The hai hour is between 9 pm and 11 pm. When practice reaches the exact middle of hai and the spirit pours into the lower field, one suddenly obtains a red sun emerging from the moon at the Celestial Heart. At this time the student should be “a body without a body” (not feeling the body), and this moon seems to emerge from the Sea Bottom at the Lower Pole (the perineum). The ancients held that the Lower Pole is the water of Kan; when the waves of the water of Kan are calm and clear, the moon in the water can appear; its radiance shoots upward and, condensed by the breath of Qian, gains the brilliance of the sun, so that there is the wonder of “sun and moon in the same palace.” But verified by experience, it is no more than a change in the light of nature before the eyes: at the extreme of stillness, when the light of nature before the eyes shines on the lower field, there is a sheet of pure white like the moon in water, which then turns into a red sun — this is the origin of “the moon giving forth the sun’s brilliance.” Before the sun rises it is still hidden at the Sea Bottom; once it rises, it should pass straight up along the Governing vessel.

After the Governing vessel opens, a drop of cool fluid like a spring descends from empty space; this is obtained in still greater stillness and settledness. This fluid falls straight to the Crimson Palace and drips down very quickly; extremely cool on reaching the heart and extremely hot after passing it — only that is true fluid.

Key Terms

  • Twelve double-hours: zi, chou, yin, mao, chen, si, wu, wei, shen, you, xu, hai, each about two hours; the “exact middle” (zheng 正) is the midpoint of the double-hour.
  • Water and moon reflecting each other; no awareness yet awareness; feeling the breath flowing; the movement of breath overflowing; keeping non-being and guarding being; appearing and disappearing unfathomably; the ten thousand images arrayed; the real and illusory changing; sweeping away each thought as it arises; a single point of numinous clarity; hearing and seeing suddenly vanishing; limpid and still: the atmospheres of the twelve double-hours listed in this section.
  • Non-abiding (wuzhu 无住): not staying, not clinging; Qiu Ling’s note: being limpid and still is non-abiding, and non-abiding is not moving.
  • The sun bathing at the Sea Bottom: the sign of the field of the belly being hot as if roasted.
  • Lifting in alarm (jing ti 惊提): pulling upward in panic.
  • Formula of forgetting: the formula of the word “forget.”
  • Iron Wall / Edge of the Dipper: other names for the pass of the Jade Pillow; see Three Passes.
  • Opening the Governing vessel: the true primordial breath passing through the Jade Pillow, the Governing vessel opened; “when cultivation has reached the opening of the Governing vessel, half the work is done.”
  • Formula of “leading”: use only “leading,” beware of using “force.”
  • True yin: the drop that drips from empty space and falls straight to the Crimson Palace.
  • Jade liquid, golden liquid: with form and quick is jade liquid, without form and quick is golden liquid; see Sweet Dew.
  • Criterion of true fluid: extremely cool on reaching the heart, extremely hot after passing it, hot as poured broth.
  • Sun and moon in the same palace, the moon giving forth the sun’s brilliance: Qiu Ling’s note: the light of nature before the eyes shines on the lower field, pure white like the moon in water, then turns into a red sun.
  • Incense stick: the time it takes one stick of incense to burn.
  • The work seizing creation: see Section 1.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The core of the whole section: “Its main point is non-abiding; its key lies entirely in not moving.” Whatever scene comes, do not stay with it, do not cling to it.
  2. The atmospheres of the twelve double-hours can serve as a “map of states of mind”: from “feeling the breath flowing” and “the movement of breath overflowing” to “sweeping away each thought as it arises,” “a single point of numinous clarity,” “hearing and seeing suddenly vanishing” and “limpid and still” — the further along, the stiller. During quiet sitting there is no need to match yourself to them, but you can know that the direction is ever stiller and ever emptier.
  3. Stop at once when something abnormal appears: “entertaining a single thought of observation, forming a single deliberation, falling into a single act of consciousness” makes the scenes change unpredictably; then “stop practicing at once,” wait until thoughts are still and breath is calm, and begin again from an earlier step. This is a very safe and very precious principle.
  4. Use only “leading,” never “force”: when passing the Jade Pillow, only lead lightly; as soon as force is used, there may be a sound like thunder in the ears. Even if it happens, do not panic; become still more settled and it will pass.
  5. Qiu Ling’s plain explanation of the “red sun”: it is no more than a change in the light of nature before the eyes at the extreme of stillness. If you see it, there is no need to marvel; if you do not, there is no need to seek it.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Respectfully noted” (jin an 谨按): a humble expression in classical prose, “respectfully examined.”
  2. “Key” (zhinao 旨脑): that is, zhunao 主脑, the main point.
  3. “If the drop is not as described above” (ruo dian wu shang shuo 若点无上说): if this drop does not show the features described above.
  4. The original text of this section is in only two passages; for ease of comparison and recitation, the second is further divided into §2–§6, and Qiu Ling’s note is placed after §6.

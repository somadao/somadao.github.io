3 · The Method for Summer and Autumn

3 · The Method for Summer and Autumn — the breath should open in front

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

In summer and autumn the movement of breath should “open in front” — descend along the front of the body. The method: first keep it for a moment at the Flowery Pool (the mouth), then go down past the Twelve-Storied Tower to the Crimson Palace and keep it there. At this point the Crimson Palace feels wide and deep, with a leisurely charm; dimly one sees the “water of nature,” its waves shining blue, and suddenly one feels cool fluid dripping down from the sky, especially cool on reaching the palace of the heart — at this time keep it at length. Then, following the movement of breath, let it slip down and divide behind the heart; on reaching the abdomen, the whole body feels rich and dense, the Lower Pole hot as fire, a light sweat front and back, as if one had just bathed. Then lightly direct the intention backward and let the breath pass through the Caudal Funnel by itself and rise to the crown; keep it at length at the Muddy Pellet, then descend from the brain to the Flowery Pool and the Crimson Palace and keep it at length again; naturally the whole body feels fresh and light. That is one circuit. Qiu Ling’s note speaks particularly of posture: when sitting, the neck and back should be straight and the face slightly raised; at the final long keeping at the Crimson Palace, the chest should draw back slightly, carrying out the principle of “emptying the heart and calming the Gate.”

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

若值夏秋，体其气机，乃贵前通。其功法乃自华池一存后，方下过重楼，抵绛宫，法当存此绛宫，局境宽邃，趣味悠闲，恍见性水，波光蓝如，顿觉得有凉液自天滴下，一到心宫，倍觉清凉。斯时大存之。

If it is summer or autumn, experience the movement of breath at that time: it should open in front. The method: after keeping it for a moment at the Flowery Pool, only then go down past the Twelve-Storied Tower to the Crimson Palace. The method is to keep it at this Crimson Palace: the realm here is wide and deep, with a leisurely charm; dimly one sees the “water of nature,” its waves shining blue; suddenly one feels cool fluid dripping down from the sky, especially cool on reaching the palace of the heart. At this time keep it at length.

§2
Original (Chinese)

继乃随机溜下，分达心后。及腹，又觉遍体氤氲，下极火热，身前身后微微汗透，得有如沐如浴景象。微以意向后注，自透尾闾而升巅顶。一到泥丸，须大存之，乃复自脑下华池，达绛宫，又大存之，自觉遍体清快。古哲谓为一周天。夏秋功法乃如此。

Then, following the movement of breath, let it slip down and divide to reach behind the heart. On reaching the abdomen, one again feels the whole body rich and dense, the Lower Pole hot as fire, a light sweat coming through front and back, as if one had just bathed. With a light intention direct it backward, and it passes through the Caudal Funnel by itself and rises to the crown. Once it reaches the Muddy Pellet, keep it there at length; then it descends again from the brain to the Flowery Pool and reaches the Crimson Palace; keep it at length again, and one feels the whole body fresh and light. The ancient sages called this one Celestial Circuit. Such is the method for summer and autumn.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§2
Original note (Chinese)

夏秋功法从华池入手，然后意存绛宫。所谓觉绛宫局境宽邃，恍见性水波光，凉液自天滴下，大多情况下有如此妙境，亦因人而异。当时学者姿势项背须直而头面须带仰势为宜。继达心后，当由心后两络分达腰肾，有露液化血、露血化精之意。及腹，当到脐轮后一寸三分处，下极火热，是又炼气化炁之验。若得如沐如浴景象，虽未满一周天而得效如此，停功亦无不可，但须静坐片时以忘热。若继续行功，微以意向后注，因功到汗透，功已足，又时值夏令，故曰微意。而透尾闾升泥丸，则须加意内透，并在泥丸大存。这一存极重要，可助打通督、任脉，亦为中通之基。若虚极静笃，功足炁腾，则眼前呈现种种光图，学者须戒欣赏。最后下绛宫又大存，此存也古称“无为功里施功”，学者此时宜胸势微向后，行“虚心靖阙（绛宫）”之旨，使气血畅通，于是自觉遍体轻快。

The method for summer and autumn begins at the Flowery Pool, then the intention is kept at the Crimson Palace. The feeling that the realm of the Crimson Palace is wide and deep, the dim sight of the shining waves of the water of nature, the cool fluid dripping from the sky — in most cases there are wonderful states like these, though they vary from person to person. At this time the student’s posture should have the neck and back straight and the face slightly raised. Then, reaching behind the heart, the breath should go by two network vessels behind the heart to the lower back and kidneys, with the sense of “dew-fluid turning into blood, and blood into essence.” On reaching the abdomen, it should reach the point one cun three fen behind the navel wheel; the Lower Pole is hot as fire, which is again a sign of refining breath into primordial breath. If one obtains the state “as if one had just bathed,” then even though a full circuit has not been completed, one has already obtained this result, and it is quite all right to stop; but one must sit still for a while to “forget the heat.” If one continues practicing, one directs the breath backward with a light intention — because the practice has reached the point of sweating, the work is already sufficient, and it is summer, so it says “a light intention.” But when passing through the Caudal Funnel and rising to the Muddy Pellet, one must add intention to pass inward, and keep it at length at the Muddy Pellet. This keeping is extremely important: it can help open the Governing and Conception vessels, and is also the foundation of “opening through the center.” If emptiness reaches its limit and stillness is complete, the work sufficient and the primordial breath surging, all kinds of light patterns appear before the eyes; the student must refrain from admiring them. Finally, descending to the Crimson Palace, one keeps it at length again; the ancients called this keeping “applying work within the work of non-action.” At this time the student’s chest should draw back slightly, carrying out the principle of “emptying the heart and calming the Gate (the Crimson Palace),” so that breath and blood flow freely, and then one naturally feels the whole body light.

Key Terms

  • Opening in front: the breath descending along the front of the body (the Conception vessel).
  • Flowery Pool: the inside of the mouth, where the Conception and Governing vessels meet.
  • Crimson Palace / palace of the heart: inside the chest.
  • Wide and deep realm (jujing kuansui 局境宽邃).
  • Water of nature (xingshui 性水): a cool, water-like state seen in stillness, a scene of the same kind as the light of Nature.
  • As if one had just bathed: comfortable as after a bath; compare “as if drunk, as if bathed” in Part One, Chapter 6.
  • Forgetting the heat: when the mind becomes still, the heat subsides of itself; see Qiu Ling’s note in Section 4, “the formula of forgetting the heat means that when the mind is still one is naturally cool.”
  • Opening through the center (zhongtong 中通): opening along the midline of the body; see Yellow Path.
  • Light patterns (guangtu 光图): images of light that appear before the eyes in stillness.
  • Applying work within the work of non-action.
  • Emptying the heart and calming the Gate (xu xin jing que 虚心靖阙): making the chest (the Crimson Gate) empty and calm.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The direction for summer and autumn is “descending in front”: in the hot seasons the movement of breath should go down the front of the body, cool and descending.
  2. The center of gravity is the chest (the Crimson Palace): this section twice “keeps at length” the Crimson Palace — letting the chest become wide, calm and cool. This is very practical for people who easily become irritable or feel tightness in the chest in summer.
  3. Posture: neck and back straight, face slightly raised; at the final keeping at the chest, draw the chest back slightly (do not push it out) and let the chest become “empty.”
  4. You can stop once you sweat: Qiu Ling says that once you obtain the effect “as if one had just bathed,” it is all right to stop even if you have not completed a circuit, but sit still for a while to let the heat subside.
  5. When light patterns appear: “refrain from admiring them” — you see them; do not admire them, do not chase them.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Its waves shining blue” (boguang lan ru 波光蓝如): ru is an adjectival suffix, “bluish.”
  2. “Dew-fluid turning into blood, and blood into essence”: in Qiu Ling’s note, the second lu 露 (“dew”) is probably superfluous or an error for er 而 (“and”); the meaning is “dew-fluid turns into blood, and blood into essence,” and the translation follows this.
  3. The “light sweat front and back” after “the Lower Pole hot as fire” is a sign recorded by the ancients for summer practice; a light sweat is the measure, and there is no need to seek heavy sweating. This is the translator’s remark.

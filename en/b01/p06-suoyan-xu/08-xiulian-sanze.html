8 · The Three Rules of Cultivation

8 · The Three Rules of Cultivation — keep the body upright, keep the mind empty, keep the intention genuine

About This Chapter

The last section returns to the three-sentence outline set out in Section 1 and explains each in turn:

  • Keep the body upright: in the Conception and Governing vessels, when the body is not upright the movement of breath is blocked; once the body is upright, the movement of breath meets no obstruction and all the network vessels flow freely. At first there may be breath surging upward and swelling, stagnation and soreness front and back — these are precisely signs of “opening.”
  • Keep the mind empty: when the mind is not empty, breath and spirit scatter with the thoughts; once the mind is empty, the primordial breath is united in one, and even if there is stagnation, it dissolves as soon as the primordial breath arrives. It also has a wonderful use: the work of opening the Conception vessel easily falls into torpor, but with an empty mind the breath is calm and the spirit clear; the work of opening the Governing vessel easily brings a swollen head and red eyes, but with it these troubles do not occur.
  • Keep the intention genuine: begin from “no delusion”: take no delusion as absorption, and removing a delusion the moment it arises as wisdom; first remove coarse delusions, then subtle ones, and finally even “the delusion of no delusion,” until one is naturally pure and one. This genuine intention is the true seed of the Celestial Heart.

Qiu Ling’s note says: to know the living formulas, one must first know the constant work; so one should first become familiar with the methods of the Scripture of Opening and Closing and the Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall above, and then combine them with the flexible use described in this part — having both the fixed, constant method and the living method is the complete method.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

若夫气机既通，阴精既化，宿疾既除，大可从事修炼三则。

When the movement of breath is open, the yin essence transformed and old illnesses removed, one may well devote oneself to the three rules of cultivation.

§2Keep the body upright
Original (Chinese)

何以端直其体？要知任督二脉，体不端直，则气机塞。体一端直，则气机无阻，百络通利，关系非细。第初行此诀，气不易降，每有气翻逆涌，或觉身前身后胀滞发酸，皆因络有淤泥，尚滞未消，经炁透冲，现此不舒，正是通兆。急须加行翘臀等诀以逼督，虚心实腹以逼任，任督大通，前患自平。任督既通，除子午两时循行采取大法外，须于得药之后，循行翘臀虚心等功，加行平直功法，以炼以养，三年有成。

Why keep the body upright? Know that, in the Conception and Governing vessels, when the body is not upright the movement of breath is blocked. Once the body is upright, the movement of breath meets no obstruction and all the network vessels flow freely; it matters a great deal. But when one first practices this formula, the breath does not descend easily; often the breath surges upward against the current, or one feels swelling, stagnation and soreness front and back. This is all because there is mud in the network vessels that still lingers and has not dissolved; as the primordial breath pushes through, this discomfort appears — it is precisely a sign of opening. One must quickly add formulas such as “raising the buttocks” to press the Governing vessel open, and “emptying the heart and filling the belly” to press the Conception vessel open; when the Conception and Governing vessels are thoroughly open, the earlier troubles settle by themselves. Once the Conception and Governing vessels are open, besides practicing the great method of collecting at the zi and wu hours as usual, after obtaining the Medicine one should continue practicing raising the buttocks, emptying the heart and so on, and add the method of “level and upright,” refining and nourishing; in three years there will be success.

§3Keep the mind empty
Original (Chinese)

何以空洞其心？心不空洞，气神随念散注，理失其端，治无其绪，厥功难行。心一空洞，炁统于一，纵有淤滞，炁到立化，厥功易效。且犹有妙用寓焉。若或从事通任一功，其既必行坠腹澄神，有坎阳陷阴之象，易落昏沉。备是诀者，气静神清，于斯之际，且必得有一灵独露之功。其若从事通督者，功既，必行存乾涤髓一诀。此功失备，升已杂浊，每有脑胀目赤，因动燥念，引起雷阳，致召雷震神飞之险。备斯一诀，万无此等患，纵或但犯胀眩，略加虚松退透等诀，患自立失。此句妙用，可胜述哉！

Why keep the mind empty? When the mind is not empty, breath and spirit follow the thoughts and scatter, the principle loses its thread and the treatment has no order, and the work is hard to carry out. Once the mind is empty, the primordial breath is united in one; even if there is stagnation, it dissolves at once when the primordial breath arrives, and the work easily takes effect. Moreover, there is a wonderful use in it. If one engages in the work of opening the Conception vessel, afterward one must practice “weighting the belly and clarifying the spirit,” which has the image of the yang of Kan sunk in yin and easily falls into torpor; one who has this formula will have calm breath and a clear spirit at that moment, and will surely attain the work of “a single point of numinous clarity showing itself alone.” If one engages in the work of opening the Governing vessel, afterward one must practice the formula of “keeping Qian and washing the marrow.” If this work is not done well, what has risen is already mixed and turbid; there is often a swollen head and red eyes, because a dry, restless thought has stirred and set off the yang of thunder, bringing the danger of “thunder shaking and the spirit flying off.” With this one formula, there will never be such troubles; even if there is just some swelling and dizziness of the head, add a little of the formulas of loosening, withdrawing and passing through, and the trouble disappears at once. The wonderful use of this one sentence is beyond description!

§4Keep the intention genuine
Original (Chinese)

谨按古哲，先从无妄入手。惟以无妄为定，以妄起立除为慧。其次第，初除粗妄，继除微妄，终除无妄之妄，造至自然纯一为了当。谨先立此真念，为吾天心真种子，是名迳炼上关。究其趋向，不外除妄存真。参其功诀，无非假一除万。推其功法，乃是由浅入深，归于无住。

Respectfully following the ancient sages: begin from “no delusion.” Take only the absence of deluded thought as absorption, and removing a deluded thought the moment it arises as wisdom. The order is: first remove coarse delusions, then subtle delusions, and finally remove “the delusion of no delusion,” until one reaches natural purity and oneness — then it is done. First establish this genuine intention as the true seed of my Celestial Heart; this is called “refining the upper pass directly.” Its direction is nothing but removing delusion and preserving the true. Its formula is nothing but using the One to remove the ten thousand. Its method is to go from shallow to deep and return to non-abiding.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

修炼三则，即本篇开始所谓端直其体；空洞其心；真实其念。此段进一步对此三则解释之。

要知行功活诀，必先知常功为何。故须先悉本书所收入之《尹真人阖辟仙经》、《尹真人寥阳殿问答编》中所述功法，然后可以合此功法而活用。此乃一贯而下，有定法常法，亦有活法，则为全法矣！

The three rules of cultivation are what was said at the beginning of this part: keep the body upright; keep the mind empty; keep the intention genuine. This passage explains these three rules further.

To know the living formulas of practice, one must first know what the constant work is. So one must first become familiar with the methods described in Perfected Yin’s Scripture of Opening and Closing and Perfected Yin’s Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall included in this book, and then one can combine them with this method and use them flexibly. Running through it all like this — with the fixed, constant method and also the living method — is the complete method!

Key Terms

  • The three rules of cultivation: keep the body upright, keep the mind empty, keep the intention genuine.
  • Breath surging upward against the current.
  • Sign of opening (tong zhao 通兆).
  • Pressing the Governing vessel open, pressing the Conception vessel open.
  • Emptying the heart and filling the belly: from the Laozi, “empty their hearts, fill their bellies.”
  • Method of “level and upright”: not explained in the text; literally, a method that makes the body level and upright.
  • Weighting the belly and clarifying the spirit: the breath sinking to the lower abdomen, the spirit made clear.
  • The yang of Kan sunk in yin: the single yang line of the trigram Kan sunk between two yin lines, a metaphor for yang breath sinking, which easily leads to torpor.
  • A single point of numinous clarity showing itself alone.
  • Keeping Qian and washing the marrow: keeping the head and washing the brain-marrow (a formula used after opening the Governing vessel).
  • Thunder shaking and the spirit flying off: the danger to the head when a restless thought sets off yang breath rushing upward.
  • Loosening, withdrawing and passing through: formulas that make the movement of breath loose, let it withdraw downward and pass outward.
  • No delusion (wu wang 无妄): no deluded thought; taking no delusion as absorption and removing a delusion the moment it arises as wisdom.
  • Coarse delusions, subtle delusions, the delusion of no delusion: three levels of deluded thought.
  • True seed of the Celestial Heart: the genuine intention is the seed of the Celestial Heart.
  • Refining the upper pass directly.
  • Using the One to remove the ten thousand: using one thing (the genuine intention) to remove ten thousand (deluded thoughts).

Putting It into Practice

  1. Keep the body upright: sit upright — not stiffly pushing out the chest, but with the spine naturally erect and the crown lightly lifted. At first there may be swelling, soreness and breath surging upward; these are “signs of opening,” and there is no need to panic. If the discomfort persists, relax and shorten the session.
  2. Keeping the mind empty is the best “insurance”: the text says its wonderful use is that downward work (opening the Conception vessel) does not easily fall into torpor, and upward work (opening the Governing vessel) does not easily bring a swollen head and red eyes. Whatever method you practice, first let the mind become empty.
  3. When the head swells or feels dizzy: “add a little of the formulas of loosening, withdrawing and passing through, and the trouble disappears at once” — relax, let the breath withdraw downward and pass outward, and the discomfort in the head will disappear.
  4. The three levels of keeping the intention genuine: first remove coarse delusions (obvious stray thoughts), then subtle delusions (faint thoughts), and finally “the delusion of no delusion” (the very thought “now I have no stray thoughts”).
  5. The simplest definitions of absorption and wisdom: no deluded thought is absorption; removing a deluded thought the moment it arises is wisdom.
  6. Qiu Ling’s summary: first learn the constant method, then the living method. The methods of the preceding parts are the foundation; this part is their flexible use on that foundation.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Afterward” (gong ji 功既): after the work is done.
  2. “If this work is not done well, what has risen is already mixed and turbid” (ci gong shi bei, sheng yi za zhuo 此功失备，升已杂浊).
  3. The “three rules of cultivation” of this section echo exactly the opening of Section 1, so that the whole part comes full circle.

4 · Methods for Treating Illness

4 · Methods for Treating Illness — five situations, and “the heart toiling while the body idles”

Important note (translator): This section is a record of the experience of Qing-dynasty practitioners in “regulating the body through practice.” The accumulated cold, much phlegm, inner heat with dryness, hot blood with stagnant breath and accumulated yin turning into fire that it speaks of are concepts of traditional Chinese medicine and of the alchemists, and do not correspond one to one with modern medical diagnoses. The content of this section cannot replace medical care. If you are ill, please see a doctor first and follow medical advice; practice can only be a supplement, preferably under the guidance of an experienced teacher. Operations in the text such as holding the breath, “raising the buttocks,” breaking through the passes with the intention and seeking heavy sweating are especially unsuitable for people with high blood pressure, cardiovascular or cerebrovascular disease, or a weak constitution. The text itself says at the end that these “martial exercises” are only borrowed to treat illness: “how could they be relied on to attain the Dao?”

About This Chapter

What has been described so far is the constant method of practice for people without illness. People who are ill have other methods, which they must weigh for themselves. The general principle: when practice fits the substance of the Dao and also fits the season, its effects are wondrous; above all, do not be rigid (“beware of being fixed and absolute”). This section lists five situations:

  1. Accumulated cold stagnating within during summer or autumn — first use the method of alternating winter and summer practice, and practice the summer–autumn method alone only after the accumulated cold has been fully expelled;
  2. A constitution with much phlegm — best treated in summer: first let the breath descend smoothly, then add methods such as weighting the abdomen, pouring in the spirit and raising the buttocks, with thorough sweating as the measure;
  3. Inner heat with dryness — use only the descending formula, add the method of emptying the heart, then lead the fire back to its source and pour the spirit into the Gate of Life;
  4. Hot blood with stagnant breath, the chest and diaphragm stiff and full — first practice the method of emptying the heart, then add the work of withdrawing to the back; and from here the text goes on to “opening the middle pass” and “opening the Mysterious Pass”;
  5. Accumulated yin turning into fire — treated differently according to whether the accumulated yin has already turned into fire and whether it has stagnated in all the network vessels; finally the text points out that this illness mostly comes from “the heart toiling while the body idles,” and that to cure it one must first change one’s habits.

Original Text and Translation

§1The general principle; accumulated cold stagnating within
Original (Chinese)

以上所述，乃行功之常法，为身无疾病者言之。苟有疾病，其中另有功法，须自酌施。总以行合道体，而又得时。厥验乃神，慎毋固必焉。

一值夏秋，而病患积寒内滞，还宜先事冬夏互行功诀。其行夏秋本法惟主气降，若行冬春，于气得降腹后，大加存运，能得腹暖，有气后攻，乃加翘臀，注穿尾闾，透脊达巅，以得汗透为得功。其后行功，但存忘热一诀。次第加减，即为互行功诀。若欲专行夏秋，必俟积寒尽出后乃可行焉。不尔，不惟无效，适能增病。

What has been described above is the constant method of practice, spoken for people without illness. If one is ill, there are other methods, which one must weigh and apply oneself. The general principle is that when practice fits the substance of the Dao and also fits the season, its effects are wondrous; beware of being fixed and absolute.

One case: it is summer or autumn, and the patient suffers from accumulated cold stagnating within. One should still first practice the formula of alternating winter and summer. The summer–autumn method proper makes the descent of breath its main concern; if one practices the winter–spring method, then after the breath has descended to the abdomen, one keeps and moves it vigorously, until the abdomen becomes warm and there is breath attacking backward; then one adds “raising the buttocks,” directing the attention through the Caudal Funnel, up the spine to the crown, and counts it a success when a thorough sweat is obtained. In practice after that, keep only the one formula of “forgetting the heat.” Increasing and reducing in this order is the formula of alternating practice. If one wishes to practice the summer–autumn method alone, one must wait until the accumulated cold has been fully expelled. Otherwise it is not only ineffective but may actually worsen the illness.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

汗透最要，只有大加存运才能汗透，汗不透则积寒不得解。忘热一诀指心静自然凉。

A thorough sweat is most important, and only vigorous keeping and moving can produce a thorough sweat; if the sweat does not come through thoroughly, the accumulated cold cannot be released. The formula of “forgetting the heat” means that when the mind is still, one is naturally cool.

§2A constitution with much phlegm
Original (Chinese)

一患素体多痰者，宜于夏令治之。其法先以顺气下降，得气既降，加行坠注功法。总以腹有气行，更得暖气后攻，方行翘臀等诀。行后得汗，倘不耐热，加行清降之功，以气降胸宽为度。既得气降心凉，立即嗣行神注命门，觉有气攻，加行翘臀等诀，其汗自透。于既透后，即续行清降法。如是循环，间行一七二七后，必易暖易升。犹须加坠腹注神等功。如是行去，其于升后，必多凉液自项自胸而下，切勿认作甘露，此乃泥精，经神烘活，又为炁御下降之物，不得脐轮大炼一番，犹能为祸。法当加意神注命门，得逢火发，热若炭炙，存而烁之，且缓行透后功法。惟循行忘热，加用真意，横栏臆下，禁炁上透，此为要嘱。如或疏虞致透，意用两目上视，兜引乾气降压，其炁便自回下，须急随其机，向后关注透，其炁必破关后透。仍以意引透至顶，又必大汗如雨。渐渐复自顶下降绛阙，倘得有一点滴下，其捷而极凉者，乃是真液。若有滴下，不捷亦不凉，尚是泥物所化，不是甘露。必行得有甘露，方为大效。然总以积阴尽除，再图后效是嘱。

One case: a person whose constitution has always had much phlegm is best treated in summer. The method: first let the breath descend smoothly; once the breath has descended, add the method of “weighting and pouring.” Always wait until there is breath moving in the abdomen and warm breath attacking backward before practicing the formulas of “raising the buttocks” and so on. If, having practiced them, one sweats and cannot bear the heat, add the work of “clearing and descending,” until the breath descends and the chest feels open. Once the breath has descended and the heart is cool, immediately go on to “pouring the spirit into the Gate of Life”; when one feels the breath attacking, add the formulas of raising the buttocks and so on, and the sweat will come through by itself. After it has come through, at once continue with the clearing-and-descending method. Circulating like this, practicing at intervals for seven or fourteen days, it will surely become easy to warm and easy to rise. One must still add the work of “weighting the abdomen” and “pouring the spirit.” Practicing like this, after the breath has risen, a great deal of cool fluid will certainly flow down from the neck and from the chest. On no account take it for sweet dew: this is “muddy essence,” which has been roused by the warming of the spirit and is being carried downward by the primordial breath; unless it is thoroughly refined at the navel wheel, it can still cause trouble. The method is to apply the intention to pour the spirit into the Gate of Life until the fire arises, hot as glowing charcoal, and to keep it there and melt it, while for the time being postponing the method of “passing through behind.” Simply follow “forgetting the heat,” add the True Intention, and set it crosswise below the chest to forbid the primordial breath to pass upward — this is an important instruction. If through a moment’s carelessness it does pass upward, use the intention to raise both eyes toward the crown and draw the breath of Qian (the head) round and down to press it, and the primordial breath will return downward by itself; then one must quickly follow its momentum and direct the attention to passing through behind, and the primordial breath will surely break through the pass and pass up the back. Still lead it with the intention up to the crown, and there will surely be a great sweat like rain. Gradually it descends again from the crown to the Crimson Gate; if a drop falls that is quick and extremely cool, that is the true fluid. If a drop falls that is neither quick nor cool, it is still something transformed from the muddy substance, not sweet dew. Only when one has obtained sweet dew is it a great effect. But always wait until the accumulated yin is completely removed before seeking further effects — this is the instruction.

Qiu Ling's noteon §2
Original note (Chinese)

夏令气机向外透，对素患多痰者，顺攻其内积随气外透，正是其时。

此段升降功法姿势须讲究。降法头颈定、腹直、胸带仰；而升法头颈带仰，胸背俱直。当凉液自项自胸而下，必须面带仰势。

坠腹功法乃从胸右降下，兜脐转左，中存推进下达，则痰自大便而出。注神功法乃自胸左降下，兜脐转右，中存推进，神附命门。盖左下法进火，右下法退符，各有妙义。

得到大汗如雨，乃体内积阴化汗而出，切勿以为是汗多亡阳。

In summer the movement of breath passes outward; for those who have long suffered from much phlegm, this is precisely the time to attack what has accumulated within, following the current, so that it passes out with the breath.

The posture for the rising and descending methods of this passage must be carefully observed. For the descending method, the head and neck are steady, the abdomen straight, the chest slightly leaning back; for the rising method, the head and neck are slightly raised and the chest and back both straight. When cool fluid flows down from the neck and chest, the face must be slightly raised.

The method of “weighting the abdomen” descends from the right side of the chest, curves round the navel to the left, keeps it in the middle, pushes it forward and down, and the phlegm will be expelled with the stool. The method of “pouring the spirit” descends from the left side of the chest, curves round the navel to the right, keeps it in the middle, pushes it forward, and lets the spirit attach itself to the Gate of Life. The left-descending method advances the fire, the right-descending method withdraws the response; each has its own wondrous meaning.

When a great sweat like rain is obtained, it is the accumulated yin within the body turning into sweat and coming out; on no account think that it is excessive sweating that exhausts the yang.

§3Inner heat with dryness
Original (Chinese)

一患内热发燥者，惟用降诀，或加行虚心功法，有食自消，再行引火归源，神注命门，得有气行下极，随机旋运，得有浊火下注，微以意送，放出狱门，随以两目上视泥丸，收回清气。门闭，乃由绛阙下注脐轮，透入祖窍，加以忘字诀守之，既得神凝息定，而胸膈必舒，病已去矣！乃可续行实腹，须兼神注命门，其腹自实。此后可行通督法。督通，则必有液下，而须细辨真凡，此亦要嘱。其降既真，方可循行炼精化炁、炼炁化神、炼神还虚等等功法。

One case: a person with inner heat and dryness should use only the “descending formula,” or add the method of “emptying the heart”; any accumulated food will then digest of itself. Then practice “leading the fire back to its source,” pouring the spirit into the Gate of Life; when there is breath moving to the Lower Pole, follow its momentum and turn it; when turbid fire pours downward, send it out lightly with the intention through the “prison gate,” then at once look up with both eyes to the Muddy Pellet and gather back the clear breath. When the gate is closed, pour it down from the Crimson Gate into the navel wheel and through into the ancestral opening, and guard it with the formula of “forgetting.” Once the spirit is concentrated and the breath settled, the chest and diaphragm will surely be at ease, and the illness is already gone! Only then may one go on to “filling the abdomen,” which must be combined with pouring the spirit into the Gate of Life; the abdomen will then fill of itself. After this one may practice the “method of opening the Governing vessel.” When the Governing vessel is open, there will surely be fluid descending, and one must carefully distinguish whether it is true or ordinary — this too is an important instruction. Only when what descends is truly genuine may one proceed in order to the methods of refining essence into breath, refining breath into spirit, refining spirit and returning to emptiness, and so on.

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

祖窍指脐轮后一寸三分处。细辨液之真凡，乃言行功已毕之后理当细辨之，若正值行功之际则切戒于中辨认。

The “ancestral opening” here means the point one cun three fen behind the navel wheel. “Carefully distinguish whether the fluid is true or ordinary” means that one should distinguish carefully after the practice is over; while one is actually practicing, on no account try to identify it.

§4Hot blood with stagnant breath; the chest and diaphragm stiff and full
Original (Chinese)

一患血热气滞，胸膈板满者，先行虚心法，继加退背功，便觉心后有炁二股，从心后际分达腰肾，热如汤注，渐觉此热绕兜脐轮，满腹大热，遍体微汗。斯时心地必觉松宽，是全赖退背一诀也。退背功法乃于阙盆以意后导。阙盆即绛阙化血之府，又名炁藏。有患络血狂吐者，亦须先行此诀，继行辟豁中关，而虚心功法寓焉。或自华池引降乾金，加行虚心功诀，厥病亦痊。统名夏秋功法。或谓俟腹大热，行加翘臀、达脊、抵脑等功法。俟炁达脑，乃大存之，加用返视泥丸，引此乾宫真炁，下会任督于华池，自得甘露满口，咽咽送至绛阙一蒸，乃达中黄，觉此绛阙清凉，而中黄温润。学者如此，加行溯古神功，是名开中关，又名辟混沌，丹书谓之开玄关。其法诀，从事忘忘而已。此关辟后，生药有地，藏药有所矣！得开真境惟何？宽广无涯，深邃如之。盖此一则，乃造命立命之真功，学者须宝志之。

One case: for a person with hot blood and stagnant breath, the chest and diaphragm stiff and full, first practice the “method of emptying the heart,” then add the “work of withdrawing to the back,” and one will feel two currents of primordial breath behind the heart, going from behind the heart separately to the lower back and kidneys, hot as poured broth; gradually one feels this heat curve round the navel wheel, the whole belly very hot and the whole body lightly sweating. At this time the mind will surely feel relaxed and open — this depends entirely on the formula of withdrawing to the back. The method of withdrawing to the back is to guide the breath backward with the intention at the “Gate Basin.” The Gate Basin is the storehouse of the Crimson Gate where fluid turns into blood, also called the “Store of Primordial Breath.” Those who suffer from bleeding of the network vessels and violent vomiting of blood must also first practice this formula, then practice “throwing open the middle pass,” within which the method of emptying the heart is contained. Or, drawing down the metal of Qian from the Flowery Pool and adding the formula of emptying the heart, the illness can also be cured. All these are called summer–autumn methods. Some say: when the belly is very hot, add the methods of raising the buttocks, reaching the spine and reaching the brain. When the primordial breath reaches the brain, keep it there at length, and add turning the gaze back to the Muddy Pellet, drawing this true primordial breath of the palace of Qian down to meet the Conception and Governing vessels at the Flowery Pool; naturally the mouth fills with sweet dew; swallow it mouthful by mouthful and send it to the Crimson Gate to be steamed, then on to the Central Yellow, and one feels the Crimson Gate cool and the Central Yellow warm and moist. When students reach this point and add the “divine work of tracing back to antiquity,” this is called “opening the middle pass,” also “splitting primal chaos”; the alchemical books call it “opening the Mysterious Pass.” Its formula is simply to engage in “forgetting forgetting.” Once this pass is open, the Medicine has a place to be born and a place to be stored! What is the true realm that opens like? Vast and boundless, and just as deep. This one rule is the true work of creating and establishing life; students must treasure it and remember it.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4
Original note (Chinese)

虚心法及退背法均属夏秋功法，主降，乃意存绛宫或过心后而下。虚心之诀，虚松其气机而已。所谓虚者，不滞不著，又不竟忘所在，若又忘其所事，则神安炁凝，气机松活灵圆。

中黄在心后夹脊关前夹缝中。此窍之气上通泥丸，下达尾闾，周流百节之处。古人谓为玄窍，学者行功到虚极静笃时，此窍乃现，自觉此中大无外细无内，皎皎明明，如月在水，胎息息于此，我身元神于此升降。此即溯古神功，又名开中关、开玄关。其真境即文中所言：宽广无涯，深邃如之。

The method of emptying the heart and the method of withdrawing to the back are both summer–autumn methods whose main concern is descending: the intention is kept at the Crimson Palace, or passes behind the heart and goes down. The formula of emptying the heart is simply to let the movement of breath become empty and loose. What is called “empty” is neither stagnating nor clinging, yet not entirely forgetting where it is; if one also forgets what one is doing, the spirit is at peace and the primordial breath congeals, and the movement of breath becomes loose, lively, numinous and round.

The Central Yellow is in the gap behind the heart and in front of the Spine-Straddling Pass. The breath of this opening reaches up to the Muddy Pellet and down to the Caudal Funnel; it is the place from which breath circulates through all the joints. The ancients called it the Mysterious Opening. Only when students reach utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness in practice does this opening appear; one feels it to be so vast it has no outside and so fine it has no inside, clear and bright, like the moon in water; embryonic breathing rests here, and the Original Spirit of my body rises and descends here. This is the “divine work of tracing back to antiquity,” also called “opening the middle pass,” “opening the Mysterious Pass.” Its true realm is what the text says: vast and boundless, and just as deep.

§5Accumulated yin turning into fire
Original (Chinese)

一患积阴化火，状若有余者，法惟先行夏秋功法，以治其标。病减，加行冬春。此指积阴未全化火者。若已全化火，法惟引火归源，而酌加夏秋降诀，助火归源，乃得济。若其积阴内滞，尚未火化者，诀惟内运真火，而外加擦耸，循环无间，庶几有效。俟腹知暖，再加透后功。盖以此中真气不旺，阳不胜阴，见效甚难，法惟外加擦耸，而内行闭息。俟其阳充，得有自透自攻真景，始用意逼破关。其阳已充，故能得透。若其积阴兼滞百络者，亦惟先行闭息，次加存运于坤腹，使其炁自透络，得徵通泰。法于此际，尚须加意存运，以培真火。何以故？百络向滞之物，得炁以烘，理必随炁而行，彙趋下极。则斯下极，群阴势旺，譬如盆火室中，围以冰墙，有暖不胜寒之象。法惟大加炭火，犹恐不济，故惟加炭功勤，不计时日，总以冰墙尽化，乃为大济。无如学者不悟，每撤废于斯时，古今不少。故法惟恒且勤，勤且勇，行效其然，不效亦然，则滞物虽夥，而尽化有期，何患终不大效哉。

旷观斯世之患斯症者，十有二三，大抵非商即儒。何以故？其症成于心劳身逸。盖心劳者神必衰，身不劳者气必滞。神衰气滞者，愈恶劳而喜逸，此阴寒积滞之由。欲愈斯症，须反素习。素习能反，厥病自痊，再求功法。此乃王道，愿者行之，如上所示，化功也。非大根器人，未有不半途而废。此八段、十二段、十六段、二十四段等等锦法，所以慈示也。盖此阴精，成于气滞，气滞则精泥血瘀，不得武功以荡以磨，万无见效之期。学者不察，竟弃勿事，非厌烦也，殆以伎小不屑为。噫，譬如登高必自卑，况夫等等武功，藉以治病，何尝取藉成道者哉！学者其省之。

One case: for a person whose accumulated yin has turned into fire, so that it looks like “excess,” the method is first to practice the summer–autumn method to treat the symptoms. When the illness lessens, add the winter–spring method. This refers to those whose accumulated yin has not yet entirely turned into fire. If it has entirely turned into fire, the only method is to lead the fire back to its source, adding the summer–autumn descending formula as appropriate to help the fire return to its source; then it will succeed. If the accumulated yin stagnates within and has not yet turned into fire, the only formula is to move the true fire within and add “rubbing and shrugging” without, circulating without interruption; then perhaps it will be effective. When the belly knows warmth, add the work of “passing through behind.” For at this time the true breath within is not strong, yang does not overcome yin, and results are very hard to see; the only method is to add rubbing and shrugging without and practice holding the breath within. When the yang is full and one has the true experience of it passing through and attacking by itself, only then use the intention to press it to break through the pass. Since the yang is already full, it can pass through. For those whose accumulated yin also stagnates in all the network vessels, again the only way is first to practice holding the breath, then add keeping and moving in the abdomen of Kun (the lower abdomen), so that the primordial breath passes into the network vessels by itself and there are signs of free flow. At this point one must still apply the intention to keep and move it, to cultivate the true fire. Why? The things that have long stagnated in all the network vessels, warmed by the primordial breath, must naturally move with it and gather at the Lower Pole. Then at this Lower Pole the host of yin is strong — like a basin of fire in a room surrounded by walls of ice, where warmth cannot overcome the cold. The only method is to add a great deal of charcoal, and even that may not be enough; so one must diligently keep adding charcoal, without counting the days, until the walls of ice have all melted — only that is a great success. Unfortunately students do not understand, and often give up just at this point; there have been many such people, past and present. So the only method is to be constant and diligent, diligent and bold, continuing in the same way whether there are results or not; then, however much has stagnated, there will come a day when it is all dissolved — why worry that there will be no great success in the end?

Looking broadly at the world, among those who suffer from this illness, two or three in ten are either merchants or scholars. Why? This illness arises from “the heart toiling while the body idles.” For when the heart toils, the spirit surely weakens; when the body does not labor, the breath surely stagnates. Those whose spirit is weak and breath stagnant hate labor and love ease all the more — this is the origin of the accumulation and stagnation of yin cold. To cure this illness, one must reverse one’s usual habits. If one’s usual habits can be reversed, the illness will heal of itself; then seek methods of practice. This is the kingly way; let those who are willing practice it as shown above — this is the work of transformation. Unless one is a person of great capacity, one is sure to give up halfway. That is why the brocade methods — the Eight Pieces, Twelve Pieces, Sixteen Pieces, Twenty-Four Pieces of Brocade and so on — were compassionately handed down. For this yin essence is formed by stagnation of breath; when the breath stagnates, essence becomes muddy and blood stagnant, and without the “martial exercises” to stir and grind it away, there will never be any result. Students who do not see this abandon them altogether — not because they find them troublesome, but probably because they think them petty skills beneath them. Alas! To climb high one must begin from below; moreover, these martial exercises are borrowed to treat illness — how could they be relied on to attain the Dao! Students, reflect on this.

Qiu Ling's noteon §5
Original note (Chinese)

上文所谓“擦耸”，即如擦腰肾及腹、腿、膝、足、手以及耸肩、撒腿、摆腰、扭颈、浴面、闭息之类。外加擦耸，内行闭息，有助于暖腹和后透，若欲破关，尚须于阳生时行功。此症得汗透数次而效，故须持之以恒，万勿半途而废。

“Rubbing and shrugging,” mentioned above, means movements such as rubbing the lower back and kidneys, the abdomen, legs, knees, feet and hands, as well as shrugging the shoulders, swinging the legs, swaying the waist, twisting the neck, “bathing the face” (rubbing the face) and holding the breath. Adding rubbing and shrugging without and practicing holding the breath within helps to warm the abdomen and let the breath pass through behind; if one wishes to break through the pass, one must also practice when yang is born. This illness responds after several thorough sweats, so one must persevere and on no account give up halfway.

Key Terms

  • Beware of being fixed and absolute (shen wu gu bi 慎毋固必): an echo of the Analects, “free of conjecture, dogmatism, obstinacy and egoism.”
  • Alternating winter and summer: using the winter–spring method and the summer–autumn method in alternation.
  • Raising the buttocks (qiao tun 翘臀): a formula not explained in detail in the text, used together with the breath “attacking backward” and “directing the attention through the Caudal Funnel”; see the Translator’s Notes.
  • Forgetting the heat: when the mind is still, one is naturally cool.
  • Weighting and pouring, weighting the abdomen, pouring the spirit: Qiu Ling’s note: weighting the abdomen descends from the right side of the chest and curves round the navel to the left; pouring the spirit descends from the left side of the chest, curves round the navel to the right, and attaches the spirit to the Gate of Life.
  • Clearing and descending: a method that makes the breath descend, the chest open and the heart cool.
  • Muddy essence (nijing 泥精): cool fluid flowing down from the neck and chest; not sweet dew, but turbid matter roused by the warming of the spirit and carried down by the primordial breath, which must be thoroughly refined at the navel wheel.
  • Set crosswise below the chest (heng lan yi xia 横栏臆下): using the True Intention as a barrier across the lower chest so that the primordial breath does not pass upward.
  • True fluid / Sweet Dew: what drips down quick and extremely cool is the true fluid; what is neither quick nor cool is “transformed from the muddy substance.”
  • Leading the fire back to its source: making the floating “empty” fire return to the lower origin.
  • Prison gate (yumen 狱门): the gate through which turbid matter is expelled (the lower orifices).
  • Ancestral opening: in this section, Qiu Ling’s note: the point one cun three fen behind the navel wheel (different from the ancestral opening behind the heart and in front of the Spine-Straddling Pass in Part Two, Chapter 10).
  • Method of emptying the heart: letting the movement of breath become empty and loose, “neither stagnating nor clinging, yet not entirely forgetting where it is.”
  • Work of withdrawing to the back: guiding the breath backward with the intention at the Gate Basin (the Crimson Gate).
  • Gate Basin / Store of Primordial Breath: the storehouse of the Crimson Gate where fluid turns into blood; see Crimson Palace.
  • Opening the middle pass / splitting primal chaos / opening the Mysterious Pass / divine work of tracing back to antiquity: opening the opening of the Central Yellow; see Mysterious Opening, Central Yellow.
  • Forgetting forgetting (wang wang 忘忘): forgetting even the forgetting.
  • Rubbing and shrugging (ca song 擦耸): Qiu Ling’s note: rubbing the lower back and kidneys, abdomen, legs, knees, feet and hands; shrugging the shoulders, swinging the legs, swaying the waist, twisting the neck, bathing the face, holding the breath, and the like.
  • The heart toiling while the body idles (xin lao shen yi 心劳身逸): a tired mind and an inactive body.
  • Eight Pieces of Brocade and other brocade methods: traditional guiding exercises in movement.
  • Martial exercises (wugong 武功): in this section, guiding exercises, massage and other exercises in movement (not martial arts).

Putting It into Practice

  1. See a doctor first, then practice. This section is the experience of the ancients, not a plan of diagnosis and treatment.
  2. The passage most worth remembering (the second paragraph of §5): many people’s illnesses “arise from the heart toiling while the body idles” — thinking much and moving little. The cure is first to “reverse one’s usual habits”: change this habit, move the body more, spend the mind less; then seek methods of practice.
  3. Exercises in movement are not petty skills: the text especially criticizes those who look down on “martial exercises” such as the Eight Pieces of Brocade — “to climb high one must begin from below.” Besides quiet sitting, doing some guiding exercises, self-massage, patting and joint movements every day is very helpful for people whose breath and blood do not flow freely, and is also the safest.
  4. Two practical little formulas:
    • “Forgetting the heat”: when you feel dry heat and discomfort during practice, let the mind become still and the heat will subside by itself.
    • “Emptying the heart”: when the chest feels oppressed and stiff, let it become empty and loose — neither stagnating nor clinging, yet not entirely forgetting.
  5. Distinguish the fluid after practice: Qiu Ling specially reminds us that whether the fluid in the mouth or chest is true or ordinary should be distinguished after practice is over, not during it.
  6. On “a great sweat like rain,” “breaking through the pass” and the like: these are forceful methods the ancients used to treat illness; please do not imitate them on your own. After heavy sweating, keep warm and drink water; they are unsuitable for those with a weak constitution.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Raising the buttocks”: neither the text nor Qiu Ling’s note explains how it is done; from the context, it is a bodily posture or way of applying effort used when the breath attacks backward and passes through the Caudal Funnel. The original name is kept without speculation.
  2. “Practicing at intervals for seven or fourteen days” (jian xing yi qi er qi 间行一七二七): yi qi is seven days, er qi fourteen days.
  3. “Draw the breath of Qian round and down to press it”: the “breath of Qian” is the clear breath of the head (the palace of Qian).
  4. “Those who suffer from bleeding of the network vessels and violent vomiting of blood”: this refers to bleeding with uncontrollable vomiting of blood. This is an emergency that requires immediate medical care. This is the translator’s remark.
  5. “That is why the brocade methods … were compassionately handed down”: that is, these brocade methods (guiding exercises in movement) were compassionately transmitted for precisely this purpose; the translation follows this sense.
  6. “Probably because they think them petty skills beneath them” (dai yi ji xiao bu xie wei 殆以伎小不屑为): ji 伎 is used for ji 技, “skill.”

2 · The Method for Winter and Spring

2 · The Method for Winter and Spring — the breath should pass through behind

About This Chapter

In winter and spring the movement of breath should “pass through behind” — rise up the back. The route: from the Lower Pole (the perineum) the breath passes through the Caudal Funnel, rises to the Spine-Straddling Pass, passes the Jade Pillow, enters the Muddy Pellet, pauses briefly and descends again; from the Flowery Pool (the mouth) it goes down the Twelve-Storied Tower, stops for a moment at the Crimson Palace and passes the heart; or, behind the heart, it divides into two paths to the lower back and kidneys, pauses briefly, and the warm breath is felt to curve round toward the abdomen on each side and converge at the navel wheel, where it must be “kept at length”; then with the intention it is poured into the Gate of Life, where it must be “kept for a long time”; then it passes down through the Caudal Funnel again and rises, descends and is kept as before. Starting from the Caudal Funnel and returning to it is one circuit. Qiu Ling’s note explains the reason for each stop: pausing briefly at the Muddy Pellet lets the warm breath turn into fluid; the Flowery Pool is where the Conception and Governing vessels meet; the Crimson Palace is the storehouse of breath; pausing briefly at the lower back and kidneys replenishes the kidneys; keeping at length at the navel wheel is refining fluid into breath; keeping for a long time at the Gate of Life is refining breath into primordial breath. He also points out that the Muddy Pellet, the Crimson Palace and the Gate of Life are the three key places of this method.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

原夫冬春，气机贵后透。法自下极气穿尾闾，上夹脊，透玉枕，入泥丸，略存而降，又自华池下重楼，由绛宫一停，乃过心。或经心后分两路达下腰肾，又略存之，觉此暖炁，各向腹兜，环拱至脐轮，须大存之，乃以意注命门，又须久存。然后下穿尾闾，如前法升而降而存。按功诀，谓以未穿尾闾为一周天。古哲题为冬春功法。

In winter and spring, the movement of breath should pass through behind. The method: from the Lower Pole the breath passes through the Caudal Funnel, rises to the Spine-Straddling Pass, passes the Jade Pillow and enters the Muddy Pellet; keep it there briefly, then let it descend; from the Flowery Pool it goes down the Twelve-Storied Tower, stops for a moment at the Crimson Palace, and then passes the heart. Or, behind the heart, it divides into two paths down to the lower back and kidneys; keep it there briefly too, and you will feel this warm breath curving round toward the abdomen on each side and converging at the navel wheel; there you must keep it at length. Then pour it with the intention into the Gate of Life, and there you must keep it for a long time. Then it passes down through the Caudal Funnel again and, as before, rises, descends and is kept. According to the formula, returning to the point before passing through the Caudal Funnel is one circuit. The ancient sages called this “the method for winter and spring.”

Qiu Ling's noteon §1
Original note (Chinese)

自下极气穿三关，所以在泥丸略存（停），因泥丸又名髓海，略存便使暖气达脑而化液，有如甘露而降。到华池（此处指口内）又须略停，因此穴为任、督脉会宫。绛宫乃藏气之府，古人认为所降之液，可在此化为血。

另途在绛宫一停后，乃经心后退降腰肾，略存便可使腰肾得补，而肾气充足。

至脐轮须大存，乃炼液化气之妙用。到命门（此处指在脐轮后一寸三分处）则须久存，乃炼气化炁之妙用。

此段功法，以泥丸、绛宫、命门三处为要地，凡人于此三处能著意，则精、气、神充足。盖但水性润下，火性炎上，今欲使水上升，火下降，则非运用真意以指挥之不可。既上有泥丸布下真阴以覆之，中有华池、绛宫神水以润之，下有命门之火不或熄，此天造地设自然真境，提供了真意维持之条件。

按此功法，从穿尾闾起手，又回到尾闾，已满一周天。

The breath passes through the three passes from the Lower Pole. The reason for pausing briefly at the Muddy Pellet is that the Muddy Pellet is also called the “Sea of Marrow”; a brief pause lets the warm breath reach the brain and turn into fluid, which descends like sweet dew. At the Flowery Pool (here meaning the inside of the mouth) one must again pause briefly, because this point is the meeting place of the Conception and Governing vessels. The Crimson Palace is the storehouse of breath; the ancients held that the descending fluid can turn into blood here.

On the other path, after stopping for a moment at the Crimson Palace, the breath withdraws behind the heart down to the lower back and kidneys; a brief pause there replenishes the lower back and kidneys, and the kidney breath becomes abundant.

At the navel wheel one must keep it at length; this is the wonderful function of refining fluid into breath. At the Gate of Life (here meaning the point one cun three fen behind the navel wheel) one must keep it for a long time; this is the wonderful function of refining breath into primordial breath.

This method takes three places as key: the Muddy Pellet, the Crimson Palace and the Gate of Life. If a person can apply the intention at these three places, essence, breath and spirit will be abundant. For the nature of water is to moisten downward and the nature of fire is to flare upward; to make water rise and fire descend, one must use the True Intention to direct them. Since above there is the Muddy Pellet spreading true yin down to cover, in the middle there is the divine water of the Flowery Pool and the Crimson Palace to moisten, and below there is the fire of the Gate of Life, which never goes out, this natural, true realm, made by Heaven and set by Earth, provides the conditions for the True Intention to be maintained.

In this method, starting from passing through the Caudal Funnel and returning to the Caudal Funnel completes one Celestial Circuit.

Key Terms

  • Passing through behind: the breath passing upward along the back (the Governing vessel).
  • Lower Pole: the perineum area; see Perineum.
  • Three Passes: the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Jade Pillow.
  • Sea of Marrow: another name for the Muddy Pellet (Chinese medicine calls the brain the sea of marrow).
  • Flowery Pool: Qiu Ling’s note: here the inside of the mouth, where the Conception and Governing vessels meet.
  • Twelve-Storied Tower: the throat.
  • Crimson Palace: the storehouse of breath; the ancients held that the descending fluid turns into blood here.
  • Keeping briefly, keeping at length, keeping for a long time: stops of increasing length.
  • Gate of Life: in this part, the point one cun three fen behind the navel wheel, that is, the lower Cinnabar Field (not the Gate of Life of Chinese medicine between the two kidneys).
  • Refining fluid into breath, refining breath into primordial breath: Qiu Ling’s note: keeping at length at the navel wheel is refining fluid into breath; keeping for a long time at the Gate of Life is refining breath into primordial breath.
  • Celestial Circuit: in this section, setting out from the Caudal Funnel and returning to it is one circuit.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The direction for winter and spring is “rising behind”: in the cold seasons the movement of breath should go up the back, helping the yang breath to grow.
  2. Three key places: the Muddy Pellet (the crown), the Crimson Palace (the chest), the Gate of Life (the lower Cinnabar Field behind the navel). A little attention at these three places makes essence, breath and spirit easily abundant.
  3. The stops differ in length: the Muddy Pellet, the Flowery Pool, the Crimson Palace and the lower back and kidneys are only “kept briefly”; the navel wheel is “kept at length,” the Gate of Life “kept for a long time” — the center of gravity is below. This is especially important for people who easily get heated or have a swollen head.
  4. Saliva in the mouth: when the breath descends to the Flowery Pool (the mouth) there will be saliva; swallow it and send it to the Crimson Palace (the chest).
  5. Do not push hard: this is a route guided by the “True Intention”; the intention must be light. If you cannot manage it, simply guard the navel wheel and the Gate of Life below — that is also very good work.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Returning to the point before passing through the Caudal Funnel is one circuit”: that is, returning to the starting point before passing through the Caudal Funnel counts as one circuit, the same meaning as Qiu Ling’s note, “starting from passing through the Caudal Funnel and returning to the Caudal Funnel completes one Celestial Circuit.”
  2. “For the nature of water …” (gai dan shuixing run xia 盖但水性润下): gai dan together means “because.”
  3. The “Gate of Life” in this section follows this book’s usage (one cun three fen behind the navel wheel), which differs from the Gate of Life point of Chinese medicine (below the spinous process of the second lumbar vertebra); keep this in mind when reading.

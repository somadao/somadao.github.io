6 · The Method for the Zi and Wu Hours

6 · The Method for the Zi and Wu Hours — the living zi, the living wu, and “the cultivation of nature is the city wall that protects life”

About This Chapter

This section has two passages, dealing with the “living zi” and the “living wu,” the terms most often used and most often left vague in the alchemical books.

The living zi: the winter and summer solstices and the zi and wu of the year, month, day and hour are all “fixed zi and wu”; only the zi and wu of the two double-hours within a day are the “living zi and wu” of the human body — yet even this is a way of speaking that the alchemical books are forced to use, namely “the first stirring of the one yang.” The true living zi must be sought in what has no form and no image: when the work reaches “stillness with nothing left to be still,” one suddenly feels as if the mechanism within had given a little start — this is the beginning of the living zi; then the mechanism appears vigorously — this is the true appearance of the living zi; it passes inward abundantly and is about to reach the lower body — this is the inner primordial breath of the living zi becoming full. At this point concentrate it with the Celestial Eye, and the primordial breath rises by itself along the Governing vessel to the crown … this is “taking from Kan to fill Li.” If the outer kidney is already aroused and a thought arises, one must quickly lead it back with the intention, up through the back to the crown, and keep it in the cauldron of Qian. This passage also speaks specially of the scenes that may appear at this time (golden splendor, mirages, even “lewd scenes”); the method in every case is “set it aside and do not cling,” drawing the gaze and the hearing back into the place where nothing is seen and nothing heard.

The living wu: the living wu is in fact what follows the living zi. The author says that the ancients began practice from the living zi, while Shen Taixu’s teaching emphasizes the living wu; seeing the “golden world” is the supreme true state of the living wu. At this point one should collect it with the method of the three contemplations of the Scripture of Purity and Stillness. Then comes the most candid passage of the section: at this time the two primordial breaths are knotting together, and the greatest fear is that they become tainted with sexual desire — once ordinary passion stirs, the two breaths “fight and devour each other, the passions run loose,” the body trembles, the breathing becomes coarse, the lower body becomes hot, and there is “the calamity of being almost unable to renew oneself.” The text says that the ancients did not dare to reveal this point because very few students still have a virgin body. Qiu Ling’s note says that many alchemical books speak of the living zi but very few of the living wu; this part describes the method of the living wu in great detail, which “is very rare.”

Note (translator): This section deals with sexual arousal arising during cultivation and describes illusory scenes such as “lewd scenes.” These are phenomena of cultivation faithfully recorded in the old alchemical books, and we translate the text as it stands. The text’s attitude is very clear: beware of being drawn by passion, set it aside and do not cling, and take the cultivation of nature (the stability of the mind) as the city wall that protects life.

Original Text and Translation

§1The living *zi*
Original (Chinese)

原夫子午，子有活子，午有活午。岁之二至，乃天地之活子午。运年、月、日、时，乃天地之定子午。即人而论，天地之二至，运年、月、日之子午，皆属定子午。惟二时之子午，乃人身之活子午也。然犹属丹书不得已示人之活子午，所谓一阳初动之活子是也。究其真正之活子午，犹有辨焉。其真正者，须于无形无象中求之。

If we look into zi and wu: zi has a living zi, and wu has a living wu. The two solstices of the year are the living zi and wu of Heaven and Earth. The year, month, day and hour in their course are the fixed zi and wu of Heaven and Earth. Speaking of humans, the two solstices of Heaven and Earth and the zi and wu of the year, month and day in their course all belong to the fixed zi and wu. Only the zi and wu of the two double-hours are the living zi and wu of the human body. Yet even these are the living zi and wu that the alchemical books are forced to teach — the so-called living zi of “the first stirring of the one yang.” If we look into the truly living zi and wu, there is still a distinction. The true ones must be sought in what has no form and no image.

§2
Original (Chinese)

其说惟何？乃于功到寂无所寂，忽觉内机有若得得焉，此是活子之初。继觉勃然机现，乃是活子正象。油然内透，将达男根，已是活子内炁充盈。法用天目凝之，其炁自循督脉逆上昆仑。微以意留，觉此髓海波宁，油然下注，华池风生，彙临绛阙。斯时天君泰定，万国咸安，是名取坎填离。

What does this mean? When the work reaches stillness with nothing left to be still, one suddenly feels as if the mechanism within had given a little start — this is the beginning of the living zi. Then one feels the mechanism appear vigorously — this is the true appearance of the living zi. It passes inward abundantly and is about to reach the male root — this is already the inner primordial breath of the living zi becoming full. The method is to concentrate it with the Celestial Eye, and the primordial breath rises by itself along the Governing vessel against the current up to Kunlun (the crown). Keep it there slightly with the intention, and one feels the waves in the Sea of Marrow (the brain) become calm; it pours down abundantly, a wind rises in the Flowery Pool, and it gathers at the Crimson Gate. At this time the Heavenly Lord (the heart) is serene and settled, and all the states are at peace — this is called “taking from Kan to fill Li.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

若至外肾已举，更值念生，斯时外肾必大举。古哲于此，急以意引回，乃循海底，逆透上巅，存于乾鼎，勿忘勿助，气得髓涵；自化玉露，油然注池，下降阙盆。露得盆存，自化赤液，分注心后。得遇坎阳应升而上者，另有一种春深趣味得尝。但可领会，切戒情牵。倘一心动，急引乾宫真炁降压，立自新清。或以意包现象，敛下下田，大煅一番，引其后透达巅，存于泥丸，大行淘汰。所谓淘汰者，置此见见闻闻于意外耳。

If the outer kidney (the penis) is already aroused and a thought happens to arise, the outer kidney will certainly be fully aroused. At this point the ancient sages quickly led it back with the intention along the Sea Bottom (the perineum), up through the back to the crown, and kept it in the cauldron of Qian (the Muddy Pellet), neither forgetting nor forcing. The breath is nourished by the marrow and naturally turns into jade dew, which pours abundantly into the Flowery Pool and descends to the Gate Basin (the Crimson Gate). When the dew is kept in the Gate Basin, it naturally turns into red fluid, which pours separately behind the heart. When it meets the yang of Kan rising as it should, there is another kind of deep spring-like delight to taste. It may only be experienced; beware of being drawn by passion. If the heart stirs once, quickly draw down the true primordial breath of the palace of Qian (the head) to press it, and at once one is fresh and clear again. Or wrap up the appearing phenomena with the intention, gather them down into the lower field and smelt them thoroughly, lead them through the back to the crown, keep them in the Muddy Pellet, and carry out a great “sifting.” What is called sifting is simply placing all this seeing and hearing outside the intention.

§4
Original (Chinese)

已而华池液涌，咽咽咽下，觉此绛阙，金碧辉煌，旋更宽广无涯，现有海市蜃楼气局，而有乍远乍近情形。学者如此，始悉性功为保命之郛墉也。法惟置而勿著，否必现有淫席，荡吾心志。法惟以意一包一敛，置勿之审，急将目光耳神，敛入无见无闻之处，如是一存，即造身世咸忘之境。已复觉有氤氲气象现于湛如寂如之中，法亦置而勿问，则又造夫人法双忘。到此地位，忽觉有身，乃以意审窍中窍而止。丹书名曰采药入炉，又曰活子行功，其效乃尔。

Soon fluid wells up in the Flowery Pool; swallow it mouthful by mouthful, and one feels the Crimson Gate resplendent with gold and jade, then wider and boundless, with an atmosphere like a mirage over the sea, now far, now near. When students reach this point, only then do they fully understand that the cultivation of nature is the city wall that protects life. The only method is to set it aside and not cling; otherwise lewd scenes will surely appear and shake my resolve. The only method is to wrap and gather it with the intention, set it aside without examining it, and quickly draw the gaze and the spirit of hearing back into the place where nothing is seen and nothing heard. Keeping it like this, one reaches the state where body and world are both forgotten. Soon one again feels a rich, dense atmosphere appearing within the limpid stillness; the method again is to set it aside and not ask about it, and then one reaches the state where self and method are both forgotten. At this point, when one suddenly feels that one has a body again, examine the “opening within the opening” with the intention, and stop. The alchemical books call this “collecting the Medicine and putting it into the furnace,” also “practicing at the living zi”; its effects are like this.

§5
Original (Chinese)

第味丹书，其说吞吐，广设譬喻，未径直陈，似属运心未溥。岂知活午用验，具于活子言下，咸备述有。此盖古哲令由不令知耳。然足误事。故此一节，翻覆示以觉诀，而功采功取，功炼功凝，井然内具。若照丹书所示，外肾无念而举，洵是活子阳生，然须加行引提到脑，脑中一存，引下中黄，方始事采，而用加无念一诀，再示封炉。于中手法，较诸师示，作用详减不符，而说法仅完活子功诀。非也。丹书所言活子后事，学者自失体认，而自泥一见，是于愈行愈惑，卒有望洋之叹！

But when one savors the alchemical books, their way of speaking is halting, they set up many metaphors and do not state things directly, as if their care were not complete. Who would have known that the use and effects of the living wu are all contained in what they say about the living zi, fully set out? This is probably because the ancient sages “let people follow it but did not let them know why.” But it is enough to lead people astray. So this section repeatedly shows the formula of awareness, and the work of collecting, taking, refining and congealing is all contained within it in good order. According to what the alchemical books show, when the outer kidney is aroused without any thought, that is truly the birth of yang at the living zi; but one must still add “leading it up to the brain,” keep it in the brain a moment, lead it down to the Central Yellow, and only then begin to collect, adding the formula of “no-thought,” and then the instruction to “seal the furnace.” The hand-methods in this, compared with what various teachers show, do not agree in detail, and the explanation covers only the formula of the living zi — this is not so. The alchemical books do speak of what comes after the living zi; it is students who have failed to experience it and cling to a single view, so that the more they practice the more confused they become, and in the end can only sigh before the vast sea!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

此段言活子功法甚详，分为活子之初、活子正象及活子内炁充盈三段，特别指出炁将达男根，即为活子内炁充盈阶段，使学者行功有所依循。法用天目凝之，天目在两眉之间，若已练得天目开启当用天目；若天目未开启，当运用两目神光凝之。炁循督脉逆上泥丸，如觉波宁风生，乃情归性初之验，此乃得上乘功夫；如略有此作用，为中乘功夫；一无作用，则为下乘。后觉绛宫有金碧辉煌等气象，即古称黄金世界，乃上上真境。

This passage describes the method of the living zi in great detail, dividing it into three stages — the beginning of the living zi, its true appearance, and the fullness of its inner primordial breath — and points out specifically that when the primordial breath is about to reach the male root, that is the stage of the fullness of the inner primordial breath of the living zi, giving students something to go by in their practice. The method is to concentrate it with the Celestial Eye. The Celestial Eye is between the eyebrows: if one has already opened the Celestial Eye through practice, one should use it; if it has not opened, one should concentrate it with the spiritual light of the two eyes. When the primordial breath rises along the Governing vessel to the Muddy Pellet, if one feels the waves calm (in the Sea of Marrow) and the wind rising (in the Flowery Pool), that is the sign of “passion returning to the original nature,” and one has obtained superior work; if there is only a little of this effect, it is middling work; if there is none at all, it is inferior. Afterward one feels an atmosphere of golden splendor in the Crimson Palace, which the ancients called the “golden world”; it is the supreme true state.

§6The living *wu*
Original (Chinese)

盖其所谓活午者，核即古哲活子后事，并非别开生面也。第古哲之行功，起自活子，而太虚说法，重在活午。味其创申十二时诀，谓到现有黄金世界，此正活午上上真境。功宜事采，究其采诀，谓用《清静经》三观功法。其最上者，从事无无，而又不住于空寂，及其归缩仍寄于无。如是循环，炼至聚则成形，散则成炁而止。其中功法，有可揭示者，第宜会而体之，密而持之。一若按图而索骥，实复得鱼而忘筌。

What is called the living wu, when examined, is simply what the ancient sages called the things after the living zi; it is not something new. Only, the ancient sages began their practice from the living zi, while Shen Taixu’s teaching puts its emphasis on the living wu. Savoring the formula of the twelve double-hours that he set out, he says that the appearance of the golden world is precisely the supreme true state of the living wu. At this point the work should be “collecting,” and the formula for collecting is said to be the method of the three contemplations of the Scripture of Purity and Stillness. The highest of these engages in “non-being of non-being,” yet does not abide in empty stillness; and when gathering back, still entrusts itself to non-being. Circulating like this, one refines until “when it gathers it takes form, when it disperses it becomes primordial breath,” and stops there. Of the methods involved, those that can be revealed should only be understood and experienced, kept secret and held to — as if looking for a horse by its picture, yet in truth, having caught the fish, forgetting the trap.

§7
Original (Chinese)

其则惟何？学者如得见黄金世界，急起身后无上灵炁，透至极上极远，光不照到处落下，统将灵境一罩，敛成黍米，或由宝瓶吸入，或以意收入腹，或乘罩劲，由我身前极远处，兜下极深，透上极高，下瞩灵境，有我色身，坐立焰中，急存忘热一诀，顿觉色身熔化，惟见光明，急以意收意敛，转见一珠如豆，悬于太虚，急以意收，安于虚寂玄窍之中，仍得湛如寂如玄境而止。

What is its rule? If students see the golden world, quickly raise the supreme numinous primordial breath behind the body and send it up to the highest and farthest point; let it come down where the light does not reach and cover the whole numinous scene with it, gathering it into a grain of millet. Either draw it in through the “precious vase,” or gather it into the belly with the intention, or, riding the force of the covering, from a point very far in front of me, curve down to the very depths and pass up to the very heights, and look down on the numinous scene: there is my physical body sitting or standing in flames. Quickly keep the formula of “forgetting the heat,” and at once one feels the physical body melt away and sees only brightness. Quickly gather it in and draw it together with the intention, and one sees instead a pearl like a bean hanging in the Great Void. Quickly gather it in with the intention and settle it in the empty, still Mysterious Opening, and again obtain the mysterious state of limpid stillness, and stop there.

§8
Original (Chinese)

或于灵境现时，微用真意，摄此灵境纳向身后，默用提诀，由身后提，加用两眼上视泥丸，觉我泥丸，真炁氤氲，仍以意引下注，觉有炁溢一境，恍见池水盈盈，此则已到华池，便有玉液金液沛注，咽咽咽下，由重楼抵绛阙，自得无上清凉。继复下注中黄，另有一番趣味，而遍体充和。

Or, when the numinous scene appears, use a little True Intention to draw this numinous scene in toward the back of the body, silently use the formula of “lifting,” lifting it from behind the body, and add looking up with both eyes to the Muddy Pellet; one feels the true primordial breath of my Muddy Pellet rich and dense. Still lead it down with the intention, and one feels the primordial breath overflowing a realm, and dimly sees pool water brimming — this is already the Flowery Pool, and jade liquid and golden liquid pour down abundantly. Swallow them mouthful by mouthful, through the Twelve-Storied Tower to the Crimson Gate, and one naturally obtains supreme coolness. Then pour it on down into the Central Yellow; there is another kind of delight, and the whole body is full and harmonious.

§9
Original (Chinese)

此时觉有二炁左右盘旋，又有一炁上升，一炁下降，一旦针锋准对，乃有上就下迎，又复此追彼退，此退彼追，更有相纽莫放之情。斯时切戒贪著，戒动凡思。略染夫情，则此二炁战吞情肆，三家纯化后天，体必发颤，呼吸必粗，外肾火热，便有万难自新之厄。此时救法，惟凭两目上视，引降真阴以压情焰。然此至宝已化后天，法惟大集真火，下极猛烹而猛煅之，令其重透尾闾，达泥丸，下重楼，下绛阙，得大清新一番。否则此宝不得复原。

At this time one feels two primordial breaths circling left and right, and one breath rising and one descending. As soon as they meet exactly, point to point, there is the upper coming down and the lower rising to meet it; and again this one pursuing and that one retreating, that one retreating and this one pursuing, and moreover a tendency to knot together and not let go. At this time beware of greed and clinging; beware of stirring ordinary thoughts. If one is tainted even slightly by passion, these two breaths will fight and devour each other, the passions will run loose, the three families will all turn into Posterior Heaven; the body will surely tremble, the breathing will surely become coarse, the outer kidney will become hot as fire, and there will be the calamity of being almost unable to renew oneself. The only rescue at this time is to rely on looking up with both eyes and drawing down true yin to press down the flames of passion. But this supreme treasure has already turned into Posterior Heaven; the only method is to gather a great deal of true fire and cook and smelt it fiercely at the Lower Pole, so that it passes through the Caudal Funnel again, reaches the Muddy Pellet, descends the Twelve-Storied Tower and the Crimson Gate, and is thoroughly refreshed. Otherwise this treasure cannot be restored.

§10
Original (Chinese)

故古哲于此一节，不敢泄漏，盖以学者极少童真，其身情窦，既已开破，功行到此，如何不动情思？且此一径，乃是熟路，欲不夺关而出，难乎其难。惟彼童真，此窦未启，此事不知，行功到此，不过觉大春生，而神机上透，自不下达，万无此变。古哲性功淳洳，功行至此，急引真阴以压浊火，赖此绛阙清凉，化炁下凝，藏于炁穴，以意封之，湛寂片刻而止。

That is why the ancient sages did not dare to reveal this point: very few students have a virgin body. Since the opening of passion in their bodies has already been broken, how could they not have amorous thoughts when the practice reaches this point? Moreover, this path is a well-trodden one; to keep it from forcing the pass and going out is difficult in the extreme. Only those with a virgin body, whose opening of passion has not yet been opened and who know nothing of this matter, when they reach this point, merely feel a great spring arising, and the spiritual mechanism passes upward and naturally does not go down; there is no chance of this change happening to them. The ancient sages, whose cultivation of nature was pure and deep, when their practice reached this point, quickly drew down true yin to press down the turbid fire, relied on the coolness of the Crimson Gate to transform the primordial breath and let it congeal downward, stored it in the Cavity of Breath, sealed it with the intention, remained limpid and still for a moment, and stopped.

§11
Original (Chinese)

大凡功从活午入手者，乾宫为至要之功，淘此炁机，下注华池，灌夫绛阙，活子到来，但凭神审。子午会交，惟凭性靖，功以终造清新为合度。若从活子入手者，坤腹为至要之功，炼此液气，上达泥丸，下灌华池，清乎绛阙。活午到来，切戒情漏。午子会交，亦凭性靖，以终造充和为合度。若夫闭目内观，自有种种灵境得现，法贵无著，逐步进功，而移步换影，境不胜述，法惟毋住焉而已。及至炁到坤住，总以得暖为功。俟炁后透，达巅降阙，总以得凉为功。大旨如此，惟能内观不二而一者，全以神体而神会之。此天仙功法，能者从之。

In general, for those whose work begins from the living wu, the palace of Qian (the head) is the most essential work: sift this movement of primordial breath, pour it down into the Flowery Pool, irrigate the Crimson Gate; when the living zi comes, rely only on the spirit to examine it. When zi and wu meet, rely only on the stillness of nature; the work is in proper measure when it ends in clarity and freshness. For those who begin from the living zi, the abdomen of Kun is the most essential work: refine this fluid and breath, send it up to the Muddy Pellet, irrigate the Flowery Pool below, and cool the Crimson Gate. When the living wu comes, beware above all of passion leaking. When wu and zi meet, again rely on the stillness of nature; the work is in proper measure when it ends in fullness and harmony. As for closing the eyes and contemplating within, all kinds of numinous scenes will naturally appear; the method values not clinging, advancing step by step; with each step the scenery changes, and the states are too many to describe — the only method is not to abide. When the primordial breath comes to rest in the abdomen of Kun, always take warmth as the sign of success. When the primordial breath passes through behind, reaches the crown and descends to the Gate, always take coolness as the sign of success. The main point is this. Only those who can “contemplate within, not two but one” fully experience and understand it with the spirit. This is the method of the celestial immortals; let those who can, follow it.

Qiu Ling's noteon §6–§11
Original note (Chinese)

丹书言活子功法详者多，言活午功法详者极少，此篇言活午功法甚详，列举黄金世界等种种化景及其相应功法，殊为难得。此乃古人经历实录，学者虽非按图索骥，亦可供参考和引证。此乃高一层功法，更要求能内观不二而一，二乃指两目，一则指天目。若天目已开，闭目内观，自有种种化景，故言能者从之。

古人修炼每得于活子，而失于活午，盖性功未足，性为情移，杂念骤起，弃而勿坐，坐而勿制，制而勿定，皆为失手。时到正午，无奇不现，种种化景令人顾惑，偶一情系，便为自误。

Many alchemical books describe the method of the living zi in detail, but very few describe the method of the living wu in detail. This part describes the method of the living wu very fully, listing the golden world and other transformed scenes together with the corresponding methods — this is very rare. It is a true record of the ancients’ own experience; although students cannot look for the horse by its picture, it can serve for reference and confirmation. This is a higher level of method, which further requires being able to “contemplate within, not two but one”: “two” means the two eyes, “one” means the Celestial Eye. If the Celestial Eye has opened, closing the eyes and contemplating within, all kinds of transformed scenes will naturally appear; that is why it says “let those who can, follow it.”

In their cultivation the ancients often succeeded at the living zi and failed at the living wu, because their cultivation of nature was insufficient and their nature was moved by passion: stray thoughts arise suddenly; giving up and no longer sitting, sitting but unable to subdue them, subduing them but unable to settle — all of these are failures. When the time reaches the true wu, every kind of marvel appears, and all kinds of transformed scenes make people linger and be confused; if one is caught by passion even once, one has led oneself astray.

Key Terms

  • Fixed zi and wu / living zi and wu: the zi and wu of the year, month, day and hour of Heaven and Earth are “fixed”; the first stirring of the one yang in the human body is “living.”
  • Living Zi Hour: the true living zi: “when the work reaches stillness with nothing left to be still, one suddenly feels as if the mechanism within had given a little start.”
  • Beginning of the living zi, true appearance of the living zi, fullness of the inner primordial breath of the living zi: the three stages of the living zi (Qiu Ling’s note).
  • Living wu: what follows the living zi, with the “golden world” as its supreme true state.
  • Male root / outer kidney: the penis.
  • Celestial Eye: between the eyebrows; Qiu Ling’s note: those whose Celestial Eye has not opened use the spiritual light of the two eyes instead.
  • Kunlun, cauldron of Qian: the Muddy Pellet at the crown.
  • Sea of Marrow: the brain.
  • Flowery Pool: the mouth.
  • Gate Basin: the Crimson Gate (inside the chest).
  • Taking from Kan to fill Li: see conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Deep spring-like delight: a pleasure like deep spring; the text requires that it “may only be experienced; beware of being drawn by passion.”
  • Sifting (taotai 淘汰): placing all seeing and hearing outside the intention.
  • City wall (fuyong 郛墉): outer city and city wall, a metaphor for protection; “the cultivation of nature is the city wall that protects life.”
  • Lewd scenes (yinxi 淫席): licentious illusory scenes.
  • Body and world both forgotten, self and method both forgotten: two levels of forgetting.
  • Opening within the opening: see Mysterious Opening.
  • Collecting the Medicine and putting it into the furnace / practicing at the living zi: see Medicine.
  • “Let people follow it but did not let them know why”: an echo of the Analects, “the people can be made to follow it, but cannot be made to understand it.”
  • Golden world: the state of golden splendor in the Crimson Palace; Qiu Ling’s note: the supreme true state.
  • The three contemplations of the Scripture of Purity and Stillness: contemplating the mind within, contemplating the form without, contemplating things afar.
  • Non-being of non-being (wu wu 无无): even non-being is not.
  • “When it gathers it takes form, when it disperses it becomes primordial breath”: see Holy Embryo.
  • Having caught the fish, forgetting the trap: a metaphor for not clinging to the method itself.
  • Precious vase: not explained in the text; see the Translator’s Notes.
  • The two breaths knotting together: the two breaths coming down and rising to meet, pursuing and retreating.
  • Fighting and devouring, the passions running loose.
  • Three families: essence, breath and spirit.
  • Virgin body (tongzhen 童真).
  • Contemplating within, not two but one: Qiu Ling’s note: “two” means the two eyes, “one” the Celestial Eye.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The true living zi comes after “stillness with nothing left to be still”: it is not a matter of looking for some reaction in the body, but of a little stirring arising naturally within after stillness has reached its extreme. First there is stillness; only then is there a living zi.
  2. When sexual arousal appears (the text’s way of handling it): do not follow the thought; lead the attention back with the intention, lightly up from the perineum along the back to the crown, keep it there a moment, and let it descend naturally. If the heart stirs, look up with both eyes and “draw down the true primordial breath of the palace of Qian to press it,” letting the head become cool.
  3. “The cultivation of nature is the city wall that protects life”: this is the most important sentence of the section. The bodily movements of breath (the cultivation of existence) become more and more intense as one goes on; without the stability of the mind as a city wall, they cannot be held.
  4. The general principle for all scenes: set it aside and do not cling, set it aside without examining it — put it to one side, do not look at it, do not examine it; draw the gaze and the hearing back into “the place where nothing is seen and nothing heard.”
  5. Two standards: when the primordial breath reaches the lower abdomen, take “warmth” as the sign of success; when it rises to the head and descends again to the chest, take “coolness” as the sign of success.
  6. This section belongs to “a higher level of method” (Qiu Ling’s words); beginners may read it for understanding only, while their daily work remains the Reverting the Light and regulating the breath of the earlier parts.

Translator's Notes

  1. “As if the mechanism within had given a little start” (hu jue nei ji you ruo de de yan 忽觉内机有若得得焉): de de is an onomatopoeia describing a slight start of the inner mechanism; it is kept as in the original.
  2. “As if their care were not complete” (yun xin wei pu 运心未溥): pu means “all-encompassing”; that is, their care was not thorough.
  3. “Precious vase” (baoping 宝瓶): not explained in the text; it may be a veiled name for some visualization method or part of the body. The original name is kept without speculation.
  4. “Whose cultivation of nature was pure and deep” (xinggong chunru 性功淳洳): chunru means pure, rich and deep.
  5. “The three contemplations of the Scripture of Purity and Stillness”: the Scripture of Constant Purity and Stillness Spoken by the Most High Lord Lao says: “Contemplate the mind within: the mind has no mind. Contemplate the form without: the form has no form. Contemplate things afar: things have no things. When these three are understood, only emptiness is seen.” Noted under Key Terms.
  6. The original text of this section is in two passages; for ease of comparison and recitation, the first is divided into §1–§5 and the second into §6–§11, with Qiu Ling’s notes placed after §5 and §11 respectively.

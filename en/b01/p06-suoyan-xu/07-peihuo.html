7 · The Formula for Cultivating the Fire

7 · The Formula for Cultivating the Fire — the spirit pours into the abdomen of Kun, the breath returns to the Gate of Life

About This Chapter

Why can some people’s breath simply not rise up the back? This section says: it is always because “muddy essence” has blocked the network vessels and openings. Essence is originally moved by breath, and breath must be warmed by spirit: when spirit is strong, breath is warm; when spirit is weak, breath is cool; when breath is cool, essence congeals and does not transform, turns into phlegm on the way, and fills the meridians. So the only remedy is “cultivating the fire”: the spirit pours into the abdomen of Kun (the lower abdomen), and the primordial breath returns to the Gate of Life (behind the navel). The fire of the Gate of Life cannot be seen, but when experienced it is the root of the spirit-fire; when the fire of the Gate of Life is strong, even the essence of food and drink can be transformed into breath. The author also analyzes the harm of the breath passing through in front instead of rising behind, and the fault of seeking only to “open” without “cultivating”: the old mud remains in the network vessels and openings, and there is only temporary peace. Qiu Ling’s note finally explains how to “contract the gen”: as if holding back urine and stool.

Safety note (translator): “Contracting the gen” mentioned in this section (Qiu Ling’s note: as if holding back urine and stool) is a forceful operation of contracting the lower body; practice it only under the guidance of an experienced teacher. The section’s explanations of the causes of abscesses, paralysis, “bone-steaming” fever, consumption and other illnesses are the theories of the ancients and cannot replace medical diagnosis.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

古哲有言曰：前关闭，降心炁，功从夏秋功乃济。又曰：后关通，一半功，缩艮开乾是正功。要知气不后升，皆因泥精塞络及窍之故。法惟神注坤腹，炁归命门，火旺则气暖，泥精遇暖，则融如水，络窍不为滞塞，厥气自能后透。

An ancient sage said: “When the front pass is closed, lower the breath of the heart; with the summer–autumn work the work succeeds.” And: “When the rear pass is open, half the work is done; contracting the gen and opening Qian is the correct work.” Know that when the breath does not rise behind, it is always because muddy essence has blocked the network vessels and openings. The only method is to pour the spirit into the abdomen of Kun and return the primordial breath to the Gate of Life. When the fire is strong, the breath is warm; when muddy essence meets warmth, it melts like water, the network vessels and openings are no longer blocked, and the breath can naturally pass through behind.

§2
Original (Chinese)

原夫气行循络，络塞则气滞。要知塞络之物，即昔气御以行之精。然则气之不升，精中淤故。精之中淤，气不暖故。气之不暖，命门衰故。命门火衰，神失守故。盖精得行以气，气之得暖以神，神旺则气暖，神衰则气凉，气凉则精凝不化，中变成痰，充塞经络，而孔窍咸塞。其流弊，非惟大道难行，性命亦难暂保。法惟坤腹极热以挽之。

If we look into it: breath travels along the network vessels; when the network vessels are blocked, breath stagnates. Know that what blocks the network vessels is the essence that breath formerly carried along. So the breath does not rise because essence is clogged in between; essence is clogged because breath is not warm; breath is not warm because the Gate of Life is weak; the fire of the Gate of Life is weak because the spirit has lost its guard. For essence moves by means of breath, and breath is warmed by means of spirit. When spirit is strong, breath is warm; when spirit is weak, breath is cool; when breath is cool, essence congeals and does not transform, turns into phlegm on the way, fills the meridians, and all the openings are blocked. Its harm is not only that the Great Way becomes hard to practice; even life itself becomes hard to preserve for a while. The only method is to make the abdomen of Kun extremely hot to remedy it.

§3
Original (Chinese)

要知精之为物，旺气御之，如云乘燥风，升则成霞，降则如露。衰气御之，如雾乘湿风，升成滞云，降化尤雨。况此阴精下注，逗留膀胱，偶经相火一烘，油然外泄，此又滞下之大弊。若经气御，散至百络，一干风火，变成痈疽，或成瘫痪，更或积久化火，便成骨蒸，世之痨瘵等症，皆此物以酿成者，古哲忧之，此所以谕有培火之说也。

Know that essence, when driven by strong breath, is like clouds riding a dry wind: rising, they become rosy clouds; descending, they are like dew. Driven by weak breath, it is like mist riding a damp wind: rising, it becomes stagnant clouds; descending, it turns into endless rain. Moreover, when this yin essence pours downward and lingers in the bladder, once it is warmed by the ministerial fire it leaks out abundantly — this is another great harm of stagnation below. If, carried by breath, it spreads into all the network vessels, then as soon as it meets wind and fire it turns into abscesses, or into paralysis, or, accumulating over time and turning into fire, it becomes “bone-steaming” fever; consumption and similar illnesses in the world are all brewed by this substance. The ancient sages were concerned about this, and that is why they taught “cultivating the fire.”

§4
Original (Chinese)

培火之说，惟有意存坤腹，炁归命门。命门之火，视之不见，体之则有神火之根。命门火旺，饮食之精，便可化气。更得意注坤腹，燥者培真土，润者泽百骸滋气机，以涵雷阳，真火之用大矣哉！我辈平时，饮食日化，向昧培火一说。百络之间，或鲜被累，膀胱一地，此物必多，阴不化阳，其流弊必致如上说也。可不知所加注乎。

The teaching of cultivating the fire is simply to keep the intention in the abdomen of Kun and return the primordial breath to the Gate of Life. The fire of the Gate of Life cannot be seen when looked for, yet when experienced it is the root of the spirit-fire. When the fire of the Gate of Life is strong, the essence of food and drink can be transformed into breath. Moreover, when the intention pours into the abdomen of Kun, on the dry side it nourishes the true earth, on the moist side it moistens all the bones and nourishes the movement of breath, so as to contain the yang of thunder — great indeed is the use of the true fire! In ordinary times we digest our food and drink every day, yet have never understood the teaching of cultivating the fire. The network vessels throughout the body may seldom be affected, but in the bladder this substance is surely plentiful; if the yin does not transform into yang, its harm will surely lead to what was described above. How can one fail to know to apply the intention there?

§5
Original (Chinese)

气不后升，其去斯祸不远矣！且气不后升，升必自前。气若前透，其祸更大，而病发必春。春发犹可，夏发乃烈。盖此泥精成痰，先塞中宫，阻气下注，间有随炁下流，而命门火衰，降物凝塞于下，而尾闾穴壅，则其夹脊顶枕必咸壅塞，是已任督咸闭，祸岂能测？欲预救挽，法兼勤缩艮功，汇集臣民，以助以培，命门真火乃旺，用以烘炙中下阴精，日自熔销。真炁势大，用以破关通络。如是一七二七，行至七七不间，则其前滞后滞之物化，而任督通，不惟病愈已也。

When the breath does not rise behind, one is not far from these disasters! Moreover, if the breath does not rise behind, it will surely rise in front. If the breath passes through in front, the disaster is greater, and the illness will surely break out in spring. Breaking out in spring is still tolerable; breaking out in summer is fierce. For this muddy essence turns into phlegm and first blocks the Central Palace, preventing the breath from pouring downward; occasionally some flows down with the primordial breath, but since the fire of the Gate of Life is weak, what descends congeals and blocks below, the cavity of the Caudal Funnel is obstructed, and then the Spine-Straddling Pass, the crown and the Pillow are surely all obstructed — then the Conception and Governing vessels are both closed, and who can predict the disaster? To rescue it in advance, the method must also include diligent work of “contracting the gen,” gathering the subjects and people (the breaths of the whole body) to help and to cultivate; then the true fire of the Gate of Life becomes strong, and is used to warm and roast the yin essence of the middle and lower body, which melts away day by day. When the true primordial breath is powerful, use it to break through the passes and open the network vessels. Practicing like this for seven days, fourteen days, up to forty-nine days without interruption, the stagnant things in front and behind are dissolved and the Conception and Governing vessels are opened — not merely is the illness cured.

§6
Original (Chinese)

若但求通，不事培助，往昔淤泥仍留络窍，不过瞬暂或安。究其所致，志在苟安，实因阳衰气弱，振率觉难耳。积习难挽，乃至于此，可慨也夫！我侪志士，可切戒之！

If one seeks only to open, without the work of cultivating and helping, the old mud still remains in the network vessels and openings, and there is no more than temporary peace. If we look into its cause, it is aiming only at a makeshift peace — in fact because yang is weak and breath feeble, and rousing oneself feels hard. Ingrained habits are hard to reverse, and so it comes to this — how lamentable! We who are resolved on the Way must strictly avoid it!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§6
Original note (Chinese)

络窍滞塞，患莫大焉，若非实行培火之功，神注坤腹，炁归命门（即脐轮后一寸三分处），不但功无所进，而且百病从生，此篇义旨在此。

缩艮之诀，如忍大小便然。艮者背也身也。

Blockage of the network vessels and openings is the greatest of troubles. Without the work of cultivating the fire — pouring the spirit into the abdomen of Kun and returning the primordial breath to the Gate of Life (the point one cun three fen behind the navel wheel) — not only will the work make no progress, but a hundred illnesses will arise. This is the main point of this section.

The formula of contracting the gen is like holding back urine and stool. Gen is the back, and also the body.

Key Terms

  • Front pass, rear pass: the passes and openings in front (along the Conception vessel) and behind (along the Governing vessel).
  • Contracting the gen (suo gen 缩艮): Qiu Ling’s note: like holding back urine and stool; “gen is the back, and also the body.”
  • Opening Qian: opening the crown (the palace of Qian).
  • Muddy essence (nijing 泥精): congealed, untransformed essence that blocks the network vessels and openings (Section 4 also uses this term).
  • Abdomen of Kun: the lower abdomen.
  • Gate of Life: in this part, the point one cun three fen behind the navel wheel, that is, the lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Cultivating the fire: pouring the spirit into the abdomen of Kun and returning the primordial breath to the Gate of Life, so that the fire of the Gate of Life becomes strong.
  • Root of the spirit-fire: the fire of the Gate of Life “cannot be seen when looked for, yet when experienced it is there.”
  • Ministerial fire (xianghuo 相火): in Chinese medicine, the fire lodged in the liver and kidneys.
  • Abscesses, paralysis, bone-steaming fever, consumption: ancient names of illnesses; consumption roughly corresponds to tuberculosis and other chronic wasting diseases.
  • True earth: the breath of the spleen and stomach (the central earth).
  • Yang of thunder: newly born yang (the trigram Zhen is thunder, with one yang line at the bottom).
  • Gathering the subjects and people: likening the breaths of the whole body to subjects, gathered to help the Gate of Life.
  • Seven, fourteen, forty-nine days (yi qi, er qi, qi qi 一七、二七、七七).

Putting It into Practice

  1. A chain of cause and effect worth remembering: weak spirit → cool breath → congealed essence → phlegm → blocked network vessels → breath cannot rise. Reversed, it is cultivating the fire: spirit poured into the lower abdomen → warm breath → essence transformed → network vessels open → breath rises behind naturally.
  2. The method of cultivating the fire is very simple: hold the intention on the lower abdomen (the abdomen of Kun), placing the attention and the feeling of warmth behind the navel (the Gate of Life). This is one of the safest and most basic practices.
  3. A warm lower abdomen is the standard: “the only method is to make the abdomen of Kun extremely hot to remedy it” — the core of the whole section is to let the lower abdomen become warm.
  4. Do not seek only to open: “if one seeks only to open, without the work of cultivating and helping,” there is only temporary peace. Laying the foundation (cultivating) matters more than seeking a breakthrough (opening).
  5. On contracting the gen: “like holding back urine and stool,” it is an action of contracting the perineum and anus. If you practice it, do it only very lightly and briefly, alternating with relaxation; do not hold it for long. Guidance from a teacher is best.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Descending, it turns into endless rain” (jiang hua you yu 降化尤雨): you 尤 may also be written yin 淫, meaning continuous rain; the translation follows this sense.
  2. “On the dry side it nourishes the true earth, on the moist side it moistens all the bones and nourishes the movement of breath”: that is, the true fire of the Gate of Life, on the dry side, can nourish the earth of the spleen, and on the moist side can moisten the whole body and nourish the movement of breath.
  3. “Rousing oneself” (zhen shuai 振率): to rouse and lead oneself.
  4. Qiu Ling did not divide the original text of this section into paragraphs; for ease of comparison, it is divided by content into §1–§6.

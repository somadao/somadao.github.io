3 · The Main Points of Women’s Cultivation

3 · The Main Points of Women’s Cultivation — concentration, gentleness, stillness; “dual cultivation within oneself”

Listen to this chapterENEnglish

About This Chapter

The last section sums up the main points of women’s practice. Women are yin within and yang without, and their trigram is Li; and their true yin is often lost with the menses. So stillness prevailing over movement is auspicious, and movement prevailing over stillness is inauspicious. Men take essence as their foundation, women blood: essence grows strong through warmth, blood is produced through coolness — understanding this, one knows how to nourish oneself. Women’s practice does not abandon massage and keeping-and-pouring, for two reasons: first, to free the movement of breath; second, to refine the fluids so that they do not stagnate and turn into phlegm. The ancients took “keeping in stillness” as their principle, also for two reasons: when the sovereign is at peace, the subjects are at peace, and the spirit is clear and does not fly off; and stillness gives rise to wisdom, undisturbed by desire. This is the formula of “dual cultivation within oneself.” Persevering, one will finally realize the body equal to empty space, the three fields joined as one, every opening bright — “the body beyond one’s own body is complete.” Qiu Ling’s note specifies that the “body beyond the body” here means the physical body being pure and white inside and out: “when the work is raised, the body is gone; when the work stops, the body is there” — it does not mean sending out the Yang Spirit.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

女子内阴而外阳，卦义属离，而真阴每随月信漏失，故静胜动者吉，动胜静者凶。男子以精为本，女子以血为本，精以暖旺，血以凉生。知此，则知所以养矣！

Women are yin within and yang without; in the meaning of the trigrams they belong to Li (☲); and their true yin is often lost with the menses. So stillness prevailing over movement is auspicious, and movement prevailing over stillness is inauspicious. Men take essence as their foundation, women blood; essence grows strong through warmth, blood is produced through coolness. Knowing this, one knows how to nourish oneself!

§2
Original (Chinese)

女功之不废按摩存注者，其义有二：一以通其气机，则经络疏畅；二以炼其津液，不使液滞化痰，而液乃化血。古哲谓以静存为宗者亦有二义：君安臣庶安，则神清不飞；又静则慧生，不为欲搅，而命得保固。此则一己双修之诀也。

Women’s practice does not abandon massage and keeping-and-pouring, for two reasons: first, to free the movement of breath, so that the meridians flow freely; second, to refine the fluids, so that they do not stagnate and turn into phlegm, but turn into blood. The ancient sages took “keeping in stillness” as their principle, also for two reasons: when the sovereign is at peace, the ministers and people are at peace, and the spirit is clear and does not fly off; and stillness gives rise to wisdom, undisturbed by desire, so that life is protected and made firm. This is the formula of “dual cultivation within oneself.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

气机既舒，志意净寂，加之以充和，继之以贞白，日计不足，月计有余，踵而事之，一旦证夫身等虚空，三田一贯，惟觉肢肢节节窍窍光明。功修至此，一己之身外身具矣！果能踵事不退，神足气充，念不外驰，则神不逐念，血生必旺，真阴亦足，气精自有弥天塞地局境。

When the movement of breath is at ease and the will and intention pure and still, add fullness and harmony, follow with purity and whiteness; counted by the day it seems not enough, counted by the month there is a surplus. Continue step after step, and one day you will realize the body equal to empty space, the three fields joined as one, feeling only that every limb, every joint and every opening is bright. When cultivation reaches this point, the body beyond one’s own body is complete! If one can truly continue without turning back, the spirit sufficient and the breath full, thoughts not running outward, then the spirit does not chase after thoughts, blood is surely produced abundantly, true yin is also sufficient, and breath and essence naturally have a realm that fills Heaven and Earth.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

此节言女功大旨在于以专以柔，以静为主，不为物诱，调其心炁，一其气机。所以按摩提缩与存运频加，不过灵活其气机而已，但过行则非。

身外身者法身之意。此处乃指锻炼得色身内外贞白，是身非身，非身是身，所谓功举则身无，功停则身有，并非指出阳神而言。

This section says that the main point of women’s practice lies in concentration and gentleness, with stillness as the main thing, not being lured by things, regulating the mind and the primordial breath and unifying the movement of breath. So frequently adding massage, lifting and contracting, and keeping and moving does no more than keep the movement of breath lively; but overdoing it is wrong.

“The body beyond the body” means the Dharma Body. Here it refers to training the physical body until it is pure and white inside and out — a body and yet not a body, not a body and yet a body — what is called “when the work is raised, the body is gone; when the work stops, the body is there.” It does not refer to sending out the Yang Spirit.

Key Terms

  • Yin within and yang without, belonging to Li: the trigram Li ☲ has one yin line in the middle and two yang lines outside.
  • Menses (yuexin 月信).
  • Stillness prevailing over movement is auspicious: women’s practice takes stillness as the main thing.
  • Essence grows strong through warmth, blood is produced through coolness: for men, nourishing essence calls for warmth; for women, nourishing blood calls for coolness.
  • Massage and keeping-and-pouring: the two methods of women’s practice, one freeing the movement of breath, the other refining the fluids.
  • Keeping in stillness as the principle.
  • When the sovereign is at peace, the subjects are at peace: when the heart is at peace, the whole body is at peace.
  • Dual cultivation within oneself: one person cultivating both inner nature and existence (not men and women practicing together).
  • Fullness and harmony, purity and whiteness.
  • Continuing step after step (zhong er shi zhi 踵而事之).
  • Body beyond the body: Qiu Ling’s note: the physical body pure and white inside and out; when the work is raised the body is gone, when the work stops the body is there; not sending out the Yang Spirit. See spontaneous Dharma Body.
  • Filling Heaven and Earth.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The general outline of women’s practice: concentration and gentleness, with stillness as the main thing (Qiu Ling).
  2. What “blood is produced through coolness” means in practice: the heart should be cool (still), the emotions not agitated; but the body should not be cold — as Section 1 said, women’s practice forbids “escaping warmth to seek coolness.” A cool heart and a warm body is the basic state of women’s practice.
  3. Massage is an aid; stillness is the root: massage, lifting and contracting, and keeping and moving “do no more than keep the movement of breath lively; but overdoing it is wrong.”
  4. “Stillness gives rise to wisdom, undisturbed by desire”: this sentence applies to both women and men who cultivate.
  5. Counted by the day not enough, counted by the month a surplus: do not measure your progress every day; persevere for a month and then look back.

Translator's Notes

  1. “Dual cultivation within oneself”: the “dual cultivation” in the title of this book, The Precious Raft of the Women’s School of Dual Cultivation, is precisely this “dual cultivation within oneself,” that is, one person cultivating both inner nature and existence; this is noted here specially.
  2. “Counted by the day it seems not enough, counted by the month there is a surplus”: from the Zhuangzi, chapter “Gengsang Chu”; little can be seen each day, but a month’s accumulation is considerable.

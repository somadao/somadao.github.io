Introduction to Part Seven

What this book is

The six preceding parts are all written from the point of view of male practitioners. This part is a method specifically for women. The Precious Raft of the Women’s School of Dual Cultivation (Nüzong shuangxiu baofa 女宗双修宝筏) is said to have been transmitted by Li Niwan, recorded by Shen Taixu of the Qing dynasty and corrected by Min Yide; it is a book of the Mount Jingai collection near Huzhou, Zhejiang, and one of the authentic transmissions of women’s practice in the Longmen lineage of the Northern School of Daoism.

The “dual cultivation” of the title means the dual cultivation of inner nature and existence — what Section 3 of this part calls “dual cultivation within oneself”: one person cultivating both nature and life, not men and women practicing together.

Qiu Ling's editorial note

《女宗双修宝筏》一书乃李泥丸氏所传，由清代沈太虚述，闵一得订正，浙湖金盖山藏本，为道教北宗龙门派女功真传之一。历代丹书汗牛充栋，多重男功，女子丹书世少全册，且较之男丹书未及百分之一。当代道学大师陈撄宁氏对此有深刻研究，于三十年代多有著述，曾编撰女子道学小丛书，注释《孙不二女功内丹次第诗》和《灵源大道歌》等。今人对女功鲜于介绍，甚至有人不知世有专为女子而设之气功功法存在者。现特将《女宗双修宝筏》一书的功法部分选刊于此。

The Precious Raft of the Women’s School of Dual Cultivation was transmitted by Li Niwan, recorded by Shen Taixu of the Qing dynasty and corrected by Min Yide; it is a book of the Mount Jingai collection in Huzhou, Zhejiang, and one of the authentic transmissions of women’s practice in the Longmen lineage of the Northern School of Daoism. The alchemical books of the ages are enough to make an ox sweat and fill a house to the rafters, but most emphasize men’s practice; complete books of alchemy for women are rare in the world, and they number less than one percent of the books for men. The contemporary master of Daoist learning Chen Yingning made a profound study of this subject and wrote a great deal in the 1930s; he compiled the Little Collection of Daoist Learning for Women and annotated Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Inner Alchemy and the Song of the Great Way of the Numinous Source (Lingyuan dadao ge 灵源大道歌), among others. Today women’s practice is seldom introduced, and some people do not even know that there exist qigong methods designed specifically for women. I have therefore selected the method sections of The Precious Raft of the Women’s School of Dual Cultivation and published them here.

What this part is about (in one sentence)

Women take blood as their foundation; their nature tends toward yin and likes coolness. So they begin by stopping thoughts and regulating the mind, then add massage (attending to the Breast Stream and circling massage of the breasts) to free the movement of breath, so as not to sink into pure yin; then they guide the movement of breath in a circuit between the chest (the Southern Ocean), the waist, the navel wheel, the Caudal Funnel and the Muddy Pellet. The whole is to be done with concentration, gentleness and stillness as the main points.

Reading tips

  1. The main differences between women’s and men’s practice: both begin by stopping thoughts and regulating the mind; women’s practice adds a step of massage, and its key area is between the breasts (the Breast Stream, the Chest Center point) and the breasts themselves, rather than the lower Cinnabar Field so often spoken of in men’s practice. Qiu Ling’s note says: “Women’s practice first refines form and substance, then refines the original root. Form means the two breasts; substance means the menses.”
  2. This part speaks of massaging the breasts, lifting and contracting the two lower orifices, and so on; these are normal elements of the old women’s alchemy, and we translate them faithfully. Qiu Ling cautions that massage “should not be too vigorous,” and that lifting and contracting “is wrong if overdone.”
  3. Safety note (translator): during pregnancy, menstruation or after childbirth, or if you have any breast or gynecological condition, please consult a doctor first and do not practice the massage or lifting-and-contracting methods on your own. This site only translates the texts; it does not constitute practice instruction.
  4. For more on women’s alchemy, see the appendix of this book: Chen Yingning’s Commentary on Sun Bu’er’s Poems on the Stages of Women’s Alchemy.

Chapter 60 · Neither Harms the Other

Governing a great state is like cooking a small fish: what matters is blending the five flavours and simmering slowly over a gentle fire without stirring it to pieces. In the alchemical Way, this means unhurriedly tending the medicine furnace, simply settling spirit and breath and leaving all to nature, so that yin stillness and yang movement do not harm each other

About This Chapter

Chapter 60 of the Daodejing opens with the timeless metaphor "governing a great state is like cooking a small fish," and then unfolds a step-by-step progression: "its ghosts have no spiritual power; their spiritual power does not harm people; the sage also does not harm people; neither harms the other, so their virtues converge." Huang Yuanji first draws on a series of portents and omens from the Zuo Commentary to analyse the great principle of governance that "humans are the masters of ghosts and spirits," and then shifts course to turn "cooking a small fish" and "ghosts and spirits do no harm" into a remarkably lucid formula for warm nurturing in alchemy:

  1. Governing a state is like cooking a fish: never stir it about (§2): A great state has complex affairs and a vast population. If one meddles blindly with harsh punishments, oppressive government, stratagems and calculations, one openly incurs the resentment of the people and secretly invites calamities and uncanny portents. Only by cultivating oneself and transforming the people through the Way, being reverent within and simple in government, are people at peace, and then both the seen and the unseen worlds are at peace.
  2. Refining the elixir is like cooking a small fish: a warm fire, leaving all to nature (§3): Cooking a tender fish depends entirely on blending the five flavours with salt and plum and simmering it slowly over a gentle fire; the worst thing is to keep turning it over with a spatula until it falls apart. Refining the elixir is just the same: once the five phases are gathered and the three flowers and five breaths are assembled in the central palace, one relies solely on the spontaneous fire of the spirit for continuous warm nurturing. "Unhurriedly tend the medicine furnace and watch the fire phasing; simply settle spirit and breath and leave all to nature." The worst thing is contrived adding and subtracting.
  3. Ghosts are yin stillness; spirits are yang movement (§3): Huang Yuanji gives an exquisitely subtle interpretation, drawn from inner experience, of the "ghosts and spirits" in the human body: ghosts stand for yin stillness without awareness, spirits for yang movement with action. In the accomplished practitioner there is true awareness within non-awareness, and action accords with non-action; yin stillness does not fall into inert emptiness, and yang movement does not fall into deluded clinging. The two are rooted in and complement each other without the slightest harm, and the true elixir forms of itself.

Safety note: §3 of this chapter discusses advanced alchemical states such as "Five Breaths return to the Origin; Three Flowers gather at the Summit" and "warm nurturing by the spontaneous fire of the spirit," but the whole chapter's purpose is precisely to oppose contrived forcing and deliberately stirring up the mechanism of the breath. In everyday sitting practice, simply settle the breath and stay relaxed, still and natural; never follow the names of the five phases and three flowers to forcibly manipulate, add to or subtract from anything inside the body with the mind.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：治大国者，若烹小鲜。以道莅天下，其鬼不神；非其鬼不神，其神不伤人；非其神不伤人，圣人亦不伤人。夫两不相伤，故德交归焉。

Lord Lao says: Governing a great state is like cooking a small, tender fish (the heat must be gentle, and one must never keep turning it over until it breaks apart). When the world is governed by the great Way, ghosts and demons do not stir up trouble or work uncanny wonders; in fact it is not that the ghosts lose their spiritual power, but that their spiritual power does not harm people; and not only do ghosts and spirits not harm people, the sage who has attained the Way also never harms people with harsh government. Since the ghosts and spirits of the unseen world and the sage king of the human world do not harm each other, the virtuous blessings of heaven and of the human world naturally converge and return to one place.

§2
Original (Chinese)

夫道者，天下人物，共有之理也。以此理修身，即以此理治世。欲立立人，欲达达人，不待转念，无俟移时，何其易而简欤？故太上曰：「治大国若烹小鲜。」夫国大则事必烦、人必众，苟不得其道，则必杂乱繁冗，犹治乱丝之不得其绪，势必愈治而愈棼。惟以人所共有之道，修诸一人之身，统御万民之众，其理相通，其气相贯，而其势亦甚便焉。不然，徒以法制禁令，权谋术数之条，号召天下，明则结怨于民，而民心变诈多端矣；幽则触怒于鬼，而鬼怪灾殃叠见矣。盖人者，鬼神之主也。人君横征暴敛，淫威肆毒，民无所依，则鬼怪神奸，亦无所附丽，不得不兴妖作祟，凶荒疫疠所不免焉。故石言于晋，彗见于齐，蛇斗于郑，伯有为厉，申生降灵，二竖梦而病入膏肓，有神降莘而虢遂灭亡，若皆鬼神为之，亦由上无道以致之也。为民上者，诚能以道修身，即以道化民，鬼虽阴气，得所依归，鬼即冥顽，咸为趋附，人无怨讟，鬼不灾殃，山川弗见崩颓，物产不闻怪异，熙熙皞皞，坐享升平。《书》曰：「古我先王，方懋厥德，罔有天灾，山川鬼神亦莫不宁」是也。此岂鬼之不神哉？盖魑魅魍魉，以及山精水怪，亦皆依傍有所，血食有方，顺其自然，毫无事事，虽有神亦无所施，即有施，亦乌得为祟？故阴阳人鬼，共嬉游于光天化日之中，又何伤人之有哉？亦非神不伤人也，由圣人有道，无事察察之智，无矜煦煦之仁，慎厥身修，敦叙彝伦，居敬行简，不务纷纭，无有一毫伤乎人者，在乎阴阳和而民物育，祀典举而鬼神安，幽冥之间，两不侵害，故天下咸服圣人之德而交归焉。呜呼！无为之治，近取诸身，远取诸物，不识不知，顺帝之则，以视尚政令者严诰诫，希勤勉者重典型，孰难孰易，为简为烦，奚啻云泥之判，人何不反求其本哉？

The great Way is the fundamental law shared by all people and all things under heaven. Use this law to cultivate oneself, and by the same token use it to govern the human world: wishing to stand firm oneself, one helps others to stand firm; wishing to succeed oneself, one helps others to succeed. There is no need for roundabout second thoughts, nor any need for delay: how easy and direct this is!

That is why Lord Lao says: "Governing a great state is like cooking a small fish." The larger the state, the more complex its affairs and the larger its population are bound to be. If one does not know how to grasp the overall principle of the great Way, government will inevitably become chaotic and cumbersome, like trying to sort out a tangled skein of silk without finding the end of the thread: the harder one pulls, the tighter and more tangled it becomes! Only by first cultivating one's own self with the great Way that everyone shares, and then using it to lead millions of people, do the principles communicate and the breath flow through; then governing proceeds naturally and with the greatest ease.

Conversely, if one only knows how to command the world with harsh laws and prohibitions and clauses of stratagem and calculation, openly one will incur the resentment of the people and drive their hearts to all kinds of deceit; secretly one will stir up the hostile breath of heaven and earth and invite calamities and uncanny portents one after another. Know that it is humans who are the masters of the ghosts and spirits of heaven and earth! If the ruler levies exorbitant taxes and wantonly wields cruel power, leaving the people at their wits' end with nothing to rely on, then the breath of ghosts and spirits in the unseen world also loses its harmonious support and has no choice but to turn into baleful influences and haunting, and famine and pestilence follow close behind.

Look at the events recorded in the Zuo Commentary: in Jin a stone spoke; in Qi a broom-star (comet) appeared in the sky; outside the city gate of Zheng two snakes fought; in Zheng, Boyou became a vengeful ghost after his death and came to claim lives; Shensheng, heir apparent of Jin, appeared as a spirit to protest his wrongful death; Duke Jing of Jin dreamt of two little boys hiding in his chest and abdomen, and his illness went beyond the vitals; a spirit descended at Xin, and the state of Guo was destroyed soon after. On the surface it all seems to be the mischief of ghosts and spirits, but trace it to its source: was it not all brought on by the loss of virtue and the lawlessness of those above?

If those above the people can truly cultivate themselves through the Way and transform the people through the Way, then although ghosts belong to yin breath, they find a place to settle and belong; even the most benighted breaths all submit and attach themselves. The people harbour no resentment or curses in their hearts, and the unseen world sends no calamities or hauntings; mountains and rivers do not collapse, no strange things are produced, and everyone lives in joyful harmony, peacefully enjoying an age of great peace. The Book of Documents says: "Of old, our former kings diligently cultivated their virtue, and there were no natural calamities; the ghosts and spirits of mountains and rivers were all at peace." This is exactly the principle!

Is this because the ghosts and spirits have lost their potency? No! It is because even the goblins and demons, the spirits of mountains and the monsters of waters, each have their place of belonging and each rest content in their position, following nature and living in peace with one another. Even if they have numinous power, they have no need to use it wildly, and even if they manifest, how would they come out to harm people or haunt them? And so the world of the living and the world of the dead, living people and ghosts and spirits alike, bask together in broad daylight; how could there be the slightest danger of harm to people?

Moreover, it is not merely that ghosts and spirits do not harm people; at root it is because the sage himself embraces the great Way. He neither shows off the petty cleverness of exacting nitpicking (the "wisdom of prying") nor plays with the contrived, hypocritical little favours of false kindness (the "warm, cosy benevolence"); he simply cultivates himself with care, strengthens human relationships, is reverent within and simple in government and makes no fuss or commotion, doing the people not the slightest harm! When yin and yang are in harmony, the people and all things thrive; when the sacrificial rites are in good order, ghosts and spirits each find their peace. The seen and unseen worlds do not encroach on each other, and so everyone under heaven sincerely submits and attributes the converging blessings of virtue to the sage.

Alas! Government by clear, still non-action takes its model near at hand from one's own body and far off from all things, letting the people, without knowing or realising it, naturally follow the laws of Heaven. Compare it with the practice of putting blind faith in stern decrees and admonitions or being obsessed with cumbersome regulations and evaluations: which is hard and which is easy, which simple and which complicated? The difference is greater than that between the clouds in the sky and the mud on the ground! Why do people not turn back and seek their own root?

§3
Original (Chinese)

此大国喻大道，烹小鲜喻炼丹。小鲜者，羔羊鱼肉之类，其烹也，惟以醯醢盐梅，调和五味，扶其不及，抑其太过，而以温养之火，慢慢烹煎，不霎时而滋味出，口体宜矣。大丹之炼，亦惟取和合四象，攒簇五行，使三花聚于一鼎，五气聚于中田，于是天然神火，慢慢温养，不用加减，无事矫持，逆而取之，顺而行之，七返九还，易于反掌间矣。古云：「漫守药炉看火候，但安神息任天然。」何便如之？是故无为之道，即临驭天下之道，亦即炼吾人大还之丹。太平盛世，治臻上理，庆洽重熙，上无为而自治，下无为而自化，一切鬼怪神奸，皆不知消归何有，非谓其灭迹亡形也，亦化于无为自然之道，而诪张变幻无所施，旱潦疫病无从作矣。其在人身，鬼阴静无知觉者，神阳动有作为者也。大修行人，心普万物而无心，情顺万物而无情，阴中含阳，阳中含阴，静而无静，动而无动，一动一静，交相为用，一阴一阳，互为其根。非谓无觉竟无觉、有为竟有为也，其实无觉中有觉，有为中无为焉。曰「其鬼不神」，非谓蚩蠢而无灵爽也，盖无觉之觉实为正等正觉，无为之为无非顺天所为，岂似有觉者之流于伪妄、有为者之类于固守，而有伤于本来之丹也哉？曰「其神不伤人」，亦非神不伤人也，以无为而为之道，原人生固有之天真，生生不已之灵气，至诚无息，体物不遗，虽有造化，实无存亡，虽有盈虚，原无消息，所谓「不扰不惊，无忧无虑」者此也，又何伤人之有耶？亦非圣人之不伤人也，盖以勃发之生机，裕本来之真面，以调和之三昧，养自在之灵丹，立见神火一煅，而鬼哭神号，阴邪退听，真人出现矣，谓为两不相伤，谁曰不宜？天上人间，皆归美其德。噫！幽明交格，非德之神，乌能至此？

In terms of the actual practice of internal alchemy, this chapter uses "the great state" as a metaphor for "the great Way" and "cooking a small fish" as a metaphor for "refining the elixir." What is a "small fish"? It means tender meat such as fish or lamb. Cooking such a fresh dish depends entirely on properly blending the five flavours with vinegar, sauce, salt and plum, adding a little where the taste is weak and holding back where it is strong, and then simmering it slowly over a gentle, even fire. Before long the delicious flavour comes out of itself, pleasing to the palate and nourishing to the body. The worst thing is a fierce fire and wildly shovelling and turning it!

Refining the Great Elixir follows exactly the same principle: it is no more than harmonising the four images and gathering the five phases, so that Essence, Breath and Spirit achieve "Three Flowers gather at the Summit, Five Breaths return to the Origin," all converging in the elixir tripod of the central palace. Then one relies entirely on one's own spontaneous fire of the spirit for continuous warm nurturing, with no need to deliberately add or reduce the fire, nor to force anything artificially. First one gathers back to the origin by reversal, then lets it flow and be warmly nurtured according to nature, and the Great Elixir work of the so-called "seven returns and nine reversions" becomes as easy as turning over one's hand! An ancient immortal's alchemical poem puts it well: "Unhurriedly tend the medicine furnace and watch the fire phasing; simply settle Spirit and Breath and leave all to nature." Is there any method more stable and convenient than this?

From this we see that the Way of non-action is both the great Way of governing the world and the secret of cooking and refining the Great Returning Golden Elixir. In an age of great peace, when the art of government reaches its pinnacle and generations live in joy and prosperity, those above govern themselves by non-action and the people transform themselves by non-action; all ghosts, monsters and evil spirits vanish without a trace before anyone notices. It is not that they are utterly annihilated, but that they are all transformed and melted into the great Way of non-action and naturalness; their deceitful transformations can no longer be practised, and droughts, floods and plagues have nowhere to break out.

Shine this principle into the interior of the human body: "ghosts" represent the aspect of yin stillness without awareness, and "spirits" represent the aspect of yang movement with action.

The truly great practitioner's heart extends to all things yet harbours no selfish mind; his feelings accord with all things yet do not fall into private emotion. Within yin stillness true yang is contained, and within yang movement true stillness is hidden; when still, he does not cling to dead stillness; when moving, he does not fall into agitation. Movement and stillness serve each other in turn, and yin and yang are each other's root. This by no means says that "non-awareness" turns one into utter ignorance like wood or stone, nor that "action" becomes busy, deliberate fussing; quite the opposite: it is "within non-awareness there is clear, true awareness; within action there is still pure non-action"!

Therefore: - When the classic says "its ghosts have no spiritual power," it does not mean that yin stillness becomes dull, stupid and wholly lacking in numinosity. It means that this "true awareness within non-awareness" is perfect, bright and unobstructed supreme enlightenment, and this "action within non-action" is nothing but the natural flow that accords with the Way of Heaven. It will never, like the ordinary clinging to "having awareness," drift into vain fantasy, nor, like the clinging to "having action," fall into rigid holding, needlessly damaging the true elixir one originally possesses in full! - When the classic says "their spiritual power does not harm people," it does not merely mean that the spirit of yang movement does not harm people. It is because the great Way of "acting through non-action" is precisely the heavenly genuineness and numinous breath innate in human beings, ceaselessly generating, supremely sincere and never resting, pervading all things and omitting none. Though it has creative transformations, it truly has no birth and death, no survival or extinction; though it shows waxing and waning, it originally neither increases nor decreases. This is exactly what is called "undisturbed and unalarmed, without worry or care"; how could it harm anyone? - As for "the sage also does not harm people," it is because the sage uses this burgeoning vitality of Prior Heaven to nourish and fill out the original true face, and uses the balanced, harmonising work of Samādhi True Fire to warmly nurture the free, perfect and bright numinous elixir. One sees the spontaneous fire of the spirit forge it lightly once, and all the dark, evil influences in the body immediately retreat and melt away, and the clear, free true person naturally appears! If one calls this state, in which yin stillness and yang movement, the unseen and the seen, inside and outside "do not harm each other," who could say it is not apt? Heaven and the human world alike attribute the beauty of this true virtue to it. Alas! To bring the unseen world and the human world into mutual resonance and accord: if not through the wondrous transforming influence of the great Way's true virtue, how could one reach such a state?

Key Terms

  • Cooking a small fish (warm-nurturing fire phasing): simmering a fresh fish slowly over a gentle fire with the five flavours blended, a metaphor for how, once the internal elixir has condensed, one must avoid stirring and turning it about, and need only "unhurriedly tend the medicine furnace and watch the fire phasing; simply settle spirit and breath and leave all to nature."
  • Five Breaths return to the Origin; Three Flowers gather at the Summit: an image of an advanced stage of the alchemical Way, referring to the breaths of the five organs and five phases and the three treasures of Essence, Breath and Spirit merging perfectly into one and converging in the central palace, without any need for deliberate adding, subtracting or contrivance.
  • Ghosts and spirits in the human body: Huang Yuanji's distinctive inner interpretation. "Ghosts" stand for yin stillness without awareness, "spirits" for yang movement with action; the two are each other's root, with awareness within stillness and non-action within movement, which is "neither harms the other."
  • The wisdom of prying and the warm, cosy benevolence: respectively, the petty cleverness of exacting nitpicking and the contrived, sentimental kindness of a soft heart. Both belong to the contrived action of Posterior Heaven and run counter to the sage's great Way of non-action, reverent within and simple in government.

Putting It into Practice

  1. When something is getting better, resist the urge to keep "flipping the pan." Whether leading a team, raising children, caring for your health or running a long-term project, once the direction is right and the heat is steady, the worst thing is anxious daily meddling and fine-tuning, like flipping a fish over and over with a spatula until a perfectly good fish crumbles into mush.
  2. "Settle spirit and breath, leave all to nature" is the best everyday warm nurturing. Many imbalances of body and mind come not from too little nourishment but from too much fussing. Setting aside ten-odd minutes a day without scrolling and without judging yourself, simply breathing quietly and relaxing, keeps a gentle, unalarmed "slow fire" burning for you.
  3. In sitting, guard against "dead stillness"; in work, guard against "agitation." When still, do not sink into drowsy torpor ("awareness within non-awareness"); when busy, do not become impatient and absent-minded ("non-action within action"). When movement and stillness are rooted in each other and neither harms the other, your energy lasts longest.
  4. First cultivate your own "Way," and the "strange happenings" around you will recede of themselves. "Humans are the masters of ghosts and spirits." Many of life's frustrations, inner drains and interpersonal frictions stem from first losing one's own composure. Steady your routine, your state of mind and your principles, and many troubles will dissolve without a fight.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Wishing to stand firm oneself, one helps others to stand firm; wishing to succeed oneself, one helps others to succeed": From the Analects, "Yong Ye," Confucius's statement of the principle of humaneness as extending oneself to others.
  2. Seven portents and spirit stories from the Zuo Commentary: "A stone spoke in Jin" (Duke Zhao, year 8: Duke Ping of Jin built palaces extravagantly, and a stone spoke at Weiyu in Jin); "a comet appeared in Qi" (Duke Zhao, year 26: a comet appeared in Qi, and Yanzi urged Duke Jing of Qi to cultivate virtue to avert calamity); "snakes fought in Zheng" (Duke Zhuang, year 14: a snake inside and a snake outside the south gate of Zheng fought each other); "Boyou became a vengeful ghost" (Duke Zhao, year 7: the Zheng minister Liang Xiao, known as Boyou, haunted the living after his death, and Zichan appointed his heir to pacify him); "Shensheng manifested as a spirit" (Duke Xi, year 10: after his wrongful death, Shensheng, heir apparent of Jin, appeared to Hu Tu); "dreaming of two boys, his illness went beyond the vitals" (Duke Cheng, year 10: Duke Jing of Jin dreamt of two boys hiding above the diaphragm and below the heart, and the physician Huan said nothing could be done); "a spirit descended at Xin, and Guo was destroyed" (Duke Zhuang, year 32: a spirit descended at Xin; the Duke of Guo sought a grant of land from it and lost his virtue, and Guo was later destroyed by Jin). Huang Yuanji cites these seven events in a row to prove that "losing virtue invites calamity, and humans are the masters of ghosts and spirits."
  3. "Of old, our former kings diligently cultivated their virtue, and there were no natural calamities; the ghosts and spirits of mountains and rivers were all at peace": From the Book of Documents, "Instructions of Yi," in which Yi Yin tells Taijia that if he cultivates virtue, Heaven, humans, ghosts and spirits will all be at peace.
  4. "Without knowing or realising, they follow the laws of the Lord": From the Classic of Poetry, "Greater Odes," "Huang Yi," describing people so simple and natural that they accord with the laws of Heaven without knowing it.
  5. "Unhurriedly tend the medicine furnace and watch the fire phasing; simply settle spirit and breath and leave all to nature": A famous alchemical line, found in the Awakening to Reality tradition and quoted in alchemical books of the Yuan and Ming, stressing that during the period of warm nurturing one must avoid all contrived, reckless action.
  6. "Deceitful transformations" (zhouzhang bianhuan): Adapted from the Book of Documents, "Wuyi": "among the people none deceive one another or make illusions." 诪张 (zhōu zhāng) means deceitful trickery and stirring up trouble.
  7. Textual note: In the title, the misrecognised dash character "一" in "一两不相伤" has been removed. "太上日" in the classic text and "《书》日" and "日『其神不伤人』" in the commentary have all been corrected to 曰; "杂乱繁元" to "杂乱繁冗"; "兴妖作票" to "兴妖作祟"; "篲见于齐" to the standard character 彗; "二竖梦面病人膏育，有辛降而虢逐火亡" to "二竖梦而病入膏肓，有神降莘而虢遂灭亡," based on the Zuo Commentary stories and the character forms; "鬼即冥顶" to "鬼即冥顽"; "祀典景而鬼神安" to "祀典举而鬼神安," based on the sense and the parallelism; "吗呼！无为之洽……远取请物" to "呜呼！无为之治……远取诸物"; "幔守药炉" to "漫守药炉"; "涛张变幻" to "诪张变幻"; "无觉竞无觉" to "竟无觉"; "也战" to "也哉"; "体物不遠" to "体物不遗"; and the superfluous character in "谁日曰不宜" has been deleted to read "谁曰不宜."

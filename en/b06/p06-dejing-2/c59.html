Chapter 59 · Long Life and Lasting Vision

In governing people and serving Heaven nothing equals "frugality," like the farmer who ploughs deep and accumulates virtue. In the alchemical Way, the Mercury-child seeks the Lead-mother until child and mother embrace; and the beginner must first pass the trial of "chewing a wax pill," the battle between Heaven's principle and human desire

About This Chapter

Chapter 59 of the Daodejing pivots on "in governing people and serving Heaven nothing equals frugality," and strings together the whole path: "early submission, accumulating virtue deeply, nothing that cannot be overcome, the mother of the state, deep roots and a firm stem, long life and lasting vision." Huang Yuanji first explains the way of governing the world through "valuing agriculture and the work of sowing and reaping," then moves step by step into gathering the Medicine and refining the elixir in the dual cultivation of inner nature and life (xing and ming), and into the resolve needed to seek the Way:

  1. Heaven and humanity share one breath; governing people is serving Heaven (§2): The Way of Heaven is not far off in the clouds; it lies right before us in the people's livelihood and human relationships. When the ruler values agriculture, attends to the root and spares the people's strength ("frugality"), the people lack neither food nor clothing and rites and music flourish; it is like deep roots and a distant source, able to preserve the "mother breath" on which the state is founded.
  2. The Lead-mother and the Mercury-child, child and mother united (§3): In the actual practice of internal alchemy, laying the foundation and refining the self are like a farmer clearing a field and pulling weeds. Next one takes from Kan to fill Li, using the lead of true yang of Prior Heaven to subdue the easily volatilised mercury of Posterior Heaven, and then uses one's own true mercury to warmly nurture the Lead-mother. "Stealing the breath of heaven and earth to make the mother of the elixir," child and mother bound together in the central palace: this is the Way of deep roots, a firm stem, long life and lasting vision.
  3. Passing the trial of "chewing a wax pill," the battle between Heaven and the human (§4): The end of this chapter is especially moving. Huang Yuanji frankly describes the real difficulty of beginning practice: at first there is often no crack to enter and it tastes as bland as chewing a wax pill; principle and desire are mixed together, Heaven and the human at war, and most people wither and retreat at precisely this pass. Only by summoning a great resolve, shouldering the Way and righteousness with iron shoulders and enduring a stretch of dry blandness can one find sweetness after bitterness and enter the hall and the inner chamber.

Safety note: §3 of this chapter touches on advanced alchemical methods and coded language such as "turning the Celestial Circuit," "taking from Kan to fill Li," "the lead-metal flying and floating," and "forming the Holy Embryo." As Huang Yuanji himself warns, "before the time comes one cannot know"; do not blindly follow the metaphors of the alchemical classics to move the breath along channels with the mind or force extraordinary experiences. Ordinary practitioners should focus on moderating desire, nourishing the spirit, accumulating virtue, establishing sincerity and steadily building a sound foundation of body and mind.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：治人事天莫如啬。夫惟啬，是谓早服，早服谓之重积德，重积德则无不克，无不克则莫知其极，莫知其极可以有国，有国之母可以长久，是谓深根固蒂，长生久视之道。

Lord Lao says: In governing the people and serving Heaven, no principle is more essential than "frugality" (sparing and storing up, valuing agriculture and attending to the root). Precisely because one knows how to spare and store up, it is called "early submission" (submitting early to the great Way and subduing deluded attachments). Submitting early to the Way is called accumulating virtue deeply; when virtue is accumulated deeply, there is no obstacle that cannot be overcome; when nothing cannot be overcome, one's power and attainment are so deep and vast that no one can fathom their limit; when one's attainment is boundless, one can securely possess and protect the state; when one keeps the root mother breath on which the state is founded, the state's fortunes can endure firmly. This is called "deepening the roots and strengthening the stem": the great Way of long life without decline and lasting vision!

§2
Original (Chinese)

治人之道，即事天之道，天人固一气也，故治人所以事天，事天不外治人。莫谓天道甚远，即寓于人道至迩之中。不知天道，且观人心。能尽人事，即合天道。虽一高一卑，迥相悬绝，惟在于安民为主，民治定则天心一矣。其要在于重农务本，教民稼穑为先。夫以民为邦本，食为民天，啬事既治，则衣食有出，身家无虞，孟子所谓「树艺五谷，五谷熟而民人育」。又曰：「圣人治天下，使有菽粟如水火，而民焉有不仁者乎？」是知为人上者，以啬为急图，而民得以乐业安居，养生送死，早有以服民心于不睹不闻之际，而欣然向往，如享太牢之菜，如登春台之乐矣，是不言修德而德自修，不言尚德而德日尚。且耕三余一，耕九余三，多黍多稌，为酒为醴，以畀祖妣，以洽百礼，其德之积与积之重，不谓此而谁谓耶？如此重开有道之天，大被无穷之泽，自然兼弱攻昧，取乱侮亡，而无往不克矣。既所向披靡，无敢交锋，非特接壤邻封，云霓慰望，即彼殊方异域，亦时雨交欢。若此东被西渐，北达南通，声教四讫，伊于胡底，夫谁知其极也哉？既无其极，立见帝道遐昌，皇图巩固，而得有其国也。《汉书》云：「黄河如带，泰山若砺。国以永宁，爰及苗裔。」夫固有不爽者。人既抚有一国，即有得国之由，其由维何？国之母气也。若无母气，焉能得国？此根本之地，人所宜急讲者。在未有其国，必须寻母；既有其国，尤当恋母。国之有母，犹树之有根，水之有源，可以长久而不息。此治世之道，通乎治身。学道人，能守中抱一，凝神调息，始以汞子求铅母，继以铅母养汞子，终则铅汞相投，子母混合，复乎本来，还乎太朴，是谓深根固蒂，长生久视之道。如此则凡也而圣，人也而天矣。治身之道，又岂异治世哉？

The principle of governing the people is the very principle of serving Heaven, because Heaven and humanity are originally threaded together by one and the same breath! Therefore governing human affairs well is the best way to serve Heaven, and serving Heaven can never be separated from settling the human world. Do not imagine that the Way of Heaven is far beyond the ninth heaven; it is concretely contained in the human relationships and daily life closest to us. Cannot see the Way of Heaven clearly? Look at the human heart and you will know. If one can faithfully fulfil human affairs, one accords with the Way of Heaven right then and there. Although Heaven and earth, one high and one low, seem far apart, everything hinges on taking the peace of the people as the foundation; when the people live in peace and work happily and society is stable, Heaven's heart is in harmony and all becomes one.

The most crucial lever for bringing peace to the people lies in valuing agriculture and attending to the root, putting first the teaching of sowing and reaping ("husbandry"; here Huang Yuanji also draws on the sense of 啬 as a loan for 穑, "harvest"). The people are the root of the state, and food is the people's heaven. When farming and storage are well managed, the common people have food and clothing and no worries for themselves or their families, just as Mencius said: "Plant the five grains; when the five grains ripen, the people thrive." Mencius also said: "When the sage governs the world, he makes beans and grain as plentiful as water and fire; how then could any of the people be lacking in humaneness?"

So when those above make valuing agriculture and thrift their foremost concern, letting the people live in peace and work happily, with means to raise their families and to care for and bury their elders without regret, they have already won the people's hearts silently and unseen. The people turn to them gladly and sincerely, as joyful and contented as if enjoying a sumptuous sacrificial feast or climbing a terrace in spring to admire the view! This is precisely how, without advertising the cultivation of virtue, virtue is naturally cultivated, and without loudly exalting virtue, virtue grows loftier by the day.

What is more, three years of farming leave one year's grain in reserve, and nine years of farming leave three years' reserves; millet and rice fill the granaries, clear wine and sweet liquor are brewed to offer to the ancestors and to carry out the hundred rites. If "accumulating virtue deeply" does not refer to this, what else could it refer to? Thus one reopens a new heaven of peace and of the Way and broadly bestows boundless blessings; faced with benighted, tyrannical opponents, one naturally prevails everywhere and overcomes everything. Since one's humane virtue is unrivalled and no one dares to oppose it, not only do neighbouring states long to submit, as people in a drought long for rain clouds and rainbows, but even distant foreign lands rejoice as at a timely rain. Thus the reputation of one's civilising influence spreads in all directions, east and west, north and south, without any visible end: who could measure its limit?

Since this virtuous renown is boundless, one immediately sees the kingly Way flourishing and the foundations of the realm secure, and so one securely possesses the state. The oath for enfeoffing meritorious officials in the Book of Han says: "Even when the Yellow River is as thin as a sash and Mount Tai worn down like a whetstone, the state shall enjoy lasting peace, extending to your descendants." This law of cause and effect truly never fails in the slightest!

Once a person possesses a state, there must be a root reason why he obtained it. What is that reason? It is the "mother breath" on which the state is founded! Without this unceasingly generative root mother breath, how could one keep the state? This root is what people should most urgently understand and firmly guard: before obtaining the state, one must first seek the mother breath; after obtaining it, one must all the more cling to and guard the mother breath. A state that has its mother is like a great tree with deep roots or a river with a living source: only thus can it last without ceasing.

This great Way of governing the world is entirely continuous with cultivating the self and refining the elixir! If students of the Way can guard the centre and embrace the One, concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, they first use the true yin spirit-nature of their own body (the "Mercury-child") to resonate with and seek the true yang of Prior Heaven of heaven and earth (the "Lead-mother"); next they use the Lead-mother of true yang of Prior Heaven to nourish their own Mercury-child; and finally Lead and Mercury meet, child and mother merge seamlessly into one, returning to the original face and to the pure simplicity of high antiquity. This is precisely "the Way of deep roots, a firm stem, long life and lasting vision"! When one's cultivation reaches this point, the ordinary person becomes a sage and a true being, and the human body accords with the Way of Heaven. How could the principle of governing oneself differ from that of governing the world?

§3
Original (Chinese)

此治人事天，即尽人事以合天道。以「天人本一气，彼此感而通。阳自空中来，抱我主人翁」，非易易事也。其道不外虚无，其功同乎稼穑。始而存养省察，继而以性摄情，迨水火混融，坎离和合，先天气动，运转周天，所谓「乾坤交媾罢，一点落黄庭」是。此取坎中之满，填离中之虚，即命基筑固，人仙之功了矣。此犹治啬者，开田辟土，载芟载柞，然后可得而耕种，以树艺乎五谷也。由是再将离中阴精，下入于坎户之中，将坎中阳气，合离中阴精，配成一家，种于丹田，炼而为药，所谓「彼家无而自我有之，彼家虚而由我实之」。直待此中真铅发生，即以阳铅制阴汞，汞性之好飞者不飞矣；又以阴汞养阳铅，铅情之好沉者不沉矣。《悟真》云：「金鼎欲留朱里汞，玉池先下水中银。」待至铅金飞浮，如明窗中射日之尘，片片飞扬而去，将坎府外之余阳化尽，收入离宫，又将离己阴汞私识，一并消化，复还纯阳至宝之丹，可以升汉冲霄，飞灵走圣，即神胎成、仙婴就矣。虽然，其功岂易及者？始须持志养气，如农者之耕耘，不无辛苦，终则神闲气定，内而一理浑然，外而随时处中，非偶一为之即与道大适，由其修性炼命，早有以宾服乎后起之缘，而万累齐绝，一丝不存，尽人道以合天德也，匪伊朝夕矣。犹国家然，保赤诚求，深仁厚泽入于民心，沦肌浃髓，其德之积与积之重也，岂有涯哉？自是欲无不除，己无不克，天怀淡定，步伍安详，无论处变处常，自有素位而行、无入不得之概。若此者，以之炼性而性尽，以之修命而命立矣，冲漠无朕之中，万象森然毕具，真有莫知其底极者焉。太上所谓「内观其心，心无其心；外观其身，身无其身；远观其物，物无其物；空无所空，无无亦无。能悟之者，可传圣道」。此即外其身而身存。身犹国也，即如王者无为而治，可以正南面而有天下有国，亦如阴精在己，杂于父精母血之中者已久，非得先天阳气，不能自生自长，盖后天阴精原从先天生来，但阴精难固，情欲易摇，非得天地外来灵阳之气，必不能结而成丹，长生不死。故曰：「有国之母，可以长久。」惟圣人，以真阴真阳二气合为一气，三家融成一家，煅出黍米一珠，号曰金丹，曰真铅，曰白虎首经，要无非先天一气而已，从色身中千烧万炼，千磨万洗，渐采渐凝，时烹时炼，而金丹外成，英英有象，所谓「人盗天地之气，以为丹母」者是，是即深根固蒂，长生久视之道。夫以天地灵阳，合一己真气，结成圣胎，即古仙云「先天一阳初动，运一点己汞以迎之，于是内触外激而有象，外触内感而有灵，如磁吸铁，自然吻合」，即汞子造水府而求铅母，既得其母，复依其子，子母和谐，团结中宫，而大丹成、神仙证矣。

The "governing people and serving Heaven" of this chapter means fully carrying out one's own practical human work in order to accord with the Way of Heaven. An alchemical classic says: "Heaven and humanity are originally one breath; when they resonate, they communicate. True yang of Prior Heaven is drawn in from the void and embraces the master within me" (the Original Spirit). This is by no means easily done!

Its root principle is nothing other than "emptiness," yet the solid work of setting about it is exactly like a farmer tilling the land ("husbandry"): - First clear the wasteland and lay the foundation: At first one must constantly keep and nourish the mind and spirit and examine stray thoughts, then use the clear original nature to gather in the floating emotions and desires. When water and fire in the body merge and the conjunction of Kan and Li occurs, the true breath of Prior Heaven stirs of itself and one follows it to turn the Celestial Circuit. This is what the alchemical formula calls "when the intercourse of Qian and Kun is over, a single point of true yang drops into the Yellow Court." This step takes the single point of solid yang within the Kan trigram to fill the single point of empty yin within the Li trigram; only then is the foundation of life firmly laid, and the basic work of the human immortal reaches a stage of completion. It is just like a farmer preparing a field: one must first break open the wasteland and cut and clear away all the weeds and thorns before there can be any talk of turning the soil and sowing the five grains. - Then plant the Medicine and cook and refine it: Once the foundation is firm, one lets the essence of true yin in the Li palace sink down into the door of Kan, so that the breath of true yang in Kan and the essence of true yin in Li pair up into one household; this is sown in the lower Cinnabar Field and cooked and refined into the Medicine. This is what the alchemical classics call "what the other house originally lacks becomes truly present through my cultivation; what the other house originally has empty becomes full through my gathering and refining." Wait until the true lead of Prior Heaven (the breath of true yang) arises within, and at once use the firm, strong yang lead to subdue the restless yin mercury, so that the mind and spirit (the nature of mercury), which originally love to fly off in every direction, settle and do not fly; conversely, use the warm, moist yin mercury to nourish the heavy yang lead, so that the true breath (the disposition of lead), which easily sinks and slides downward, rises up and does not sink. Awakening to Reality puts it well: "If you wish to keep the numinous mercury within the cinnabar in the golden tripod, you must first lower the true silver (true lead) from the water into the jade pool." - Finally return to the pure-yang Holy Embryo: When the fire phasing is fine and continuous and the lead-metal becomes light and buoyant, flying and floating like the dust motes seen in sunlight through a bright window, melting flake by flake and rising up, all the residual yang outside the Kan palace is completely transformed and gathered into the Li palace; at the same time, the residual yin mercury of Posterior Heaven and the selfish cognitive spirit in the Li palace are wholly dissolved, and everything returns to the pure-yang, supremely precious Golden Elixir. At this point one can transcend the ordinary and the worldly, numinous, bright and free: the Holy Embryo is complete and the immortal infant achieved!

Even so, how could this work be easily attained? At the beginning one must grit one's teeth, hold to one's resolve and nourish the breath, like a farmer weeding and tilling under the scorching sun; real hardship is unavoidable. Only when the work has matured does the spirit become at ease and the breath settled: within, a single principle undivided and complete; without, always balanced and fitting according to the moment. This is by no means something one can attune to the great Way by sitting for a couple of days now and then! It depends entirely on cultivating nature and refining life in ordinary times, subduing early the clinging and stray thoughts of Posterior Heaven ("early submission"), so that a thousand worldly entanglements are all cut off and not a thread of defilement remains, fulfilling the human Way to accord with Heaven's virtue. This is not the work of a single morning or evening!

It is just like governing a state: caring with true sincerity, as parents protect an infant, so that deep humaneness and rich blessings soak into the people's flesh and marrow; this virtue accumulates day by day and month by month, weighty and boundless; where could its end be seen? From then on there is no selfish desire in the heart that cannot be removed and no selfishness that cannot be overcome; the heart is calm and the step serene, and whether facing upheaval or smooth times, one keeps to one's station and is at ease everywhere. Achieve this, and when used to refine nature, nature is fulfilled; when used to cultivate life, true life stands firm. In the empty, vast brightness without any trace, the myriad phenomena of the universe are nonetheless densely complete; truly it is so profound that no one can fathom its depth!

This accords exactly with what Lord Lao says in the Classic of Clarity and Stillness: "Look inward at the mind, and the mind has no mind of clinging; look outward at the body, and the body has forgotten its bodily form; look far off at things, and things have no obstructing thingness; empty even the 'emptiness,' and dissolve even 'non-being of non-being.' Only one who can awaken to this is fit to receive the Way of the sages!" This is what Chapter 7 means by "putting one's own self aside, and the self is preserved."

The human body is like a state, and the practitioner is like a sage king who governs by non-action: seated at the centre, he naturally commands the whole body. Yet the yin essence of Posterior Heaven, remaining in one's own flesh, has been mixed with the essence, blood and temperament inherited from father and mother for too long. Without the resonance and support of the true yang breath of Prior Heaven from heaven and earth, it simply cannot arise and grow by itself, because the yin essence of Posterior Heaven originally differentiated out of Prior Heaven; by nature it is fragile and hard to secure and is most easily shaken by emotion and desire. Without resonating with the numinous yang breath that comes from outside, it can never condense into the elixir and achieve long life and lasting vision. That is why the classic says: "Keep the root mother breath on which the state is founded, and one can endure!"

Only the sage who has attained the Way can unite the two breaths of true yin and true yang into one breath, merge the three houses of Essence, Breath and Spirit into one household, and forge a single mysterious pearl as lustrous as a grain of millet. Call it the Golden Elixir, call it true lead, call it the "first menses of the White Tiger": in the end it is nothing but that one One Breath of Prior Heaven! Within the physical body it passes through a thousand firings and ten thousand refinings, a thousand grindings and ten thousand washings, gathered and condensed little by little, cooked and refined and warmly nurtured at every moment, until at last the Golden Elixir is accomplished and its numinous light shines clearly. This is what the ancients called "people steal the breath of heaven and earth to make the mother of the elixir," which is the true "Way of deep roots, a firm stem, long life and lasting vision."

Using the numinous yang breath of heaven and earth, fused with the true breath of one's own body, to form the Holy Embryo is just as an ancient immortal said: "When the single yang of Prior Heaven first stirs, gently raise a single point of your own true-intention numinous mercury to meet it. Then the inner triggers the outer and a numinous image appears; the outer breath resonates with the inner spirit and true numinosity is born. Like a lodestone drawing iron, they naturally fit together without the slightest gap!" This is the Mercury-child going into the palace of water to seek the Lead-mother. Having found its mother again, the mother in turn embraces and protects the child; child and mother are harmonious and close, bound tightly together in the central palace. At this point the Great Elixir is accomplished, and the great Way of the immortals is fully realised!

§4
Original (Chinese)

夫炼丹始终本末，太上已曾道尽，学者细心体会，迹象虽相似，而精粗大有分别。然未到其时不能知，非得真师指授，亦无由明，此须天缘、地缘、人缘，三缘辏合，始可入室行工。后之学者，第一以积诚修德，虚己求师，庶可结三缘而入室，切勿一得自喜，即无向上之志，务要矢志投诚，一力前进，迤逦做去可也。惟下手之初，无缝可入，无隙可乘，不啻咀嚼蜡丸，淡泊无味。朱子云：「为学须猛奋体认，耐烦辛苦做一晌，久之苦尽甘回，闷极乐生，道进而心有得矣。」当此理欲杂乘，天人交战，最难措手。其进其退，就在此关。此关若攻得破，孔子所谓「宗庙之美，百官之富」，赏玩之不置矣。切不可萎靡不振，自家精神放弱，则终身不得其门而入焉。尤要虚其心，大其志，鼓其神，立德立功，修性修命，须知是天地间第一大事，非有大力量不能成。吾闻对云：「修来铁肩担当道义，放开辣手做出文章。」噫！世间一材一艺，小小科名之取，犹要辛苦耐烦，做几件大功德，用满腹真精神，始可为神天默佑，用观厥成，何况道也者，天大一件事乎？所以佛说「我为大事因缘下界」，吾亦尔尔。学者既遇真师，须以真心诚意，体认吾言，始可算人间一大丈夫也。

Lord Lao has in fact already explained everything about refining the elixir from beginning to end. If students quietly and carefully savour it, they will find that although the outward signs of different stages and states of the work look similar, the inner purity and coarseness differ as heaven from earth. But until the fire phasing has reached that step, one cannot truly understand it by mere speculation, and without an enlightened teacher's personal guidance and confirmation one cannot grasp it thoroughly. The three affinities of Heaven, earth and people must all be complete and come together before one can truly enter the chamber and work in earnest.

Therefore, for later students, the first essential is to accumulate sincerity, cultivate virtue, lower themselves and humbly seek a teacher; only thus can they hope to bring the three affinities together and enter the hall and the chamber. On no account should one become smug the moment one tastes a little sweetness and lose the aspiration to keep rising; one must make a firm great vow, commit oneself sincerely, gather all one's strength and press forward steadily, one solid step at a time!

But let me state the hard truth in advance: in the early days of setting to work, it often feels like a solid sheet of iron, with no seam to bore into and no crack to enter, as dry as chewing a wax pill, utterly bland and tasteless! Zhu Xi put it well: "In learning, one must summon fierce effort to realise it personally, be patient, bear hardship and grit one's teeth through a long stretch of time. Over time, sweetness naturally follows bitterness and joy is born at the height of tedium; the work advances and the heart truly gains something!"

It is at precisely this juncture, when principle and desire are mixed together and Heaven's principle and selfish desire are locked in fierce battle, that it is hardest to set one's hand to the work. Whether a person will advance and attain the Way or retreat halfway is decided entirely at this pass! Once you grit your teeth and break through it, what opens before you is what Confucius praised as "the beauty of the ancestral temple and the richness of the hundred officials," marvellous scenery that you will linger over and delight in, unwilling to put it down. On no account should you wilt and lose heart at the first dryness, draining away your own Essence, Breath and Spirit; otherwise you will never find the threshold of the great Way in your whole life!

All the more must you be open-hearted as a valley, make a great vow and summon your spirit, establishing virtue and merit and cultivating both nature and life. Know that this is the greatest matter between heaven and earth; without great perseverance and great commitment it can never succeed! I once heard a couplet: "Cultivate iron shoulders to bear the Way and righteousness; let loose a fierce hand to write true compositions." Alas! Even to learn a small craft in the human world or obtain a minor examination degree, one must patiently bear hardship and toil, perform some genuine meritorious deeds and pour in one's whole true spirit before receiving the silent protection of heaven and earth and seeing results; how much more so for "the great Way," a matter as vast as Heaven itself?

That is why the Buddha said, "I appeared in the world for the sake of one great matter." My earnest and repeated exhortations today come from the same wish. Since students have had the good fortune to read the true formulas and meet an enlightened teacher, they must bring true sincerity to personally realise these words of mine; only then are they worthy of being called great persons of the human world, standing tall between heaven and earth!

Key Terms

  • Frugality (se; early submission, accumulating virtue deeply): originally meaning to spare and store up, and anciently interchangeable with 穑 (se, the agricultural harvest). Huang Yuanji builds his argument on this double meaning: in governing the world, it means valuing agriculture, attending to the root and sparing the people's strength; in cultivating the self, it means gathering in spirit and breath, subduing early the deluded attachments of Posterior Heaven and accumulating virtue deeply.
  • Lead-mother and Mercury-child (see Lead and Mercury): the "Lead-mother" stands for the true yang breath of heaven and earth that comes through resonance in Prior Heaven (the true yang within Kan), and the "Mercury-child" for the true yin spirit-nature originally in one's own body (the true yin within Li). "The Mercury-child seeks the Lead-mother, the Lead-mother nourishes the Mercury-child" means child and mother uniting to form the Golden Elixir.
  • Mysterious pearl like a grain of millet: a classic image in the alchemical texts, describing the crystallisation of the One Breath of Prior Heaven forged from the merging of true yin and true yang and the three houses of Essence, Breath and Spirit: as minute as a grain of millet yet infinitely radiant.
  • The pass of chewing a wax pill: Huang Yuanji's incisive portrait of the bottleneck beginners face in real practice. In the early stage there are often no remarkable bodily sensations; it is dry and bland, with principle and desire at war. Only by patiently persisting for a long time, without wilting or retreating, does sweetness come after bitterness.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Turn "frugality" into everyday energy management. It is not only about saving money, but also about sparing your mind, your words, your emotions and your late nights. Reclaiming the energy scattered on pointless drains is the most concrete "accumulating virtue deeply" and "early submission."
  2. Prepare yourself mentally to get through the "wax-chewing phase." Whether in sitting meditation, study, fitness or mastering a skill in depth, there is always an initial dry, tedious "wax-chewing phase" with no visible positive feedback. Know that this is the unavoidable pass of the battle between Heaven and the human; only by getting through it does sweetness follow bitterness.
  3. The Way of Heaven lies in "the human Way closest at hand." Do not fantasise about leaving real life to seek immortals in the clouds. Fulfilling your own responsibilities, treating the family around you well and smoothing out the restlessness deep in your heart: this is the most grounded "governing people and serving Heaven."
  4. Guard against "becoming smug at a small gain" and "letting your spirit go slack." Getting carried away by a small insight, or losing heart and giving up at a minor setback, are the common ailments of those who quit halfway. Steady your resolve, shoulder your responsibilities with iron shoulders, and over time great achievement will surely come.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Plant the five grains; when the five grains ripen, the people thrive" and "make beans and grain as plentiful as water and fire…": From Mencius, "Teng Wen Gong I" and "Jinxin I" respectively, setting out the root of the kingly Way: bringing peace to the people must begin with securing their livelihood.
  2. "Much millet and much rice, made into wine and sweet liquor, offered to the ancestors, to carry out the hundred rites": Adapted from the Classic of Poetry, "Hymns of Zhou," "Abundant Year": "In an abundant year there is much millet and much rice … made into wine and sweet liquor, presented to the ancestors, to carry out the hundred rites," depicting the abundance of a good harvest and the flourishing of rites and music.
  3. "Even when the Yellow River is as thin as a sash and Mount Tai like a whetstone, the state shall enjoy lasting peace, extending to your descendants": From the Book of Han, "Table of Meritorious Officials under Gaozu, Hui, Empress Gao and Wen," the oath sworn by Emperor Gaozu of Han when enfeoffing meritorious officials. 砺 is a whetstone, 爰 means "thereupon," and 苗裔 means descendants.
  4. "If you wish to keep the mercury within the cinnabar in the golden tripod, first lower the silver from the water into the jade pool": From a seven-character quatrain in Zhang Boduan's Awakening to Reality (Song dynasty), meaning that to settle the easily volatilised mind and spirit (the mercury within cinnabar), one must first mobilise the true yang breath of Prior Heaven (the silver within water / true lead) to subdue it.
  5. "Look inward at the mind, and the mind has no mind…": Quoted from the Daoist scripture Taishang Laojun shuo chang qingjing jing (Classic of Constant Clarity and Stillness Spoken by the Most High Lord Lao).
  6. "The beauty of the ancestral temple and the richness of the hundred officials": From the Analects, "Zizhang," Zigong's metaphor for the loftiness and vastness of Confucius's Way, whose splendour can be seen only by those who find the gate and enter.
  7. "Cultivate iron shoulders to bear the Way and righteousness; let loose a fierce hand to write true compositions": Adapted from the famous couplet of the loyal Ming minister Yang Jisheng: "Iron shoulders bear the Way and righteousness; a fierce hand writes compositions."
  8. Textual note: In the classic text, "太上日" and "莫如音" have been corrected to "太上曰" and "莫如啬." In the commentary, "莫请天道甚远，即寓于人道至逐之中" has been corrected, based on the sense, to "莫谓" and "至迩"; "爱及苗裔" to "爰及苗裔"; "乾坤交婷罢" to "乾坤交媾罢"; a stray single quotation mark within "可得" has been removed; "纯阳至宝之开" has been corrected to "纯阳至宝之丹," based on the alchemical sense and the character forms; "岜易" to "岂易"; "与道大造" to "与道大适," based on the sense; "沧肌浃髓" to "沦肌浃髓"; "已无不克" and "已汞" to 己; "泰米一珠" to "黍米一珠"; "号日…日…日…" throughout to 曰; and "永子造水府" to "汞子造水府." The second half of the base text is long, and has here been divided by meaning into two sections, §3 and §4: "the whole course of refining the elixir" and "seeking a teacher and breaking through the pass."

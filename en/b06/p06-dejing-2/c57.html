Chapter 57 · Govern the State with Rectitude

Rectitude for governing the state, surprise for waging war, non-interference for winning the world: the alchemical path matches these with refining the self and keeping upright, gathering the Medicine at the furnace, and warm nurturing without action; and here, unusually, the secret of embryonic breathing, "concentrate the spirit in emptiness, merge the breath with the vastness," is laid out in full

About This Chapter

The first half of Chapter 57 of the Daodejing sets out a grand strategy for governing the world: "govern the state with rectitude, wage war with surprise, win the world through non-interference." The second half lists the ills of harsh laws and proliferating cleverness and returns to the sage's four "withouts." Huang Yuanji first matches the governance of the world point by point with the alchemical governance of the body. Then, in the second half (§3–§4), he abruptly changes course and lays bare, without holding anything back, the secret of embryonic breathing and the Yellow Court that immortals and sages of every age kept hidden:

  1. Governing the state, waging war and non-interference are the three stages of the alchemical Way (§2): Gathering in the mind and keeping to propriety in daily life is "governing the state with rectitude." When yang arises after long stillness and one stands at the furnace to gather and refine, one must read the timing and the situation and advance or withdraw as the moment demands: this is "waging war with surprise." When the Medicine is sufficient, one carries out stopping the fire and warmly nurtures the Holy Embryo, relying entirely on clear stillness and non-interference: this is "winning the world through non-interference."
  2. Keeping awareness to nourish non-action (§3): Once sitting has passed from "yang arising at the extreme of yin" into "three yang bring prosperity," one no longer rigidly counts breaths or forcibly drives the fire. Contrived effort stops, and only a single point of clear, illuminating awareness remains, knowing lucidly and constantly. It neither ties down the lively true mechanism (as with "putting the stray pig back in its pen," one must never bind it again) nor forgets to illuminate, lest one be swamped by deluded thoughts that arise and perish.
  3. The Yellow Court as "neither inside nor outside," and the secret of true embryonic breathing (§4): Huang Yuanji does something rare and states outright where "concentrate the spirit in emptiness, merge the breath with the vastness" is actually practised. One neither stares rigidly into the flesh of the belly nor lets attention drift outside the body; instead one gently concentrates and illumines the Cave of Emptiness, about 1.3 inches (cun) below the navel and not far from the skin. When ordinary breathing naturally grows faint and pauses, and the true breath of the Cinnabar Field (dantian) interpenetrates with the numinous breath of heaven and earth, one has returned to the True Breath (embryonic breathing) of the womb, when the navel was joined to the mother's navel wheel, and to "the true person breathes from the heels."

Safety note: §3–§4 of this chapter deal with advanced meditative experiences such as "the breathing of mouth and nose pauses," "embryonic breathing," and "concentrating the spirit 1.3 cun below the navel." The text's "the breath of mouth and nose pauses" refers to an extremely subtle state of breathing that appears naturally once spirit and breath are deeply tranquil; it is never deliberate breath-holding or intentional suspension of the breath! Ordinary readers must not forcibly hold their breath, press the breath down into the belly, or fixate on the lower abdomen to chase sensations of heat. Practice should always remain relaxed and natural. If you feel chest tightness, dizziness, shortness of breath or anxiety, return at once to normal, deep, unhurried breathing, get up and walk around.

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：以正治国，以奇用兵，以无事取天下。吾何以知其然乎？以此。夫天下多忌讳，而民弥贫；人多利器，国家滋昏；人多技巧，奇物滋起；法令滋彰，盗贼多有。故圣人云：「我无为而民自化，我好静而民自正，我无事而民自富，我无欲而民自朴。」

Lord Lao says: Govern the state with upright, aboveboard rectitude; wage war with unexpected stratagems; win the hearts of the world with a clear, still, non-interfering disposition that does not trouble the people. How do I know that this is so? By the following facts:

The more prohibitions and taboos there are in the world, the poorer the people become; the more sharp and luxurious implements the people chase after, the more benighted the state's customs grow; the more tricks and skills people show off, the more strange and outlandish things keep appearing; the more stringent and detailed the laws and decrees become, the more thieves and robbers there are. Therefore the sage says: "I am clear, still and act without contrivance, and the people transform of themselves; I love stillness, and the people become upright of themselves; I make no needless stirrings, and the people grow prosperous of themselves; I am without desire, and the people become simple of themselves."

§2
Original (Chinese)

孔子曰：「吾道一以贯之。」是知道只一道，而天下万事万物，无不是此道贯通流行，所谓「一本散为万殊，万殊仍归一本」是。治身治世，其大端也。治世之道，无过士农工商，各安生理，孝悌忠信，各循天良。此日用常行之事，即天下之大经，万古之大法，固常道也，亦正道也。人人当尽之事，即人人固有之良。为民上者，躬行节俭，力尽孝慈，为天下先，而又庄以莅之，顺以导之，不息机以言静镇，不好事以壮规模，一正无不正，自有风行草偃，捷于影响者焉。孟子曰：「一正君而国定矣。」又曰：「天下之生久矣」，一治一乱，循环相因，自古及今，未有或爽。虽然，治则用礼乐，乱则用兵戎。一旦两军对垒，大敌交锋，社稷安危，人民生死，系于一将，顾不重哉？虽权谋术数之学，智计机变之巧，非君子所尚，然奉天命以讨贼，仗大义以吊民，又不妨出奇制胜也，兵法所以有掩袭暗侵、乘劳乘倦、离间反间、示弱示强、神出鬼没之奇谋焉。惟以奇用兵，战无不胜，攻无不克，不伤民命，不竭民财，而万民长安有道之天，共享太平之福，不诚无事也哉？然联山河为一统，合乾坤归一人，此中岂无事事？但任他事物纷投，而此心从容坐镇，自然上与天通而天心眷顾，下为民慕而万民归依，天下于焉可取也。故曰：「唐虞揖让三杯酒，汤武征诛一局棋。」惟见天下不甚希奇，取天下亦不介意，所以胸中无事，其量与天地同，故莅中国，抚四夷，有不期然而然者。此治世之道如是。吾何以知其然哉？以治世之道，不外治身，身犹国也。视听言动，一准乎礼，心思智虑，一定以情，内想不出，外想不入，性定而身克正矣。至于静养既久，天机自动，以顺生之常道，为逆修之丹法。临炉进火，大有危险，太上喻为用兵，务须因时而进，相机而行，采取有时，烹炼有地，野战有候，守城有方，不得不待时乘势，出之以奇计也。他如药足止火，丹熟温炉，超阳神于虚境，养仙胎于不坏，又当静养神室，毫无一事于心，而后仙可成、丹可就。此治身之道，即寓治世之功。吾所以知治世之道者，即此治身之法而知之也。夫取天下者在无事，而守天下者，又不可以多事，否则兴条兴款，悬禁悬令，使斯民动辄龃龉，势必奸宄因之作弊，民事于焉废弛，天下多忌讳，而民所以日贫也。金玉玑珠，舆马衣服，民间之利器弥多，而贪心一起，欲壑难填，神焉有不昏，气焉有不浊者哉？浑朴不闻，奸诈是尚，一有技巧者出，人方爱之慕之，且群起而效尤之，于是奇奇怪怪之物，悉罗致于前。呜呼，噫嘻！三代盛时，君皆神圣，民尽淳良，令悬而不用，法设而不施，所以称盛世也。今则法网高张，稠密如罗，五等刑威，违者无赦，三章法典，犯者必诛，顾何以法愈严而奸愈出，令愈繁而盗愈多乎？盖德不足以服民心，斯法不足以畏民志耳。古来民之职为乱阶者，未有不自此刑驱势迫使然也。秦汉以来，可知矣。古圣云：「天以无为而尊，人以有为而累。」我若居敬行简，不繁冗以扰民，不纷更以误国，但端居九重之上，静处深宫之中，斯民日迁善而不知为之者，且淡定为怀，渊默自守，惟以诚意正心为事，而孰知正一己即以正朝廷，正百官即以正万民，皆自此静镇间来也。万民一正，各亲其亲、长其长，无越厥命，永建乃家，于是耕田而食，凿井而饮，日出而作，日入而息，仓箱有庆，俯仰无虞，而民自富矣。若此者，皆由上之人，顺其自然，行所无事，有以致之也。又况宁静守寂，恬默无为，一安浑浑噩噩之真，而民之感之化之者，有不底于忠厚长者之风、浑朴无华之俗，未之有也。《书》曰：「一人元良，万国以贞。」其机伏于隐微，其效察乎天地。吾愿治世者，以正君心为主；治身者，以养天君为先焉。

Confucius said: "My Way has one thread running through it." From this we see that there is only one Way, and all the myriad affairs and things in the world are threaded through and set in motion by this same great Way. This is what is meant by "the one root disperses into ten thousand particulars, and the ten thousand particulars return once again to the one root." Governing the self and governing the world are its two grandest aspects.

The constant way of governing the world is nothing more than letting scholars, farmers, artisans and merchants each make their living in peace, honouring parents, loving brothers, being loyal and trustworthy, everyone living according to their innate conscience. These utterly ordinary daily norms are themselves the great canon and great law of the world, the constant Way that never changes through the ages, and also the upright Way. If those placed above the people take the lead in frugality, exert themselves in filial piety and kindness, and set an example for the world; if they deal with the people with dignity and rectitude while guiding them in accord with their feelings; if they neither feign stillness by deadening all initiative and merely talking about calm control, nor put on a show of grandeur through love of novelty and constant stirrings: then once they themselves are upright, nothing fails to become upright with them. Of itself the people's transformation comes as swiftly as grass bending before the wind, faster than a shadow follows a form or an echo follows a sound! Mencius said: "Once the ruler is rectified, the state is settled."

Mencius also said: "The world has existed for a long time, and order and disorder have followed each other in cycles"; from antiquity to the present this has never failed. Even so, in times of peace one uses rites and music to teach, and when disorder breaks out one has no choice but to take up arms. Once two armies face each other and great foes clash, the safety of the altars of the state and the lives of countless people hang on a single commanding general: is that not a weighty charge? Although stratagems and calculations, cunning plans and shifting tactics are not what the noble person esteems in ordinary times, when one carries out Heaven's mandate to punish rebels and upholds great righteousness to rescue suffering people, there is nothing wrong with winning through surprise. That is why the art of war includes all kinds of surprising schemes: sudden raids and covert crossings, striking when the enemy is weary, sowing discord and turning the enemy's spies, feigning weakness or feigning strength, appearing and vanishing like spirits. Precisely because one is skilled at waging war with surprise, one wins every battle and takes every position, harming few lives and not exhausting the wealth of the state or the people, so that everyone under heaven enjoys the blessings of lasting peace. Is this not, in the end, a return to "non-interference"?

Yet in uniting a broken land and bringing the whole world to give its allegiance, how could there truly be nothing to do? The key is this: however affairs come rushing in from outside, the ruler's heart stays composed and steady, seated firmly at the centre. Then of itself he moves Heaven's heart above to look upon him with favour, and wins the allegiance of the people below, and the world submits as a matter of course. An old verse says: "Yao and Shun's yielding of the throne was like sharing three cups of mild wine; Tang and Wu's punitive campaigns were like a single game of chess." Only because they never regarded "possessing the world" as some rare treasure, and did not let gain or loss weigh on their minds, were their hearts clear and free of affairs, their magnanimity as wide as heaven and earth. Thus governing the central lands and pacifying the four quarters came about naturally, without being sought. Such is the Way of governing the world.

How do I know that the principle of governing the world is like this? Because the Way of governing the world never departs from governing the body: this body of ours is just like a state!

  • In daily life, looking, listening, speaking and moving all strictly keep to propriety; thoughts and deliberations are gathered into a balanced, upright disposition; deluded thoughts within do not leak out, and distracting thoughts from outside do not break in; one's nature is settled and body and mind are upright. This is "governing the state with rectitude" in self-cultivation.
  • When quiet nurturing has gone on for a long time, the vital mechanism of Prior Heaven stirs of itself, and the ordinary way of following the current of generation and dispersal is turned around into the alchemical work of reversal. Now one stands at the furnace and advances the fire, and handling the fire phasing is extremely hazardous; that is why Lord Lao likens it to "waging war." One must advance and withdraw according to the timing and act only after reading the mechanism of the breath: gathering the Medicine has its proper time, cooking and refining have their fixed place, "field battles" when the mechanism of the breath surges have their phasing, and "defending the city" when it is gathered back into the central palace has its rules. One has no choice but to wait for the right moment, ride the momentum and win by surprise. This is "waging war with surprise" in self-cultivation.
  • As for when the Great Medicine is sufficient and it is time for stopping the fire, when the elixir is ripe and one keeps the furnace warm, liberates the Yang Spirit into the Great Void, and warmly nurtures the Holy Embryo until it becomes indestructible as diamond: at that point one must once again quietly nourish the chamber of the spirit, without mixing a single affair into the mind or letting a single thought stir. Only then can the Golden Elixir be achieved and the Way of immortality be fulfilled. This is "winning the world through non-interference" in self-cultivation.

So the principle of governing the body already contains the great achievement of governing the world; I understand the laws of governing the world precisely because I have realised them through this method of governing the body!

Winning the world depends on "non-interference," and keeping the world equally forbids "making a stir." Otherwise, if one issues tedious regulations all day long and hangs up all kinds of prohibitions, so that the people run into taboos at every turn and are hampered everywhere, crafty people will inevitably exploit the loopholes and cheat, while the ordinary livelihoods of farmers, artisans and merchants fall into neglect. "The more prohibitions and taboos in the world," the poorer the people grow day by day; this is how it happens. When gold, jade, pearls, splendid carriages, horses and garments, all the implements that stir up material desire, spread ever more widely, and greed is once aroused, the abyss of desire can never be filled: how could the human spirit not become benighted, and how could the general mood not become turbid?

Simple, honest customs are heard of no more, and everyone esteems guile and craft. The moment someone produces some clever contraption, people immediately admire and covet it and rush to imitate it, so that all manner of strange, outlandish things that waste the people's strength are laid out before their eyes. Alas, how lamentable! In the flourishing ages of the Three Dynasties (Xia, Shang and Zhou), the rulers were sage and enlightened and the people simple and good; prohibitions hung there and were hardly ever used, punishments were laid down and hardly ever applied, and that is why those ages are called flourishing. And now? The net of the law is stretched as tight as a snare covering heaven and earth: the five grades of punishment spare no offender, and the stern legal codes put every transgressor to death. Yet why is it that the harsher the laws, the more wickedness springs up, and the more numerous the prohibitions, the more widespread the robbers? Put plainly, it is because those above lack the virtue to win people's hearts, and harsh laws and severe punishments alone can never hold down the will of the people! Since antiquity, every rebellion and upheaval among the people has been driven by exactly this kind of coercion through cruel punishment and relentless harassment; look at history since the Qin and Han and it becomes perfectly clear.

An ancient sage said: "Heaven is honoured for its non-action; people are wearied by their striving." If the ruler keeps himself reverent and cautious and strives for simplicity in government, does not harass the people with cumbersome decrees, and does not hinder the affairs of state by changing orders morning and night, but simply dwells serenely in the depths of the palace, seated in clear stillness, then the people will turn towards goodness day by day without feeling that anyone is forcing them. Moreover, when the one above is calm at heart, profound and self-contained, and treats making his intention sincere and his mind upright as the foremost matter, how few realise that rectifying himself rectifies the court, and rectifying the officials rectifies the people! All of this grows out of this "calm control."

Once the hearts of the people are upright, everyone loves their own parents and respects their own elders, keeps to their station and builds their household in earnest. And so they till the fields and eat, dig wells and drink, work when the sun rises and rest when it sets; every granary is full to overflowing and old and young lack neither food nor clothing. The people grow prosperous of themselves! All of this is brought about by those above following what is natural and acting without making a stir. How much more so when those above keep tranquil and dwell in silence, serene and non-acting, resting in simple, unspoiled genuineness: how could the people, quietly influenced and transformed by this, fail to acquire the manner of loyal, generous elders and return to simple, unadorned customs? The Book of Documents says: "When the One Man is supremely good, the ten thousand states are thereby made upright." Its pivot lies hidden in the subtlest movements of the mind, yet its effects shine out across heaven and earth. Therefore I sincerely hope that those who govern the world will take rectifying the ruler's heart as their foundation, and that those who govern themselves will take nourishing the Heavenly Lord of the heart (the Original Spirit) as their first task!

Original (Chinese)

此理已明，不容再赘。吾想打坐之顷，其始阳气沉于海底，犹冬残腊尽，四顾寂然。以神光下照，即是冬至阳回，此时虽有阳生，而阒寂无声，四壁萧条，仍如故也。从此慢慢气机旋运，不觉三阳开泰，而万物回春，花红叶绿，水丽山明，已见阳极之甚。天道如斯，人身奚若？惟有以头稍稍向下，以目微微下顾，即是阴极阳生。第此个工夫，不似前此下手执着一个意思去数呼吸之息，须将外火不用，内火停工，一任天然自然，随其气机之运动，但用一个觉照之心，以了照之，犹恐稍不及防，又堕于夙根习气而不自知。此即存有觉之心，以养无为之性是也。迨至觉照已久，义精仁熟，又何须存，又何须养？一顺其天然之常而已。不然，起初不用力操持，则狂猿烈马，一时恐难降伏。及至猿马来归，即孟子所谓「放豚入苙」，切不可从而束缚之，反令彼活泼自如者，转而跼蹐难安也。其法维何？《易》曰：「天地絪缊，万物化醇。」这个絪缊之气，在人身中，即是停内火外符，浑然不动，任气息之流行。在工夫纯熟者，斯时全不用意，若未到此境，觉照之心，不可忘也。若或忘之，又恐不知不觉，一念起，一念灭，转转生生，将一个本来物事，竟为此生灭之心而汩没焉。古佛云：「了知起处，便知灭处。」如此存养，久久而见起灭之始，又久久而见未有念之始，斯得之矣。

The principle above has been fully explained and needs no further words. Now let me speak about it in light of what actually happens during sitting practice:

When one first begins to sit, the yang breath in the body still lies dormant at the deepest bottom ("the sea bottom"), like the end of the twelfth month in deep winter, when all around is silent and desolate. If one now gently turns one's clear Light of the spirit downward to illumine it, this is like the arrival of the winter solstice, when the first yang begins to return (the Living Zi Hour). At the moment it first arises, although the yang breath has stirred, all around remains soundless and as desolate as before; there is no earth-shaking commotion. Then the mechanism of the breath slowly and gently begins to circle and turn, and without one noticing, the springtime scene of "three yang bring prosperity" arrives: the myriad things revive, flowers are red and leaves green, mountains bright and waters lovely, and the vitality within the body has become abundant to the utmost.

Such is the turning of the seasons in the Way of Heaven, and how could the changes of the human body in sitting be any different? In sitting one need only lower the head slightly and let the gaze turn gently downward; when stillness reaches its extreme, yang naturally arises at the extreme of yin. However, once one reaches this stage of the work, one must never, as at the beginning, cling rigidly to a single thought and forcibly count the breaths! At this point one must set aside the "outer fire" of contrived blowing and fanning, and also stop the "inner fire" of deliberate holding and manipulation, leaving everything to its natural spontaneity and following the mechanism of the breath as it flows and moves by itself. One need only keep a fresh, clear "illuminating awareness," lucidly and gently watching over it, for fear that at the slightest lapse of attention one might unwittingly slide back into old habitual tendencies and stray thoughts. This is what is called "keeping a mind of awareness in order to nourish the nature of non-action."

Once this illuminating awareness has been used for a long time and the work has become ever more refined and seamless, why then deliberately "keep" anything, or deliberately "nourish" anything? Simply following the constancy of what is natural is enough. The reason is simple: at the start, if one does not put effort into gathering and holding the mind, the "mad monkey and wild horse" of the mind cannot be tamed for some time. But once the monkey-mind and horse-will have obediently come home, as in Mencius's words, "the stray pig has already run back into its pen," one must on no account go and tie it up with rope! That would only cramp the originally lively, free vitality of Prior Heaven into restless confinement.

So how exactly should one proceed? The Book of Changes says: "Heaven and earth intermingle in dense profusion, and the myriad things are transformed and made rich." In practice, this dense, rich intermingling breath within the human body means stopping all deliberate inner fire and outer tallies, letting body and mind remain wholly unmoving and allowing the true breath to flow of itself. For a seasoned practitioner whose work is fully mature, there is no need at this time to raise even the slightest intention. If one has not yet reached that degree of ripeness, however, the gentle "illuminating awareness" must on no account be forgotten. If one forgets to illuminate, one may well find, before one knows it, a thought arising here and a thought vanishing there, arising and perishing in an unbroken chain, until one's originally clear and pure "original thing" is drowned by this mind of arising and perishing. An ancient Buddha said: "Know clearly where a thought arises, and you will know where it ceases." Keeping and nourishing like this, closely and continuously, over a long time one comes to see the source where thoughts arise and cease; nourishing still longer, one directly sees the original empty brightness "before any thought has arisen." Only on reaching this point has one truly attained the Way!

§4
Original (Chinese)

至于黄庭之说，在不有不无，不内不外，又在色身中，又不在色身中。此个妙窍，到底在何处？古所谓「凝神于虚，合气于漠」是也。夫凝神于虚，合气于漠，亦犹是在丹田中，但眼光不死死向内而观耳，神气不死死入内而团耳，惟凝神于脐下，离色身肉皮不远，此即不内不外之说也。以意凝照于此，但觉口鼻呼吸之气一停，而丹田之气，滚滚辘辘，在于内外两相交结之处，纽成一团，直见絪絪缊缊，浑浑沦沦，悠扬活泼之机，一出一入，真与天之元气，两相通于无间。生精、生气、生神，即在此处，与天相属不远，此即「合气于漠」之说也。昔人谓之「元气」、「胎息」、「真人之息以踵」者，非此而何？所谓元气者，即无思无虑、无名无象中，浑沦一团，清空一气是也。所谓胎息者，盖人受气之初，此身养于母腹，此时口鼻未开，从何纳气而生？惟此脐田之气，与母之脐轮相通，是以日见其长。及至呱地一声，生下地来，此气即从口鼻出入往来，所谓「各立乾坤」者此也。吾示脐轮之气，与外来之天气相接，不内不外，絪缊混合，打成一片，即是返还于受气之初，而与母气相连之时，即是胎息也。所谓「真人之息以踵」者，盖以真人之息，藏之深深，达之亹亹，视不见，听不闻，搏不得，深而又密，如气之极于脚底是也。彼口鼻之气，非不可用，但当顺其自然，不可专以此气为进退出入。若第用此气，而不知凝神于脐下一寸三分之地，寻出这个虚无窟子，以纳天气于无穷，终嫌清浊相间，难以成丹。昔人云：「天以一元之气生人」，此气非口非鼻，非知觉运动之灵可比。又云：「玄牝之门世罕知，休将口鼻妄施为。饶君吐纳经千载，怎得金乌搦兔儿？」即此数语观之，明明道「出玄入牝」，实在脐下丹田，离肉一寸三分之间，氤氤氲氲，凝成一片者是。学道人，无论茶时饭时，言语应酬时，微微用一点意思，凝神于虚无一穴之中，自然合气于漠，直见真气调动，有不可名言之妙。然于此调息，则知觉不入于内，而坎水自然澄清。此历代仙圣不传之秘，吾今一口吐出，后之学者，勿视为具文而忽之也。

As for the "Yellow Court" so often mentioned in the alchemical classics, it is always described as "neither existent nor non-existent, neither inside nor outside, both within the physical body and not within the physical body." Where exactly is this marvellous opening? It is in fact what the ancients called "concentrate the spirit in emptiness, merge the breath with the vastness"!

What does "concentrate the spirit in emptiness, merge the breath with the vastness" mean? Put plainly, its location is still around the lower Cinnabar Field. The key is this: do not let the eyes bore rigidly into the belly and stare at it, and do not force spirit and breath rigidly into the flesh and clench them into a lump; instead, gently gather the mind and spirit below the navel, at the empty boundary not far from the surface of the skin. This is the true meaning of the alchemical classics' "neither inside nor outside"!

Concentrate a single gentle point of True Intention and let it illumine this place. When stillness deepens, you will feel the coarse everyday breathing of mouth and nose (ordinary breathing) seem to grow faint and pause of its own accord, while the true breath in the lower Cinnabar Field rolls and melts, turning softly and continuously, twisting together into a single mass in the empty realm where inside and outside the body meet. Everything becomes dense and profuse, undivided and whole; a lilting, lively vitality goes out and comes in with each exhalation and inhalation, truly and seamlessly interpenetrating with the One Breath of Prior Heaven of heaven and earth! The generation of Essence, of Breath and of Spirit in the human body all takes place here, only a hair's breadth from the original source of Heaven. This is the reality of "merging the breath with the vastness" (the breath becoming one with the boundless Great Void).

The ancients called this "original breath" and "embryonic breathing" (True Breath); Zhuangzi called it "the true person breathes from the heels." Is this state not exactly what they meant?

  • What is "original breath"? It is the clear, empty single breath that coalesces as an undivided whole in the luminous emptiness free of thought and deliberation, free of name and image.
  • What is "embryonic breathing"? When a person first receives breath and takes form, growing in the mother's womb, the mouth and nose have not yet opened to breathe: by what means, then, does the fetus take in breath and grow? Entirely through the true breath of the Cinnabar Field below the navel, which communicates directly with the mother's navel wheel through the umbilical cord; that is why the fetus grows day by day. Once it is born with a cry and the cord is cut, the person's breathing shifts to going in and out through the mouth and nose; this is what is called "each establishing its own Qian and Kun." The navel-wheel breath I am now pointing out to you reconnects, at the place that is neither inside nor outside, with the heavenly breath of great nature, intermingling densely and fusing into one piece. This is like returning to the state at the beginning of conception when one was joined to the mother's breath: this is true "embryonic breathing"!
  • What is "the true person breathes from the heels"? It does not literally mean breathing through one's heels. It describes how the true breath of one who has attained the Way is hidden extremely deep and sunk extremely low, moving in an unbroken, continuous stream: the eyes cannot see it, the ears cannot hear it, the hand cannot grasp it, so deep, settled and fine that the breath seems to reach all the way down to the soles of the feet.

As for the everyday breathing of mouth and nose, it is not that it can never be used, but it should only be allowed to follow its natural course; one must never rely solely on the in-and-out exchanges of mouth-and-nose breathing to serve as the advance and withdrawal of the alchemical method. If one only knows how to drill mouth-and-nose breathing exercises, without knowing to gently concentrate the spirit 1.3 cun below the navel and recognise that open, spacious Cave of Emptiness in order to take in the inexhaustible heavenly breath, one will in the end remain mixed with the clear and turbid breaths of Posterior Heaven and find it hard to form the true elixir.

An ancient said: "Heaven gives birth to human beings through the breath of the One Origin." This breath is not in the mouth nor in the nose, nor can it be compared with the liveliness of ordinary sensory perception and bodily movement. An old alchemical poem also says: "The gate of the Mysterious Pass and the Mysterious Female few in the world know; do not fiddle wildly with the breathing of mouth and nose. Even if you practise breathing exercises for a thousand years, how could you truly bring together the essence of sun and moon (the Golden Crow and the Jade Hare)?" Understand these lines and everything becomes perfectly clear: "going out at the Mysterious and entering at the Female" lies quite concretely in the empty realm below the navel, in the Cinnabar Field, 1.3 cun from the skin, where the breath condenses into a single dense, misty mass!

Those who study the Way, whether drinking tea, eating a meal, or talking and dealing with people in daily life, can keep a slight, gentle intention like this, letting the mind and spirit rest lightly in this one opening of emptiness. Then they will naturally "merge the breath with the vastness" and gradually experience an indescribable marvel as the true breath within the body adjusts itself. Moreover, when one regulates the breath at this place that is neither inside nor outside, the confused perceptions of the cognitive spirit of Posterior Heaven do not burrow inward to muddy the mechanism of the breath, and the Kan water in the conjunction of Kan and Li naturally becomes ever clearer and more limpid.

This was originally a supremely secret mind-method transmitted orally by immortals and sages of every age and never lightly divulged in writing. Today I have laid it out in full in one breath; let later students on no account treat it as an ordinary piece of writing on paper and pass it over lightly!

Key Terms

  • Keeping a mind of awareness to nourish the nature of non-action: after yang arises in sitting, one stops contrived fire phasing, neither manipulating deliberately nor abandoning a clear, illuminating awareness, so as to avoid falling into torpor and distraction or being carried off by deluded thoughts that arise and perish.
  • Yellow Court (neither inside nor outside): here Huang Yuanji breaks the attachment to "rigidly guarding the flesh inside the belly," pointing out that the marvellous opening of the Yellow Court is really an empty realm below the navel, not far from the skin, where the mechanisms of breath inside and outside merge.
  • Concentrate the spirit in emptiness, merge the breath with the vastness: the general principle of advanced Daoist embryonic breathing. The mind and spirit gather lightly in empty brightness without fixating on the flesh, and the breath naturally communicates with the boundless original breath of heaven and earth.
  • True Breath (embryonic breathing / the true person breathes from the heels): unlike the coarse in-and-out ordinary breathing of mouth and nose, the spontaneous breath of Prior Heaven that appears when body and mind are deeply tranquil, in which the true breath of the navel wheel intermingles with the numinous breath of heaven and earth, deep, settled and fine as though reaching to the soles of the feet.
  • Cave of Emptiness: in this chapter, specifically the empty, luminous marvellous realm about 1.3 cun below the navel, neither inside nor outside, where the clear breath of heaven and earth is received and the Kan water is settled and clarified.

Putting It into Practice

  1. When managing a team or a household, start by "subtracting." "The more stringent the laws and decrees, the more thieves and robbers": the more cumbersome and harsh the rules and the more suspicion there is, the more readily people comply outwardly while evading inwardly and exploiting loopholes. Hold firm on the essentials, lead by example, give ample trust and room, and you will achieve twice the result with half the effort.
  2. Once a habit is formed, loosen the tight "rope." Just as "once the stray pig is back in its pen, never tie it up again," breaking a bad habit or learning to sit still at first requires rules and effort; but once the rhythm is steady, drop the tense sense of deliberate self-monitoring and let the good state flow naturally.
  3. In sitting, never "stare at the belly" or "hold the breath to chase embryonic breathing." Huang Yuanji specifically warns: "do not let the eyes rigidly look inward, do not rigidly force spirit and breath inward into a lump." Rest your attention lightly in the warm, open, spacious area just in front of the lower abdomen and let the breath follow its natural course; this is actually the easiest way into a deep, relaxed state.
  4. Carry "a slight thought returning to emptiness" into tea time and mealtimes. Truly calming the spirit does not only happen in half an hour of sitting. While walking, drinking tea or dealing with people, keep a small share of unhurried awareness resting in the lower body, and you will immediately feel steadier and less easily carried away by the moods of your surroundings.

Translator's Notes

  1. "The one root disperses into ten thousand particulars, and the ten thousand particulars return to the one root": A proposition often used in Song Neo-Confucianism (Cheng Yi, Zhu Xi and others), meaning that all phenomena of the cosmos spring from a single source and ultimately return to a single principle.
  2. "Yao and Shun's yielding of the throne was like three cups of wine; Tang and Wu's punitive campaigns were like a game of chess": A maxim widely circulated from the Song through the Qing (attributed by some to Shao Yong, or traced to the tradition of drama and poetry talks), describing the broad-mindedness of sage kings who did not cling to power, treating both abdication and conquest with equal composure.
  3. "When the One Man is supremely good, the ten thousand states are thereby made upright": From the Book of Documents, "Taijia III," meaning that when the ruler alone is benevolent and pure, all the states of the world follow him onto the upright path.
  4. "Putting the stray pig back in its pen": Adapted from Mencius, "Jinxin II": "like chasing a stray pig: once it is back in its pen, they go on to tie it up." 苙 (lì) means a pigpen, and 招 here means tying the pig's legs; the image means that once the monkey-mind and horse-will have returned to their place, they must not be forcibly bound again.
  5. "The true person breathes from the heels": From Zhuangzi, "The Great Ancestral Teacher": "The true person breathes from the heels; the multitude breathe from the throat," meaning that one who has attained the Way breathes in an extremely deep, settled and fine way, unlike the shallow, cramped breathing of ordinary people confined to the throat.
  6. "The gate of the Mysterious Female few in the world know … how could you bring the Golden Crow to grasp the Hare": Found in a quatrain of Zhang Boduan's Awakening to Reality (Wuzhen pian, Song dynasty); the original line reads 争得金乌搦兔儿, where 搦 (nuò) means to seize or bring together. The Golden Crow stands for the sun / the true yin within Li, and the Jade Hare for the moon / the true yang within Kan.
  7. Textual note: In the classic text, "人多利，国家滋昏" has been supplemented with the missing character to read "人多利器," following the received editions and the commentary's own "民间之利器弥多." In the commentary, "孔子日," "又日" and "《易》日" have been corrected to 曰; "两车对垒" to "两军对垒"; "社樱" to "社稷"; "外想不人" and "日人而息" to 入; "金玉现珠" to "金玉玑珠"; "气焉有不独者哉" to "不浊," following the preceding "神焉有不昏"; "不有不自此……便然也" to "未有不自此……使然也"; "仓籍有庆" to "仓箱有庆" (the original edition's footnote 1 says "庆: the base text has 度; corrected after the Xiao Tianshi edition"; it has been moved into these notes and the interlinear footnote removed); "四顾叙然" to "四顾寂然"; "党照" (twice) to "觉照"; "放豚入芝" and "从而来缚之" to "放豚入苙" and "从而束缚之"; "天地绸縕" and "铟縕" throughout to "絪缊"; "所销「各立乾坤」" to "所谓"; and "达之疊疊" to "达之亹亹." The second half of the base text is long, and has here been divided by meaning into two sections, §3 and §4: "illuminating awareness nourishing non-action" and "the secret of the Yellow Court and embryonic breathing."

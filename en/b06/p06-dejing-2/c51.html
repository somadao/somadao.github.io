Chapter 51 · Revering the Way and Treasuring Virtue

The Way gives birth to all things and Virtue nourishes them, so all creatures revere the Way and treasure Virtue by pure nature; cultivating the Way likewise follows the four seasons, awaiting the stirring of yang at the Mysterious Pass—acting without presuming, presiding without controlling

About This Chapter

This chapter traces a continuous arc from cosmic generation to internal alchemical practice, centering on the four watchwords "revering the Way and treasuring Virtue" and "Mysterious Virtue" (xuande). Huang Yuanji unfolds it on two levels:

  1. The Way is the shared source; Virtue is each creature's own portion: The "Way" is the boundless Great Emptiness and clear, vacuous One Breath on which all things rely in common; "Virtue" is the heavenly principle and earthly breath each creature receives for itself. Thus "the Way gives birth to them, Virtue nourishes them" lays the root, while "matter shapes them, circumstances complete them" brings them gradually to full form and vigor. All creatures revere the Way and treasure Virtue spontaneously—not because anyone commands it, but because without them nothing could live.
  2. Cultivating the Way means "stealing the pivot" in rhythm with the four seasons to return to the source: In practice, one first lays aside all entanglements and concentrates Spirit in emptiness until, out of undivided chaos, a true vital impulse suddenly stirs (the first opening of the Mysterious Pass, like yang returning at the winter solstice)—this is "the Way giving birth." Next, one gently warms and nourishes it in stillness while gathering it in movement, like lush summer vegetation—this is "Virtue nourishing." Bringing the Holy Embryo to maturity and recovering one's original face, like the autumn harvest, is "completing and ripening." Finally, nurturing it through "three years of nursing and nine years of facing the wall" until pure yang returns to the root is "tending and sheltering." Throughout the entire course, though there are advancing and retreating fire times, there is not a trace of pride or force; Original Spirit naturally presides over all—this is the Mysterious Virtue of "giving birth without possessing, acting without presuming, presiding without controlling."

Safety note: The latter half of this chapter uses advanced traditional alchemical fire-phasing terms such as "the first stirring of the Mysterious Pass," "gentle and martial fire adjustments," and "ablution and sealing." In daily quiet sitting, simply let body and mind grow relaxed and calm, breathe naturally, and avoid impatience; never rigidly lock attention onto a spot or force the movement of breath, lest you provoke head pressure, chest tightness, or mental agitation.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：道生之，德蓄之，物形之，势成之，是以万物莫不尊道而贵德。道之尊，德之贵，夫莫之命而常自然。故道生之，德蓄之，长之育之，成之熟之，养之覆之。生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰，是谓玄德。

Lord Lao says: The Way gives birth to all things; Virtue nourishes them; matter gives them form; circumstances and momentum bring them to completion. Therefore, among the myriad things, none fails to revere the Way and treasure Virtue. This reverence for the Way and treasuring of Virtue is decreed by no one—it is always spontaneously so.

Hence the Way gives birth to them and Virtue nourishes them, causes them to grow and rears them, forms them and ripens them, tends them and shelters them. Giving birth without claiming possession, acting without presuming on merit, presiding over them without imposing mastery—this is called "Mysterious Virtue."

§2
Original (Chinese)

道，无名也，无名即无极，所谓清空一气，天地人物公共生生之本。以其非有非无，不大不小，无物不包涵遍覆，故曰大道。德者，万物得天之理以成性，得地之气以成形，物各得其所得，无稍欠缺者，故曰大德。道即万物所共之太极也，德又万物各具之太极也。是故万物资生，本太虚之理，一元之气，溥博弥纶，无巨细，无隐显，莫不赖此道以为生而托灵属命。阴阳燮理于其中，日月斡旋于其内，有如草木然，日夜之所息，雨露之所润，而得以培植其本根，是即「道生之，德蓄之」也。万物得所涵育，则薰蒸陶熔，始而有气，久则有形，由是潜滋暗长，日充月盛，而人成其为人，物成其为物，又即「物形之，势成之」也。惟其生也以道，蓄也以德，万物虽繁，皆无遗漏，是以万物莫不以道为尊，以德为贵焉。盖道为生人之理，非道则无以资生；德为蓄物之原，非德则无由蕴蓄。道之尊，德之贵，为何如乎？然皆自天而授，因物为缘，不待强为，天然中道，无事造作，自能合德，若或使之，莫或命之，而常常如是，无一勉强不归自然者。

The Way has no name; the Nameless is the Boundless (wuji)—that clear, vacuous One Breath which is the shared root of ceaseless generation for heaven, earth, humanity, and all creatures. Because it is neither tangible "being" nor sheer "non-being," neither bounded by greatness nor exhausted by smallness, and there is nothing it does not embrace and overspread, it is called the Great Way. As for "Virtue," every creature receives Heaven's principle to form its inner nature and Earth's breath to form its physical frame; each obtains its own proper share without the slightest lack—hence it is called Great Virtue. To put it simply, the Way is the one Supreme Ultimate shared by all things, while Virtue is the Supreme Ultimate individually complete in every single thing.

Therefore, the life-sustaining nourishment of all creatures stems from the principle of Great Emptiness and this One Breath of Prior Heaven. Vast and all-pervading, encompassing the entire cosmos, whether great or tiny, hidden or manifest, nothing fails to depend on this Way for life and to entrust its spirit and destiny to it. Yin and yang harmonize and regulate within it, and sun and moon revolve inside it—just like plants and trees that rest quietly through day and night and are moistened by rain and dew, thereby thickening and firming their roots. That is precisely "the Way gives birth to them, Virtue nourishes them." Once nourished and sheltered in this way, all things are steamed, tempered, and molded by the breath of heaven and earth: at first there is only a subtle breath; over time it condenses into form; then it grows quietly in secret, fuller day by day and richer month by month, until humans become fully human and things become fully themselves—that is "matter shapes them, circumstances complete them."

Precisely because all things are born by the Way and nourished by Virtue, however countless the species of the world may be, not one is left out; hence none fails to hold the Way in highest reverence and Virtue in highest esteem. After all, the Way is the fundamental principle that sustains human life—without it there is no way to live; Virtue is the fountainhead that nurtures all creatures—without it there is no means to grow. How immense, then, are the dignity of the Way and the worth of Virtue! Yet all of this is bestowed by Heaven and unfolds according to each creature's conditions: without forced effort it naturally hits the central Way; without artificial contrivance it effortlessly accords with Great Virtue. It seems as though some hidden power moves it, yet no one ever issued a command; always thus, eternally thus, without a trace of strain, everything returns to spontaneity.

§3
Original (Chinese)

是道也，何道也？天地大中至正之途，圣人成仙证圣之要也。欲修金仙者，舍道奚由入哉？是以凝神于虚，合气于漠，虚无之际，淡漠之中，一元真气出焉，此即道之生也。道既生矣，于是致养于静，取材于动，一真在抱，万象咸空，常操常存，勿忘勿助，则蓄德有基矣。然顺其道而生之，则道必日长；因其德而蓄之，则德必日育。以长以育，犹物之畅茂繁殖，一到秋临，而成熟有期也。夫道之既成，且熟如此，而其间以养以覆，又岂有异于人哉？要不过反乎未形之初，复乎不二之真而已矣。究之，生有何生？其生也，一虚无之气自运，我又何生之有，而敢以为有乎？虽阳生之候，内运天罡，外推斗柄，似有为也，而纯任自然，毫无矜心作意于其际，非「为而不恃」者欤？以此修道，则德益进而道日长，自然造化在手，天地由心，虽万变当前，亦不能乱我有主之胸襟。此不宰而宰之胜于宰也，非深且远之玄德哉？

What Way is this? It is the grand, central, utterly upright path of heaven and earth, and the key by which sages become immortals and realize sainthood. For anyone wishing to cultivate the Way of the Golden Immortal, leaving this path aside, by what other gate could one enter?

Therefore, the practitioner concentrates Spirit in clear emptiness and merges breath into quiet serenity. Right at that juncture of utter emptiness, within that tranquil calm, a single stream of unitary True Breath quietly emerges—this is "the Way giving birth" in actual cultivation. Once the Way has come to birth, one gently nourishes it in stillness and gathers its impulse in movement, holding fast to that one spark of truth while emptying out the ten thousand phenomena. Constantly watching over it and keeping it present—neither forgetting through slackness nor forcing growth through haste—one lays a firm foundation for "nourishing Virtue."

Following the Way to bring it forth, the Way grows stronger day by day; relying on Virtue to nurture it, Virtue deepens day by day. Growing and rearing thus, like crops and trees spreading lush foliage in full vitality, the moment autumn arrives, ripening and harvest are assured. And once the Way is thus formed and ripened, what is so strange or exotic about the work of tending and sheltering it? At bottom, it is nothing more than returning to the source prior to physical form and restoring that pure, undivided authenticity.

Pressing to the root of the matter: in this "giving birth," what has been produced? Its arising is merely that one Breath of emptiness revolving on its own—what did my personal ego produce that I should dare claim it as private property? Even at the moment yang arises, when inwardly turning the celestial pivot and outwardly moving the Handle of the Dipper, it may appear like deliberate action, yet at heart it rests wholly in spontaneity without the slightest pride or forced contrivance—is this not "acting without presuming"? Cultivating the Way in this spirit, Virtue advances ever deeper and the Way grows ever broader; naturally the pivot of creation rests in one's hand and heaven and earth move in accord with one's mind, so that even ten thousand shifting events right before one cannot disturb a heart that has its own steady master. Presiding without trying to control—far superior to forceful control—what else is this but profound, far-reaching "Mysterious Virtue"?

§4
Original (Chinese)

此言人能盗天地之元气，以为丹本，而后生之蓄之，长之育之，以还乎本来之天，即得道矣。然欲盗天地之元气，须先识天地之玄关。玄关安在？鸿蒙未判之先，天地初开之始，混混沌沌中，忽然感触，真机自动，此正元气所在也。而修炼者，必采此以为开头，有如群阴凝闭，万物退藏，忽遇冬至阳回，即道生矣。由是成性存存，温养于八卦炉中，久久气势充盈，一如夏日之万物畅茂，即德蓄矣。物生既盈，花开成实，一如秋来之万宝告成。其在人身，养育胎婴，返转本来面目，即成之熟之矣。物既成熟，仍还本初，一如冬日之草木成实，叶落归根，还原返本，《易》云「硕果不食」，又为将来发生之机。其在人身，三年乳哺，九载面壁，炼就纯阳之体，实成金色法身，必须养之覆之，而后可飞空走电。然下手之初，岂易臻此？必须万缘齐放，片念不存，空空洞洞，静候阳生。虽然，其生也，原来自有，而不可执以为有；即用升降之术，进退之工，未免有为，要皆顺气机之自然，而无一毫矫强，非有为而不恃所为耶？至德日进，道日长，而文武抽添，沐浴封固，无不以元神主宰其间，此有主而无主，无宰而有宰存焉。如此修道，道不深且远哉？故曰玄德。

What this chapter truly teaches is that if a person can "steal" the primordial Breath of heaven and earth as the root of the Elixir, and then step by step bring it to birth, nourish it, let it grow, and rear it until returning to one's original Heaven of Prior Heaven, that is attaining the Way.

Yet to harvest the primordial Breath of heaven and earth, one must first recognize the Mysterious Pass of heaven and earth. Where is the Mysterious Pass? Not in some rigid anatomical spot, but prior to the division of primordial chaos, at the very instant heaven and earth first split open—when your mind rests in undivided stillness without a single stray thought, and suddenly a subtle sensation stirs and the true vital impulse moves of itself. Right there is where primordial Breath abides!

The practitioner must harvest this very first vital impulse as the starting point. It is just like deep winter when yin congeals and locks everything away and all creatures withdraw into hiding, and suddenly the winter solstice arrives and yang turns back—this is "the Way giving birth." From here, preserving one's perfected nature without loss, one gently warms and nourishes that spark of True Breath within the Eight-Trigram furnace of the body; over time its momentum fills and brims, like all plants flourishing in midsummer—this is "Virtue nourishing." Once growth reaches fullness, blossoms open and set fruit, just as autumn brings all the myriad treasures of the fields to harvest; in the human body, this is nurturing the Holy Embryo to maturity and recovering one's original face—this is "completing and ripening." When the fruit has ripened, it returns to its origin, just as winter trees form seeds and shed their leaves back to the roots to revert to the source; as the Book of Changes says in hexagram Bo (Splitting Apart), "the great fruit is not eaten"—that plump remaining seed holds the vital sprout of future renewal. In cultivation, this corresponds to the ancients' "three years of nursing and nine years of facing the wall": having refined a body of pure yang and realized the golden Dharma Body, one must still tend, shelter, and conceal its brilliance before attaining unhindered freedom and swift transformation.

At the outset of practice, however, how could one easily arrive at this? One must lay down every worldly entanglement, keep not half a stray thought in mind, and wait quietly in open emptiness for yang to arise of itself. Even then, when it arises, it was originally inherent in heaven, earth, and humanity—never cling to it as some prized "possession." Even when employing methods of ascent and descent or advancing and retreating work, which inevitably involve some deliberate action, everything must follow the natural drift of the vital impulse without a hair's breadth of strain—is this not acting without relying on one's own doing? Once Virtue advances daily and the Way deepens daily, every adjustment of gentle and martial fire, every timely ablution and sealing, is presided over solely by Original Spirit within. In this state there seems to be a master yet no deliberate master is seen; there seems to be no controlling hand yet everything is steadily governed. Cultivating the Way like this, how could one's Way fail to be deep and far-reaching? Hence Laozi praises it as "Mysterious Virtue."

Key Terms

  • One Breath of Prior Heaven: Called "clear, vacuous One Breath" and "unitary True Breath" in this chapter, referring to the primordial life-force shared by heaven, earth, humanity, and all creatures—the starting point of "the Way gives birth to them."
  • Mysterious Pass: Not a fixed anatomical organ; this chapter explicitly defines it as the moment "prior to the division of primordial chaos, at the first opening of heaven and earth, when out of undivided stillness a subtle sensation suddenly stirs and the true vital impulse moves of itself."
  • Handle of the Dipper: The handle of the Big Dipper, used in antiquity to mark the four seasons; in internal alchemy it symbolizes True Intention turning in rhythm with the vital impulse and timing the phases of practice.
  • Fire phasing and ablution: Gentle and martial adjustments govern the lightness or vigor of awareness and breath, while ablution refers to the pause midway through the cycle when one withdraws active effort to warm and nourish without contrivance.
  • Holy Embryo and spontaneous Dharma Body: Alchemical metaphors for the clear, luminous fruition matured when pure yang condenses—symbolizing the state in which life and inner nature have wholly returned to their source.
  • Original Spirit: The innate, luminous prior-heaven master as distinct from posterior discursive thought; this chapter states that "Original Spirit presides over all," meaning effortless, natural awareness free of calculating intent.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Subtract first, then wait for vitality to stir. Many people sit in meditation or approach projects impatient to "produce something," which only breeds restlessness. This chapter reminds us that only when we lay aside entanglements and quiet the mind does genuine inner vitality surface on its own.
  2. Practice "neither forgetting nor forcing." Whether building a good habit, studying, or quieting the mind, slackness leads to abandonment ("forgetting"), while anxious pushing burns out enthusiasm ("forcing"). Following natural rhythms day by day, as one tends a plant, is true "nourishing of Virtue."
  3. After succeeding, do not claim credit or over-control. With a team, a child's growth, or one's own cultivation, learn to step back after giving wholehearted care ("giving birth without possessing, presiding without controlling"). The less you interfere out of a urge to dominate, the more naturally both others and yourself mature.
  4. Bring the "four-seasons perspective" into daily life. Low ebbs are winter storage; steady effort is summer growth; after a harvest, remember to gather your energy back to the root. Never let winter's stillness make you doubt spring's return, nor demand autumn fruit the day a seed sprouts.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Completes them and ripens them" (cheng zhi shu zhi): The received Wang Bi edition of the Daodejing reads ting zhi du zhi (亭之毒之); the Heshang Gong edition and many Daoist commentaries read cheng zhi shu zhi (成之熟之), meaning to shape and ripen them, which Huang Yuanji's text follows.
  2. "Harmonize and regulate yin and yang" (yinyang xieli): Xie (燮) means to harmonize. The phrase comes from the Book of Documents ("Officers of Zhou"), originally describing a prime minister harmonizing state governance with cosmic yin and yang, here adapted to the Way's own inward harmonization.
  3. "Preserving one's perfected nature" (chengxing cuncun): From the Book of Changes, "Appended Remarks, Part I": "Preserving one's perfected nature is the gateway to the Way and righteousness"—meaning fulfilling innate nature and keeping it intact over time.
  4. "The great fruit is not eaten" (shuo guo bu shi): From the top yang line of hexagram Bo (Splitting Apart) in the Book of Changes, referring to the last large fruit left unpicked on the branch to become the seed of next year's rebirth—a classic image of reversal at the extreme returning to new life.
  5. "Three years of nursing, nine years of facing the wall": A traditional metaphor combining internal alchemy and Chan Buddhism, signifying that even after initial attainment one must continue long, patient nourishment (like a mother nursing an infant) and deep contemplative stability (like Bodhidharma's nine years facing the wall), never stopping halfway.
  6. Textual note: Woodblock/OCR misprints in the base edition have been corrected according to context, including 草术 → 草木 ("plants and trees"), 雨簬 → 雨露 ("rain and dew"), 鸿濠 → 鸿蒙 ("primordial chaos"), 兵机 → 真机 ("true vital impulse"), 芭易 → 岂易 ("how could it be easy"), and 请 / 日 → 谓 / 曰 in closing formulas.

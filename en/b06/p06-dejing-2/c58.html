Chapter 58 · Fortune Leans on Misfortune

When government is prying, the people turn to cunning; when cultivation falls into contrived action, it drops into the yin-yang cycle of fortune and misfortune. Only by taking non-action and naturalness as the substance, and forgetting the trap once the fish is caught, can one be square without cutting and bright without dazzling

About This Chapter

Chapter 58 of the Daodejing left us the proverb everyone knows, "Misfortune is what fortune leans on; fortune is where misfortune lies hidden." Through the stark contrast between "government that is dull and muddled" and "government that is sharp and prying," it points to the sage's cultivated state of being "square without cutting, edged without wounding, straight without overbearing, bright without dazzling." Huang Yuanji applies this dialectical wisdom to both politics and the actual practice of internal alchemy:

  1. Dull simplicity versus prying cunning (§2): Government that follows non-action seems lax and plain, yet the people are honest and settled; government that is overly exacting and shrewd seems to see every detail, yet it drives the people to scheming and exploiting loopholes. Whatever falls into contrived action of Posterior Heaven is inevitably bound up with the waxing and waning of yin and yang, with fortune and misfortune leaning on each other, with opposites giving rise to each other.
  2. Refining life through action, ultimately returning to non-action (§3): Although the alchemical Way, when it begins with refining life (ming), cannot do without expedient names such as fire phasing, the Medicine, dragon and tiger, and tripod and furnace, these are all merely the fish trap and the rabbit snare. If one clings to rigidly guarding the lower Cinnabar Field and stubbornly fixes on the river-cart route of the Celestial Circuit without awakening to the substance of non-action, then even if one achieves something, it will only fall into the yin substance of Posterior Heaven and will hardly escape the cycle of yin and yang, fortune and misfortune.

Safety note: §3 of this chapter specifically criticises the one-sided practice of "talking about the elixir and at once rigidly guarding the Cinnabar Field, stubbornly fixing on the river-cart route." Beginners in sitting meditation should never fixate on one point of the body or forcibly use the mind to drive the breath along a route inside the body. Relaxation and stillness of body and mind, and natural non-action, should be the foundation, to avoid headaches, chest tightness or mental tension caused by clinging and contrivance.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：其政闷闷，其民醇醇；其政察察，其民缺缺。祸兮福所倚，福兮祸所伏，孰知其极？其无正耶？正复为奇，善复为妖。人之迷，其日固久。是以圣人方而不割，廉而不刿，直而不肆，光而不耀。

Lord Lao says: When government is lenient and plain and does not pry into details, the people are honest and simple; when government is shrewd and exacting and inspects everything, the people become sly and estranged and love to exploit loopholes. Misfortune! It is precisely where fortune leans; fortune! Within it misfortune already lies hidden. Who can fathom the end of this endless cycle? Is there then no constant, unchanging standard in the world? But look: what was originally upright swiftly slides into the crooked, and what was originally a good measure swiftly turns into something monstrous. People have been caught in this delusion for a very long time indeed!

Therefore the sage who has attained the Way is square yet does not cut and injure others with rigid edges; scrupulous and principled yet not sharp enough to wound the hand; straight yet not overbearing towards others; bright within yet not flaunting a dazzling light outward.

§2
Original (Chinese)

天地无心而化育，帝王无为而平成，此无为之道，圣人开天辟地、综世理物之大经大法，人主统摄万民，纲纪庶物，无有过于此者。若涉于有为，则政非其政，治非其治，虽文章灿著，事业辉煌，而欲其熙熙皞皞，共乐时雍之化也不能，故孔子云：「政者，正也」，以己正，正人之不正也。自古为民上者，肇修人纪，整饬天常，有知若无知，有作若无作，一任天机之自动，初无有妄作聪明，创矩陈规，悬书读律，而一德相感，自有默喻于言语之表者，故其政闷闷，若愚朴无知者然，而其民之感孚，亦醇醇有太古之风，无稍或易。上以无为自治，下以无为自化，上下共安无事之天，休哉，何其盛欤！苟为上者，励精图治，竭力谋为，拔去凶邪，登崇俊杰，小善必录，大过必惩，赏罚无殊冰镜，监观俨若神明，其政之察察，无有逃其藻鉴者，此岂不足重乎？而无如上好苛求，下即化为机巧，缺缺然无不以小智自矜；上以有为倡之，下以有为应之，甚矣，民心之难治也！夫非上无以清其源，斯下无以正其本也哉！盖无为者，先天浑朴之真；有为者，后天人为之伪。闷闷察察，其效纯驳如此，此可知道，一而已，二之则非。况先天，太极未判，纯朴未分，无阴阳之可名，无善恶之可见，《易》曰：「易则易知，简则易从」，其政之所以可大可久也。若后天，太朴不完，贯阴阳于始终，互祸福为倚伏，祸中有福，福中有祸，祸福所以循环无端也。故有为之为，未必不善，但物穷则变，时极则反，阴阳往复之机，原属如此，有孰知其底极而克守其正耶？且正之复则为奇，善之反则为妖。无为之政，政纯乎天；有为之政，政杂以人。杂以人者，正中有奇，善中有妖，其机肇于隐微，其应捷于影响，其势诚有不容稍间者，无怪乎尔虞我诈，习与性成，执迷而不悟也，其日固已久矣。是以圣人御宇，一本无为之道，整躬率物，正己化人。本方也，不知其为方，殆达变通权而不假裁截者欤？本廉也，竟忘其为廉，殆混俗和光而不致伤残者欤？时而直也，虽无唯诺之风，亦非径情之遂，认理行持，不敢自肆，其梗概风规，真有可敬可畏者。他如化及群生，恩周四表，几与星辉云灿，上下争光，而独自韫藏，不稍炫耀，其匿迹销声，为何如哉！此无为为体，自然为用，从欲以治，顺理以施，四方风动，有不於变时雍，共游于太古之天也，有是理乎？

Heaven and earth have no selfish mind, yet they transform and nurture the myriad things; sage kings are clear, still and non-acting, yet they bring about an age of great peace. This Way of non-action is the great canon and great law by which sages order heaven and earth and manage the affairs of the world; for a ruler who leads the people and holds together every trade and calling, there is no principle higher than this.

Once one falls into deliberate, meddling "action," the decrees are no longer good decrees and governance loses its true flavour. However brilliant the ritual codes and writings may look on the surface, however grand and spectacular the achievements and pageantry, it is utterly impossible to bring the people to peaceful contentment and a shared civilising influence of harmony and peace! That is why Confucius said: "To govern means to be upright." First make yourself upright, then influence and rectify the crookedness of others.

Since antiquity, the truly wise rulers set the bonds of human relationships in order and upheld the constant norms of Heaven's principle. Though they saw clearly into the affairs of the world, they seemed wholly unknowing; though they accomplished things, they seemed to make no effort, letting everything follow the natural workings of Heaven's mechanism. They never presumed to be clever by setting up vexatious rules or hanging up legal codes every day to frighten people; they relied entirely on pure, generous virtue to move people's hearts, so that the people naturally understood beyond words. Therefore their government looked lax and "dull," as if simple-minded and unknowing, and the people, moved by it, were "honest and simple," keeping the pure manners of high antiquity unchanged. The ruler governed himself by non-action, the people transformed themselves by non-action, and those above and below together enjoyed peaceful years free of affairs: how beautiful, how flourishing!

Conversely, if the ruler is eager to show off his abilities and racks his brains meddling everywhere, intent on rooting out the wicked and promoting the talented, recording merit for the tiniest good deed and severely punishing the slightest fault, with rewards and punishments as clear as an ice mirror and surveillance of officials and people as strict as if possessed by a god, so that with such "sharp and prying" shrewdness nothing escapes his discerning eye, would this not at first glance seem worthy of esteem? Yet, quite unexpectedly, the more exacting and fault-finding those above become, the faster those below learn to be slippery and cunning; everyone trusts to luck and preens themselves on petty cleverness. Those above use scheming action to control, and those below respond at once with scheming action to evade. How hard it truly is to govern people's hearts through calculation! In the end, is it not because those above have muddied the source themselves that those below cannot set the root right?

For "non-action" belongs to the genuineness of the simple wholeness of Prior Heaven, while "action" belongs to the artifice of human contrivance in Posterior Heaven. Lenient dullness and exacting prying yield results that differ so starkly in purity! From this we can see that the great Way is originally pure and single, without duality; once it is split into two, it goes astray.

In the realm of Prior Heaven, the Supreme Ultimate has not yet divided and pure simplicity is whole and complete; there are no names of yin and yang in opposition, and no signs of good and evil in conflict. The Book of Changes says: "What is easy is easily known; what is simple is easily followed." This is the secret of why government by non-action can be great and lasting. But once one falls into Posterior Heaven, this complete simplicity is broken: the opposition of yin and yang runs through from beginning to end, and misfortune and fortune lean on and lie hidden in each other. The root of fortune lies buried within misfortune, the bud of misfortune hides within blessing, and so fortune and misfortune revolve in an endless cycle.

Therefore human, contrived "action" is not necessarily ill-intended at the outset, but things reverse when they reach their extreme and change when they are exhausted; such is the original law of the back-and-forth of yin and yang in Posterior Heaven. Who can fathom its bottom line and keep to the upright forever? The upright, pushed too far, slides into the crooked; the good, pushed too far, turns into the monstrous. Government by non-action accords purely with the Way of Heaven; government by action is mixed with the private cleverness of the human heart. Once the human heart is mixed in, the crooked hides within the upright and the monstrous grows within the good; the first signs arise in the subtlest places, yet the evil consequences come faster than a shadow follows the body, and the momentum allows not the slightest chance of escape! No wonder people have gradually come to deceive one another and to make scheming second nature; they have persisted in their delusion without awakening for far too long.

For this very reason, when the sage governs the world, he rests entirely on the Way of non-action, rectifying himself to set an example for all things and cultivating himself to transform others: - Square without cutting: His heart is square and measured, yet he does not pride himself on his squareness; he knows how to adapt flexibly to the times and never uses a rigid frame to cut and injure others. - Edged without wounding: His integrity is pure and principled, yet he forgets his own loftiness; he knows how to soften his light and mingle with the dust, living plainly, and never uses his sharpness to pierce others. - Straight without overbearing: He is upright and open, with neither the obsequious air of a yes-man nor the rashness of acting wilfully on his own whims; he holds to Heaven's principle and conducts himself steadily, never overbearing towards others. That upright strength of character truly inspires both respect and awe. - Bright without dazzling: As for his civilising influence reaching all beings and his benevolence spreading across the four seas, its brilliance almost vying with the sun, moon, stars and clouds, he alone keeps himself deeply concealed and does not flaunt it in the least. How profound is that self-cultivation that shows no sharp edges!

This is taking non-action as the substance and naturalness as the marvellous function, governing in accord with the people's rightful wishes and acting in accord with the constant Way of Heaven's principle. When one reaches this point, the four quarters move as if stirred by the wind; how could the people fail to become harmonious and turn towards goodness, roaming together in the pure customs of high antiquity?

§3
Original (Chinese)

道曰大道，丹曰金丹，究皆无名无象，在天则清空一气，在人则虚无自然。修炼始终，要不出此而已。人能知冲漠无朕，是大道根源、金丹本始，从虚极静笃中，养得浑浑沦沦，无知识、无念虑之真本面，则我之性情精气神，皆是先天太和一气中的物事，以之修道则道成，以之炼丹则丹就，又何奇邪可云，危险可畏哉？惟不知无为为本，第以有为为功，则知识不断，纷扰愈多，虽有性有情，皆后天气质之私，物欲之伪，至于精气神，又乌得不落后天有形有色之杂妄耶？太上以政喻道，以民比身。道炼先天无为，则成不坏金身；道炼后天有识，安有不二元神？纵炼得好，亦不过守尸鬼耳，乌能超出阴阳，脱离生死，永为万代神仙？又况一堕有为，则太极判而阴阳分，阴阳分而善恶出，祸福于以相往来也。孰知修道之极功，虽其中炼命一步，不无作为之用，然必从「有用用中无用，无功功里施功」，方不落边际。孟子曰：「必有事焉而勿正。」修道之要，即在于此。论人心，一动则有一静，一阴则有一阳，邪正善恶，原是循环相因，往来不息，故有正即有邪，有善即有恶。惟一归浑忘，不分正邪，安有善恶？否则正反为奇，善复为妖。庄子曰：「天以无为为尊，人以有为为累。」是知有为之时，亦必归于无为，方免倾丹倒鼎之患。无奈世上凡夫俗子，开口言丹，即死守丹田，固执河车路径，即在身形之中，其未了悟无为之旨也久矣。惟圣人，知修炼之道，虽有火候药物、龙虎男女、鼎炉琴剑，种种名色，犹取鱼兔之筌蹄。鱼兔未得，当用筌蹄；鱼兔入手，即忘筌蹄。若著名著象，皆非道也。故方则方之，廉则廉之，直则直之，光则光之，要皆为无为、事无事，一归浑穆之天焉。愿学者，以无为自然之道为体，体立然后用行，虽有为，仍是无为也。知否？信否？

Let us bring this chapter back to cultivation. The Way is called the "Great Way," the elixir is called the "Golden Elixir," but ultimately both are without name and without image: in Heaven they are the clear, empty, limpid One Breath of Prior Heaven; in human beings they are the original state of mind, empty and natural. From the first moment one sets to work until the work is complete and the fruit full, one never goes beyond these few words.

If a person can realise that the empty, vast brightness without the slightest trace is the root of the great Way and the starting point of the Golden Elixir, and from then on, in utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, nourishes the original true face, whole and undivided, without the cunning knowledge of Posterior Heaven and without confused thoughts, then my own inner nature and dispositions, Essence, Breath and Spirit are all pure and genuine things within the One Breath of supreme harmony of Prior Heaven. Cultivate the Way with this true breath of Prior Heaven and the Way is accomplished; refine the elixir with it and the elixir is achieved. What crookedness or deviation could there be to speak of, what dangers to fear?

But people do not understand that "non-action" is the root and devote themselves entirely to "action" as their work: the cunning discriminations in their minds never stop, and their inner turmoil grows ever greater. The nature and dispositions cultivated in this way are entirely the selfishness of the temperament of Posterior Heaven and the falseness of material desire; and as for the Essence, Breath and Spirit in the body, how could they avoid falling into the mire of the mixed falsehoods of Posterior Heaven, with its forms and colours?

Here Lord Lao uses "government" as a metaphor for "cultivating the Way" and "the people" as a metaphor for "one's own body." If cultivation of the Way begins from the non-action of Prior Heaven, one can refine an indestructible diamond body; if one merely circles within the contrived action of the cognitive spirit of Posterior Heaven, how could one cultivate a pure and single Original Spirit? Even if you train hard and gain a fine physique or some small supernatural power, you are merely a "corpse-guarding ghost" clinging to the shell of flesh. How could you leap out of the bonds of yin and yang, escape the cycle of birth and death, and achieve the eternal, free great Way?

What is more, once one's practice falls into the action of Posterior Heaven, the whole Supreme Ultimate is immediately split into the two halves of yin and yang; once yin and yang divide, the opposition of good and evil appears, and fortune and misfortune follow, coming and going and entangling without end. Little do people know that in the most profound fire phasing of cultivating the Way, although the step of refining life cannot avoid borrowing a little contrived effort, one must achieve what the ancients called "within useful function, not clinging to use; within effortless work, secretly applying true work"; only then does one avoid falling into the dead ends of clinging and one-sidedness. Mencius said: "There must be work going on in the mind, but do not expect results" (bi you shi yan er wu zheng). The most essential secret of cultivating the Way lies entirely here!

Looking at the human heart of Posterior Heaven alone, every movement has its stillness and every yin has its yang; crooked and upright, good and evil arise from one another in cycles, coming and going without cease. As soon as one sets up an "upright" in the heart, a "crooked" immediately arises opposite it; set up a "good" and an "evil" immediately arises opposite it. Only by returning completely to simple wholeness and forgetting all scheming, dissolving even the distinction between upright and crooked, could the entanglement of good and evil, fortune and misfortune, no longer arise. Otherwise the uprightness one deliberately advertises soon turns crooked, and the goodness one deliberately flaunts soon turns monstrous.

Zhuangzi said: "Heaven is honoured for its non-action; people are wearied by their action." From this we see that even when one has no choice but to borrow contrived effort, its final destination must be non-action; only then can one avoid the disaster of "overturning the elixir and upsetting the tripod," losing all one's earlier work. Alas, the common people of the world, the moment they open their mouths to talk of refining the elixir, rigidly fix their eyes on the lower Cinnabar Field, cling stubbornly to the river-cart route of the Celestial Circuit, and devote themselves to fussing within the flesh-and-blood physical body. They have failed to grasp the principle of "non-action" for far too long!

Only the sage who has attained the Way sees clearly: although the Way of cultivation sets up all kinds of names, such as fire phasing, the Medicine, dragon and tiger, male and female, tripod and furnace, zither and sword, they are all just like the bamboo fish trap and the rabbit snare (quan and ti). Before the fish or rabbit has been caught, one must of course use the trap and the snare; once the fish or rabbit is in hand, one immediately forgets the tools. If one clings rigidly to names and images, none of it is the true Way.

Therefore the sage is square when it is fitting to be square, scrupulous when it is fitting to be scrupulous, straight when it is fitting to be straight, bright when it is fitting to be bright. In the end it is all "acting without action, engaging in affairs without affairs," returning wholly to the rich, simple Heaven of naturalness. I sincerely hope that students will first establish the Way of non-action and naturalness as their foundational substance; once the substance stands firm, all the marvellous functions can unfold, and then even when there is action, at its core it is still clear, still non-action! Do you understand? Do you truly believe it?

Key Terms

  • Dull (menmen) and prying (chacha): Laozi uses "dull" (lenient and plain) as a figure for the non-acting governance of Prior Heaven, and "prying" (exacting and shrewd) for the contrived government of Posterior Heaven; Huang Yuanji further extends these to the non-action of Prior Heaven and the contrivances of the cognitive spirit of Posterior Heaven in cultivating the Way.
  • Corpse-guarding ghost (shoushi gui): a term of reproach among alchemists for those who do not know how to cultivate the Original Spirit of Prior Heaven and the great Way of non-action, but merely circle around the fleshly body and rigidly guard the breath channels; even if they prolong their lives or gain some small spiritual powers, they can hardly escape yin and yang, birth and death.
  • "There must be work going on, but do not expect results": from Mencius, "Gongsun Chou I"; Huang Yuanji often cites it as a mind-method for the fire phasing of internal alchemy: one must maintain close, continuous awareness ("work going on") yet must never deliberately force or anticipate results ("do not expect").
  • Trap and snare (forgetting the trap once the fish is caught): from Zhuangzi, "External Things"; signifies that the names in the alchemical classics, such as fire phasing, the Medicine, dragon and tiger, and tripod and furnace, are all tools for entering the Way; once the Way is attained, one must let go of attachment to them and never mistake the finger for the moon.

Putting It into Practice

  1. When managing people and affairs, leave some room for "dullness." Watching everything too closely and tightening rules to the extreme ("prying") only drives teams and family members to be defensive and to go through the motions. Focus on what matters, let go of the small stuff and lead by example, and trust and initiative will grow.
  2. Keep a measure of clarity in good times and a measure of composure in hard times. "Misfortune is what fortune leans on; fortune is where misfortune lies hidden." When things go your way, do not forget the hidden risks; when you suffer a setback, do not rush to despair: many turning points grow out of the low points.
  3. The highest form of cultivation is "not hurting others." Being disciplined and upright yourself (square, scrupulous, straight, bright) is admirable, but if it makes you sharp-tongued and arrogant, wounding others at every turn, that is "cutting, wounding, overbearing, dazzling." The truly noble person is strict with themselves and generous with others.
  4. Tools are only "traps and snares"; do not mistake the method for the goal. Meditation techniques, note-taking systems and alchemical terminology alike are tools to help us return to calm and peace; once clinging to their form starts producing anxiety, it is time to "forget the trap" decisively.

Translator's Notes

  1. Old gloss on the classic text: The base text appends an old gloss after the classic text: "闷闷 (menmen) means plain and simple. 缺缺 (quēquē) means careless. 刿 (guì) means to wound and injure." In keeping with the editorial conventions, it has been moved into these notes. 缺缺 is also understood as sly, discontented or estranged; 刿 (guì) means to cut or pierce.
  2. "To govern means to be upright": From the Analects, "Yan Yuan," Confucius's reply to Ji Kangzi's question about government.
  3. "What is easy is easily known; what is simple is easily followed": From the Book of Changes, "Great Commentary I," praising the Way of Qian and Kun as easy and simple, readily known and readily practised, and therefore lasting and great.
  4. "Transformed and made harmonious" (wu bian shi yong): From the Book of Documents, "Canon of Yao": "the black-haired people were transformed and became harmonious," meaning that the people's customs changed greatly and became peaceful and concordant. 於 is read wū.
  5. "Empty and vast, without a trace" (chong mo wu zhen): From the Surviving Works of Cheng Yi (Song dynasty): "Empty and vast, without a trace, yet the myriad images are already densely present," describing the substance of the Way in Prior Heaven as empty and vast, without any visible trace, yet containing all phenomena.
  6. "There must be work going on, but do not expect results": From the chapter on nourishing the flood-like breath in Mencius, "Gongsun Chou I," meaning that nourishment must not be interrupted, yet one must not anxiously anticipate results or pull up the sprouts to help them grow.
  7. "Trap and snare": From Zhuangzi, "External Things": "The fish trap exists for the fish; once you have the fish, you forget the trap. The rabbit snare exists for the rabbit; once you have the rabbit, you forget the snare." It expresses the relationship between means and ends.
  8. Textual note: In the title, the misrecognised dash character "一" in "一 祸兮福倚" has been removed. "太上日" in the classic text and "道日大道" in the commentary have been corrected to 曰; "熙熙嗥皞" to "熙熙皞皞"; "整伤天常" to "整饬天常"; "此岂不是重乎" to "此岂不足重乎," based on the sense and the character forms; "御字" to "御宇"; "惟诺" to "唯诺." The stray garbled characters "J7" at the start of §3 have been deleted; "冤皆无名无象" has been corrected to "究皆无名无象"; the superfluous comma in "道炼，先天无为" has been deleted; and the closing quotation mark after "无功功里施功" has been corrected.

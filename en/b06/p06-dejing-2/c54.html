Chapter 54 · Cultivating It in Oneself

Self, family, village, state and world are five layers of one source, matching the five stages of refining Essence, Breath, Spirit and emptiness and uniting with the Way; yet the Great Way, in the end, is only ordinary accord with principle—living at ease and dying in peace

About This Chapter

Chapter 54 of the Daodejing opens with "what is well established cannot be uprooted, what is well embraced cannot slip away," and unfolds a sequence of cultivating Virtue from oneself out to the family, the village, the state and the whole world. Huang Yuanji builds a rigorous bridge between the Confucian program of "cultivating oneself, ordering the family, governing the state and pacifying the world" and the actual practice of Daoist internal alchemy, going deeper layer by layer:

  1. Principle and Breath set up the pole, and Virtue's blessings run long (§2–§3): Even a thing with form as mighty as Mount Tai will one day collapse; only formless principle and Breath stay ever new through the ages. The sage takes the Limitless as the foundation of the self and the Original Spirit as its function; Spirit and Breath interact and embrace the One. Within, one's life is established by oneself; without, humane Virtue blesses a hundred generations and the descendants' sacrifices never cease.
  2. The five-layer progression is five stages of the elixir (§4): Cultivating oneself is like refining Essence into Breath; ordering the family, like refining Breath into Spirit; transforming the village, like refining Spirit to return to emptiness; governing the state, like refining emptiness to unite with the Way; and pacifying the world is becoming one with the Way, purely spontaneous. These five steps are not a broken staircase but a deepening from near to far in which Essence, Breath and Spirit are never separated from beginning to end.
  3. Fine distinctions about inner and outer medicine, Kan and Li, Lead and Mercury (§5): Huang Yuanji, unusually, pierces the veil of alchemical terminology. The so-called inner and outer medicine, Kan and Li as water and fire, Green Dragon and White Tiger, the extracting and adding of Lead and Mercury—none of this means rigidly identifying the heart as Li and the kidneys as Kan, nor hacking Essence, Breath and Spirit into three pieces. They are layered images for a single Breath being cooked and refined in the body, rising and descending, warmly nourishing the Holy Embryo until it leaves the womb and returns to emptiness.
  4. The Great Way is ordinary: follow principle and rest in destiny (§6): At the close the chapter widens its view and bluntly punctures the world's obsession with "living forever." Immortals are not people who cling to life and fear death; they see through life and death as day and night, rising and lying down, seeking only that the Spirit be preserved and principle be whole, following heaven and resting in destiny. Anyone who harbors the likes and dislikes of craving life and loathing death has long since thrown their Spirit into disorder and lost their nature; they have no share in immortality.

Safety note: §5 of this chapter touches on advanced internal-alchemy methods and coded alchemical language such as "subduing the Heavenly Mainstay within, pushing the Handle of the Dipper without, advancing and withdrawing the River Chariot, extracting lead and adding mercury, and warmly nourishing the Holy Embryo." Ordinary readers must not take these words at face value or forcibly use the mind to drive Breath through the body or circulate Essence and Breath in reverse. If discomfort such as a swollen feeling in the head, palpitations, chest tightness, agitation or tension in the lower abdomen arises during quiet sitting, stop the visualization at once, get up and walk slowly, and return to natural breathing and ordinary daily routine.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：善建者不拔，善抱者不脱，子孙祭祀不辍。修之于身，其德乃真；修之于家，其德乃余；修之于乡，其德乃长；修之于国，其德乃丰；修之于天下，其德乃普。故以身观身，以家观家，以乡观乡，以国观国，以天下观天下。吾何以知天下之然哉？以此。

Lord Lao says: One who is good at establishing a foundation lays one that cannot be uprooted; one who is good at embracing and holding keeps a root that never slips away; and by this unbroken blessing of Virtue, the sacrifices of children and grandchildren will never cease. Cultivate the Great Way in your own person, and its Virtue is genuine and solid; extend that cultivation to your family, and its Virtue is abundant and to spare; extend it to your village, and its Virtue is long and lasting; extend it to the state, and its Virtue is rich and great; extend it to the whole world, and its Virtue is universal and all-embracing.

Therefore, observe other persons through your own person, all families through your own family, all villages through your own village, all states through your own state, and the whole world through the Great Way of the world. How do I know that the world is truly so? By this very principle of extending from oneself to others.

§2
Original (Chinese)

天地之生人也，赋之气以立命，即赋之理以成性，理气原来合一，性命两不相离，要皆清空一气，盘旋天地，盈虚消息，纯乎自然，造化往来，至于百代者也。人类虽有不齐，造物纵有不等，而此气同，即此理同，终无有或易者。圣人居中建极，亭亭直矗，独立而不倚，中行而不殆，虽穷通得丧，忧乐死生，万有不同，而此理此气，流行于一身之中，充塞乎两大之内，绝不为之稍挫，谓非「善建者不拔」乎？否则，有形有质，即岩岩泰山，高矣厚矣，犹有崩颓之患，盖以有形者，虽坚固而难久。惟无形之理气，不随物变，不为数迁，历万古而常新焉。此道立于己，化洽诸人，自然深仁厚泽，沦肌浃髓，斯民自爱戴输忱，归依恐后，无有一息之脱离不相联属者。虽曰胶漆相投，可谓坚矣，水乳融洽，可谓和矣，而聚散无常，变迁亦易，不转瞬而立见睽违。惟仁心仁闻，被其泽者，爱之不忘，即闻其风者，亦怀之不置，何异子弟之依父兄、臂指之随身心，无有隔膜不属者，谓非「善抱者不脱」乎？自此君子贤其贤而亲其亲，小人乐其乐而利其利，无非垂裳以治，共仰无为之休。圣人虽不常存，而其德泽之深入人心者，终古未尝稍息。《诗》曰：「子子孙孙，勿替引之。」其斯之谓乎？昔孔子赞舜之大孝曰：「宗庙享之，子孙保之。」足见德至无疆，子孙祭祀，亦万古蒸尝不绝，千秋俎豆维新。语云：「有十世之德者，必有十世子孙保之；有百世之德者，必有百世子孙保之。」至于大德垂诸永久，虽亿千万年，而子孙继继绳绳，愈悠久，愈繁盛，其理固有如是之不爽者。此皆以无为自然之道，内修诸己而不坠，外及诸人而勿忘，所以天休滋至，世享无穷焉。人以此道修之于一身，而形神俱妙，与道合真，道即身，身即道，是道是身，两无岐也，德何真乎？以道修诸于一家，亲疏虽异，一道相联，亲者道亲，疏者道疏，亲疏虽别，道无二也，德何余乎？且道修之乡，乡里联为一体；道修之于国，国家视如一人，其德之长之丰又何如乎？果能静镇无为，恬淡无欲，自然四方风动，天下归仁，民怀其德，无有穷期，德何普乎？此非以势迫之，以利啖之也，盖本固有之天良，以修自在之身心，如游子之还家、故老之重逢，其乐有莫之致而至者。人与己，异体而不异心，同命而应同性，故明德即新民，安人由修己，无或异也。况乡为家之所积，国为乡之所增，天下之大，万民之众，无非一家、一乡、一国之所渐推而渐广，愈凑而愈多。知一人之道，即家国天下之道；一己之修，即家国天下之修。反求诸己，顺推诸人，自有潜孚默化，易俗移风，而熙熙皞皞，共乐其乐也。故曰：「有德化而后有人心，有人心而后有风俗。」其道在乎身，其德及乎家，而其化若草偃风行，无远弗届，将遍乡国以至于天下。呜呼噫嘻！吾何以知天下之然哉？以此故也。

When heaven and earth produce human beings, they bestow a breath of original Breath to establish life, and at the same time bestow a portion of heavenly principle to complete the nature. Principle and Breath were originally one; inner nature and life have never been separate. In the end both are a single clear, empty, limpid original Breath circling between heaven and earth, waxing and waning, ebbing and growing, wholly of itself, the workings of creation coming and going and continuing unbroken for a hundred generations.

Though human beings are uneven in endowment and creatures differ in a thousand ways, the Breath they receive is the same, and so the principle they possess is the same; it has never changed in the least. The sage dwells firmly in the center and sets up the pole, standing straight and tall like a solitary peak, walking alone without leaning on outer things, keeping to the center in action and never in peril. Whether meeting hardship or success, gain or loss, sorrow or joy, life or death—however different the circumstances—this principle and this Breath always fill his person and pervade the space between heaven and earth, never suffering the slightest setback from outer conditions. Is this not "what is well established cannot be uprooted"?

Look at the opposite: anything with form and substance, even something as towering as Mount Tai, high and massive as it is, still carries the threat of crumbling and collapse, because things with form, however solid, can hardly endure. Only formless, imageless heavenly principle and original Breath do not change with outer things, are not shifted by the turns of fate, and remain ever new through the ages.

Establish this Great Way first in yourself, then let it gently transform and influence others, and humane heart and deep blessing will naturally soak into people's very flesh and marrow. The people will love and serve with heartfelt loyalty, vying to come and follow, never leaving or breaking away for a single moment. People say that being "close as glue and lacquer" is firm enough and "blended like milk and water" is harmonious enough, yet worldly bonds gather and scatter unpredictably and change most easily; in the blink of an eye friends can turn into estranged enemies. Only genuine humane Virtue and good repute leave those who receive their blessing forever grateful, and even those who merely hear of their example cherish them always—how is that different from sons and younger brothers depending on fathers and elder brothers, or arm and fingers obeying body and mind? There is no barrier between them at all. Is this not "what is well embraced cannot slip away"?

From this, the noble above honor the worthy and love their kin, the common people below enjoy their own contentment and benefit from their own livelihoods, and the sage-king merely lets his robes hang down and folds his hands like Yao and Shun, governing without action, while all under heaven bathe together in the blessing of great peace and purity. Though the sage's body cannot remain in the world forever, the blessing of his Virtue, deeply rooted in people's hearts, never ceases through a thousand ages. The Classic of Poetry says: "Sons and grandsons, do not let it lapse; carry it on forever." Is this not exactly that state?

Long ago Confucius praised the great filial piety of Shun, saying that he "received the offerings of the ancestral temple and was preserved by his descendants." Thus when Virtue is vast and boundless, the sacrifices of the descendants naturally continue unbroken through the ages, the incense ever renewed for a thousand autumns. An old saying goes: "One who accumulates the Virtue of ten generations will surely have ten generations of descendants to carry it on and protect it; one who accumulates the Virtue of a hundred generations will surely have a hundred generations of descendants to carry it on and protect it." As for the sage who hands great Virtue down to eternity, even through hundreds of millions of years his line continues without end, growing more flourishing the longer it lasts—so unfailing is the law of heaven's recompense. All of this comes from following the Way of non-action and spontaneity: cultivating oneself within without ever slackening, and blessing others without without ever forgetting them. Therefore the blessings sent down by heaven grow ever richer, and generation after generation enjoy them without end.

When a person cultivates this Great Way in their own person, form and spirit both enter the wondrous and merge with the true Way: the Way is the body and the body is the Way, with no difference whatsoever between them—how genuine that Virtue is! When the Great Way is extended to cultivating a family, though family members are near or distant, they are linked by one and the same Way; those who should be close are loved according to the Way, those who should be more distant are treated according to the Way; near and far are distinguished, yet the Way is not two—how abundant that Virtue is! Extend it further to the village, and the whole village becomes of one heart; extend it to the state, and the whole state becomes like a single person—how long-lasting, how rich that Virtue is!

If one can truly sit in stillness at the center, govern without action, and be serene and free of desire, then naturally the four quarters will stir as grass in the wind, all under heaven will turn to humaneness, and the people will cherish this blessing without end—how universal and far-reaching that Virtue is! This is never forced by power, nor lured out by petty gain; it rests on the innate conscience everyone possesses, cultivating a body and mind that are already at peace and at ease—like a wandering son returning home, or old friends unexpectedly reunited after long separation: that joy, rising from the depths of the heart, comes of itself without being summoned.

Others and oneself differ only in body; the original heart is not two. We receive the same life and possess the same nature. That is why the Confucians say that "illuminating bright Virtue" is "renewing the people," and that bringing peace to others begins entirely with cultivating oneself—the principle does not differ in the least. Moreover, the village is built up from household after household, the state expanded from village after village; the whole vast world, with its millions of people, is nothing but one family, one village, one state gradually extended and brought together step by step. Understand the Way of cultivating one person, and you understand the Way of family, state and world; cultivate this one self well, and you have the root of cultivating family, state and world.

In all things, seek within your own heart, then extend it to others according to principle, and you will naturally transform them unseen, changing customs and habits, so that all under heaven each find their own joy in peace and gladness. Hence the old saying: "First there is the transforming influence of Virtue, and only then are people's hearts gathered; first there are honest hearts, and only then are good customs formed." When the Great Way is rooted in oneself and Virtue's blessing overflows to the family, this transforming force, like a clear wind bending the grass, sweeps everything before it and reaches every distance, spreading through village and state to the entire world. Ah, how wondrous! How do I know that the world is truly so? By this very principle.

Original (Chinese)

《易》曰：「大哉乾乎！刚健中正，纯粹精矣！」是知道为先天乾金，至刚至健，卓立于天地之间，流行于万物之内，体物不遗，至诚不息，势常伸而不屈、直而不挠，擎天顶地，摩汉冲霄，固未尝稍拔也。然皆无极之极、不神之神，以至于卓卓不摇如此。人能以无极立其体，元神端其用，即古云「采大药于不动之中，行火候于无为之内」，居中建极，浩然之气，常充塞于宇宙间焉。自此一得永得，一立永立，神依于气，气依于神，神气交感，纽结一团，即归根复命，道常存矣。夫人之生也，神与气合；其死也，神与气离。人能性命混合，神气融和，即抱元守一，我命在我不由天矣，何脱之有？由是神神相依，气气相守，一脉流传，一真贯注，自能千变万化，没鬼出神，有百千万亿之化身，享百千万亿之大年，谓非「子生孙，孙又生子，子子孙孙，根深叶茂，源远流长，万代明禋不辍」乎？要不过以元气为药物，以元神为火候而已。夫元气者，无气也；元神者，不神也。以神炼气而成道，如以火炼药而成丹。凡丹有成有毁，神丹则无终始，故曰「金丹大道，历万古而不磨」，无非以己之德，修己之身，非由后起，不自外来，其德乃真矣。天地生人，虽清浊不同，贤否各异，而维皇诞降，由家庭以及天下，无不厥有恒性，故一心可以贯万姓，一德可以孚万民，是修身齐家，德有余矣；修身化乡，德乃长矣。至于治国平天下，莫非垂衣裳而天下化，究无有外修身而可以普获帡幪者，此治世之常道也。

The Book of Changes exclaims: "Great indeed is Qian! Firm, strong, central and correct, pure and unmixed to the utmost subtlety!" From this we know that the Great Way, in Prior Heaven, is like the supremely firm and strong metal of Qian (the One Breath of Prior Heaven): it stands out alone between heaven and earth, circulates through all things, becomes the very substance of every creature without leaving one out, and moves on with utmost sincerity and without ceasing. Its force always extends and never yields, is upright and never bends, holds up heaven and props up earth, soaring straight to the clouds, and has never once been shaken or uprooted. Yet all of this rests on the formless, wondrous working of "the pole of the Limitless, the spirit that is not spiritual" for it to stand so firm and unshakable.

If practitioners can establish the substance in empty, bright limitlessness and let the luminous Original Spirit govern its wondrous function—that is, what the ancients called "gathering the Great Medicine within motionlessness, and conducting the fire phasing within non-action"—then, firmly dwelling in the center and setting up the pole, the vast, upright Breath within the breast will constantly fill the universe. From then on, once attained it is attained forever, once firmly established it is established forever: Spirit clings to Breath and Breath nestles against Spirit, Spirit and Breath interact, merge and congeal into a single mass. This is returning to the root and restoring life, and the true Way then endures imperishable.

A person lives because Spirit and Breath are united, and dies because Spirit and Breath separate. If one can blend inner nature and life into one and bring Spirit and Breath into perfect harmony, this is embracing the Origin and guarding the One; from then on "my life lies with me, not with heaven"—how could anything slip away or be lost? From then on spirit depends on spirit and breath keeps to breath; one vein of the true source flows on unbroken, one point of true numinosity pervades everywhere, and one naturally gains spiritual transformation and enters the wondrous, manifesting boundless wondrous functions and enjoying immeasurable years. Is this not what internal alchemy calls "son begets grandson, grandson again begets son; generation after generation, deep roots and luxuriant leaves, a distant source and a long flow, the incense of the sacrifices never ceasing for ten thousand generations"?

In the end, all this is simply taking the Prior-Heaven original Breath as the medicinal substance and the Prior-Heaven Original Spirit as the fire phasing. But what is original Breath? It is a formless breath beyond tangible respiration. What is Original Spirit? It is "the spirit that is not spiritual," beyond ordinary thinking. Using the Original Spirit to cook and refine the original Breath and realize the Way is like using the furnace fire to refine the medicinal substances and form an elixir. Ordinary elixirs fired from minerals and plants are one day completed and one day destroyed; only this inner spirit elixir has neither beginning nor end. Hence it is said, "The Great Way of the Golden Elixir endures through the ages and is never worn away." All of this is simply using one's own innate true Virtue to cultivate one's own person—not something manufactured in Posterior Heaven, nor begged from outside. Only Virtue of this kind can truly be called "genuine"!

When heaven and earth produce human beings, endowments may be clear or turbid and characters worthy or foolish, yet the constant nature that heaven bestows on all is complete in everyone, from the family to the whole world. Thus one true heart can reach all the clans, and one true Virtue can win over the myriad people: cultivate yourself and then order your family, and Virtue is abundant and to spare; cultivate yourself and then transform your village, and Virtue is long and lasting. As for governing the state and pacifying the world, it too is nothing but letting the robes hang down in non-action while the world transforms of itself. In the final analysis, there has never been a way to bring the shelter of protection to all the people while leaving aside "cultivating oneself"—this is the eternal constant Way of governing the human world.

§4
Original (Chinese)

反之修身，又何异耶？论国家天下，原是由近而远，一层深一层之意，如精气神三者一齐都有，不是一步还一步。自初工言曰炼精，而气与神在焉；二步曰炼气，而神与精在焉；三步曰炼神，而精与气亦在焉；即还虚合道，道合自然，自始至终，俱不离也，离则非道矣。身比精，精非交感之精，乃受气生形之初，所禀太虚中二五之元精。修之身，即炼精化气。修行人，初行持也，人得此精以生，亦得此精以长，以精修身，不啻以身修身矣，其真为何如哉？以气而论，精为近于身者，气则稍远，故曰「修之于家，其德乃余」。夫采外边真阳之气，炼内里真阴之精，即如以身齐家，其得于己者不绰绰然有余裕耶？乡视身又更远，比家稍近，犹之神然。神如火也，热者属气，光者属神，是二而是一，修之乡即炼神还虚，故曰「其德乃长」，以其长生而悠久也。至于国视乡为近，比身又更远，其广宽非一目可睹。国比虚也，修之国即炼虚合道。夫炼至于虚，与清虚为一，朗照大千，而况天下乎？故曰「其德乃丰」。至于天下，则与道为一，纯乎自然，可以建天地而不悖，质鬼神而无疑，百世以俟圣人而不惑矣，此皆自然之精、之气、之神、之虚之道，非有加增者也，故曰「其德乃普」。他如以身观身、家观家、乡观乡、国观国、天下观天下，无非以一己之身家，为天下身家之表率，以一人之乡国，为天下乡国之观型，默契潜孚，相观而化，天下皆然，何况托处宇内者哉？太上取喻，其意切近，其义精微。大道无他，精之又精，以至于虚无自然，尽矣！学大道者，亦无他，惟损之又损，以至于无为自然，无为而无不为，尽矣！

Turning back and looking at "cultivating the self and refining the elixir" through this same framework, how is it any different? Speaking of family, village, state and world is a matter of moving from near to far, each layer deeper than the last—just as the Three Treasures of the body, Essence, Breath and Spirit, are all present together from the start; it is not that the second step only appears after the first has been mechanically completed. Speaking of the very first work, it is called "refining Essence," yet Breath and Spirit are already within it; the second step is called "refining Breath," and Spirit and Essence are also within it; the third step is called "refining Spirit," and Essence and Breath are still within it. Even at the highest stages of refining emptiness to unite with the Way and the Way uniting with spontaneity, the Three Treasures are never separated from beginning to end—once they are split apart, it is no longer the true Way.

Concretely:

  • "Self" stands for "Essence": The Essence meant here is not the posterior-heaven turbid essence emitted in sexual intercourse, but the original Essence of yin, yang and the Five Phases received from the Great Void when a person first takes in Breath and assumes form. When the classic says "cultivate it in oneself," this corresponds to the first of the three transmutations, "refining Essence into Breath." When practitioners first begin to practice, they rely on this very Prior-Heaven original Essence that gave them life and nourishes them; to cultivate the self with this original Essence is no different from cultivating the self with the self—need one say how genuine and real that is? Hence "its Virtue is genuine."
  • "Family" stands for "Breath": Essence is closest to the physical body, while Breath reaches one layer further out than Essence; hence the classic says, "cultivate it in the family, and its Virtue is abundant." Gathering the true yang Breath that comes through resonance from beyond the body to cook and refine the true yin Essence inherent within is like ordering the family through cultivating oneself—are not the gains within then ample, abundant and more than enough?
  • "Village" stands for "Spirit": The village is one layer farther away than the self, yet close neighbor to the family, just like the relation of Spirit to Breath. Spirit is like a fire: the heat it gives off belongs to Breath, the light it radiates belongs to Spirit; they appear to be two, yet are really one. When the classic says "cultivate it in the village," this corresponds to "refining Spirit to return to emptiness"; cultivation at this stage brings long life and endurance, hence "its Virtue is long."
  • "State" stands for "emptiness": The state adjoins the village but is far vaster than one person; its borders cannot be seen at a single glance, which makes it a fitting figure for the realm of "clear emptiness." "Cultivate it in the state" corresponds to "refining emptiness to unite with the Way." Once one has refined oneself into oneness with the clear emptiness of heaven and earth, the luminous clarity shines through the great chiliocosm—how much more the human world! Hence "its Virtue is rich."
  • "The world" stands for "the Way": When one reaches "cultivate it in the world," one is wholly one with the Great Way, purely spontaneous—in accord with the workings of heaven and earth without contradiction, questioned by ghosts and spirits without doubt, and unconfused even if one waits a hundred generations for a sage to test it. All of this is simply the Essence, Breath, Spirit, emptiness and Way that were naturally there from the start; not a bit of it has been forcibly added. Hence "its Virtue is universal."

As for the classic's "observe persons through one's person, families through one's family, villages through one's village, states through one's state, and the world through the world," it simply means making one's own well-cultivated self and family a model for every self and family in the world, and the village and state transformed by one person an example for every village and state in the world. Hearts meet in silent accord and are transformed imperceptibly; by observing one another they are naturally won over. Look across the whole world and the principle is the same—how much more so for people who live together between heaven and earth?

The set of metaphors Lord Lao lays out here is plain and apt in wording, yet supremely subtle in meaning. The Great Way has no other secret than refining and refining again until one reaches emptiness and spontaneity—and there it ends. Those who study the Great Way likewise have no other trick than diminishing desire and diminishing it again until one reaches non-action and spontaneity, doing nothing yet leaving nothing undone—and then it is complete!

§5
Original (Chinese)

然内药外药，内丹外丹，取坎填离，抽铅添汞，种种喻象比名，要无非以身中禀受于天地之精气神，以其生来素具，只因陷于血肉躯壳之中，故曰「阴精、阴气、阴神」，以其与生俱来，故曰「内药」。修士兴工之始，必垂帘塞兑，凝其神，调其息，将三元混合于一鼎，一鼎烹炼乎三元，名曰炼精，实则神气俱归一窍；直待神融气畅，和合为一，于是气机发动，蒸蒸浮浮，是曰气化，又曰水底金生，又曰凡父凡母交而产药，此是人世男女顺以生人之道。若不知逆修之法，顷刻化为后天有形之精，从肾管而泄，故「固气留精，决定长生」。人欲长生，此精之化气，即是长生妙药。如有冲突之状，急需内伏天罡，外推斗柄，进退河车，收回中宫再造，此为炼内药也，精气神亦混合为一者也，岂仅气化云哉？一内一外，一坎一离，始而以身之所具，交会黄房，温养片晌，则气生焉，此以神入气，以身中之精，炼出天地外来灵阳之气，即炼精化气。继以此气采之而升，导之而降，送归土釜，再烹再炼，即是以铅制汞，以阳气伏阴精。盖精原己身素具，故曰「离己阴精」；气由精化而产，故曰「坎戊阳气」。非精属心中，气生肾内也。自涌泉以至气海，皆属阳，阳则为坎；自泥丸以至玄关，皆属阴，阴则为离。是水火之气为坎离，非以心肾为坎离也明矣。又曰坎中有气曰地魄，在外药，白虎是也，在内药，金丹是也。此丹从抽铅添汞合一而生者也，均属水府玄珠。内外之说，一层剥一层，非真有内外也。离宫有精曰天魂，在外药，青龙是也，在内药，己之真精是也。水中金生，即精中气化，在外药，白虎初弦之气是也，在内药，铅中之银是也，又曰金丹长生大药。只此乾元一气，陷入人身，非以神火下煅，则沉而不起，且欲动而倾，此如灯之油，灯无油则息，人无气则灭，人之生，生于此，故为长生大药。以其自乾而失于坎，今复由坎还乾，金丹之说，所由来也。夫人欲求长生，除此水乡铅一味，别无他物。但此金丹，虽曰人人自有，然非神火烹煎，别无由生。及真金一生，再将白虎擒龙，自使青龙伏虎，龙虎二气，复会黄房，二气相吞相啖，而结金丹。运回土釜，会己真精，再以神火温养，而结圣胎。胎既结，内用天然真火，绵绵于神房之中，外加抽添凡火，流转于一身之际，即曰运己汞包固真精，久则脱胎而出，升上泥丸，炼诸虚空，务归本来自然之地。不是精气神三宝攸分，亦不是内外二药各别，苟非坐破蒲团，磨穿膝盖，自苦自炼，安能了悟底蕴？吾今聊注大概，不过为后学指条大路耳。

Yet the many metaphorical names in the alchemical classics—"inner medicine and outer medicine," "inner elixir and outer elixir," "taking from Kan to fill Li," "extracting lead and adding mercury"—all refer, in the end, to the Essence, Breath and Spirit that the body receives from heaven and earth. Because they are inborn yet have sunk into this shell of flesh and blood and taken on a posterior-heaven yin quality, they are called "yin Essence, yin Breath and yin Spirit"; and because they are possessed from birth, they are called "inner medicine" (see Medicine).

When practitioners begin the work, they must first lower the curtains and close the eyes, gather in the mouth and senses ("blocking the openings"), concentrate the mind and Spirit, and finely regulate the breath, merging the three origins of Essence, Breath and Spirit in a single cauldron and cooking all three in that one cauldron. In name this is "refining Essence," but in fact Spirit and Breath have already been gathered together into one and the same empty, wondrous opening. Wait until Spirit melts and Breath flows freely, merging seamlessly into one, and the inner pivot of Breath will begin to stir subtly, rising warm and buoyant like steam. The alchemical classics call this "the transformation of Breath," also "metal born at the bottom of the water," also "the mundane father and mundane mother uniting to produce the medicine." If one follows ordinary habit downward, this is the forward path of worldly men and women giving birth to children; if one does not know how to turn it back through reversal, this vital force turns in an instant into posterior-heaven tangible turbid essence and leaks out through the reproductive passage.

Hence the ancients said, "Secure the Breath and retain the Essence, and long life is assured." If one wishes to nourish life and extend one's years, this Prior-Heaven true Breath transformed from Essence is the wondrous medicine of long life. As soon as one feels the pivot of Breath surging and pushing, one must, following its momentum, steady the center with inner attention ("subduing the Heavenly Mainstay within") and circulate it with the True Breath ("pushing the Handle of the Dipper without," advancing and withdrawing the River Chariot of the Celestial Circuit), gathering it back into the central palace to be cooked anew. This is what is called "refining the inner medicine"—Essence, Breath and Spirit are in fact always blended into one; how could it be merely a matter of "the transformation of Breath"?

Now for the distinction of inner and outer, of Kan and Li: at first, one brings one's inherent Essence and Spirit together in the central palace of the Yellow Court and warmly nourishes them quietly for a while, and the Prior-Heaven true Breath arises. This is Spirit entering Breath—using one's own inherent Essence to refine, through resonance, the numinous yang Breath that communicates with heaven and earth; it is "refining Essence into Breath." Next one gathers this newly born true Breath, refining it upward and guiding it downward along its course, sending it back into the earthen pot of the central palace to be cooked and refined again and again. This is "using lead to control mercury," subduing the true yin Essence with true yang Breath (see Lead and Mercury).

Understand that yin Essence is inherent in oneself, hence it is called "the yin Essence of Li, which is ji"; yang Breath is produced from Essence through transformation and resonance, hence it is called "the yang Breath of Kan, which is wu." Never rigidly suppose that Essence grows in the heart and Breath is produced in the kidneys! Dividing by the rising and falling of the pivot of Breath, everything from the Bubbling Spring on the soles of the feet up to the Sea of Breath in the lower abdomen belongs to yang, and where yang Breath is stored is Kan; everything from the Muddy Pellet at the crown down to the Mysterious Pass at the center belongs to yin, and what yin Essence governs is Li. Clearly, then, Kan and Li refer to the yin and yang qualities of the water-and-fire pivot of Breath, and certainly not to the anatomical heart and kidneys—nothing could be clearer.

The alchemical classics also peel this set of metaphors back layer by layer: the true yang Breath stored in the palace of Kan is called "the earthly soul (po)"; in terms of outer medicine it is called "the White Tiger," in terms of inner medicine it is the "Golden Elixir." It is born from the union of extracting lead and adding mercury, and is collectively called "the Mysterious Pearl of the Water Palace." So-called inner and outer medicine are merely names for peeling inward layer by layer from coarse to fine; there are not truly two fixed things, one inside the body and one outside. The true Essence governed by the palace of Li is called "the heavenly soul (hun)"; in terms of outer medicine it is called "the Green Dragon," in terms of inner medicine it is one's own true Essence. "Metal born in water" is Breath transformed within Essence; in outer medicine it is called "the Breath of the White Tiger's first quarter," in inner medicine "the silver within the lead," also called the Great Medicine of the Golden Elixir and of long life.

To put it plainly, it is just this one Prior-Heaven yang Breath of Qian's origin. Once it has fallen into the posterior-heaven human body, if the fire of the Original Spirit does not warm and refine it from below, it will sink and lie dormant without rising, and may even pour out and be lost the moment desire stirs. It is like the oil in a lamp: without oil the lamp goes out, and without this Breath a person declines and dies. Human life depends entirely on it, hence it is called "the Great Medicine of long life." It originally fell from the pure-yang trigram Qian into the trigram Kan; now, through cultivation, it is gathered back from Kan to Qian and restored to pure yang—this is the true origin of the words "Golden Elixir."

If one seeks long life, there is nothing else besides this one ingredient, "the lead of the water country." Yet although everyone originally possesses this Golden Elixir in full, without cooking and simmering it day and night with the fire of the Original Spirit, there is no way for it to arise. When this true metal, the first yang, is born, then let the White Tiger (true Breath) capture the Green Dragon (true Spirit), and let the Green Dragon subdue the White Tiger; the two Breaths of dragon and tiger meet again in the Yellow Chamber of the central palace, swallow and devour each other, merge, and form the Golden Elixir. Carry it back to the earthen pot, unite it with one's own true Essence, and warmly nourish it continuously with the spirit-fire, and it forms the Holy Embryo. Once the Holy Embryo is formed, within, the natural true fire nourishes it gently and continuously in the spirit chamber of the central palace; without, it is supported by the regulated advance and withdrawal of breathing circulating through the whole body—that is, setting one's own mercury in motion to wrap and firmly protect the true Essence. When the fire phasing has gone on long enough, the embryo naturally emerges, rises to the Muddy Pellet, is released into the empty realm of the Great Void to be thoroughly refined, and finally returns to the original ground of spontaneity.

It is clear, then, that this is neither splitting the Three Treasures of Essence, Breath and Spirit apart, nor dividing the inner and outer medicines into two separate camps. Unless one truly sits until the meditation cushion is worn through and the knees are rubbed raw, working hard and refining oneself in person, how could one ever truly grasp the depths of it? What I have annotated here today is only a rough outline, merely to point out a broad, level road for later students of the Way.

§6
Original (Chinese)

且道本平常，非有奇异，愈精深，愈平常。他如变化莫测，在世人视之，以为高不可望，妙无从窥，而以太上《道德》一经思之，即如三清太上，亦只是一个凡人造成。但凡人以生死为喜忧，仙则视生死如昼夜。一生一死，即如一起一卧，顺而行之，不尽安然？有谓长生不死，为仙家乐事者，非也。人以长生为荣，仙则以顺理为乐。虽杀身成仁，舍生取义，亦所素甘。不然，刀锯之惨，谁不畏哉？古来志士仁人，多视鼎镬为乐地、死亡为安途者，盖见得理明，信得命定，其生其死，无非此心为之运行，生而不安，不如速死，犹醒而抱痛，不如长眠。只要神存理圆，生何足荣，死何足辱，一听造化流行，决不偷生于人世。如好生恶死，是庸夫俗子之流，非圣贤顺时听天之学也。否则，孔子何以七十而终，颜子何以三十而卒？顺天而动，不敢违也。此岂凡人所能见哉？窃愿学者，只求于内，无务于外，患难生死，一以平等视之，此心何等宽阔，何等安闲？谚云：「认理行将去，凭天摆布来。」如此落得生安死泰，永为出世真人，岂不胜于贪生怕死之徒，时而欣欣于内，时而戚戚于怀，此心终无宁日耶？况有道高人，天欲留之，以型方训俗，我不拒之，亦不求之，但听之而已，初何容心于其间乎？盖生死皆道也，尽其道而生，尽其道而死，又何好恶之有哉？凡有好恶于中者，神早乱，性早亡，不足以云仙矣。

Moreover, the Great Way is supremely ordinary and nothing strange or mysterious—the more profound it becomes, the more ordinary it is. As for tales of unfathomable spiritual transformations, worldly people always find them lofty beyond reach and too mysterious to glimpse; yet if one reflects carefully along the lines of Lord Lao's Daodejing, even the supreme Three Pure Ones and the Most High were originally just ordinary, down-to-earth human beings who cultivated themselves to completion.

Where, then, does the real difference between ordinary people and immortals lie? Ordinary people always take life as cause for joy and death as cause for sorrow, while immortals regard life and death as ordinary as day and night. Being born and passing away are like getting up in the morning and lying down to sleep at night; if one simply goes through them as they come, is that not entirely peaceful and at ease?

Some think that "living forever without dying" is what immortals most delight in and enjoy—but this is wrong! Worldly people take living long and never aging as a glory, while immortals take only "following heavenly principle" as their joy. Even when the moment comes to give one's life to fulfill humaneness or to sacrifice life for righteousness, they go willingly and calmly. Otherwise, who would not fear the agony of the knife and saw? Since ancient times so many people of humane resolve have regarded the boiling cauldron as a land of joy and going to their deaths as a road home in peace, precisely because they saw heavenly principle with perfect clarity and trusted destiny with firm conviction—whether living or dying, it is this upright, clear heart that governs and moves them. To cling to life while betraying righteousness, with the heart restless and uneasy, is worse than a swift death—just as being awake but wracked with pain is worse than sleeping peacefully forever.

So long as the Original Spirit is not darkened and heavenly principle is whole, what is there to boast of in living, and what disgrace in dying? Leave everything to the natural flow of creation, and never cling basely to life for the sake of one more breath. If the heart is forever craving life and fearing death, that is merely the view of the ordinary and vulgar, and certainly not the great learning of the sages and worthies who follow the times and heed heaven's mandate. Otherwise, why did Confucius die past seventy, and why did Yan Hui die young, at about thirty? Because the sages and worthies act in accord with heaven's mandate and dare not force or defy it in the least. How could ordinary people see through to a breadth of heart like this?

I sincerely hope that those who study the Great Way will simply cultivate inwardly and not chase outwardly, and regard hardship and good fortune, life and death all with equanimity—with such a state of mind, how vast the heart becomes, how leisurely the days! As the saying goes, "Recognize principle and walk on; let heaven arrange the rest." Living thus at peace and dying in tranquility, one is forever a True Person beyond the toils of the dust—is that not far better than those who crave life and fear death, smug one moment and anxious the next, whose hearts never know a day of peace their whole life long?

What is more, if heaven wishes to keep a truly accomplished person in the world as a model to transform the people's ways, that person neither refuses nor deliberately seeks it, but simply lets it be as it is—how could any trace of selfish calculation be mixed in? For life too is the Way, and death too is the Way: live by fulfilling the Way one should fulfill, depart by fulfilling the Way one should fulfill—where then would craving or aversion come in? Anyone whose heart is still full of craving life and loathing death has long since thrown their Original Spirit into disorder and lost their true nature; they are in no position to speak of becoming immortal and attaining the Way.

Key Terms

  • Well established, never uprooted; well embraced, never slipping away: A foundation laid on formless heavenly principle and original Breath never wavers; holding humane Virtue and the true Way, embracing the Origin and guarding the One, one never loses it.
  • The three transmutations and the five-layer progression: Huang Yuanji likens the classic's five layers of "self, family, village, state, world" to "refining Essence into Breath, refining Breath into Spirit, refining Spirit to return to emptiness, refining emptiness to unite with the Way, and the Way uniting with spontaneity," stressing that Essence, Breath and Spirit remain one undivided whole from beginning to end and cannot be split apart.
  • Kan and Li and Lead and Mercury: Not the anatomical organs of heart and kidneys, but names for the yin-yang, water-and-fire pivot of Breath in the upper and lower body. "Extracting lead and adding mercury" and "taking from Kan to fill Li" mean subduing and dissolving posterior-heaven yin Essence and the cognitive spirit with Prior-Heaven true yang Breath.
  • Inner medicine and outer medicine (see Medicine): The Essence, Breath and Spirit inborn and complete in the physical body are called "inner medicine"; the numinous yang Breath that comes through resonance with heaven and earth at the height of stillness is called "outer medicine." Huang Yuanji states plainly that these are only metaphors peeling inward layer by layer from shallow to deep, not truly two separate things, one inside and one outside.
  • Holy Embryo: An image for the pure-yang true body that forms when the two Breaths of dragon and tiger meet in the central Yellow Court and are warmly nourished continuously by the spirit-fire.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Lay an "unuprootable" foundation first, then speak of outward achievement. Positions, reputation and assets are like mountains with form, always liable to rise and fall and even collapse; only mental steadiness, integrity of character and a healthy daily rhythm cultivated in your own person are the "genuine Virtue" no one can take away.
  2. Cultivating oneself is not solitary austerity; family life and how you deal with others are the touchstone. "Observe persons through one's person, families through one's family": however serene someone looks on the cushion in the study, if they come home and are quick to anger, petty and harsh toward others, their practice has not yet touched the ground.
  3. Let go of the obsession with "miraculous longevity," and you come closer to the Way. Huang Yuanji gets straight to the point: "the Way is ordinary; the more profound, the more ordinary." Treat practice as an anxious business of escaping aging and death, and the mind is the first thing thrown into disorder; recognize principle and walk on, live in the present, be at peace in life and in death, and body and mind naturally ease.
  4. When reading alchemical classics, never fixate on organs and terminology. Lead and Mercury, dragon and tiger, Kan and Li, inner and outer medicine are all the ancients' expedient ways of speaking about Breath through images of things. The core is always gathering in the mind, resting quietly, and harmonizing Spirit and Breath; never force them onto the physical organs.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Honor the worthy and love their kin; the common people enjoy their contentment and benefit from their livelihoods": Adapts the sense of the Great Learning (in the Book of Rites), which cites the Classic of Poetry ("Hymns of Zhou," Liewen), describing how a sage-king's blessing spreads so that the noble and the common people each find their place.
  2. "Sons and grandsons, do not let it lapse; carry it on forever": From the Classic of Poetry ("Lesser Odes," Chuci), expressing the hope that descendants will not abandon the ancestors' virtuous legacy and sacrifices but carry them on forever.
  3. "Received the offerings of the ancestral temple and was preserved by his descendants": From Chapter 17 of the Doctrine of the Mean, Confucius' praise of Shun's great filial piety. Zhengchang (蒸尝) are the autumn and winter sacrifices, standing for sacrifice in general; zudou (俎豆) are ancient sacrificial vessels.
  4. "Great indeed is Qian! Firm, strong, central and correct, pure and unmixed to the utmost subtlety": From the Book of Changes, "Words on the Text" commentary to the hexagram Qian.
  5. "The sovereign sent down… they have a constant nature": Adapts the Book of Documents ("Announcement of Tang"): "The great Lord on high has sent down the mean to the people below; if they have a constant nature…", meaning that heaven bestows on all people a constant, unchanging goodness.
  6. "In accord with heaven and earth without contradiction, questioned by ghosts and spirits without doubt, and unconfused though awaiting a sage a hundred generations hence": From Chapter 29 of the Doctrine of the Mean, describing how the sage's Way accords with heavenly principle and human feeling and stands the test of countless generations.
  7. "Recognize principle and walk on; let heaven arrange the rest": A widely circulated maxim of Neo-Confucian and Daoist self-cultivation since the Ming and Qing, found in works such as Lü Kun's Groaning Words (Shenyin yu); it means simply doing one's human part according to heavenly principle and leaving success or failure, long or short life, to heaven's mandate.

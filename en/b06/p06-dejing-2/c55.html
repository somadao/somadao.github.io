Chapter 55 · The Fullness of Virtue Held Within

One who holds Virtue in fullness is like a newborn infant; concentrating Breath and attaining softness brings ample Essence and harmonious Breath. The "aging" in "when things grow robust they age" lies not in one's years but in the medicine's Breath having grown old

About This Chapter

Chapter 55 of the Daodejing takes the newborn infant (chizi, "the red child") as its image to portray the natural aspect of deep Virtue and abundant vitality. Approaching it from the dual cultivation of inner nature and life, Huang Yuanji distills the whole chapter into two insights of great depth:

  1. The infant's innocence is the height of Essence and harmony (§2): The infant is wholly unknowing; its sinews and bones are soft, yet its hands hold a firm grip; it has no stirring of sexual desire, yet its vitality surges; it cries all day, yet its voice never grows hoarse. All of this depends on the fullness of original Essence and the undiminished original harmony. If practitioners can "let the mind govern the Breath" rather than letting agitated breath drag the mind about, they can preserve this soft, harmonious Way of true constancy.
  2. "Old medicine," not "old age" (§3): The classic's "when things grow robust they age; this is called contrary to the Way" is easily taken by ordinary people to mean that once you are old you can no longer cultivate the Way. Huang Yuanji corrects this categorically: the "aging" here does not mean entering the twilight of one's years, but that the medicine being gathered and refined has passed its fresh, tender moment and its Breath has grown old! Even at eighty or ninety, so long as a single breath remains, the original Breath of supreme harmony is complete in the body, and one can still cultivate and realize the Way. Conversely, if one does not know the true moment and trusts a false teacher, even entering the Way in youth one will hardly escape reaching white hair with nothing achieved.

Safety note: §3 of this chapter discusses coded internal-alchemy terms for gathering the medicine, such as "gathering the medicine and refining the elixir," "the fresh, tender water of Heaven's One" and "the White Tiger's first menses." These are all symbolic expressions for the arising of the pivot of Breath and never refer, in a literal sense, to "harvesting" practices or deviant sexual arts. Huang Yuanji himself sternly warns at the end of the chapter against mistaking false teachers and taking wrong paths. Everyday practice should consist only in calming the mind, nurturing harmony, moderating desire and conserving Essence; never blindly experiment with "medicine-gathering" methods of unknown origin.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：含德之厚，比于赤子。毒虫不螫，猛兽不据，攫鸷不搏。骨弱筋柔而握固，未知牝牡之合而朘作，精之至也。终日号而嗌不嗄，和之至也。知和曰常，知常曰明，益生曰祥，心使气曰强。物壮则老，是谓不道，不道早已。

Lord Lao says: One who holds Virtue in fullness is like a newborn infant. Venomous insects do not sting it, fierce beasts do not seize it, birds of prey do not strike it. Its bones are weak and its sinews soft, yet its little hands grip firmly; it does not yet know the union of male and female, yet its organ rises of itself—this is because its Prior-Heaven original Essence is full to the utmost. It cries loudly all day long, yet its throat does not grow hoarse—this is because the harmonious Breath within it is full to the utmost.

To know this harmonious Breath is called "constancy" (the eternal, unchanging law); to understand this eternal law is called "clarity" (penetrating wisdom); to follow the vital impulse and nourish life is called "auspiciousness" (an auspicious sign); to use a clear mind to govern the breath and make it soft is called true "strength" (real vigor). Once things become overbearing and robust to the extreme, they turn straight toward aging; this is called contrary to the Way, and what is contrary to the Way naturally comes to an early end.

§2
Original (Chinese)

《易》曰：「天地絪缊，万物化醇。男女媾精，万物化生。」以发生之初，去天未远，其气柔脆，顺其势而导之，迎其机而养之，犹可底于纯化之域、太和之天。孟子曰：「大人者，不失其赤子之心也。」以赤子呱地一声，脱离母腹，虽别具乾坤，另开造化，然浑浑沦沦，一团天真在抱，无知识，无念虑，静与化俱，动与天随。古仙真含宏光大，厚德无疆，较诸赤子，殆相等也。当父母怀抱之时，鞠育顾复，足不能行，手不能作，虽有毒虫，不能螫焉；虽有猛兽，不能据焉；虽有攫鸷，无从搏焉。以动不知所之，行不知所往，是无虞于毒虫，而毒虫不得螫之也，无虞于猛兽，而猛兽不得据之也，且危居在榻，偃息在床，不为攫鸷所窥，而攫鸷亦不得搏之也。倘年华已壮，动履自如，虽有游行之乐，不获静室之安，其能免恶物之患者，盖亦鲜矣。况赤子初生，气血调和，筋骨柔软，而手之握者常固，盖以阴阳不乱，情欲不生，未知牝牡之交合而朘作，足见元精溶溶，生机日畅。人能专气致柔，如婴儿之初孩，则自有精之可炼。第其时，呱呱而泣，声声不断，虽至终日呼号，而咽嗌不嗄，此非随意而唤、任口而腾也，要皆天机自动，天籁自鸣，无安排，无造作，和之至矣。知得元和内蕴，适为真常之道，不假一毫人力以矫强之，而守其真常，安其固有。《诗》曰：「既明且哲，以保其身」，其斯之谓欤？若非以和柔之气，修诸身心之中，安得生而益生，天休滋至于勿替？人之祥，莫祥于此。第自强壮而后，天心为人心所乱，精神之耗散者多。今以太和为道，大静乃能大动，至柔方克至刚，于是以心役气，务令此气同于赤子，不以气动心，致使此心乖夫太和，庶几「和而不流，强哉矫」矣。非独赤子为然也。观之万物，其始柔脆，其终强壮。柔脆者，生之机；强壮者，死之兆。是以物壮则老，不如物稚则生。生者其道存，老者其道亡，故曰物老为不道，不道不如其早已。世之修道者，盍早已其老之气，而求赤子之气乎？果得同于赤子，无恐无怖，无识无知，一片浑沦，流于象外，所谓和也。夫天道以和育物，人能知之，则健行不息，故曰常。知常则洞达阴阳，同乎造化，故曰明。修身立命，夺天地生杀之权，人之祥瑞莫大于此。炼神还虚，得长生不坏之道，强斯至也，又何不道之有哉？

The Book of Changes says: "Heaven and earth intermingle, and the myriad things are transformed into rich fullness; male and female unite their essences, and the myriad things are transformed and born." When things first come into being, they are not yet far from their Prior-Heaven source, and their breath is soft and tender. If at this moment one guides it along its natural momentum and nourishes it in step with its vital impulse, one can still reach the realm of pure transformation, the heaven of supreme, rounded harmony.

Mencius also says: "The great person is one who does not lose the heart of the newborn infant." When a newborn leaves its mother's womb with a "wah!", though from then on it becomes a small world of its own and begins its own independent creation, within it is whole and undivided, its entire being embracing a mass of heavenly innocence—without posterior-heaven cunning or knowledge, without scattered thoughts or worries; in stillness it abides with creation, in motion it follows the pivot of heaven. The immortals and perfected of old, all-embracing and generous, boundless in Virtue, were almost exactly like the infant.

When the infant is held in its parents' arms and lovingly nurtured and cared for, its feet cannot yet walk and its hands cannot yet work. Even if there are venomous insects outside, they cannot sting it; even if there are fierce beasts, they cannot seize it; even if there are birds of prey, they have no way to strike it. For when it moves it does not know where it is going, and when it goes it does not know where it is headed; it has no scheming wariness at all, nor does it blunder into danger. Not provoking venomous insects, it gives them no occasion to sting; not offending fierce beasts, it gives them no occasion to seize; lying safely on the couch and resting quietly in the cradle, it is not watched by birds of prey, and so they naturally have no occasion to strike it. By contrast, when people reach the age of strength and vigor and move about freely, though they gain the pleasure of traveling everywhere, they lose the security of dwelling in a quiet room; very, very few of them can wholly avoid danger and misfortune.

Moreover, the newborn's breath and blood are harmonious, its sinews and bones soft and supple, yet its two little hands often hold a firm grip without letting go. All of this is because the yin and yang within it are not in disorder and desire has not yet opened; it does not know what sexual union is, yet its organ rises of itself—which shows that the Prior-Heaven original Essence in its body is full and ample, and its vitality grows more vigorous by the day. If adults too can concentrate their Breath and become as soft as an infant just beginning to smile, they will naturally have abundant original Essence within to refine. Moreover, the infant cries and wails without stopping, and even after crying a whole day its throat does not grow hoarse. This is not because it is shouting at random or straining its voice on a whim; it is entirely the pivot of heaven stirring of itself, the piping of heaven sounding of itself, without the least deliberate arrangement or contrivance—truly the harmonious Breath within it is full to the utmost!

To recognize this original harmonious Breath stored within the body is precisely to accord with the Way of true constancy, which never changes. There is no need to borrow the slightest posterior-heaven human effort to force or embellish it; one need only hold securely to this true constancy and rest in the heavenly innocence that was there from the beginning. The Classic of Poetry says, "Both clear-sighted and wise, thereby he preserves his person." Is this not exactly that principle?

If one did not cultivate body and mind with this harmonious, soft Breath, how could life be ever-renewing, ever fuller, with heaven's blessings flowing on without end? Of all the auspicious signs of human life, none is more auspicious than this. Alas, once people grow up into strength and vigor, the originally clear mind of the Way is stirred into confusion by the restless human mind, and far too much of their spirit, breath and blood is spent and scattered. Now we turn this around and take "supreme harmony" as the Great Way—only utmost stillness can give rise to great movement, only utmost softness can overcome the hardest—and so we turn instead to "using the clear mind to govern the breath," making sure our breath returns to an infant-like softness, and never letting agitated blood and breath drag the mind about until it strays from supreme harmony. If one can do this, one comes close to what the Doctrine of the Mean praises: "Harmonious yet not swept along—how unyielding is such strength!"

In fact, this is not true of infants alone. Look across all the things of heaven and earth: at birth, every one of them is soft and tender; at their end, every one of them is stiff and robust. Softness and tenderness are the vital impulse of life still on the rise; stiffness and overbearing vigor are, on the contrary, the omen of withering away. Hence "when things grow robust they age," and that is not as good as the young and tender stage, full of vitality. Where there is vitality, the Great Way is still present; where there is stiff aging, the Great Way is already gone. The classic says, "When things age, this is contrary to the Way; what is contrary to the Way had better cease early." Why then do those in the world who cultivate the Way not cease early that stiff, aged, dry posterior-heaven habit of breath, and turn back to recover the soft, infant-like Prior-Heaven Breath?

If one can truly cultivate oneself to be like an infant—without fear or dread, without cognition or knowledge, wholly undivided and rounded, transcending the realm of images—this is true "harmony." It is by this very harmonious Breath that the Way of heaven nurtures the myriad things. When a person recognizes it, life moves on vigorously without ceasing; hence it is called "constancy." Understanding "constancy," one sees deeply into the creative workings of yin and yang and moves in step with heaven and earth; hence it is called "clarity." By cultivating oneself and establishing one's life, one takes hold of the initiative over the waxing and waning of life; there is no greater auspiciousness in the human world ("auspiciousness"). Going further to refine Spirit and return to emptiness, realizing the Great Way of long life and incorruptibility—this is true strength ("strength"). Having reached this stage, where would there be any worry of being "contrary to the Way"?

§3
Original (Chinese)

此教人修身之法，取象于赤子。庄子曰：「儿子动不知所为，行不知所之，身若槁木，心如死灰，祸亦不至，福亦不来。祸福无有，乌有人灾物害哉？」「毒虫」等句，即此意。后云采药炼丹，须取天一新嫩之水，此水即人生生之本，犹如一轮红日，夜半子初，清清朗朗，照耀于沧海之中，又如一弯秋月，发生于庚震之方，正是修士玄关窍开，恍惚杳冥，方有此境。盖以初气致柔，犹万物甲坼抽芽，于此培之养之，方能日增月长，至于复命归根，以成硕果之用。若桑榆晚景，则物既老而将衰，不堪采以为药。但老非年迈之谓也，是云药老不可以为丹。若以年而论，即老至八九十岁，俱可修炼以成长生不老之仙。何者？一息尚存，此个太和之气，具足于身，无稍欠缺。非至人抉破水中之天，一身内外两个消息，则当面错过者多矣。学者欲修金丹大道，非虚心访道，积德回天，则真师无由感格，白虎首经莫觉，一任青年入道，必至皓首无成，更有误认邪师，错走歧路，一生之精力，竟流落于禽兽之域者不少。学者慎之！

This chapter's method of teaching self-cultivation takes its image precisely from the infant. The Zhuangzi says: "The infant moves without knowing what it is doing and goes without knowing where it is going; its body is at rest like withered wood, its mind still as dead ashes. Misfortune does not come to it, nor does good fortune seek it out. Since there is not even any reckoning of misfortune and fortune in its heart, how could it incur calamities from people or harm from things?" The lines in the classic—"venomous insects do not sting, fierce beasts do not seize, birds of prey do not strike"—express exactly this meaning.

The latter part turns to alchemical method and speaks of the timing and opportunity of the fire phasing in gathering the medicine and refining the elixir: what is gathered and refined must be the Prior-Heaven, newly born, fresh, tender and pure "true water of Heaven's One." This true water is the very root of a person's endless life. To give an image, it is like a red sun rising at the Living Zi Hour at midnight, shining clear and bright over the vast sea; or like the crescent of a new moon just showing its first thread of light in the southwest at geng and the east at zhen. This is the Prior-Heaven scene that appears naturally only when a practitioner's Mysterious Pass opens and they enter the state of the dim and obscure.

For this first-born Prior-Heaven One Breath is at its softest and most tender, like a seed of grass or tree just breaking its shell and putting out a sprout; it is precisely at this moment that one must carefully cultivate and nourish it, so that it can grow day by day and finally return to the root and restore life, bearing ripe fruit. Conversely, if one misses this tender moment of first birth and lets it drag on until the pivot of Breath has grown old and declining, like the setting sun in the mulberries and elms, it is no longer fit to serve as medicine for refining the elixir.

But be sure to understand clearly: the "aging" spoken of here never means that a person has grown old in years; it means that once "the medicine's Breath has grown old," it can no longer be used to form the elixir! If we speak only of a person's age, even someone who has reached eighty or ninety can cultivate and attain the Way. Why? So long as a single breath remains, this Breath of supreme harmony is complete and whole in the body, lacking nothing at all. It is only that, without an accomplished master to break open the mystery of "the heaven within the water" and explain the two layers of tidings of the pivot of Breath, inner and outer, in one's body, far too many people let the Prior-Heaven true moment slip past them even when it is right before their eyes.

If students wish to cultivate the Great Way of the Golden Elixir but are unwilling to seek the Way with an open mind and accumulate hidden virtue widely so as to move heaven's heart, it will be very hard for them to meet a truly enlightened teacher, and they will never perceive the true moment of the first stirring of Prior-Heaven yang (which the alchemical classics figuratively call "the White Tiger's first menses"). In that case, even if you enter the Way and practice while still young, you will surely end up white-haired with nothing achieved. Worse still, out of impatience and greed for quick results, some mistake charlatan teachers for true ones and take crooked, deviant paths, squandering a whole lifetime's Essence, Breath and Spirit and ending up no better than beasts. Such painful lessons are not rare in the world; students of the Way must be careful, and careful again!

Key Terms

  • Firm grip: The newborn's posture of tucking the thumb into the palm with the four fingers naturally wrapped around it; Daoists regard it as a natural bodily form that firmly guards the original Essence and gathers in Spirit and Breath.
  • Zuizuo (朘作, zuī zuò): The infant's organ rising of itself before it knows any desire. The classic uses this to show that the Prior-Heaven original Essence is full and vitality is self-sufficient, not stirred by posterior-heaven lust.
  • Letting the mind govern the Breath: Using the clear, settled mind of the Way to direct a soft, harmonious breath, not letting posterior-heaven agitated blood and breath drag the mind about, and so achieving the true strength of being "harmonious yet not swept along."
  • Old medicine, not old age (see Medicine): A key distinction in Huang Yuanji's alchemical method. The "aging" in "when things grow robust they age," in terms of the fire phasing of gathering the medicine, means missing the moment of the first birth of Prior-Heaven yang (tender medicine) and falling into posterior-heaven turbidity (old medicine), not the practitioner's advanced physiological age.
  • White Tiger's first menses: Coded alchemical language. "White Tiger" stands for the true yang Breath of the Prior-Heaven palace of Kan, and "first menses" for the purest, freshest, most tender moment of the first birth of Prior-Heaven yang; it never refers literally to blood or to deviant sexual arts between men and women.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Keep a softness that "doesn't strain." Most of the wear on an adult's body and mind comes from forcing through everything and holding the mind and breath tense. "Softness is vitality; stiffness and overbearing are the omen of death." In high-pressure work or in an argument, deliberately letting your shoulders drop and slowing your speech is practicing "concentrating Breath and attaining softness."
  2. When emotions surge, practice "letting the mind govern the Breath." When anger or anxiety makes your breathing quick and your heart race, that is "the breath moving the mind." Pause for three seconds and bring your attention back to the out-breath, letting the mind take charge again and calm the agitated breath—this is the everyday version of "letting the mind govern the Breath."
  3. Never use "I'm too old" as an excuse to give up cultivation. So long as a single breath remains, the body's power of recovery and the mind's clarity are there; at any age it is not too late to begin a regular routine, calm the mind and regulate the breath.
  4. Stay on the highest alert toward mysterious "quick-fix harvesting methods." Anything claiming to reach immortality quickly through sexual "harvesting" between men and women, bizarre secret arts or expensive "transmissions of power" belongs, without exception, to the "false teachers and wrong paths" Huang Yuanji denounces; keeping away from them is itself protection.

Translator's Notes

  1. Old glosses on the sound and sense of the classic: In the base text the classic is followed by an old gloss: "嗌 is pronounced yi. 嗄 is pronounced sha (today read shà). … 朘 (misread in the base text as 峻) is the genitals. 号 means to cry out. 嗌 is the throat. 嗄 means the voice breaking, also said to be breath going against its course (misprinted in the base text as 气递)." In keeping with this edition's format it has been moved into the translator's notes for reference.
  2. "Does not yet know the union of male and female, yet its organ rises": Received editions of the Daodejing read 朘作 (Wang Bi and Heshang Gong editions) or 全作 (the silk manuscripts). 朘 (pronounced zuī) is glossed in the Shuowen jiezi as "the infant's genitals," that is, the infant's external genitalia. The base text misreads it as 媵作 in the classic and as 峻作 in the commentary; both have been corrected to 朘作 following the standard alchemical editions and dictionaries.
  3. "Heaven and earth intermingle, and the myriad things are transformed into rich fullness; male and female unite their essences, and the myriad things are transformed and born": From the Book of Changes, "Appended Statements II," describing how the two Breaths of yin and yang interact and merge to give birth to all things.
  4. "Both clear-sighted and wise, thereby he preserves his person": From the Classic of Poetry ("Greater Odes," Zhengmin), meaning being both intelligent and perceptive, and thereby keeping oneself safe and preserving one's life.
  5. "Harmonious yet not swept along—how unyielding is such strength!": From Chapter 10 of the Doctrine of the Mean, where Confucius discusses the true "strength of the noble person": generous and harmonious without drifting with the current—this is true, vigorous strength.
  6. "The infant moves without knowing what it is doing… how could it incur calamities from people or harm from things?": Abridged from the Zhuangzi, "Gengsang Chu": "The infant cries all day yet its throat does not grow hoarse—this is the height of harmony… It moves without knowing what it is doing, goes without knowing where it is going; its body is like a withered branch and its mind like dead ashes. Being like this, misfortune does not come, nor does good fortune arrive. Where there is neither misfortune nor fortune, how could there be calamities from people!"
  7. "The quarters of geng and zhen": A term from the najia ("allotted stems") system of Changes imagery. On the evening of the third day of each lunar month the new moon first appears in the southwest, the quarter of geng; in trigram terms this corresponds to zhen (☳), with one yang line born at the bottom. Alchemical classics often use it as an image for the moment of the first birth of Prior-Heaven yang.
  8. Textual note: In the base text's title, 含徳之厚 has been changed to the standard form of the character 德. In the classic, 日 wrongly read for 曰 in 太上日, 知和日常, 知常日明, 益生日祥 and 心使气日强, and in the commentary in 《易》日, 《诗》日 and 故日, has been corrected throughout; in the classic, 肯弱筋柔 and 是请不道 → 骨弱筋柔 and 是谓不道; in the commentary, 天地纲组 → 天地絪缊; 掺鸷 → 攫鸷; 筋音柔软 → 筋骨柔软; 修请身心 → 修诸身心; 赤子之气平 → 赤子之气乎; 恍惚香冥 → 恍惚杳冥; 不堪米以为药 → 不堪采以为药; and 岐路 → 歧路.

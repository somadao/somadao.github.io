III · Classic of the Way (Ch. 21–30) · 道经

Chapter 25 · The Way Models Itself on What Is So of Itself

"The Way models itself on naturalness" is never an excuse for idle talk about letting things slide—to verify the Mother Breath born before heaven and earth, one must begin with concentrating Spirit and regulating breath so they stay braided into one mass, putting one's destiny in one's own hands

About This Chapter

Chapter 25 is the most cosmologically sweeping chapter of the Daodejing, bequeathing the immortal lines "There is something indistinctly formed, born before heaven and earth" and "Humanity models itself on earth, earth on heaven, heaven on the Way, and the Way on what is so of itself." In his commentary, Huang Yuanji lifts up the grand vision of cosmic genesis with one hand, while with the other immediately drawing the reader's gaze back to their own breath and thoughts:

  1. The "Mother of All Under Heaven," from Boundless to Supreme Ultimate: Before heaven and earth separated, that undivided, boundless One Breath of Prior Heaven harbored vitality within quiet emptiness, standing alone and unchanging, circulating through the three realms without ever tiring. Able to embrace heaven and earth and nourish all living beings, it is styled "the Way" and reluctantly named "Great."
  2. "Great → Passing On → Far → Returning" is an endless cycle of renewal: The Way is so vast that nothing falls outside it, flowing onward everywhere—yet it never departs without return. Reaching its extreme, it turns back, circling and cycling without end: this is both the law of cosmic motion and the basis of internal alchemy's return to the source.
  3. Stop mouthing empty talk of "naturalness"—do the real work of concentrating Spirit and regulating the breath: Huang Yuanji's sharpest stroke comes at the chapter's close, warning students never to use "the Way models itself on naturalness" as a lazy excuse. Cultivating the Way takes place right within "one heart, one body, and the breath inches from one's nose": apart from eating when hungry and sleeping when tired, maintain unbroken awareness at all times so Spirit and Breath stay tightly braided together. Even before reaching laying the foundation, as long as Spirit and Breath never scatter, external afflictions cannot enter, and one truly lives out the maxim "my destiny lies with me, not with Heaven."

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：有物混成，先天地生，寂兮寥兮，独立而不改，周行而不殆，可以为天下母。吾不知其名，字之曰道，强为名之曰大。大曰逝，逝曰远，远曰反。故道大，天大，地大，王亦大。域中有四大，而王居其一焉。人法地，地法天，天法道，道法自然。

Laozi says: There is a reality indistinctly and completely formed, born before heaven and earth. Silent and soundless! Vast and formless! It stands alone and never alters; it revolves everywhere without wearying or failing; it may be called the Mother of all under heaven. I do not know its proper name: I style it "the Way," and if forced to give it a name, I call it "Great." Being great, it flows onward ("passing on"); flowing onward, it reaches afar ("far"); reaching afar, it turns back to its source ("returning").

Therefore the Way is great, heaven is great, earth is great, and the sage-king too is great. Within the cosmos there are four greats, and the king occupies one of them. Humanity models itself on earth, earth models itself on heaven, heaven models itself on the Way, and the Way models itself on what is so of itself (naturalness).

§2
Original (Chinese)

道者何？即鸿濛未判之前，天地未兆，人物无形，混混沌沌，浑然一气，无涯无际，无量无边，似有一物，由混沌而成，盘旋空际，先天地而生者，所谓「无极」是也。寂虚而毓生机，寥廓而含动意，所谓「太极」是也。万物皆有两，惟太极无二。自一动而开天地、分阴阳，四象五行，包含个内，人物繁衍，日月充盈，岂不生育多而太极衰乎？不知此个混成之物，视不见，听不闻，无物不有，无时不在，孑然独立，浑然中处，却又生生不已，化化无穷，自混沌以迄于今，初不改其常度，且独立之中，一气流行，周通法界，开阖自如，循环不已。以凡物而论，似乎其有困殆矣。孰知周流三界，充满群生，天赖之以清，地赖之以宁，谷赖之以盈，人赖之以生，无非顺其自然之运，其间生者自生，成者自成，而太极浑然完全，却不因之而稍殆，虽千变万化，迭出不穷，莫不由此而有兆有名，故可为天下母也。

What is "the Way"? Before primeval chaos had split apart—when neither heaven nor earth showed a sign, and humans and creatures had no form—there was an undifferentiated mass of the One Breath of Prior Heaven, shoreless, boundless, immeasurable. As though some reality had formed naturally out of chaos, whirling in the expanse of emptiness before heaven and earth came to be: this is what the ancients called the "Boundless" (wuji). Silent and empty yet breeding vitality, vast and open yet holding the first impulse of motion: this is what they called the "Supreme Ultimate" (taiji).

Everything in the ten thousand things comes in pairs; only the Supreme Ultimate has no second. From its single first movement, heaven and earth split open, yin and yang separated, and the Four Emblems and Five Phases were all encompassed within, so that humanity and creatures multiplied and sun and moon shone full. Someone might ask: having birthed and nourished so many beings, does the Supreme Ultimate itself not grow depleted and weak?

Little do they realize that this indistinctly formed reality—unseen by the eye, unheard by the ear—is absent from no creature and absent at no moment. Standing utterly alone, dwelling whole at the center of all things, it nevertheless generates without pause and transforms without limit, never once altering its constant measure from primeval chaos down to today. And right within its solitary constancy, one single breath circulates through the entire cosmos, opening and closing with effortless ease in an unbroken cycle.

Judged by ordinary objects, such unceasing motion would surely end in exhaustion. Yet as it flows through the three realms and fills all living things—heaven relying on it to stay clear, earth relying on it to stay tranquil, valleys relying on it to stay full, human beings relying on it for life—everything unfolds purely through its own natural rotation. What is due to be born is born of itself; what is due to mature matures of itself; and the Supreme Ultimate remains wholly intact, not worn down in the slightest by nourishing all things. Even across a thousand shifts and ten thousand transformations emerging without end, nothing takes a first sign or receives a name except from this source—so it truly merits the title "Mother of all under heaven."

§3
Original (Chinese)

夫天至高也，以高而可名；地至厚也，以厚而可名。惟此无极之极，不神之神，无声无臭，无象无形，而於穆不已，吾亦不知其所名，惟字之曰「道」，以道为天地群生共由之路、公共之端。道可包天地，天地不能包道；道可育群生，群生不能育道。以其浩浩渊渊，靡有穷极，强名之曰「大」。大哉道乎！何其前者往，后者续，长逝而靡底乎？大之外又曰「逝」。何其超沙界、充绝域、悠远而难测乎？逝之外又曰「远」。凡事变极则通，穷极则反，何其宛转流通，回环而不已乎？故又名之曰「反」。如此之名，不一其称，只可稍状其大。然大孰有过于道哉？道之外，惟天为大；天之外，惟地为大；地之上，惟王为大。故东西南北之中，有四大焉，王处其一。王为庶物首出之元，以管理河山，统辖人物，可与天地并称为大。但王为地载，故王法地以出治也。地为天覆，故地法天以行令也。且天为道毓，故天法道以行政也。而要皆本于自然，无俟勉强，不待安排。是道岂别有所法哉？吾亦强名之曰「道法自然」而已矣。学者性命交修，惟法天地之理气以为体，法天地之功效以为用，斯修性而性尽，炼命而命立矣，岂空言自然者所可比哉？

Heaven is supremely high, so it can be named by its height; earth is supremely thick, so it can be named by its thickness. Only this Ultimate of the Boundless—wondrous without flaunting marvels, without sound or scent, without image or shape, yet turning endlessly in deep, silent mystery—defies my naming. I can only style it "the Way," for the Way is the shared road walked by heaven, earth, and all living beings, the common origin from which all begin.

The Way can embrace heaven and earth, whereas heaven and earth cannot embrace the Way; the Way can nourish all beings, whereas all beings cannot nourish the Way. Because it is vast and fathomless without limit, I reluctantly name it "Great." How great is the Way! Why do waves pass on ahead while new waves follow seamlessly, flowing onward without bottom? Beyond "Great," I therefore call it "Passing On." Why does it transcend worlds numerous as the sands of the Ganges, filling the remotest reaches, too far to measure? Beyond "Passing On," I therefore call it "Far." Every affair, reaching its extreme of change, finds passage; reaching its limit, it turns back—why does the Way flow so gracefully around and circle back without pause? Therefore I also call it "Returning."

All these varied names can only hint at a tiny fraction of its greatness. Yet what could surpass the Way in greatness? Next to the Way, heaven alone is great; next to heaven, earth alone is great; upon the earth, the sage-king alone is great. Thus within the four quarters of the cosmos stand these "four greats," and the king holds one of them. Standing foremost among living creatures, ordering rivers and mountains and governing humanity and things, the king justly ranks beside heaven and earth as great.

Yet the king is borne up by the earth, so the king models his governance on the earth's thick virtue; earth is sheltered by heaven, so earth models its seasons of growth on heaven's decrees; and heaven is bred by the Way, so heaven models its administration on the Way. At bottom, all of this springs from naturalness, requiring neither forced effort nor human contrivance. What else, then, could the Way model itself upon? One can only say, as a makeshift phrase, "the Way models itself on what is so of itself"!

In dual cultivation of inner nature and life, the student models the substance of practice on the Principle and Breath of heaven and earth, and models its function on their creative efficacy. Only thus does cultivating nature fulfill nature completely, and refining life establish life securely—how could those who merely mouth empty talk of "naturalness" ever compare with this?

§4
Original (Chinese)

天地间，浑沦磅礴，浩荡弥纶，至显至微，最虚最实，而凡形形色色，莫不自个中生来。此何物耶？生于天地之先，宰乎天地之内，立清虚而不稍改易，周沙界而无有殆危，真可为天下母也。未开辟以前，有此母气而后天地生；既开辟以后，有此母气而后人物肇，吾不知其名，强字之曰「道」、曰「大」。大则无所不包，逝则无所不到。无曰远莫能致，须知穷极必反，道之大，不诚四大中所特出者哉？学人欲修至道，漫言自然，务须凝神调息。凝神则神不纷驰，人之心正，即天地之心亦正；调息则息不乖舛，人之气顺，即天地之气亦顺。参赞乾坤，经纶天地，功岂多乎哉？只在一心一身之间，咫尺呼吸而已矣。《中庸》云：「致中和，天地位，万物育。」其即此欤？人果时时存心，刻刻养气，除饥时食饭、困时打眠之外，随时随处，常常觉照，不许一念游移，一息间断，方免疾病之虞。否则稍纵即逝，外邪得而扰之，正气不存，邪气易入，有必然者。古云：「人能一念不起，片欲不生，天地莫能窥其隐，鬼神不能测其机。」洵非诬也。人谓筑基乃可长生，那知学道人就未筑基，只要神气常常纽成一团，毫不分散，则鬼神无从追魂摄魄，我命由我不由天也。吾不惜泄漏之咎，后之学者，苟不照此修持，则无以对我焉。

Between heaven and earth flows a vast, surging, all-pervading power—at once most manifest and most subtle, most empty and most real—from which every single form and phenomenon in existence takes birth. What reality is this? Born prior to heaven and earth, reigning within heaven and earth, standing in pure emptiness without the slightest change, circling countless worlds without danger of exhaustion—it truly deserves to be called the Mother of all under heaven. Before cosmic separation, this Mother Breath came first so that heaven and earth could be born; after separation, this same Mother Breath initiated humanity and all things. Unable to name it, I reluctantly style it "the Way" and "Great": "Great" encompasses everything, and "Passing On" reaches everywhere. Never say it is too distant to touch—know that what reaches its extreme always turns back! Is the greatness of the Way not truly the most preeminent of the four greats?

A student who wishes to cultivate the supreme Way must never blithely babble about "naturalness": you must put your hands earnestly to concentrating Spirit and regulating the breath! When you concentrate Spirit, the mind stops racing in confusion; when the human heart is upright, the very heart of heaven and earth is upright. When you regulate breath, breathing stops turning erratic and harsh; when human breath runs smoothly, the very breath of heaven and earth runs smoothly. Assisting the creative work of Qian and Kun and ordering heaven and earth—does that demand complicated formulas? It rests right here within one heart and one body, in the inches of a single inhale and exhale! When the Doctrine of the Mean says, "Carry equilibrium and harmony to their fullest, and heaven and earth find their proper places while the myriad things flourish," is this not what it means?

If a person genuinely preserves the heart from hour to hour and nurtures breath from moment to moment—apart from eating when hungry and sleeping when tired, maintaining steady, lucid awareness anytime and anywhere, never allowing a single thought to drift off or a single breath to lose awareness—only then can one stay free from the threat of illness. Otherwise, slacken for a split second and vitality slips away, letting external harms rush in: when upright breath cannot hold its ground, pathogenic forces enter inevitably. As the ancients said: "When a person lets not one stray thought arise and not a sliver of craving stir, even heaven and earth cannot spy out his secret, nor spirits fathom his pivot." Those words are utterly true!

People say one must complete laying the foundation before longevity is possible. Little do they know that even before laying the foundation, if a student of the Way simply keeps Spirit and Breath tightly braided together into one mass without ever letting them scatter, then spirits and ghosts have no handle to seize one's ethereal or corporeal soul, and one truly fulfills the saying, "My destiny lies with me, not with Heaven!" I have risked the blame of leaking heaven's secret to lay this plainest formula bare before you; if students of later times refuse to practice earnestly in this very way, they will truly have let my heartfelt effort down!

Key Terms

  • Boundless (wuji) and Supreme Ultimate (taiji): Huang Yuanji's reading of the cosmic source "indistinctly formed"—formless, undifferentiated single breath is the Boundless; quiet emptiness harboring vitality and vastness holding the impulse of motion is the Supreme Ultimate.
  • One Breath of Prior Heaven: called "Mother Breath" and "undivided single breath" in this chapter—the primordial energy of cosmos and life born before heaven and earth that circulates through the three realms without exhausting itself.
  • concentrating Spirit and regulating the breath: Huang Yuanji's concrete starting point for realizing "the Way models itself on naturalness"—gathering scattered spirit into calm focus and tending the breath softly and evenly until it flows smoothly.
  • Realizing equilibrium and harmony (zhi zhonghe): from the Doctrine of the Mean, referring to unbiased stillness before emotions stir (zhong) and fitting measure once they arise (he); used here to show how an upright mind and smooth breath resonate directly with cosmic order.
  • laying the foundation: the opening stage of internal alchemy, replenishing depleted essence and breath to secure physical and mental stability; here Huang Yuanji stresses that even prior to completing it, keeping Spirit and Breath daily "braided into one mass" already guards health and vitality.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Never use "the Way models itself on naturalness" as an excuse to drop discipline. Huang Yuanji puts it bluntly: "When cultivating the Way, do not blithely talk of naturalness." Only after steady work in concentrating Spirit and regulating breath brings mind and body back to uprightness and smoothness does genuine naturalness emerge; without that, "being natural" is just drifting on habit.
  2. The main arena of practice is the breath right beneath your nose. You do not need to retreat to a remote mountain or wait for a legendary master. Today—on your commute, on a walk, between work tasks—gently bringing scattered attention back to your immediate breathing is the most direct form of concentrating Spirit and regulating breath.
  3. Outside eating and sleeping, hold onto a thread of steady presence. What drains modern people most is rarely honest work; it is hours of mindless scrolling, anxious rumination, and emotional friction. Keeping Spirit and Breath "braided together" instead of leaking attention everywhere noticeably restores focus and resilience.
  4. Respect the rhythm of "reaching the extreme turns back." Whether in workload or emotional intensity, pushing anything to the absolute limit forces a reversal. Learning to pull back and recharge while reserves are still strong is far wiser than running yourself dry until exhaustion stops you.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Turning endlessly in deep, silent mystery" (wumu buyi): Quoted from the Classic of Poetry ("Odes of Zhou," Wei tian zhi ming), where wu (於) is an exclamation and mu evokes profound, quiet beauty describing Heaven's ceaseless Mandate.
  2. "Worlds numerous as sands" (shajie): A Buddhist loanword short for "worlds numerous as the sands of the Ganges," evoking boundless cosmic space.
  3. "My destiny lies with me, not with Heaven" (wo ming you wo bu you tian): A celebrated Daoist maxim cited in Ge Hong's Baopuzi (from the Turtle-Shell Inscription) and throughout later alchemical literature, affirming that autonomous cultivation of essence, breath, and spirit can transform ordinary decline.
  4. "Breathing does not turn erratic" (xi bu guaichuan) and "utterly true" (xun fei wu ye): In the base edition, chuan (舛) was misprinted as wai (外), and xun fei wu ye (洵非诬也) as gou fei zheng ye (沟非证也); both have been emended from context and parallel phrasing.
  5. Textual collation: Base-edition misprints for "standing alone" (孑然独立), a stray comma inside "one breath flows" (一气流行), "ten thousand transformations" (千变万化), "beyond Great also called" (大之外又曰), "preeminent!" (特出者哉), and "a single breath interrupted" (一息间断), along with repeated confusions of yue (曰) and ri (日), have been corrected in the Chinese source.

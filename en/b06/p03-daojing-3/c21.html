Chapter 21 · The Form of Great Virtue

The form of great virtue is the single Mysterious Pass—within the utter stillness of the dim and obscure, the light of Nature shines downward and True Essence first stirs; guarding this single thought without leakage is the root of returning to the origin

About This Chapter

Chapter 21 is one of the most mysterious passages in the Daodejing and one of those most treasured by masters of internal alchemy. Laozi layers the terms "dim and indistinct" (huanghu), "deep and obscure" (yaoming), "within it is an image," "within it is a thing," "within it is essence," and "within it is a reliable sign" to portray how the Way manifests. Huang Yuanji reads the entire chapter directly as a precise practical manual for "laying the foundation in the initial pass and recognizing the first stirring of the Mysterious Pass":

  1. "The form of great virtue" (kongde zhi rong) is the single aperture of the Mysterious Pass: Kong means an empty aperture, and rong means capacity to contain. This is no lifeless "stubborn emptiness," but a doorway within emptiness that harbors boundless vitality—called the "Gate of the Mysterious Female" in Daoism, the "Gate of the Way and Righteousness" in Confucianism, and the "Dharma Gate of Non-Duality" in Buddhism.
  2. Stage-by-stage fire phasing from "dim and indistinct" to "deep and obscure": Setting aside all worldly entanglements to sit quietly and contemplate, one first encounters "an image" (the light of Nature softly surfacing within the mind as subtle yang prepares to stir). Next, uniting the gaze of the eyes with the light of spirit to shine downward into the lower Cinnabar Field, one enters "a thing" (yang breath gently moving as water and fire first meet). Finally, entering the "deep and obscure," one reaches "essence" and "a reliable sign"—the entire body turns soft, warm, and harmonious, as the essence and breath of the limbs and frame gather to one point, melting like spring ice and surging like a rising tide. This is the unmistakable proof that true yang has stirred.
  3. After deep stillness, the greatest threshold is "preventing leakage": When warm, harmonious breath arises in the Cinnabar Field, stray thoughts and sensual desires are the chief danger. Huang Yuanji pointedly warns that losing Original Essence does not wait for sexual intercourse: the instant a single lustful thought stirs, True Essence has already left its seat. Only through steady lifting-and-gathering and patient restraint—holding back habitual impulses again and again—does one gradually reach the state where "essence is full and no longer craves sensuality."

Safety note: This chapter discusses deep internal-alchemy experiences such as "the first stirring of the Mysterious Pass," "the two-phase method of harvesting the Maṇi pearl," and "the breath of the Yin Heel vessel," as well as sexual impulse and the conservation of essence and breath. Modern readers should not forcibly hold the breath, strain to fix attention tightly on the lower abdomen, or harshly suppress normal physiological responses, as this can cause head pressure, stagnation of breath, or physical and emotional imbalance. Daily practice should center on relaxed quiet sitting, smooth breathing, and settling the mind into clarity.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：孔德之容，惟道是从。道之为物，惟恍惟惚。惚兮恍，其中有象；恍兮惚，其中有物；窈兮冥，其中有精。其精甚真，其中有信。自古及今，其名不去，以阅众甫。吾何以知众甫之然哉？以此。（甫者，始也，言万物初生之始。）

Laozi says: The bearing of great virtue—capable of containing all things—follows the Way alone. As a reality, the Way is utterly dim and indistinct. Indistinct and dim, within it there is an image; dim and indistinct, within it there is a thing; deep and obscure, within it there is essence. This essence is supremely real, and within it there is a trustworthy sign.

From ancient times down to the present, its name has never departed; by means of it we survey the beginnings of the myriad things. How do I know that the beginnings of all things are thus? By this very verification of True Essence.

(Original note: Fu means "beginning," referring to the first emergence of the myriad things.)

§2
Original (Chinese)

孔德之容，即玄关窍也。古云：「一孔玄关窍，乾坤共合成。中藏神气穴，名为坎离精。」又曰：「一孔玄关大道门，造铅结丹此中存。」《契》曰：「此两孔穴法，金气亦相胥。」故道曰「玄牝之门」，儒曰「道义之门」，佛曰「不二法门」，总之皆孔之德器能容，天地人物咸生自个中，无非是空是道，非空非道，即空即道，空与道两不相离，无空则无道，无道亦即无空，故曰「惟道是从」。欲求道者，舍此空器，何所从哉？

What is called "the form of great virtue" (kongde zhi rong) refers precisely to the single aperture of the Mysterious Pass in the human body. The ancients said: "A single aperture of the Mysterious Pass, formed together by Qian and Kun; inside it hides the Cavity of Spirit and Breath, named the Essence of Kan and Li." They also said: "The single aperture of the Mysterious Pass is the gate of the Great Way; forging Lead and congealing the Elixir reside within it." The Cantong qi likewise states: "This method of the two apertures—the breath of Metal sustains them in mutual accord." Therefore Daoism calls it the "Gate of the Mysterious Female," Confucianism calls it the "Gate of the Way and Righteousness," and Buddhism calls it the "Dharma Gate of Non-Duality."

At bottom, all these names point to the same principle: like a spacious vessel of vast virtue able to embrace and contain everything, heaven, earth, humanity, and the myriad creatures all spring from within it. It is emptiness and it is the Way; calling it dead emptiness is wrong, and calling it a tangible form is equally wrong—emptiness and the Way are one and inseparable. Without this emptiness, there is no Way; without the Way, there is no true emptiness. Hence Laozi says, "it follows the Way alone." For anyone wishing to seek the Way, apart from this empty vessel that holds all creation, where else could one begin?

§3
Original (Chinese)

但空而无状，即属顽空，学者又从何处以采药而结丹乎？必须虚也而含至实，无也而赅至有，方不为一偏之学。修行人，但将万缘放下，静养片晌，观照此窍，惚兮似无，恍兮若有，虚极静笃之中，神机动焉。无象者有象，此离己之性光，木火浮动之象，即微阳生时也。再以此神光偶动之机，合目光而下照，恍兮若有觉，惚兮若无知，其中之阳物动焉，此离光之初交于坎宫者。其时气机微弱，无可采取，惟有二候采牟尼法，调度阴跷之气，相会于气穴之中。调度采取为一候，归炉温养为一候。依法行持，不片晌间，火入水底，水中金生，杳杳冥冥，不知其极，此神气交而坎离之精生矣。

Yet if emptiness were utterly blank and devoid of vitality, it would be nothing but lifeless "stubborn emptiness"—and where then could a practitioner harvest the Medicine and form the Elixir? There must be supreme substance hidden within emptiness, and supreme fullness encompassed within non-being; only then does it avoid becoming a one-sided teaching.

A practitioner need only set aside all worldly entanglements, rest quietly for a little while, and softly contemplate this aperture. Then the mind becomes "indistinct as if nothing, dim as if something": at the summit of emptiness and the depths of stillness, the pivot of spirit suddenly stirs. Where there was no sign before, an "image" now appears—this is the luminous light of Nature floating upward from the heart, the sign of nascent vitality (Wood and Fire gently stirring), marking the moment when subtle yang is about to arise.

Next, seizing this first stirring of spirit-light, unite the gaze of the eyes with the mind and shine gently downward into the lower Cinnabar Field. One enters a state "dim as if perceiving, indistinct as if without conceptual knowing," and the yang reality within genuinely stirs—this is the light of Li (heart-fire) meeting the Palace of Kan (kidney-water) for the very first time.

At that moment, however, the breath-impulse is still faint and delicate; it cannot yet be forcefully harvested. One uses only what the ancients called "the two-phase method of harvesting the Maṇi pearl," gently guiding the vitality of the Yin Heel region through breath and intention so that it meets Spirit within the Cavity of Breath in the lower Cinnabar Field. Regulating and gathering forms the first phase; returning it to the furnace for quiet warm nurturing forms the second phase. Practicing according to this method, in no time at all fire enters the depths of water, true Metal is born within water, and one slips into the boundless dim and obscure state with no discernible limit—Spirit and Breath have genuinely united, and the True Essence of the conjunction of Kan and Li is born.

§4
Original (Chinese)

然真精生时，身如壁立，意若寒灰，自然而然，周身苏软快乐，四肢百体之精气，尽归于玄窍之内，其中大有信在，溶溶似冰泮，浩浩如潮生，非若前此之恍恍若有，惚惚似无，不可指名者也。此个真精，实为真一之精，非后天交感之精可比，亦即为天地人物发生之初，公共一点真精是矣。如冬至之阳，半夜之子，一岁一日之成功，虽不仅此，而气机要皆自此发端，假若千层台之始于累土，万里行之始于足下一般。此为天地人物，生生之本。本原一差，末流何极？以故自古及今，举凡修道之士，皆不离此真气之采，然后有生发之象。遍阅众物初生，无不同此一点真精，成象而成形。我又何以知众物之生有同然哉？以此空窍之中，真气积累久，则玄关开而真精生焉。要之，恍是光之密，惚是机之微，离中真阴是为恍惚中之物，坎中真阳是为杳冥中之精。学者必知之真，而后行之至也。

When True Essence truly arises, the body stands upright and steady as a cliff wall, and thought rests as cool and still as cold ashes. Quite naturally, a soft, buoyant ease spreads through the entire frame, and the essence and breath of the four limbs and hundred joints all draw back into the Mysterious Opening. Within it lies an unmistakable "trustworthy sign"—melting gently like spring ice thawing, swelling amply like a rising tide—no longer like the earlier vague, elusive sense of "dimly present, indistinctly absent" that could not be pinned down.

This True Essence is in reality the Prior Heaven True-One Essence, altogether incomparable to the turbid Posterior Heaven essence produced by sexual excitement. It is the single spark of True Essence shared at the very birth of heaven, earth, humanity, and the myriad creatures. Just like the first thread of yang at the winter solstice or the first stirring of life at the midnight zi hour, the full accomplishment of a year or a day does not stop there, yet every movement of vitality starts from that single origin—just as a thousand-story terrace begins with a basket of earth, or a ten-thousand-mile journey begins beneath one's feet. This is the endlessly generative root of heaven, earth, humanity, and all things. Miss the source by even a hair, and how far astray will the downstream course wander!

Therefore, from antiquity to the present, every cultivator of the Way depends upon recognizing and nurturing this spark of true breath; only then do successive transformations unfold. Surveying the first birth of all creatures, not a single one fails to rely on this same spark of True Essence—first forming a subtle image, then growing into tangible shape. How do I know that the birth of all things shares this same principle? Because within this empty aperture of my own being, once true breath has accumulated long enough, the Mysterious Pass naturally opens and True Essence naturally arises—I have verified it firsthand!

In short, "dim" (huang) refers to the unbroken density of luminous awareness, while "indistinct" (hu) refers to the subtlety of the moving pivot; the true yin hidden within Li (the heart-mind) is the "thing" within the dim and indistinct, and the true yang hidden within Kan (kidney-water) is the "essence" within the deep and obscure. A practitioner must first know this with crystal clarity; only then can one carry the practice all the way to fruition.

§5
Original (Chinese)

此恍兮惚，是性光发越，故云有象；惚兮恍，是以性光下照坎宫，而真阳发动，故云有物。窈冥之精，乃二五之精，故云「甚真」。欲得真精，须知真信。真信者，阴阳迭运，不失其候之谓，俟其信之初至，所当不易，即行擒伏之功得矣。凡人修炼之初，必要恍惚杳冥，而后人欲净尽，天理常存，凡息自停，真息乃见。此何以故？盖人心太明，知觉易生；若到杳冥，知觉不起，即元性、元命打成一片。此个恍惚杳冥，大为修士之要。

Let us spell out the preceding stages once more: "dim and indistinct" is the outward radiance of the inner light of Nature, so the classic says "within it is an image"; "indistinct and dim" is shining that light of Nature downward into the Palace of Kan in the lower Cinnabar Field so that true yang stirs, hence "within it is a thing"; and the essence in the deep and obscure is the Prior Heaven quintessence formed by the union of yin-yang and the Five Phases, hence "this essence is supremely real."

To obtain this True Essence, one must recognize the "true sign." Like the punctual ebb and flow of the tides, the true sign is the unerring signal of yin and yang alternating in due season. The moment this sign first arrives, do not fluster or shift your mind; right then and there, gently gather and steady it in harmony with its flow, and the work is accomplished.

At the outset of cultivation, an ordinary person must pass through this threshold of the dim and obscure; only then are selfish desires washed clean, Heavenly Principle abides steadily, coarse ordinary breathing at the nose and mouth subsides of itself, and deep, seamless True Breath appears. Why is this? Because the ordinary cognitive mind is overly sharp and calculating—as long as it stays hyper-alert, discursive perceptions spring up continuously. Only when stillness deepens to the deep and obscure, where discriminative perception ceases to operate, do Original Nature and Original Life truly fuse into one undivided whole. These four characters—huanghu yaoming ("dim, indistinct, deep, obscure")—are therefore of paramount importance to every practitioner.

§6
Original (Chinese)

学人当静定之时，忽然偶生知觉，此时神气凝聚胎田，浑然粹然，自亦不知其所之，此性命返还于无极之天也。虽然，外有是理，而丹田中必有融和气机，方为实据。由此一点融和，采之归炉，封固温养，自能发为真阳一气。但行工到此，大有危险。惟有一心内守，了照当中，方能团聚为丹药，可以长生不老。若生一他念，此个元气即已杂后天而不纯矣。若动一淫思，此个气机即驰于外，而真精从此泄漏矣。古人云：「泄精一事，不必夫妻交媾，即此一念之动，真精已不守舍，如走丹一般。」学人必心与气合，息与神交，常在此腔子里，久之自有无穷趣味生来。然而真难事也。设能识透玄机，亦无难事。起初不过用提掇之功，不许这点真气，驰而在下，亦不许这个真气，分散六根门头，总是一心皈命，五体投诚，久久自然精满不思色矣。愿学者，保守元精，毫不渗漏，始因常行熟道，觉得不易。苟能一忍再忍，不许念头稍动，三两月间，外阳自收摄焉。外阳收摄，然后见身中元气充足，而长生不老之人仙，从此得矣。仙又何远乎哉？

In the depths of stillness and stability, a practitioner suddenly experiences a spontaneous spark of lucid awareness. At that moment, Spirit and Breath condense within the lower Cinnabar Field—undivided, utterly pure—so that one scarcely knows where one's own body sits. This is the state in which inner nature and life return to the heaven of the Boundless. Even so, grasping the principle intellectually is not enough: there must be a genuine sensation of warm, harmonious vitality in the lower Cinnabar Field to serve as solid proof. Following that single spark of warmth and harmony, gently returning it to the furnace, sealing it steadily, and warming it in quietude, it naturally grows into a full current of true yang breath.

Yet reaching this stage also carries serious risk. Only by guarding inwardly with an undivided mind and holding clear awareness at the center can it coalesce into the Elixir Medicine that sustains lasting vitality. If a single stray thought arises at this point, that pure Original Breath is immediately mixed with Posterior Heaven dross and loses its purity; if a single lustful thought stirs, the breath-current bolts outward at once, and True Essence leaks away. As the ancients said: "Losing essence does not require marital intercourse; the very instant a single lustful thought stirs, True Essence has already deserted its dwelling, just like losing the Elixir."

The practitioner must let mind merge with breath and breath commune with spirit, resting steadily within the square inch of one's own frame; with time, an inexhaustible quiet joy naturally wells up. Admittedly, this is truly difficult—yet once one sees clearly through its subtle mechanism, it is not so difficult after all. At the start, it is simply a matter of gentle lifting and gathering: not letting this spark of true breath rush downward to waste, nor allowing it to scatter outward through the six sense gates (eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body, and mind). Always entrusting the whole heart and settling the entire body in undivided devotion, over time one naturally arrives at the state where "essence is full and no longer craves sensuality."

I hope that every student will safeguard Original Essence without the slightest leakage. At the beginning, because we are so accustomed to the well-worn path of distraction and indulgence, it feels hard indeed. Yet if one can exercise patient restraint again and again, refusing to let craving thoughts gain a foothold, within two or three months outward restlessness will settle and draw inward of its own accord. Once outward agitation subsides and gathers in, one sees that Original Breath within the body has grown abundant—and from here begins the solid foundation of enduring health and longevity known as the Human Immortal. How could the Way of the immortals ever be far from us?

Key Terms

  • Mysterious Pass (xuanqiao / xuanguan): Huang Yuanji's reading of Laozi's "form of great virtue" (kongde zhi rong), referring to the dynamic pivot where Spirit and Breath unite and vitality first stirs in utter stillness—not a fixed anatomical organ in the body.
  • the dim and obscure (state): Layers of consciousness and breath-activity in deep quiet sitting: huanghu ("dim and indistinct") is the twilight state as discursive thoughts recede; yaoming ("deep and obscure") is the profound stillness where discriminative perception ceases and Spirit and Breath merge.
  • Two-phase method of harvesting the Maṇi pearl (erhou cai mouni fa): an internal-alchemy practice; maṇi (Sanskrit for "luminous jewel") here signifies the nascent Prior Heaven True Essence in the Cinnabar Field, while the "two phases" are (1) gently regulating and gathering it, and (2) returning it to the furnace for quiet warm nurturing.
  • Yin Heel (yinqiao): in traditional meridian and alchemical usage, the perineal region where the Yin Heel vessel originates, often used as a shorthand for the lower source from which Original Breath stirs.
  • light of Nature: the lucid, illuminating awareness emitted by the true yin within Li (the heart-mind), explained here as the "image" in "dim and indistinct, within it is an image."
  • conjunction of Kan and Li: Li represents heart-spirit (Fire) and Kan represents kidney-breath (Water); when spirit-light shines downward into the Cinnabar Field, water and fire meet to generate warm, harmonious True Essence.
  • True sign (zhenxin): the unmistakable, tangible bodily proof when True Essence first stirs (warm harmony, buoyant ease, a tide-like surge), as opposed to wishful imagination.
  • ordinary breathing and True Breath: coarse, audible inhalation and exhalation through the nose and mouth constitute ordinary breathing; in utter stillness, when outer breathing grows gossamer-fine and seems to pause, the subtle, rhythmic opening and closing of inner breath is True Breath.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Guard against both "stubborn emptiness" and "fabricating sensations." Quiet sitting does not mean turning yourself into a lifeless block of wood, nor does it mean straining your imagination to invent sensations of energy. Let worldly preoccupations fall away and rest quietly; moments of genuine warmth and ease will come of themselves.
  2. The essence of "dim and obscure" is letting an overactive mind rest. Huang Yuanji observes that "the ordinary mind is overly sharp, so discursive perception easily arises." Modern minds run at full speed all day, calculating and analyzing, which makes settling down at night hard. In practice, stop trying so hard to "see clearly"—allow yourself to soften into an effortless, gently muted quietude.
  3. Safeguarding essence begins with watching a single thought. "Preventing leakage" need not sound arcane: simply cutting back on compulsive sensory stimulation, pornography, and emotional drain already preserves your body's most precious mental and physical vitality.
  4. The hardest stretch of changing any habit is the first two or three months. As the text notes, "at first, because of the well-worn path, it feels difficult." Whether breaking late-night scrolling, curbing agitation, or steadying impulses, the first several weeks rely on patient restraint—not sliding down old grooves. Once you clear the inertia of habit, calm and clarity become far more effortless.

Translator's Notes

  1. The Cantong qi quote on "this method of the two apertures": Drawn from Wei Boyang's Eastern Han classic Zhouyi cantong qi, where the "two apertures" (variously glossed as heaven/earth, sun/moon, the nostrils, or the correspondence between Cinnabar Field and Mysterious Opening) symbolize the mutual nourishment of Metal and Breath, yin and yang.
  2. "The light of Nature of Li-ji, an image of Wood and Fire stirring": In the base edition, ji (己, the sixth Heavenly Stem) was misprinted as yi (已) and "Wood" (木) as "root" (本); both are emended according to alchemical usage. Li-ji links the trigram Li with ji-Earth (the seat of heart-spirit and True Intention), while "Wood and Fire" evoke the warm, nascent radiance of the light of Nature.
  3. "Essence of the Two and the Five" (er-wu zhi jing): Alludes to Zhou Dunyi's Song-dynasty Explanation of the Diagram of the Supreme Ultimate ("the truth of the Boundless and the essence of the Two and the Five wondrously unite and condense"), where "Two" is yin and yang and "Five" is the Five Phases (Metal, Wood, Water, Fire, Earth).
  4. "Yin and yang alternating in due season": In the base edition, "alternating" (迭) was misprinted as "selecting" (选) and "called" (谓) as "regulate" (调); emended by context.
  5. Textual collation: Several OCR/edition misprints in the base text—including variants for yaoming (杳冥), "stubborn emptiness" (顽空), "harvest the medicine" (采药), "amidst utter stillness" (之中), "fire enters the bottom of water" (火入水底), "leakage" (泄漏), and "marital intercourse" (夫妻交媾)—have been corrected individually in the Chinese source.

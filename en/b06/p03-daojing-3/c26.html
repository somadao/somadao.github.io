Chapter 26 · The Heavy Is the Root of the Light

Following the forward course begets mortals, while reversing it refines immortals—returning breath to weight establishes life, returning spirit to stillness brightens nature; the "lord of ten thousand chariots" is one's own Original Spirit, never to be squandered on fleeting glamour

About This Chapter

On the surface, Chapter 26 looks like political counsel warning a monarch never to stray from his baggage wagons or act frivolously when traveling. Yet out of these few dozen characters, Huang Yuanji unfolds the entire central secret of internal alchemy's "forward generation and reversed cultivation"—from Outer Medicine and Inner Medicine, Lesser Medicine and Greater Medicine, to the Lesser and Greater Celestial Circuits and the forming of the Holy Embryo—concluding with an urgent plea to his disciples to "inscribe this on your sashes and keep it faithfully in mind":

  1. "The heavy is the root of the light" treats refining life; "stillness is the ruler of restlessness" treats cultivating nature: Once fallen into Posterior Heaven, Breath grows light and flighty while Spirit grows agitated and restless. Cultivation means gathering flighty breath back into grounded weight (Water regenerating Metal) and drawing restless spirit back into lucid stillness (Fire regenerating Wood). The more settled the breath, the swifter its movement; the stiller the heart, the more seamlessly it responds to all things.
  2. The "lord of ten thousand chariots" is your own Original Spirit: In "lightness loses the minister; restlessness loses the sovereign," the minister is Breath and the sovereign is Spirit. Human beings possess a priceless treasure of nature and life, yet carelessly spend it chasing sensual glamour and material gain until both Spirit and Breath are lost—lamentable as the elephant slain for its own ivory tusks.
  3. A rare, detailed blueprint of the reversed sequence "Outer Medicine → Inner Medicine → Holy Embryo": Huang Yuanji clearly contrasts the "forward cultivation" of Confucianism and Buddhism with the "reversed overcoming" of Daoist internal alchemy, mapping the complete path from Spirit entering Breath to produce Lesser/Outer Medicine, uniting Lead and Mercury in the furnace to produce Greater/Inner Medicine (with the shaking of the six roots), then turning the Waterwheel, fanning the Wind of Xun ☴, and condensing the Holy Embryo. At the same time, he issues a stern warning: reversed cultivation is perilous—without a true teacher, authentic method, and the protection of virtuous conduct, blind practice rarely escapes harm.

Safety note: This chapter discusses advanced internal-alchemy stages such as "reversed refinement," "Lesser and Greater Medicine," "the shaking of the six roots," and "Waterwheel circulation." Huang Yuanji himself solemnly warns in the text that "without a true teacher and clear understanding of the upright method, recklessly harvesting and refining rarely avoids harm." Modern readers must not literally force breath, drive energy up the spine toward the crown by mental straining, or chase dramatic bodily sensations, as doing so can trigger dizziness, chest tightness, or energetic imbalance. For daily life, draw simply on its core wisdom: grounding breath in steadiness, quieting spirit into stillness, and refraining from draining oneself on sensory excess.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：重为轻根，静为躁君。是以圣人终日行不离辎重，虽有荣观，燕处超然。奈何万乘之主，而以身轻天下？轻则失臣，躁则失君。

Laozi says: The heavy is the root of the light; stillness is the ruler of restlessness. Therefore the sage travels all day without ever leaving his heavy supply wagons; even when magnificent sights lie before him, he dwells in quiet detachment, altogether unmoved. Why, then, does the lord of ten thousand war chariots treat his own person lightly for the sake of the world? Through lightness one loses the minister; through restlessness one loses the sovereign.

§2
Original (Chinese)

修炼之道，不外神气二者，调之养之，返乎元始之天而已。其在先天，气浑于无象，厚重常安，神寓于无形，虚灵难状；一到后天，气之重者而轻扬，神之静者而躁动，气不如先天之活泼、常氤氲而化醇，神不似先天之光明、脱根尘而独耀。此命之所以不立，性之所以难修也。学者欲得长生，须知气必归根。夫根何以归哉？必以气之轻浮者，复还于敦厚之域，屹然矗立，凝然一团，则气还于命，而浩浩其天矣；以神之躁妄者，复归于澄彻之乡，了了常明，如如自在，则神还于性，而浑浑无极矣。如此神返元性，气返元命，不啻天地未兆之前，浑沦无际，浩荡靡穷，斯其凝愈固，其行愈速也，其虚无朕，其用无方也。由是气愈重而愈轻，所谓「浩然之气，至大至刚，充塞天地」是；神能静而亦能动，《易》所谓「妙万物而为神」，子思子曰「至诚如神」是。是以君子之于道也，终日行不离乎辎车之重，恐气轻而累重，反滞其行之机。如此稳重自持，不愈速其行乎？纵有声色之美，货利之贵，是为众人所荣观，不为君子所介意，当前寓目，君子一如燕居独处，超然于物色之外，莫知其为有焉。奈何以万乘之主，至尊至贵，可仙可佛之身，而不自爱，反以世路荣观、人寰乐趣为缘，不亦轻其身而自视太小耶？夫轻则失臣，臣即气也，失臣则失气矣；躁则失君，君即神也，失君则失神矣。神气两失，而谓身能存，有几乎？此殆不知人身难得，中土难生，而反自轻其身也，不诚大可慨欤？在彼恋尘世之荣华，慕当途之仕宦，只说利己者多、肥家者盛，那知富贵之场，即是干戈之地，古来象以齿焚身、璧因怀获罪，其为害可历数也。人奈何只见其小，而不从其大耶？噫嘻！痛矣！

The Way of cultivation comes down to nothing other than Spirit and Breath: regulating them, nurturing them, and returning them to the heaven of the Primordial Beginning. In Prior Heaven, Breath is undivided and formless, thick, weighty, and ever calm; Spirit dwells beyond shape, empty, luminous, and beyond description. The moment they fall into Posterior Heaven, weighty Breath grows light and flighty, and still Spirit grows agitated and restless—Breath loses its Prior Heaven vitality and rich, mellow nourishment, and Spirit loses its Prior Heaven brilliance that shines alone free of the senses and their objects. That is why bodily life fails to stand firm and inner nature is so hard to perfect.

Anyone seeking lasting health and longevity must understand that Breath must return to its root. How does it return to its root? Flighty, scattered Breath must be brought back into solid, grounded depth—standing firm like a mountain, condensed into one cohesive mass—so that Breath returns to Life, vast and boundless as heaven. Restless, deluded Spirit must be brought back into limpid clarity—vividly luminous, calmly at ease just as it is—so that Spirit returns to Nature, undivided as the Boundless.

When Spirit returns to Original Nature and Breath returns to Original Life in this way, it is no different from the state before heaven and earth showed a trace: boundless, whole, and endlessly vast. At that point, the more firmly Breath condenses, the swifter its circulation; the more tracelessly empty Spirit grows, the more boundless its responsiveness. Thus the weightier Breath becomes, the lighter and freer its action—just as Mencius described: "the flood-like breath, supremely great and supremely strong, filling all between heaven and earth." And because Spirit can rest completely still, it can also move wondrously—just as the Book of Changes says, "what moves the myriad things wondrously is called Spirit," and Zisi says, "utmost sincerity is like a spirit."

Therefore, as the noble person walks the Way, he travels all day without ever abandoning the heavy stability of his supply wagons, fearing that flighty breath will breed burdens and actually clog his progress. Holding himself with such grounded poise, does he not move all the faster and farther? Even when enticing sights and sounds or coveted wealth and status—the glittering spectacles prized by the crowd—stand right before his eyes, the noble person gives them no heed; looking straight at them, he remains just as if resting quietly in solitude, detached above all appearances, as though they were not even there.

How tragic, then, that a human being—possessing a supreme, priceless body worthy of "a lord of ten thousand chariots," capable of attaining sagehood or immortality—fails to cherish himself and instead chases worldly glamour and fleeting pleasures! Is that not cheapening oneself and holding oneself far too small? "Lightness loses the minister": the minister is Breath, so lightness loses Breath. "Restlessness loses the sovereign": the sovereign is Spirit, so restlessness loses Spirit. Once both Spirit and Breath are lost, how many can expect even to preserve their physical life? Truly, this comes from forgetting how rare it is to obtain a human body and be born where the Way can be heard, and recklessly cheapening oneself—is that not deeply heartbreaking?

Those who cling to worldly splendor and envy high office boast only of how much profit they reap and how richly they fatten their households. Little do they see that the arena of wealth and rank is a battlefield of spears and shields! Since antiquity, the elephant has brought slaughter upon itself because of its ivory tusks, and a man has brought ruin upon himself for carrying a priceless jade—the calamities are too many to count. Why do people fix their eyes only on trivial gain and turn away from what is truly great? Alas, how painful!

§3
Original (Chinese)

此言水轻而浮，为后天之气，属外药；金沉而重，为先天之元命，号真铅，又号金丹，又号白虎初弦之气，其名不一，是为内药。先天金生水，为顺行之常道，生人以之，故曰「重为轻根」。夫人生于后天，纯是狂荡轻浮之气作事，以故水气轻而浮，情欲多生，命宝丧失，所以易老而衰。君子有逆修之法，无非水复生金，轻返于重，以复乎天元一气，是以终日行之而不离乎辎重，不过亭亭矗矗，屹然特立，厚重不迁，养成浩气，充塞乾坤而已矣。此为逆修之仙道，炼丹以之。总之，由有形以复无形，丹道之一事也。火燥而动，为后天之神，属外药；木静而凝，为先天之元性，曰真汞，曰真精，又曰青龙真一之气，其名亦多，要皆内药。先天木生火，为顺行之常道，生人以之，故曰「静为躁君」。夫人成形而后，纯是智虑杂妄之神用事，以故火性飞扬，变诈百出，性真梏没，所以易弱而倾。君子有倒施之工，无非火复生木，躁返于静，以还乎不二元神，于此虽有荣观，燕处超然，无非万象咸空，一真在抱，养成大觉金仙，昭回霄汉而已矣。此为逆炼之仙道，成仙以之。要之，自有觉以还无觉，又修道之一端也。皆由外药以修内药，自后天而返先天也。吾更为之畅言曰：生人之道，顺而生；修仙之道，逆而克，盖不克则不能生，亦不克则不能成，《河图》《洛书》之所以生克并用也。今之儒释修养，与吾道有异者，大抵彼用顺行，一循自然之度；吾道独逆炼，则有勉强作为之工，倘有不克，无以为生成也。但顺而修则易，逆而修则难，不得真师，不明正法，妄采妄炼，鲜不为害。既得真师，明正法矣，不结仙缘，不修功善，则神天不佑，魔魅来缠，必有将成而败，倾丹倒鼎，连身命俱丧者，此诚不可不慎也。

Speaking in terms of the Five Phases of internal alchemy: Water is light and buoyant, representing Posterior Heaven breath, which belongs to the Outer Medicine; Metal is dense and heavy, representing Prior Heaven Original Life—variously called True Lead, the Golden Elixir, or the Breath of the First-Quarter White Tiger—all of which belong to the Inner Medicine. In Prior Heaven, Metal generates Water: that is the normal forward course of creation used to beget mortal offspring, hence "the heavy is the root of the light." Once born into Posterior Heaven, human beings are driven purely by wild, flighty breath, so Water-breath floats restlessly, passions multiply, the treasure of life drains away, and one quickly ages and declines. The noble person possesses the method of reversal: simply making Water regenerate Metal, returning lightness to weight so as to restore the single breath of Heaven's origin. Thus "traveling all day without leaving the heavy supply wagons" simply means standing upright, towering and unshaken, grounded and unmoving, nurturing a flood-like upright breath that fills Qian and Kun. This is the reversed immortal path of refining Life—how the Elixir is forged. Summed up, "returning from form back to the formless" is one great half of the Elixir Way.

Now look at the other half: Fire is parched and restless, representing Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit, which belongs to the Outer Medicine; Wood is still and cohesive, representing Prior Heaven Original Nature—called True Mercury, True Essence, or the True-One Breath of the Azure Dragon—all of which belong to the Inner Medicine. In Prior Heaven, Wood generates Fire: that too is the normal forward course that begets mortals, hence "stillness is the ruler of restlessness." Once a person grows into mature form, calculating thoughts and tangled delusions take charge, so heart-fire flares wildly, schemes and deceits proliferate, authentic nature is shackled and buried, and body and mind swiftly weaken and collapse. The noble person applies an inverted work: simply making Fire regenerate Wood, returning restlessness to stillness so as to restore the non-dual Original Spirit. Here, "though magnificent sights lie before him, he dwells in quiet detachment" means emptying all outer appearances and embracing the single Truth alone, maturing into a Greatly Awakened Golden Immortal whose light illuminates the heavens. This is the reversed immortal path of refining Nature—how immortality is realized. Summed up, "returning from discursive perception back to non-discriminative awareness" is the other great half of cultivating the Way. Both halves work "through Outer Medicine to refine Inner Medicine, returning from Posterior Heaven to Prior Heaven."

Let me speak even more plainly: the Way of begetting mortals goes forward by generation; the Way of cultivating immortality goes backward by overcoming—for without overcoming, nothing new is regenerated and nothing reaches completion. That is the deep reason the Yellow River Chart and Luo River Writing employ both generation and overcoming together. Where today's Confucian and Buddhist self-cultivation differs from our Daoist alchemy is largely that they follow the forward path, abiding altogether by natural measure, whereas our alchemical Way uniquely employs reversed refinement with deliberate, effortful fire phasing—without that overcoming, there is no reversed generation.

Yet forward cultivation is easy, while reversed cultivation is exceedingly difficult! Without meeting a true master and grasping the upright method, anyone who recklessly harvests and blindly refines rarely escapes grave harm. And even after finding a true master and understanding the upright method, if one fails to form wholesome karmic bonds and cultivate hidden merit and good deeds, then Heaven and the spirits will not lend their protection, inner and outer obstructions will entangle one, and one will fail on the very brink of success—overturning the cauldron, spilling the Elixir, and endangering even one's life. On this point, one can never be too cautious!

§4
Original (Chinese)

何以逆之克之？始用顺道之常，效夫妇交媾之法，以火入水乡，即是以神入气中，此为凡父、凡母交而产药；迨至火蒸水沸，水底金生，斯时玄窍开，而真信至，是为真阳生，而小药产，此为外药；金气既生，真铅自足，于以火促水腾，木载金升，切切催之，款款运之，上升乾鼎，以真铅配真汞，以真火、真意引之，下入丹田，即入坤腹，以炉鼎和药物炼丹，此返坎为男，复离为女，颠倒女男，迭为宾主，收归坤炉，烹炼一晌，再候真阳火动，以为金丹大药，此为内药生，又曰大药产，此为灵父、圣母交媾而育者也。且前小药之生，动在肾管外，其气小，故曰小药、外药；此则动于气根之内，生时有天应星、地应潮、六根震动之状，故曰内药、大药，又曰金丹。再以此金丹，运起河车，鼓动巽风，施用坤火，合离宫真精而煅之，真气合真精，即以先天阳气，制伏后天阴精，阴精亦合真气，而化为圣胎。夫真气，自真精而生者也，为子气，气复归精，故喻子投母胎，所谓「子恋母而来，母恋子而住，子母相抱，神气相依」。即内用天然真火，外用阴符阳火，内外交炼，即结为圣胎，所谓「铅将尽，汞亦干，化成一块紫金霜」。金丹大道，与生人异者，只此处处逆施造化，颠倒乾坤耳。凡有功德、有缘、有道之士，遇吾此注，尽可施功，不受异端惑乱。然而天机尽泄于此，如有功德之人，得天启沃，明白此旨，亦毋得轻泄，致干罪咎焉。

How, concretely, does one reverse and overcome? One begins by borrowing the ordinary forward pattern—modeled on the union of husband and wife—sending heart-fire down into the realm of kidney-water, which means bringing Spirit down into Breath. This is "the union of ordinary father and ordinary mother giving birth to the Medicine." Once fire steams water to a gentle simmer and true Metal is born at the bottom of the water, the Mysterious Opening opens and the true sign arrives: true yang is born and the Lesser Medicine emerges—this is the Outer Medicine.

With Metal-breath born, True Lead is naturally ample. Using Fire to lift Water in vapor and Wood-intention to carry Metal upward—urging earnestly yet circulating gently—one raises it up to the Qian cauldron at the crown to pair True Lead with True Mercury. Then, guided by true fire and True Intention, it descends into the lower Cinnabar Field—the belly of Kun—where furnace, cauldron, and ingredients blend and cook the Elixir. Here Kan water reverses to play the masculine yang and Li fire returns to play the feminine yin, inverting male and female so they alternate as host and guest. Drawn back into the Kun furnace and cooked quietly for a spell, one awaits a deeper stirring of true yang fire to crystallize the Greater Medicine of the Golden Elixir: this is "Inner Medicine born," also called "Greater Medicine produced," nurtured by the union of the "Spiritual Father and Holy Mother."

Earlier, when the Lesser Medicine arose, its stirring occurred outside the kidney channel and its current was modest—hence "Lesser Medicine" or "Outer Medicine." Now this Greater Medicine stirs deep within the root of breath; its arising brings the grand signs of "heaven answering with stars, earth answering with tides," and the shaking of the six roots—hence "Inner Medicine," "Greater Medicine," and Golden Elixir. Next, taking this Golden Elixir, one sets the Waterwheel turning, fans the Wind of Xun ☴, applies Kun-fire, and calcines it together with the True Essence of the Palace of Li: True Breath merges with True Essence—meaning Prior Heaven yang breath subdues Posterior Heaven yin essence, and yin essence in turn fuses with True Breath to transform into the Holy Embryo.

Because True Breath was originally born out of True Essence, it is likened to the "child's breath"; when Breath now returns to reunite with Essence, it is likened to a child returning to its mother's womb—as the saying goes, "the child comes out of longing for the mother, the mother stays out of longing for the child; child and mother embrace, Spirit and Breath cling together." Inwardly applying natural true fire and outwardly timing advancing yang fire and withdrawing yin tallies, inner and outer refine each other until the Holy Embryo forms: "Lead nearly spent, Mercury dried clear, fused into a single lump of purple-gold frost."

All that separates the Great Way of the Golden Elixir from the ordinary path of mortal procreation lies right here—reversing creation at every turn and inverting Qian and Kun. Any student of virtue, affinity, and devotion to the Way who encounters this commentary of mine may study and apply its upright principles without being misled by heterodox sects. Yet heaven's secrets have been nearly laid bare here: should a person of virtue receive Heaven's illumination and grasp this purport, never leak it lightly, lest you incur blame!

§5
Original (Chinese)

至若经云「万乘之主」，即人身中之元神也。夫人之心，莫不欲一身安泰，百岁康强，奈何知诱物化，欲起情生，而以身轻用于天下也。此气虚浮而丧气，此神躁率而失神，身之存者，盖亦鲜矣，何况金丹大道乎哉？此注已将筑基炼己、结丹还丹、玉液金液、小大周天之法则，详细剖明，生等当书诸绅，佩服不忘，庶知之真而行之至也。由是功成道就，永为天上神仙，不受人间苦恼，岂不甚幸？各宜勉旃！

As for the classic's phrase "lord of ten thousand chariots," it refers precisely to the Original Spirit within each human body. Who in the world does not wish for bodily peace and vigorous health for a hundred years? Yet alas, the mind is lured outward and assimilated by external things; cravings stir and passions flare, until people recklessly throw their priceless bodies away chasing the world. Thus Breath turns hollow and flighty and is lost, Spirit turns hasty and restless and is lost—few enough manage even to preserve their ordinary health, let alone accomplish the Great Way of the Golden Elixir!

In this commentary, I have thoroughly laid open the laws of laying the foundation and refining the self, forming and reverting the Elixir, the Jade Liquid and Golden Liquid, and the Lesser and Greater Celestial Circuits. You disciples should inscribe these instructions on your sashes as the ancients did, wearing them faithfully in memory and never forgetting—so that your knowledge may be true and your practice complete. Carrying this through to fruition, enjoying lasting serenity free from worldly affliction—would that not be the highest good fortune? Let each of you strive earnestly!

Key Terms

  • Heavy supply wagons (zizhong): heavy carts carrying provisions and gear during ancient marches; a metaphor for grounded stability and deep reserves of Original Breath.
  • reversal (forward generation and reversed overcoming): following the Five Phases' generating cycle outward (Metal generating Water, Wood generating Fire) in expenditure and procreation is "forward"; reversing the flow inward (Water regenerating Metal, Fire regenerating Wood) to replenish nature and life is "reversed."
  • Outer and Inner Medicine (Lesser and Greater Medicine): Outer/Lesser Medicine refers to the nascent yang stirred in the initial pass when Spirit-light meets Posterior Heaven essence and breath; Inner/Greater Medicine refers to the Prior Heaven Great Medicine stirred deep at the root of breath after Lead and Mercury cook in the furnace, accompanied by the shaking of the six roots.
  • True Lead and Mercury: core alchemical code names—True Lead signifies the true yang inside Kan (Prior Heaven Original Life, dense and heavy like metal), while True Mercury signifies the true yin inside Li (the luminous awareness of Prior Heaven Original Nature, lively like quicksilver).
  • Waterwheel (heche): originally a waterwheel lifting irrigation water; in internal alchemy, the circulation of true breath up and down the Governing and Conception vessels to carry essence and breath back to the furnace.
  • Jade Liquid and Golden Liquid (yuye, jinye): stage names in internal alchemy—Jade Liquid Reverted Elixir generally pertains to refining essence into breath and the Lesser Celestial Circuit, while Golden Liquid Reverted Elixir pertains to refining breath into spirit and the Greater Celestial Circuit.
  • Holy Embryo: the inner spiritual body condensed once Spirit and Breath fuse seamlessly and Prior Heaven essence and breath become one.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Your "supply wagons" are the daily baseline you must never abandon. No matter how hectic your schedule or urgent a project, regular sleep, real meals, and quiet unhurried time alone are your supply wagons—ditch your baseline to sprint faster, and flighty breath will quickly make you crash.
  2. Practice "quiet detachment" in the face of temptation. When faced with compulsive scrolling, status envy, or sudden emotional provocation, remind yourself: "I am the lord of ten thousand chariots—why trade my Spirit and Breath for a passing spectacle?" Taking half a step back settles most of the storm.
  3. Weight creates speed; stillness empowers action. Truly capable people in work and life rarely look frantic, because "the firmer the condensation, the swifter the motion." Quiet the inner noise, and both judgment and execution grow sharper and faster.
  4. Respect gradualism and ethical footing. Even if internal alchemy fascinates you deeply, remember Huang Yuanji's iron rule: without a steady foundation and upright conduct, never chase dramatic bodily sensations or shortcuts—steadying character and daily behavior is the safest, truest foundation.

Translator's Notes

  1. "The elephant dies for its ivory; a man is condemned for hiding a jade": Adapts two famous sayings from the Zuo Commentary (Duke Xiang 24: "An elephant's tusks destroy its own body because of greed for wealth"; Duke Huan 10: "An ordinary man commits no crime—carrying a jade is his crime"), warning how coveting glamour invites ruin.
  2. "Zisi says: 'Utmost sincerity is like a spirit'": From the Doctrine of the Mean; the preceding quote "what moves the myriad things wondrously is called Spirit" is from the Discussion of the Trigrams in the Book of Changes.
  3. "Inscribe this on your sashes" (shu zhu shen): From the Analects (15.6, "Zizhang wrote it on his sash"); shen was the long sash hanging from a scholar's waist, where treasured maxims were inscribed for constant recall.
  4. "Lead nearly spent, Mercury dried clear, fused into a single lump of purple-gold frost": A classic Ming–Qing alchemical verse depicting the complete purification and fusion of yin and yang into the Holy Embryo.
  5. Textual collation: Base-edition misprints for "standing firm" (屹然矗立), "shackled and buried" (性真梏没), "Greatly Awakened Golden Immortal shining across the heavens" (大觉金仙，昭回霄汉), "immortal path of reversed refinement" (逆炼之仙道), "recklessly harvesting and refining" (妄采妄炼), two errors for "union" (交媾), "is it not?" (乎哉), and multiple yue (曰) / ri (日) confusions have been corrected in the Chinese source.

III · Classic of the Way (Ch. 21–30) · 道经

Chapter 28 · Constant Virtue Never Departs

Keeping to the female, keeping to the dark, keeping to the humble correspond to refining essence into breath, breath into spirit, and spirit into emptiness—concentrating Spirit in the Cavity of Breath requires regulating breath, catching the Living Zi Hour between intention and no-intention

About This Chapter

The three celebrated couplets of Chapter 28—"know the male, yet keep to the female"; "know the white, yet keep to the black"; "know glory, yet keep to disgrace"—are often read as worldly maxims on overcoming strength through softness. Huang Yuanji pairs this chapter directly with Chapter 21 ("The Form of Great Virtue"), revealing it as a complete developmental roadmap of internal alchemy's "three transmutations" (refining essence into breath, refining breath into spirit, refining spirit back into emptiness), before guiding the reader hand by hand through the subtlest riddles of concentrating Spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath, regulating the breath, and True Intention:

  1. The three pairs of "knowing and keeping" correspond to three ascending alchemical passes: "Know the male, keep to the female, return to the infant" maps onto the initial pass of yin-yang union and refining essence into breath; "know the white, keep to the black, return to the Boundless" maps onto the middle pass of driving the Three Carts, running the Greater Celestial Circuit, and refining breath into spirit; "know glory, keep to disgrace, return to the uncarved block" maps onto the upper pass of feigning simplicity, mingling humbly with the world to nurture emptiness, and refining spirit back into emptiness.
  2. Concentrating Spirit cannot do without regulating the breath: Huang Yuanji states plainly that knowing only how to gather Spirit in the lower Cinnabar Field without knowing how to regulate breath leaves no bellows to fan the fire—ordinary breathing will not subside and True Breath will not emerge. Spirit and breath must lean on each other, sinking into the deep twilight before heaven and earth parted, so that at the exact flash when one yang first stirs, one catches the Living Zi Hour.
  3. Unraveling True Intention "between intention and no-intention": Many students wonder: "If not a single thought is supposed to arise, how can I use intention to harvest the medicine? Won't using intention drop me right back into Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit?" Huang Yuanji offers an unforgettable simile: True Intention is like a "seed-fire" banked deep under hearth ashes—no flame shows on the surface, yet the live ember is always there; the moment vitality stirs, awareness responds naturally, fulfilling the Book of Changes's "utterly still and unmoving, yet when stimulated it penetrates all."

Safety note: This chapter uses traditional advanced alchemical terms such as "first menstrual tide of the White Tiger," "turning the windlass to lift the Three Carts," "passing through the Spine-Straddling Pass up to the Muddy Pellet," and "guiding breath down into the Yin Heel." Modern readers must not forcibly hold the breath, bear down hard to press air into the lower abdomen, or mentally push energy up into the head during quiet sitting, as this can cause chest tightness, head pressure, or nervous strain. In daily practice, taste its true spirit simply through soft, relaxed abdominal breathing and calm, effortless awareness "between intention and no-intention."

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：知其雄，守其雌，为天下溪；为天下溪，常德不离，复归于婴儿。知其白，守其黑，为天下式；为天下式，常德不忒，复归于无极。知其荣，守其辱，为天下谷；为天下谷，常德乃足，复归于朴。朴散则为器，圣人用之，则为官长。故大制不割。

Laozi says: Know the masculine strength, yet keep to the feminine stillness, and become the ravine for all under heaven. Being the ravine for all under heaven, constant virtue never departs, and you return once more to the state of an infant. Know bright clarity, yet keep to deep darkness, and become the standard for all under heaven. Being the standard for all under heaven, constant virtue never errs, and you return once more to the Boundless. Know glory and honor, yet keep to lowliness and disgrace, and become the valley for all under heaven. Being the valley for all under heaven, constant virtue becomes complete, and you return once more to the uncarved block.

When the uncarved block splits apart, it becomes diverse vessels; when the sage employs it, he becomes chief over all officials. Therefore the greatest tailoring never cuts.

§2
Original (Chinese)

修炼之道，气从阳生，运转河车，行凭子午，到得铅气抽尽，汞精已足，是铅汞会合为一气。此既得雄归以合丹，尤要雌伏以养丹，故曰：「知其雄，守其雌。」夫雄，阳也；雌，阴也。阴阳和合，雌雄交感，而金藏于水；复水又生金，金气足而潮信至，其势有如溪涧然，自上注下，犹溪涧之所蓄靡穷。修行人，知阳不生于阳而生于阴，故不守雄而守雌，久之微阳渐生，阴滓胥化，而归根复命之常德，不可一息偶离。从此阴阳交媾，结成仙胎，于是逐日温养，以成婴儿，有必然者。《悟真》云「雄里怀雌结圣胎」是也。既铅汞混合，打成一片，复将此交媾之精，养于坤宫煅炼，先天真铅生矣，此谓「知其白，守其黑」。夫白，精也；黑，水也。此精未产之日，坤体本虚，因上与乾交，坤实为坎，是水中金生，赖坤母以养成，故称母气。《悟真》云「黑中取白为丹母」是也。得到真铅既至，即运一点己汞以迎之，左提右挈，静候白虎首经，果听地下雷鸣，实有丹心贯日、浩气凌霄之状，我仍守吾虚无窟子，不稍惊惶，此即炼精化气时也。以后运辘轳，升三车，由夹脊双关，上至泥丸，行子午卯酉四正之工，合春夏秋冬四时之序，此即为天下式。凡人物之生长收藏，亦无丝毫差忒不与天合度焉。由是上升下降，送归土釜，化有象以还无象，复归无极之天，此大周天之候，玉炼之丹即在此矣。斯时也，金丹既归玄窍，复合青龙真一之气，炼成不二元神，此即炼气化神时也。

In the Way of cultivation, true breath springs from a spark of yang, turns the Waterwheel, and follows the zi–wu fire phasing until the breath of Lead is fully drawn and the essence of Mercury is full, merging Lead and Mercury into one single breath. Having welcomed back the "male" yang to combine the Elixir, one must all the more brood quietly in "female" stillness to nurture it—hence Laozi says, "Know the male, yet keep to the female."

"Male" is yang; "female" is yin. When yin and yang harmonize and male and female resonate together, true Metal hides inside Water, and Water in turn regenerates Metal. Once Metal-breath is ample, the true tide-sign arrives, flowing like a mountain ravine pouring steadily from above to below, brimming with an inexhaustible reservoir. Knowing that yang is never born out of yang, but nurtured inside the stillness of yin, the practitioner does not cling to outward masculinity but keeps to gentle femininity. Over time, subtle yang steadily matures, turbid yin dross dissolves completely, and this constant virtue of returning to the root and restoring life never leaves for a single breath. From then on yin and yang unite to form the Immortal Embryo, nurtured warmly day by day until the "infant" spiritual body naturally matures—just as the Wuzhen pian says, "Within the male, cherish the female to form the Holy Embryo."

Once Lead and Mercury blend seamlessly into one mass, one places this united True Essence into the Kun palace (lower Cinnabar Field) for quiet calcination, and Prior Heaven True Lead arises: this is "know the white, yet keep to the black." "White" means True Essence; "black" means kidney-water. Before this essence is born, the body of Kun is originally empty; because it unites above with Qian yang, empty Kun fills at its center to become Kan—true Metal born within water, nurtured to maturity by Mother Kun and therefore called "Mother Breath." As the Wuzhen pian says, "Draw white out of black to serve as the Elixir's mother."

The instant True Lead arrives, one brings forth a spark of central True Mercury to welcome it, cradling it gently on left and right and quietly awaiting what alchemy calls the "first menstrual tide of the White Tiger" (the first arrival of true yang). Even if one senses a deep rumble like underground thunder and a vast surge as though one's red heart pierced the sun and upright breath soared to the clouds, one holds steadily inside one's Cave of Emptiness without the slightest panic—this is the critical pass of refining essence into breath.

Thereafter, true breath rises like water drawn by a windlass—ascending via the Three Carts (goat, deer, and ox carts), passing through the twin gates of the Spine-Straddling Pass up to the Muddy Pellet at the crown, following the four cardinal times of zi, wu, mao, and you in exact accord with spring growth, summer flourishing, autumn harvest, and winter storage. This is "becoming the standard for all under heaven": every cycle of birth, growth, gathering, and storage tallies with Heaven's measure without a hair's error. Rising and descending back into the central earthen cauldron, form dissolves back into the formless, returning to the heaven of the Boundless—this is the Greater Celestial Circuit, where the jade-pure Reverted Elixir is achieved. At that stage, the Golden Elixir returns to the Mysterious Opening, unites with the True-One Breath of the Azure Dragon, and refines the non-dual Original Spirit: this is refining breath into spirit.

§3
Original (Chinese)

再修向上一层，炼神还虚之道，惟混混沌沌，涵养虚无，浑浑沦沦，完全理气，化识成智，浑圣如愚，一日一夜，言不轻发，心无他思，有如椎鲁之夫，毫无知见，纵有侮辱频来，俨若不识不知，一如舜之居深山，无异于深山野人焉。此即知成人之荣，而守成仙之辱也，不如此，不足以养虚合道。故曰：「口开神气散，意乱火功寒。能知归复法，金宝重如山。」若妄发一言，妄生一念，即同走丹。道愈高，势愈险。炼丹到此，尤为危险之地。是以古人道果圆成之后，装聋卖哑，作颠放狂，殆为养虚合道计也。否亦何乐为此耶？所以心中无一物，实为天下谷。既为天下谷，尤须意冷于冰，心清似水，而真常之玄德，于此方能充足。然而真空不空，妙有不有，始而从无入有，继而从有归无，终则有无不立，此所以由太极而复归浑朴，返本还原之道得矣。虽然，其聚则一，其散则万，以至生生不已，化化无穷，何莫非器之所在，亦何莫非朴之所散？此朴散为器之说也。而圣人用之，不尚器而尚朴，殆谓虚寂为一身之主宰、万变之总持，犹人世官长无二。又曰「大制不割」者何？盖以浑然之道，范围不过，曲成不遗，是为宰制之需；若或割焉，亦是矫揉造作，初非本来性天。圣人不割，亦还其混沌之天而已。学者知之否？

Moving up to the highest stage—the Way of refining spirit back into emptiness—one simply dwells in primeval wholeness nurturing emptiness, undividedly preserving Principle and Breath, transmuting calculating cognition into luminous wisdom, and veiling sagehood under plain simplicity. Throughout the whole day and night, one speaks no careless word and entertains no stray thought, appearing like a slow, rustic laborer with no clever views to flaunt. Even if insults rain down repeatedly, one receives them calmly as if knowing nothing—just like the sage-emperor Shun dwelling deep in the mountains, indistinguishable from an ordinary mountain woodsman.

This is knowing the worldly glory of becoming a celebrated figure, yet willingly keeping to the humble "disgrace" of cultivating immortality; short of this, one cannot nurture emptiness enough to merge with the Way. Hence the old alchemical verse warns: "Open the mouth and Spirit and Breath scatter; let intention grow disordered and the furnace fire turns cold. Know the method of returning inward, and your golden treasure outweighs a mountain." At this height, even a single reckless remark or stray thought amounts to losing the Elixir. The higher the Way climbs, the steeper the precipice—this stage is the most perilous pass of all. That is why ancient adepts, once nearing or reaching completion, often feigned deafness and muteness or acted eccentric and foolish: solely to shelter themselves from worldly clamor and quietly nurture emptiness in union with the Way! Otherwise, who would enjoy playing the fool?

Thus, with not a single thing cluttering the heart, one truly becomes "the valley for all under heaven." Having become the valley for all under heaven, one keeps thought cooler than ice and mind clearer than autumn water—only then does constant mysterious virtue reach full abundance. Yet true emptiness is not dead blankness, and wondrous being is never clung to as solid thing: first one enters being out of non-being (laying the foundation and producing Medicine), next one returns from being to non-being (refining spirit into emptiness), and at last even the pair of being and non-being ceases to stand—returning from the Supreme Ultimate back to primeval simplicity, completing the Way of returning to the root.

Gathered together it is the undivided One; dispersed outward it becomes the ten thousand things, endlessly generating and transforming—what is not a "vessel," and what is not a dispersal of the "uncarved block"? That is "when the uncarved block splits apart, it becomes vessels." Yet when the sage employs it, he prizes not the scattered vessels but the uncarved block itself—making quiet emptiness the sovereign of one's person and the master key of ten thousand changes, just like the chief official guiding subordinates in the world.

And what does "the greatest tailoring never cuts" mean? The undivided Way encompasses cosmic transformation without excess and gently completes all things without leaving any out—that is true governance. To hack and carve it with human knives would be forced artifice, destroying one's original heavenly nature. The sage refuses to cut simply to restore everything to its unspoiled primeval wholeness. Have you students taken this to heart?

§4
Original (Chinese)

此合《孔德之容》章并看，则知化精、化气、化神之旨，尽于此矣。虽然，其中细密处，吾不妨再言之。「昔日逢师亲口诀，只要凝神入气穴。」若非回光返照丹田，则金水必然浑浊。既知凝神坤宫，或作辍不常，则水火必然散漫，先天真一之气又从何生？虽然，修炼之法，凝神要矣，而调息亦不可少焉。苟知神凝气穴，而不知调呼吸之息，下入阴跷穴中，则神虽住而息不畅，无以扇风动火，使凡息停而真息见，凡心死而真心生；又况神火全凭神息，若无神息吹嘘，不惟水火不清，亦且金胎不化。既凝神调息，知所归宿矣，尤要于神融气畅之际，如天地未开，冥冥晦晦，然后一切游思浊气，方能收拾干净，犹日月剥蚀一番，自有一番新气象。如此絪絪縕縕，于无知无觉时，忽然有知有觉，即是太极开基，玄关现象，又是「一阳初动处，万物始生时」。此际能把得住，拿得定，正所谓「捉雾拿云手段」。丹经云「时至神知」，又云「真活子时」，正此谓也。此时急当采取，若稍迟晷刻，又起后天知觉之私，不堪为金丹之药矣。此个机关，总要于万缘放下、一念不起时，急以真意寻之，方得真清药物。总要静之又静，沉之又沉，于无知无觉时，寻有知有觉处，庶乎得之。

Read this chapter alongside Chapter 21 ("The Form of Great Virtue"), and the entire purport of refining essence, refining breath, and refining spirit lies completely before you. Even so, let me unfold its finest subtleties once more in plain detail.

An old oral transmission puts it plainly: "When I met my teacher in days gone by, his direct instruction was simply: concentrate Spirit and enter the Cavity of Breath." Without Reverting the Light to shine gently inward upon the lower Cinnabar Field, inner Metal-essence and kidney-water stay murky and turbid. Even if you know how to settle Spirit in the Kun palace, if you practice in fits and starts, water and fire scatter—where could Prior Heaven True-One Breath ever arise?

Yet in the art of cultivation, while concentrating Spirit is vital, regulating the breath is equally indispensable! If you only know how to hold Spirit in the Cavity of Breath without regulating fine, smooth breathing so that vitality reaches down to the Yin Heel depth, Spirit may stay put while breath stays blocked—with nothing to work the bellows and kindle the fire, neither can coarse ordinary breathing cease so that True Breath appears, nor can the restless ordinary mind die down so that the true mind emerges. Moreover, inner spirit-fire relies wholly on spirit-breath; without the gentle fanning of spirit-breath, not only do water and fire fail to clarify, the Golden Embryo cannot ripen either.

Once both concentrating Spirit and regulating breath find their steady home, what matters even more is that, right when Spirit melts and Breath flows freely, you allow body and mind to sink into the deep, dim obscurity before heaven and earth parted. Only then are wandering thoughts and turbid breath swept completely clean—like sun and moon emerging fresh after an eclipse into an altogether new clarity. Amid that dense, brooding warmth, out of utter non-conceptual stillness, a spark of lucid awareness suddenly dawns—this is the Supreme Ultimate laying its cornerstone, the Mysterious Pass revealing its sign, precisely the classic line: "Where the first yang begins to stir, the moment when the myriad things come to life!"

Being able to hold steady and grasp this exact pivot is truly the master skill of "catching mist and seizing clouds." What the alchemical classics call "when the hour arrives, Spirit knows" or the "true Living Zi Hour" points to this very instant! Right here one must immediately gather and nurture it: delay even a split second, and private Posterior Heaven discrimination resurfaces, spoiling the pure sprout so it can no longer serve as the Golden Elixir's Medicine. This pivot must always be met right when all worldly ties are dropped and not one stray thought arises, welcoming it promptly with True Intention so that truly pure Medicine is obtained. In short: grow stiller and still stiller, sink deeper and still deeper, and right within non-conceptual stillness meet that spontaneous point of living awareness—then you are close to the mark.

§5
Original (Chinese)

既曰一念不起，又何事用意去寻？岂不是有意去寻，又落后天识神乎？殊不知，此个真意，如种火然，不见有火而火自在，不过机动而神随，自然之感触，有如此者。若谓真属有意，则落于固执；若谓真果无意，又堕于顽空。此有意无意之间，学人当自会之。《易》曰：「寂然不动，感而遂通」是也。如此方是真知真觉，要皆真意为之。虽然，真意由于真心，必其心空洞了灵，不以有物而增，无物而减，有此真心，方有真意，有此真意，乃有真息。总要具有慧照，不错机宜，则炼一次自有一次之长益。到此地步，常常采取，自有真阳发生。还要炼己待时，不可略起一点求动之意，则后天识神不来夹杂，即先天至阳之精、真一之气，久久薰蒸积累，自有大药发生，可以返老还童。只怕不肯积功累行以立外功，敦伦饬纪以修内德，无以为承受之基耳。俗云：「不怕一，只怕积。不怕聚，只怕凑。」诚哉是言也。学人欲知用意之道，切勿徒听自然焉可。

Someone may ask: "You just said not a single thought should arise—why then speak of using intention to seek it? The moment you deliberately seek, haven't you fallen right back into Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit?"

What they do not realize is that this True Intention is just like a "seed-fire" buried deep in hearth ashes: no open flame is visible on top, yet the live fire is always present beneath. It is simply Spirit responding effortlessly the instant vitality stirs—a completely natural resonance! Call it deliberate "intention," and it hardens into rigid clinging; call it utter "no-intention," and it drops into dull "stubborn emptiness." That razor-fine middle ground "between intention and no-intention" must be tasted personally by the practitioner—as the Book of Changes puts it, "utterly still and unmoving, yet when stimulated it penetrates all." What arises in this way is genuine knowing and genuine awakening, all accomplished by True Intention.

Yet True Intention springs from a True Heart: the mind must first be cultivated into open, lucid clarity—neither adding a shred when things arrive nor losing a shred when things depart. Only with this True Heart is there True Intention; only with True Intention does True Breath flow. Holding a quiet, wise illumination that never misses the timing of the pivot, every single session brings tangible growth.

Reaching this stage and quietly gathering whenever the moment ripens, true yang naturally springs forth again and again. Even more important, one must steadily practice "refining the self and awaiting the hour," never stirring the slightest eager wish for sensations to happen. Free from that impatient grasping, Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit never creeps in to muddy the work; Prior Heaven supreme-yang essence and True-One Breath warm and accumulate day by day until the Great Medicine arises of itself, renewing vitality from the inside out. The only real fear is refusing to accumulate wholesome deeds to build "outer merit" and failing to honor human relationships and moral discipline to build "inner virtue," leaving oneself with no solid vessel able to bear such grace. As the proverb says: "Do not fear a single drop—trust steady accumulation; do not worry about gathering all at once—trust bit-by-bit accrual." How true those words are! Any student wishing to understand how to apply True Intention must never lazily sit back and merely say "leave it to nature."

Key Terms

  • the three transmutations: the three great passes of internal alchemy—refining essence into breath (initial pass, paired here with "know the male, keep to the female"), refining breath into spirit (middle pass, paired with "know the white, keep to the black"), and refining spirit back into emptiness (upper pass, paired with "know glory, keep to disgrace").
  • First menstrual tide of the White Tiger (baihu shoujing): an alchemical code phrase comparing the very first stirring of Prior Heaven true yang (True Lead) in the Palace of Kan to a young woman's first monthly tide.
  • Three Carts (sanche), Spine-Straddling Pass, and Muddy Pellet: traditional alchemical metaphors for the three progressive strengths carrying true breath upward (goat, deer, and ox carts—gentle, growing, full) through the twin gates at the mid-spine up to the upper Cinnabar Field at the crown.
  • the Cave of Emptiness: the empty, unshaken resting place of Spirit, signifying calm composure without panic when intense inner currents stir.
  • concentrating Spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath: gathering scattered awareness and using gentle Reverting the Light to tend the lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Living Zi Hour: not midnight on the clock, but the spontaneous instant in deep quiet sitting when one yang first stirs and true vitality awakens—also called "when the hour arrives, Spirit knows."
  • True Intention: distinct from calculating Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit, likened by Huang Yuanji to a live "seed-fire" under ashes—resting between intention and no-intention, still yet immediately responsive.
  • refining the self and awaiting the hour (lianji daishi): patiently polishing daily habits, quieting impatience and craving for sensations, and waiting calmly for Prior Heaven vitality to mature in its own time without forcing it.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Taste relaxed focus "between intention and no-intention." In quiet sitting, breathwork, or everyday focus, the most effortless and effective state is like a "seed-fire under ashes"—neither clenching tightly in anxious control nor zoning out into drowsiness, but resting open with a quiet thread of wakefulness.
  2. "Never raise the slightest wish for sensations to stir" is the golden rule against going astray. Many people sit waiting eagerly for warmth, tingling, or pulsing; the harder they look for it, the more cognitive mind takes over and makes them restless. Keep your expectations level, refine your daily conduct, and let natural ease arrive in its own time.
  3. The higher you climb, the more you should guard simplicity and humility. When ability and achievement grow, it becomes all too easy to "open the mouth and scatter Spirit and Breath." Speaking less, arguing less, enduring slights gracefully, and staying plain both protect your energy and spare you endless friction.
  4. "Trust steady accumulation"—treat character and habit like compound interest. A solid foundation is never built by one or two heroic sprints; it grows quietly from doing a little right every single day—watching your speech, steadying your heart, treating people kindly, and keeping regular rhythms.

Translator's Notes

  1. The Wuzhen pian citations ("Within the male, cherish the female..." and "Draw white out of black..."): Drawn from Zhang Boduan's Northern Song classic Folio on Awakening to Reality, here condensed by Huang Yuanji into crisp thematic summaries of its core teachings.
  2. "Shun dwelling deep in the mountains, indistinguishable from an ordinary mountain woodsman": From Mencius (7A.16: "When Shun lived deep in the mountains, dwelling among trees and rocks and roaming with deer and wild boars, there was scarce difference between him and the rustic folk of the deep mountains").
  3. "Where the first yang begins to stir, the moment when the myriad things come to life": Adapts Northern Song philosopher Shao Yong's famous Winter Solstice Poem ("At midnight of the winter solstice, the Heart of Heaven never swerves; right where the first yang begins to stir, before the myriad things are yet born").
  4. "Open the mouth and Spirit and Breath scatter; let intention grow disordered and the furnace fire turns cold": A widely cited Ming–Qing alchemical maxim (found in Xingming guizhi and related texts) warning against loose talk and scattered thought while nurturing emptiness.
  5. Textual collation: Base-edition misprints for "female brooding" (雌伏以养丹), "Prior Heaven True Lead arises" (先天真铅生矣), "zi, wu, mao, you" (子午卯酉), "cannot nurture emptiness" (不足以养虚合道), "enter being out of non-being" (从无入有), "gently completes without omission" (曲成不遗), "Yin Heel point" (阴跷穴), "dense and brooding" (絪縕), "stiller and stiller" (静之又静), "stubborn emptiness" (顽空), "refining the self and awaiting the hour" (炼己待时), and "how true!" (诚哉是言也) have been corrected in the Chinese source. Note that line 58 of the Chinese text carries the verbatim double-compound 絪絪縕縕 in the quote block.

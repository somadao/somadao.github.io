VIII · Classic of Virtue (Ch. 71–81) · 德经

Chapter 75 · Wiser Than Prizing Life

Rapacious taxation and constant meddling leave the people hungry and hard to govern, and a spirit that gallops outward drains Essence just as heavy taxes from above do; reverse cultivation must make breath the master and have spirit obey its commands, for only by not clinging to the limited life of the Posterior Heaven can one keep hold of the endless mandate of the Prior Heaven

About This Chapter

Chapter 75 of the Daodejing names three root ills in a row: "when those above eat too much in taxes, the people go hungry," "when those above meddle, the people are hard to govern," and "when people seek life too lavishly, they make light of death"; it concludes with the maxim "only those who do not make living their aim are wiser than those who prize life." Huang Yuanji first denounces bitterly the harshness of government that impoverishes the people and the world's habit of chasing things and forgetting the root, then in §3 gives a superbly subtle alchemical interpretation that reverses the master-servant relation of spirit and breath (reversal):

  1. Governing the state and nourishing life follow the same principle (§2): Unlimited taxation leaves the people hungry, and burdensome decrees make the people ever more cunning and hard to govern. For the individual, fretting all day over food, clothing and pleasure and craving "lavish nourishment" only drains Essence, Breath and Spirit dry: the more one clings to life, the faster one dies. Only those who provide for themselves as circumstances allow and set no great store by worldly pleasures can have a settled heart and a healthy body.
  2. The brilliant analogy "the ruler stands for spirit, the people for Essence" (§3, first half): When one follows the ordinary forward course, the heart-spirit is master of the house and Essence and breath rush after it. As soon as the heart-spirit gallops outward and flies off, the Essence and breath of the lower field leak away after it; this is called "those above eat too much in taxes, and the people go hungry." The immortals' Way of return is exactly the reverse: make Breath the master and let Spirit follow the rhythm and commands of the breath's movement, just as a ruler follows the people's sentiments and uses the surplus above to make up for the deficiency below. Only then are breath full and spirit complete.
  3. From non-action to action, and back again to non-action (§3, second half): Although the alchemical Way has concrete methods to begin with, one must never push rigidly and forcefully all the way (if everything is deliberate action, the more one refines, the more scattered the breath becomes); one must guide according to the trend and act in step with the right time. More importantly, do not spend all one's mental energy "protecting the limited life of the Posterior Heaven," but quietly preserve that endless Prior-Heaven mandate within Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：民之饥，以其上食税之多，是以饥。民之难治，以其上之有为，是以难治。民之轻死，以其求生之厚，是以轻死。夫惟无以生为者，是贤于贵生。

Lord Lao says: The people go hungry because those above levy too much in taxes; that is why they go hungry. The people are hard to govern because those above love to meddle and interfere recklessly; that is why they are hard to govern. The people make light of death because the world strives desperately for overly rich and extravagant enjoyment, which instead wears down the root; that is why they lose their lives so easily. Only those who do not make the craving for pleasure and the deliberate pursuit of lavish bodily nourishment their foremost concern are far wiser than those who desperately prize clinging to life and lavish nourishment.

§2
Original (Chinese)

从来民为邦本，食为民天。国无民，则国谁与辅？民无食，则民何以生？是在为人上者，有以开田辟土，濬其源于未食之先，制礼谨度，节其流于已食之后，而复省耕以补不足，省敛以助不给，民自家给人足，而无庚癸之呼，饥馑之叹矣。即干旱不一，饥馑荐臻，而仓箱有畜，自凶荒无忧。无如世之人主，骄淫不靖，糜费日繁，或珍奇玩好以为娱，或琼楼瑶室纵其欲，往往仓廪一空，而用度不减，正供尚缺，又加以重征，始而添租益税，犹胥畏乎民岩，继则暴敛横征，并不顾乎天命，声色是尚，奢华并臻，取万民之脂膏，纵一己之淫荡，即至国帑空虚，而诛求不稍贷焉。夫天地生财，只有此数，若此苛求不已，取民无度，即大有频书，丰年屡庆，而欲其不饥也，得乎？郅隆之世，衣衣食食，宅宅田田，各亲其亲，各长其长，其君子无礼义之防而自居仁由义，其小人无忠厚之好而自乐业安居，盖上以无为为治，下以无为自化，俗不期淳而自淳，风不求古而自古，懿铄休哉，何其盛欤？迨其后，科条愈设而风俗愈偷，法令频彰而盗贼弥炽，其在暴虐之君无论矣，甚至英睿之主，奋发有为，励精图治，政愈繁而伪愈多，法愈严而奸愈出，是岂气数之难回、天心之莫易乎？抑以不知穷源固本，而徒求之于末流，不惟无补于民生，反有累于世道焉。盖民心本无事也，而上以政令扰之；民情本无欲也，而上以章程乱之。朝廷多一政令，百姓多一奸欺；朝廷多一章程，百姓多一奇巧。无怪乎世道之大非、民情之日变，而愈治愈难也。惟在上者端拱垂裳，斯在下者自安分守命，上与下相安于无为之天，不亦乐乎？且民以谋衣谋食，多欲多累，为求生之计，不知逐末即以忘本，重外乃致轻内，其劳心也日繁，其损精也愈甚，而神气因之消亡，身命因之殒灭，愈贪生，愈速死矣，是以求生之厚，反轻死也。惟不以生为荣，且不以求生为重，衣食随缘自奉，用度与物无争，则心安而身泰，自性复而命延，永享无疆之福也。养其太和，自邀天眷，较之以为贵者，不贤于万万倍耶？

Since ancient times, the people have been the root of the state, and food has been the people's heaven. If a state had no people, who would support and sustain it? If the people had no food, how would they live? Everything depends on whether those in high positions can open up fields and clear land, dredging the sources of wealth before the harvest comes; establish rites and measures to restrain expenditure after the harvest; and also inspect the plowing in spring to make up for shortfalls and inspect the harvest in autumn to relieve want. If they can do this, the people will naturally be well provided for in every household and fed and clothed to a person, and there will never be cries of "Geng and Gui" calling for help in shortage of grain, nor laments over famine. Even if droughts come one after another and famine years follow in succession, because the granaries hold reserves, they can pass through the lean times in peace without worry.

Unfortunately, the rulers of the world are often arrogant, extravagant and dissolute, never at peace, and their lavish spending grows larger day by day: some indulge in rare curios and treasures for amusement, others build towers of jade and halls of jasper to give free rein to their desires. Often the state treasury is already empty, yet extravagant expenditure is not reduced in the least; the regular taxes have not yet been fully collected, yet heavy levies and miscellaneous exactions are piled on top. At first, when raising rents and adding taxes, they still feared somewhat the people's boiling resentment; later they simply extort ruthlessly, disregarding even the mandate of Heaven, caring only for indulgence in music and women and the utmost luxury, squeezing dry the sweat and blood of the myriad people to indulge the debauchery of one person alone. Even when the state treasury has long been emptied, their demands on the people are not eased in the slightest. Understand that the wealth produced between heaven and earth is only so much in total; if they make such endless, exacting demands and take without measure, then even if the histories record a great harvest year after year, how could they possibly keep the people from going hungry?

Look back at the most flourishing and peaceful ages of antiquity: everyone had clothes to wear and food to eat, a house to live in and fields to till; each was filial to their own parents and respectful to their own elders. The noble did not need rules and regulations to guard against them; they naturally dwelt in humane virtue and followed the right path. Ordinary people did not need to advertise loyalty and generosity; they naturally lived in peace and took pleasure in their work. Because those above governed through non-action and purity, the people below were transformed naturally in non-action; customs became simple of themselves without anyone striving for simplicity, and the tenor of the age became ancient of itself without anyone deliberately imitating antiquity. What a beautiful and flourishing scene that was!

In later ages, the more legal articles were established, the more frivolous and cunning the customs grew; the more densely decrees were issued, the more bandits multiplied the more they were suppressed. Cruel and benighted rulers need no mention; even some shrewd, capable rulers, energetic and enterprising and determined to govern with utmost diligence, often ended up with ever more burdensome decrees and ever more perfunctory deceit, ever harsher laws and ever more cunning people finding loopholes. Is this because the fortunes of the age cannot be reversed and the will of Heaven cannot be changed? In truth it is entirely because they did not know how to strengthen the root at its source, but only fussed recklessly over branches and trifles, so that they not only failed to help the people's livelihood but dragged down the world's morals and hearts. For the people's hearts were originally calm and untroubled, and it is those above who stir them up with burdensome decrees; the people's nature was originally simple and of few desires, and it is those above who throw it into confusion with complicated regulations. Every decree the court adds produces one more kind of deceit and evasion among the people; every set of regulations the court establishes produces one more set of opportunistic tricks among the people. No wonder the world grows worse and the people's sentiments change day by day, so that the more one governs, the harder it becomes to govern! Only when those above are reverent toward themselves, practice non-action and govern with folded hands and flowing robes will the people below keep to their station and their mandate, and those above and below enjoy together the peace of pure non-action. Would that not be a great joy?

Now look at the people of the world: in pursuit of fine clothes and rich food, their desires grow ever more numerous and their entanglements ever heavier, all in the belief that they are doing a good job of "seeking life." They do not realize that by chasing only external branches they have forgotten precisely the root of life, and that by valuing things outside the body they neglect the inner primordial truth. They wear out their hearts and spirits all day long in ceaseless activity; their Essence and breath are depleted more severely day by day, their spirit and breath perish along with them, and their lives are cut short in turn. The more desperately they crave lavish nourishment and seek life, the faster they die! This is the principle of "seeking life too lavishly, they instead make light of death."

Only those who do not treat worldly glory as something to show off, nor make the deliberate pursuit of lavish nourishment their foremost concern, who eat and dress simply as circumstances allow and are content in their use of things without contending, can have a peaceful heart and a comfortable body, naturally recover their original nature and extend the foundation of their life, and enjoy boundless blessings for a long time. Nourishing in this way the supremely harmonious primordial breath of the whole body naturally attracts Heaven's favor. Compared with those who desperately crave pleasure and think of themselves as "prizing life," are they not wiser by ten million times?

§3
Original (Chinese)

君喻神也，民喻精也。顺行常道，以神为主，而精随之以行，故神一驰，精即泄。精之消耗，由神之飞扬，喻民之饥，由上食税之多。其事不同，其理则一。心为身主，天君泰然，百体从令；天君不宁，则一身精气耗矣，岂但下田倾倒已哉？是以神仙有返还之术，以气为主，而神听其号令，犹君从人欲、顺民情，庶气足神完，而民安国泰。此以上奉下，以上之有余，补下之不足者，即以一人事天下，不以天下事一人之意也。丹道虽曰有为，亦要从无为而有为，有为仍还无为，方是先天之神气，可以入圣超凡。若一概有为，则神不静而气亦弱，势必不炼而气不聚，愈炼而气愈纷。惟因其势而利导之，顺其时而措施之，修身治民，皆作如是观。若恐货财不足，身命难存，于是竭精疲神，希图养后天之命，日夜焦劳，寤寐辗转，神气之消灭者多矣。又况惟天有命，非人所求，吾恐求生者，不惟无以幸生，且促其生于死地。惟不贵后天有限之生，而隐以持先天无穷之命，庶性全而命固，身形亦足贵矣。

Applying this chapter to inner cultivation of the body, the "ruler" stands for Spirit and the "people" stand for Essence. In the ordinary course of forward-flowing expenditure followed by ordinary people, the heart-spirit (especially the outward-running cognitive spirit) is always master of the house, and the Essence and breath of the whole body can only run passively after it. So as soon as the heart-spirit flies off and gallops outward, the Essence and breath below leak away after it at once. The root of the depletion and exhaustion of Essence and breath lies entirely in the agitation and flightiness of the heart-spirit; this is just like the people going hungry because those above levy too much in taxes. The matters differ, but the principle is exactly the same. The heart-spirit is the master of the whole body; the ancients said, "When the heavenly ruler is at ease, the hundred members obey its commands." When the heart-ruler is steady and at ease, all the members of the body naturally obey; once the heart-ruler becomes agitated and restless, the Essence and breath of the whole body are scattered and exhausted along with it. How could it be merely a matter of the lower Cinnabar Field being emptied and drained?

Precisely for this reason, the immortals have the method of inverted return, reversal: reversing things, they make Breath the master and let the heart-spirit quietly obey the rhythm and commands of the true breath, just as a wise ruler bends to the people's hearts and sentiments and does not harass the people. Only thus are breath full and spirit complete, achieving within the body "a people at peace and a state secure." This is using the "upper position" (spirit) to serve and nourish the "lower position" (Essence and breath): drawing back the surplus mental force flying off above to replenish the depleted Essence and breath below. It is just as the way of governing says: "One ruler serves the whole realm; the whole realm is never made to serve one ruler."

Although internal-alchemy cultivation speaks of the initial steps of "deliberate action," one must also achieve "action arising out of non-action, and after action, a return once more to non-action"; only this accords with the natural laws of Prior-Heaven spirit and breath, and only this lets one truly transcend the ordinary and enter sagehood. If from beginning to end one blindly insists on "deliberate action," grasping tightly and refining by force, the result is that the heart-spirit cannot settle and the true breath grows weak along with it. When one does not refine, the breath of course does not gather; yet forcing the refinement makes the breath ever more scattered and confused the more one refines! The only right path is to guide according to the natural trend of the breath's movement and to act appropriately at the right moment of the fire phasing. Whether in cultivating oneself or governing the people, one should regard things in this way.

If one is constantly afraid that money will not suffice and that this physical body will not live well, and so racks one's brains, exhausts one's Essence and wearies one's spirit, set entirely on "lavishly nourishing" this Posterior-Heaven body through desperate exertion, anxious and toiling day and night and tossing and turning even in sleep, then without realizing it one has long since used up more than half of one's spirit and breath. Moreover, a person's lifespan ultimately depends on Heaven's mandate and cannot be obtained by greedy, forced seeking. I fear that those who strive so painfully for life not only will not gain a lucky long life, but will with their own hands drive their lives onto deadly ground.

Only by not clinging greedily to that limited "life" of the Posterior-Heaven body, but instead quietly sinking one's effort deep within to preserve and nourish that endless Prior-Heaven true mandate within Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven (inner nature / life), can one's original nature be made whole and one's true mandate secure. In the end, even this Posterior-Heaven bodily form will thereby become truly precious and long-lasting.

Key Terms

  • The ruler stands for spirit, the people for Essence: Huang Yuanji's core alchemical metaphor for this chapter. The heart-spirit galloping outward is like those above extorting heavy taxes; it inevitably exhausts the Essence and breath of the lower field ("the people go hungry").
  • Reversal (making breath the master, with spirit obeying its commands): Breaking the forward-flowing habit of ordinary people, in which "the heart-spirit runs wild and Essence and breath passively leak away," and turning instead to gathering spirit back to the breath, listening to the breath and following it (regulating the breath), using the upper to replenish the lower so that breath is full and spirit complete.
  • When the heavenly ruler is at ease, the hundred members obey its commands: The "heavenly ruler" is the heart-spirit. When the heart is calm and settled, the functions of the whole body are coordinated and orderly; when the heart is restless and agitated, the Essence and breath of the whole body all scatter.
  • The Prior-Heaven mandate and the Posterior-Heaven life: The limited life of the Posterior Heaven refers to the sensory pleasures of the body and external pursuits; the endless mandate of the Prior Heaven refers to the inherently complete One Breath of Prior Heaven and the pure true nature.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The harder your mind spins, the faster your body is hollowed out. "The depletion of Essence comes from the flightiness of spirit" hits the nail on the head. The greatest inner drain for people today is not physical overwork but anxiety, endless scrolling and idle rumination that keep the "heavenly ruler" from a moment's peace; sparing the mind is sparing Essence and breath.
  2. When sitting or relaxing, learn to "let the mind take its cue from the breath." Don't use your brain to direct, judge or hurry your breathing; instead let your attention rest lightly on the natural rise and fall of the breath and follow its rhythm. This is the plainest form of "making breath the master, with spirit obeying its commands."
  3. Beware of "over-forceful health regimes." A head full of fear of death and illness, fussing anxiously every day over supplements and folk remedies, only costs you sleep and wears down your spirit, falling right into the traps of "seeking life too lavishly, one makes light of death" and "the more one refines, the more scattered the breath."
  4. Less meddling makes people and work easier to manage. Whether leading a team or arranging your own plans, the more cumbersome the rules and the denser the micromanagement, the greater the friction in the system; grasp the essentials and leave some room, and you will often get twice the result with half the effort.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Inspecting the plowing to make up for shortfalls, inspecting the harvest to relieve want": From Mencius ("King Hui of Liang II"): "In spring he inspects the plowing and makes up for shortfalls; in autumn he inspects the harvest and relieves want."
  2. "The cry of Geng and Gui": From the Zuo Commentary (Duke Ai, 13th year): when the Wu army ran short of food, "Geng" (standing for the west, which governs grain) and "Gui" (standing for the north, which governs water) were used as a secret code for requesting grain and water; later ages used "calling Geng and Gui" to mean an urgent famine alarm.
  3. "Fearing the people's crags": From the Book of Documents ("Announcement of the Duke of Shao"): "Look upon and fear the people's crags," meaning to fear the people's resentment as one fears a steep precipice not to be lightly provoked.
  4. "When the heavenly ruler is at ease, the hundred members obey its commands": "Heavenly ruler" comes from Xunzi ("Discourse on Heaven"): "The heart dwells in the empty center to govern the five senses; this is called the heavenly ruler." The full sentence is a maxim of Song-dynasty Neo-Confucianism (found in Reflections on Things at Hand and elsewhere), describing how a peaceful heart-spirit brings harmony to the whole body.
  5. Textual note: In the Classic, 太上日, and in §3, the 日 of 丹道虽日有为, are both corrected to 曰; 干早不一 is corrected to 干旱不一; 凶荒无优 to 凶荒无忧; 仓廪一室 is corrected to 仓廪一空 according to sense; 一已 to 一己; 风不求古面自古 to 风不求古而自古; and 徒求之于未流 to 徒求之于末流.

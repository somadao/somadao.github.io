VIII · Classic of Virtue (Ch. 71–81) · 德经

Chapter 74 · When the People Do Not Fear Death

Governing a state must not rely on harsh punishment or presume to carry out Heaven's executions; cultivating oneself depends on everyday vigilance in solitude and patient nourishment, letting a rectified Original Spirit dissolve the deviant and false of itself, and above all not digging the well only when thirsty by forcibly suppressing desire

About This Chapter

With "the people do not fear death" and "hewing in place of the master carpenter," Chapter 74 of the Daodejing warns rulers not to abuse punishment and execution. Huang Yuanji first makes clear the proper measure between the way of governing and the Way of Heaven, then turns it into a lesson that goes straight to the sore point: everyday mind-cultivation, vigilance in solitude, and guarding against the first signs:

  1. In governing, do not presume to act as Heaven's officer (§2): Laws and punishments are a last resort that follows the people's sentiments and punishes one to warn a hundred. If the age has decayed so far that the people no longer even fear death, hanging up severe punishments is wasted effort. Moreover, only a supremely humane "officer of Heaven" is worthy of wielding the power of life and death; if one lacks the Way and meets violence with violence, it is like an amateur insisting on swinging the axe in place of the master carpenter: nine times out of ten he only wounds his own hand.
  2. "Fearing death" as a metaphor for vigilance in solitude; do not forcibly suppress at the critical moment (§3, first half): The work of overcoming desire that really works lies entirely in careful self-examination in the hidden moments of everyday solitude. If one has no discipline or nourishment at ordinary times and only thinks of "uprooting evil thoughts" by brute force when emotions explode and desires surge, the result will surely be "not removing evil but multiplying it; the more one washes the mind, the more confused it becomes." This is the inner-cultivation picture of "the people do not fear death; how can death be used to frighten them?"
  3. Blocking it gradually: once the Original Spirit is upright, all deviance withdraws of itself (§3, second half): Returning to the root cannot rely on stormy, violent suppression; it relies on step-by-step prevention and persistent, constant guarding. As long as the Original Spirit is nourished to be pure and upright at ordinary times, the openings throughout the body naturally become numinous and clear, the hundred spirits naturally obey, and the deviant and false submit and disperse of themselves, never turning their weapons against one.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：民不畏死，奈何以死惧之？若使民常畏死，而为奇者，吾得执而杀之，孰敢？常有司杀者杀。夫代司杀者杀，是谓代大匠斵。夫代大匠斵者，希有不伤其手矣。

Lord Lao says: If the people no longer fear even death, how can you still frighten them with the death penalty? Suppose the people ordinarily lived in peace and contentment, cherished life and feared death; then if there were still those who dared to commit crimes and do strange, wicked things, and I seized them and put them to death according to the law, who would dare to test the law? In truth, in the unseen there is a ruling power of the Way of Heaven that is in charge of life and death and carries out punishment. If an ordinary person oversteps their role and kills on behalf of this executioner, it is like an amateur swinging the axe to hew wood in place of a highly skilled master carpenter. Of those who hew in place of the master carpenter, few fail to wound their own hands.

§2
Original (Chinese)

古之治天下者，必因乎民情之所易动，而预为之防，不因人君之喜忧，惟视民情之好恶，顺其势而利导之，所以其教不肃而成，其政不烦而治。若民之灭纪败伦，干名犯分，而毫无畏死之心，我以五刑之设，悬于象魏，读之月吉，是徒劳其设施，而无补于国计民生也，岂不枉费心力哉？惟因民之贪生而惧死，有敢为奸邪奇诡者，吾乃从而杀之，正所谓「制一以警百，少惩而多诫」，斯民自父训其子，兄勉其弟，不敢职为乱阶，以自戕生而就死。然杀之虽在乎其上，而所以杀之，亦视乎其人。惟至仁杀至不仁，则民自杀之而不怨，死之而亦甘，孟子谓「惟天吏则可以杀之」是。夫天吏乃可杀人，是常有可杀人者矣。若非天吏，而以暴诛暴，是以乱治乱，不惟民乱益甚，而且代司杀者杀，犹之代工匠而运斤成风，挥斧斵轮，其能神乎技而妙于成哉？历观古今匠士，其身不能大匠而代大匠斵者，奚有不伤其手耶？彼民不幸，不获生于有道之世，是以寇贼奸宄殊无忌惮，又不幸不遇可杀之人，则启沃无从，反还奚自？以至薄者愈薄，而厚者亦薄矣，不亦大可伤乎？

Those in ancient times who governed the world well would always discern where the people's feelings were most easily stirred, and would guide and guard against it in advance. They did not act according to the ruler's personal joys and worries, likes and dislikes; they looked only at what the people truly liked and disliked, and guided them advantageously along the trend of popular sentiment. Because of this, their teaching succeeded without harsh compulsion, and their government brought order without being burdensome or oppressive.

Conversely, if the people have already sunk to the point of destroying law and discipline, corrupting the ethical bonds, violating name and station, and not even caring about death, then even if the articles of the Five Punishments are hung high on the palace towers (the xiangwei) and solemnly read out on the first day of every month, it is a mere empty show that does nothing for the state's welfare or the people's livelihood. Would that not be a pointless waste of effort? Only when the people live settled lives and naturally cherish life and fear death will punishing according to the law those who dare to commit crimes and do strange, wicked deeds truly have the effect of "punishing one to warn a hundred, small chastisement yielding broad admonition." The people will naturally have fathers instructing their sons and elder brothers admonishing their younger brothers, and none will dare to serve as a stepping-stone to disorder and throw their own lives away for nothing.

However, although the authority of punishment and execution lies in the hands of those above, whether they are truly qualified to use it depends on what kind of person holds it. Only when one eliminates the supremely inhumane ringleader with a supremely humane heart will the people submit willingly and sincerely, not resenting punishment and even accepting it gladly. This is what Mencius meant by "only an officer of Heaven, who accords with the Way of Heaven, may carry out punitive execution." Since only an officer of Heaven is worthy of putting people to death, it is evident that in the unseen there is always an "executioner" in charge of life and death. If one does not possess the humane virtue of an officer of Heaven, yet relies on cruelty to kill another cruelty, that is using disorder to govern disorder. Not only will unrest among the people grow ever worse, but forcibly overstepping one's authority to kill on behalf of the executioner is like someone who knows nothing of carpentry insisting on swinging the hatchet to make the wind whistle, or wielding the axe to carve a wheel, in place of the skilled master. How could such a person display marvelous skill and complete the work? Looking over the craftsmen of past and present, of those who were not master carpenters themselves yet insisted on hewing in their place, which one did not wound his own hand?

The people in the world have had the misfortune not to be born in a peaceful, flourishing age that has the Way, which is why thieves, bandits and villains act without restraint. They have the further misfortune of not meeting a truly virtuous, enlightened ruler able to put a stop to evil; with no way to receive the inspiration and nourishment of good government and teaching, where could they return to the simple, upright Way? As a result, customs that were already thin grow thinner still, and even hearts that were originally sincere and generous grow thin along with them. Is this not deeply heartbreaking?

§3
Original (Chinese)

以畏死，喻慎独。人惟慎独功深，则天人辨白，理欲分明。欲寡过而未能，思免愆而不得，于此兢兢业业，汲汲皇皇，省察其几微，克治其伪妄，不难欲净理纯，立见本来面目。若于不睹不闻之地，平日无操存涵养之功，而于欲动情胜时，思拔除恶孽，顿见性天，势必不除恶而恶多，愈洗心而心乱，太上曰：「民不畏死，奈何以死惧之？」理势乃相因也。惟能慎几于幽独，既有以知欲念之非，乃克遏欲于临时，庶可以还天心之正，一念扫除，一念清净，自不萌芽再生于其际。此民常畏死，而为奇者，吾得执而杀之，孰敢颠越不恭，败坏伦常？盖以有道驱无道，犹人君抚绥万姓，统驭群黎，以至仁杀不仁，以大义诛不义，自然没者顺而存者安，近者悦而远者来，不至有倒戈相向，反戟相攻，而为仇为害也。学者欲去伪存诚，反本归根，其必杜之以渐，守之以恒，庶一窍通而窍窍俱灵，元神安而神神听命。所谓「人能常清净，天地悉皆归」，又曰：「人能一正其神，则诸邪自不敢犯」。此与司杀者从而杀之不怨、死之亦安，同一自然之道，希有之效焉。

Applied to inner cultivation, "fearing death" in the Classic is a metaphor for the work of "vigilance in solitude," valued by Confucians and Daoists alike. Only when a person's work of vigilance in solitude is deep can they distinguish heavenly principle from human desire with perfect clarity, as distinct as clear water from muddy. In the heart one wishes to commit fewer faults yet fears falling short, and longs to avoid errors yet fears negligence; so at every moment one is cautious and attentive, never daring to slacken, examining oneself in time at the subtlest point where a thought first stirs, and dissolving and overcoming falseness and deluded thinking one by one. In this way, it is really not difficult to have selfish desire washed clean, heavenly principle pure and complete, and to see one's original face right here and now.

Conversely, if in the solitude where others cannot see or hear one is unwilling at ordinary times to put in any work of holding fast and nourishing oneself, and lets thoughts run wild; and only when desire churns like a stormy sea and emotion has completely gained the upper hand does one frantically think of uprooting evil thoughts at a stroke and seeing one's pure original nature immediately, then the result will inevitably be this: the evil thoughts not only cannot be removed but multiply the more one suppresses them, and the more one tries to scrub this heart clean by force, the more confused it becomes! Lord Lao says, "The people do not fear death; how can death be used to frighten them?" It is precisely because principle and circumstance are linked link by link: neglect it at ordinary times and then try to frighten it with a death order at the critical moment, and it simply will not work.

Only if at ordinary times, in hidden solitude, one is alert to the first sprouts of thought, and already knows with perfect clarity the harm of selfish desires and deluded thoughts, can one calmly restrain desire when temptation arrives and return to the uprightness of the heavenly mind. Sweep away one portion of deluded thought in the present moment, and in the present moment there is one portion more of purity; weeds will naturally not sprout and grow again in the field of the heart. This is like saying: "The people ordinarily know what to revere; if occasionally a strange, deviant, transgressive thought pops up, I can seize and clear it away steadily. What deviant thought would then dare to run wild and wreck the order of body and mind?"

For using the Way to dissolve what lacks the Way is like a wise ruler comforting the people and leading the multitude, removing inhumanity with supreme humaneness and sweeping away injustice with great righteousness. Naturally the deviant thoughts that are dissolved become docile and obedient, the upright breath that remains is steady and secure, those near are glad and those far away come to submit. It never provokes inner rebellion as brute suppression does, with weapons turned against oneself and self-consuming inner strife.

Practitioners of the Way who wish to remove falseness, preserve sincerity, and return to the root (refining the self) must understand "blocking it gradually and guarding it with constancy": guarding against the first signs step by step in small things, and persisting constantly in daily life. When this work is sustained for a long time, naturally a single opening is penetrated (the Mysterious Pass) and all the openings of the body become numinous, the Original Spirit sits securely in command, and the hundred spirits of the body all obey. An ancient scripture says, "If a person can be constantly pure and still, heaven and earth will all return to them," and also, "If a person can make their spirit upright once and for all, all deviant influences will not dare to trespass." This state, in which deviance and falseness transform of themselves not through brute coercion but solely through the purity of an upright spirit, is exactly like the "executioner" acting in accord with the Way of Heaven so that those judged submit in their hearts without resentment: both belong to the same natural Great Way and have rare and extraordinary real effects.

Key Terms

  • Vigilance in solitude (shendu): The work of maintaining clear self-examination when alone and unsupervised and when thoughts are at their most hidden and subtle; it is the core daily method by which internal alchemy's refining the self firmly establishes mind and nature.
  • Original Spirit (making the spirit upright once and for all): The original master, steady and clear, that does not dance to the tune of desire; when the Original Spirit is at peace, the hundred spirits obey and all deviance withdraws of itself.
  • The executioner / officer of Heaven: A metaphor for the natural, impartial ruling law between heaven and earth. In inner cultivation it corresponds to dissolving deviance and falseness naturally with pure heavenly principle and a pure Original Spirit, rather than fighting thoughts with brute force by means of the irritable ordinary mind (the cognitive spirit).
  • Blocking it gradually, guarding it with constancy: The methodology of mind-cultivation that guards against the first signs and flows steadily like a small stream, as opposed to indulging oneself at ordinary times and forcibly suppressing at the critical moment, which leads to "the more one washes the mind, the more confused it becomes."

Putting It into Practice

  1. Managing emotions depends on everyday "vigilance in solitude," not on forcible suppression when they explode. If you let irritation, greed and resentment accumulate at ordinary times and only grit your teeth and tell yourself "don't get angry, don't be moved" when the emotional tsunami hits, the more you suppress the more it rebounds; this is exactly "the more one washes the mind, the more confused it becomes."
  2. Replace "self-consuming self-blame" with a "pure master mind." When fighting bad habits, using one agitated thought to beat another bad thought is "punishing violence with violence, hewing in place of the master carpenter," and in the end it is always you who gets hurt. Set your daily routine and attention upright ("making the spirit upright once and for all"), and many stray thoughts will lose their soil of their own accord.
  3. When leading a team or raising children, be careful with "punishment by intimidation." When a person feels they have no way out or has become completely numb, harsh threats lose their effect; first let people feel fairness, respect and security, and only then will boundaries and rules carry real authority.
  4. "Blocking it gradually, guarding it with constancy" is the only shortcut. Gently pulling a thought back in the one or two seconds after it first drifts, every day, works far better than swearing ten fierce oaths a year.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Their teaching succeeded without harshness, their government brought order without burden": Draws on the Classic of Filial Piety ("Sage Governance"): "Their government brought order without severity, their teaching succeeded without harshness."
  2. "Hung on the xiangwei and read out on the first of the month": From the Rites of Zhou ("Offices of Heaven," Grand Steward): "On the auspicious first day of the first month, he begins to harmonize and promulgate the laws throughout the states and their capitals and outlying districts, hanging the laws of the images of governance on the xiangwei so that the myriad people may view them." The xiangwei were the tall towers outside the ancient palace gates on which laws and decrees were displayed; "the auspicious day of the month" is the first day of the lunar month.
  3. "Only an officer of Heaven may put them to death": Draws on Mencius ("Gongsun Chou II"): "One who is an officer of Heaven may attack it," referring to a ruler who accords with the Way of Heaven and practices humaneness and rightness.
  4. "Swinging the hatchet to make the wind whistle, wielding the axe to carve a wheel": Drawn respectively from the stories of "Carpenter Shi swinging his hatchet so it made a wind" in Zhuangzi ("Xu Wugui") and "Wheelwright Bian carving a wheel" in Zhuangzi ("The Way of Heaven"), as metaphors for consummate skill.
  5. "If a person can be constantly pure and still, heaven and earth will all return to them": From the Daoist scripture Scripture of Constant Purity and Stillness Spoken by the Most High Lord Lao (Taishang Laojun shuo chang qingjing jing).
  6. Textual note: In the title "第七十四章 一 民不畏死," the "一" has been corrected to a middle dot; in the Classic, 太上日 is corrected to 太上曰; 省察其几徽 to 省察其几微; and 乃克過欲于临时 to 乃克遏欲于临时.

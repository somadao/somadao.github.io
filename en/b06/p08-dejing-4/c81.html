Chapter 81 · Acting Without Contending

Plain, trustworthy words point straight to the heaven of one's nature; as the whole classic draws to a close, Huang Yuanji entrusts everything he has: the Original Spirit is the substance and the cognitive spirit the function, and only by clearly distinguishing when to switch between the martial fire of counting breaths and the civil fire of the River Chariot is the work brought to completion

About This Chapter

Chapter 81 is the concluding summary of the Daodejing, and it is also the farewell song and final entrusting of the essential formula of Huang Yuanji's entire Commentary on the Daodejing. As he finished commenting on the whole text, Huang Yuanji, living amid the turmoil of the late Qing, called his disciples together and laid out in full the most essential, and most easily misunderstood, crucial junctures of internal alchemy in the whole book:

  1. Trustworthy words are not beautiful; the sage hoards nothing and has ever more (§2): The five thousand words of the classic do not prize ornate diction; they seek only a single direct transmission that points straight to the heaven of one's nature and lets it be seen at once. True "knowing" lies in one thread running through everything, not in showing off wide learning and a strong memory. The sage's heart is like a bright mirror, empty and hoarding nothing; precisely because it serves others selflessly and gives itself wholeheartedly to others, its merit and virtue, like a mirror reflecting all things, grow richer the more they are used, in keeping with "the Way of Heaven benefits and does not harm; the Way of the sage acts and does not contend."
  2. The Original Spirit as substance, the cognitive spirit as function (§3, first part): He breaks down the bias that condemns the cognitive spirit out of hand! One must first, in an undivided state without knowing, nurture the Original Spirit of Prior Heaven as master; then, when an awakening stirs and gives forth pure, clear awareness, this "awareness of perfect and complete enlightenment," untainted by selfish desire and wholly concentrated on regulating the breath and concentrating the spirit, is in fact the wondrous function of the Original Spirit.
  3. The true yang of Prior Heaven must be tempered with the ordinary Breath of Posterior Heaven; fire phasing is like cooking rice (§3, middle part): After ordinary breathing has reached utmost stillness and the Original Breath has returned to life, if one does not promptly turn the River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit) to transform Essence into Breath and Breath into Spirit, the yin Essence, not yet tempered, will in the end stir desire and pour out. And when running the River Chariot, one must take the Original Breath of Prior Heaven as the root while also borrowing the ordinary Breath of Posterior Heaven as yang fire and yin tally to urge it on gently. If the fire is too slow, the rice stays raw; if too fierce, it burns. At the critical moment one must achieve "the Breath vigorous and the heart unobstructed," mastering the fierce momentum with a cool, settled heart.
  4. The precise boundary between martial fire and civil fire (§3, last part): When the Mysterious Pass first appears and dragon and tiger have not yet joined, one must never talk idly of "relying purely on naturalness"; one must use the martial fire of counting breaths, urging tightly and fanning slowly, to set the bellows working. But once the River Chariot is turning and "the dragon sings and the tiger roars, the husband leads and the wife follows," one must never again press with fierce martial fire, or one will instead drive the true Breath to scatter. The whole work is laid bare, every word written in heartfelt sincerity.

Safety note: §3 of this chapter is Huang Yuanji's farewell summary of the essential formula for disciples who had long been cultivating under him. It involves advanced internal-alchemy techniques such as "the cutting off of ordinary breathing," "running the River Chariot," "borrowing the ordinary Breath of Posterior Heaven as yang fire and yin tally to press and urge it on," "thunder rumbling beneath the earth," and "the martial fire of counting breaths," and it quotes maxims of traditional Daoist faith such as "living long without growing old" and "my life is up to me, not to Heaven." Please approach these with rational caution: never forcibly close off and cut off the breath on your own, and never use violent breathing or willpower to force the movement of Breath to crash through. Nor should the transcendent symbolism of classical alchemy be understood as a guarantee of bodily longevity in the sense of modern medicine. Huang Yuanji himself repeatedly stresses "the Breath vigorous and the heart unobstructed" and "taking pure stillness and placid calm as the root." Daily practice should be steady and gentle; if there is any dizziness, palpitations, chest tightness, feverish heat or insomnia, stop immediately.

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：信言不美，美言不信。善者不辩，辩者不善。知者不博，博者不知。圣人不积，既以为人，己愈有；既以与人，己愈多。天之道，利而不害；圣人之道，为而不争。

Lord Lao says: Truly trustworthy words are often plain and not ornate; ornate, pleasing phrases are often not trustworthy. A person who is truly good and has the Way does not rely on clever argument; one skilled at sophistry and fond of wordplay is often not truly good. One who truly has great wisdom and clearly sees the Great Way does not rely on a mass of miscellaneous learning; one who only shows off broad, miscellaneous learning has not truly understood. The sage never hoards private gain for themselves; the more wholeheartedly they help others, the fuller and richer they themselves become; the more they bestow kindness and wisdom on others, the more abundant they themselves become. The law of the Way of Heaven is to nourish and benefit all things and never to harm them; the true Way of the sage is to do their utmost to bring all things to completion and never to contend for fame or gain.

§2
Original (Chinese)

此章总结通部，示人《道德》一经，皆真实无妄之言，不得以文词不美，将此经置之高阁，而不论不议也。须知道本无名，强名曰道；道本无言，有言皆障。然为教化众生，不得不权立虚名，以为后学津梁。既有言矣，则言必由衷，发皆中节，此诚笃实之论，酌于古而不谬，准之今而咸宜，无虚饰，无妄吐，不须文采，何事繁多？单传直指，立见性天。言而信也，不求美焉。若夫文章绚烂，词旨风流，殆文人学士之言，尚虚华以悦世，不足以为信也。彼言既信而为善，不求穿凿以惑人，又有何辩哉？其辩之者，殆聋耳目之聪明，饰闻见于伦类，掩耳盗铃，不足以云善也。夫善在一己，知在一心，岂必多乎？孔子曰「吾道一以贯之」，孟子曰「夫道一而已矣」，有何博欤？其博之者，殆道不明其统宗，语不知其归宿，泛滥于诸子百家，此记诵词章之学，非圣人博学于文，约之以大中至正之礼，不足以言知也。要之，道也者，浑于杳茫之际，悬于清空之中，流通于天地人物之内，无时不有，无物不然，取之无禁，用之不穷者。圣人空而不空，有而不有。不啻明镜高悬，清波朗照，何积之有？若有所积，是镜有尘垢之污，水有沙泥之染，非圣人空洞了灵之本体，不足言「廓然而大公」也。惟其空灵若此，则因应随缘，虽万姓纷纭，善难遍及，而一夫得咎，辄引为辜，其为人也，无复加矣。纵九州并列，惠有难周，而一地未沾恩，此心常抱痛，其与人也，何多让焉？故曰既以为人，而己愈有其功，既以与人，而己愈多其德。亦犹镜光之物来则照，物去则已，初无成心于其间也，圣人之心，亦如是焉耳。且夫圣人之心，即天之心也；圣人之道，一天之道也。夫天以默运为生成，虽有消长盈虚，总属生养之机，有利而无害。圣以无心为造化，虽有损益予夺，仍属仁慈之应，亦为而不争。假使天地有利有害，则天地亦私而不公，又焉能万年如一耶？圣人有为有争，则圣人亦积而不散，又安能至诚不息哉？呜呼！天地大矣，圣人大矣，虽有信言，亦因心作则，无假借也，无思为也，本诸身，征诸庶民，亦天德之良知，人心所同具，为人即为己，与人亦与己，所谓「物我一致，天人一源」者，是圣人与天合德，于此见其量焉。

This chapter sums up the whole Daodejing, solemnly warning the world that every word and phrase of these five thousand words is true, unfeigned, ultimate principle. One must never, because one finds its language plain and not ornate enough, shelve it on a high shelf and fail to investigate and realize it.

Know that the Great Way originally has no name and is only reluctantly called "the Way"; the Great Way originally transcends language, and whatever falls into speech is in fact a barrier and obstruction. Yet in order to teach and transform living beings out of compassion, one has no choice but to set up names, forms and words provisionally, as a bridge for later people to cross the river. Since one opens one's mouth and sets down words, every sentence must come from the depths of the heart and be exactly in measure: all of it plain, solid, heartfelt discourse, without the slightest error when set in antiquity and fully fitting when tested against today; without empty ornament, without wild, reckless speech, needing no display of rhetoric. Why make it verbose and complicated? It simply cuts straight in and points directly at the human heart, letting a person see their pure original nature on the spot! Words this true and trustworthy do not seek outward beauty at all. As for writings of brilliant literary color and elegant, flowing diction, they are for the most part the ink of literati and scholars who prize superficial glitter to please the world, and cannot count as genuine, trustworthy words.

Since one's words are true and substantial and one's conduct rooted in goodness, never twisting meanings far-fetchedly to mislead the world, what is there to argue about with anyone? Those who are keen on eloquent debate all day long have merely blinded the intelligence of true hearing and true seeing, using half-understood hearsay to dress up their façade, like covering one's ears to steal a bell; they cannot be called good at all.

Moreover, true goodness lies in cultivating this one self well, and true knowing lies in thoroughly awakening to this one heart; why crave quantity and miscellany? Confucius said, "My Way has one thread running through it"; Mencius said, "The Way is one, and that is all." What room is there for a mass of showy erudition? Those who only flaunt their broad learning often fail to grasp the overall principle of the Great Way, and their words find no place to rest, so they can only spill over into the branches and leaves of the Hundred Schools. That is mere learning by rote of phrases and passages, not at all the sage's true work of "broadly studying the classics and drawing them together with the ritual of great, central, perfect uprightness," and it cannot count as true knowing.

In the end, this Great Way is undivided and merged within the vast, dim void, suspended in the clear Great Emptiness, flowing through heaven, earth and all things; there is no time it is not present and no thing in which it is not so; there is no prohibition on taking it and no end to using it. The sage's state of mind is "empty without falling into dead emptiness, existing without clinging to solid existence," like a bright mirror hung on high or a pool of clear ripples, illuminating all things. How could it hoard private things? Whenever a little private hoarding is added to the heart, it is like dust and grime on a bright mirror or mud and sand mixed into clear water; it is then no longer the sage's original substance, empty, bright and transparent, and one cannot speak of being "vast and utterly impartial."

Precisely because the sage's state of mind is so empty, bright, numinous and transparent, they can respond to things as circumstances arise and give selflessly. Even though the people under heaven are numerous and varied and benevolence cannot reach them all at once, if a single person falls into fault or hardship, the sage takes it as their own responsibility; this heart that "serves others" could not be greater. Even though the Nine Provinces are vast and favor cannot reach every place, if the people of a single region have not yet been moistened by it, the sage's heart is constantly pained and concerned; who could match this feeling of "giving to others"? That is why it says: the more wholeheartedly one brings others to completion, the more profound one's own merit and work become; the more selflessly one gives to others, the broader one's own accumulated virtue grows! This is just like the light of a bright mirror: when a thing comes, it reflects it faithfully; when the thing goes, it returns to clear emptiness, and in between not a trace of private calculation is mixed in. The sage's heart is simply like this.

Moreover, the heart of the sage is the heart of heaven and earth, and the Way of the sage is originally the Way of Heaven. Heaven gives birth to and transforms all things through its silent revolutions; though there is the waxing and waning, filling and emptying of the four seasons, in the final analysis it is all the great working of giving life, nurturing and raising, benefiting all things and doing them no harm. The sage takes part in creation with a selfless heart; though there are judgments that reduce surplus and make up what is lacking, in the final analysis they are all responses of humane compassion, simply doing their utmost and never contending for merit or gain. If heaven and earth were partial, benefiting things on one side and harming them on the other, then heaven and earth themselves would be unjust, and how could they keep revolving constantly, the same for ten thousand years? If the sage, while doing things, were contending for merit and calculating, then the sage too would fall into hoarding and selfishness, and how could they be utterly sincere and unceasing?

Ah! How vast heaven and earth are, how broad the sage! Even the plain, trustworthy words the sage left behind are entirely a standard flowing naturally from the pure original heart, relying on nothing borrowed from outside and on no clever calculation; they are verified in one's own virtuous conduct and confirmed in the common innate knowing of all the people under heaven. People originally all share this heavenly virtue of innate knowing, so bringing others to completion is bringing oneself to completion, and giving to others is giving to oneself! What the ancients called "things and self are one; Heaven and humans share one source," the broad heart of the sage whose virtue matches heaven and earth, can be seen with perfect clarity in this chapter.

Original (Chinese)

此经注毕，呼群弟子而告之曰：目今大道，危如累卵，所赖尔学道诸人，以撑持天地，救正乾坤。纵说奸匪之徒，将有兵戈之动，然天有安排，总不至令尔等有不测之虞也。只怕尔等执德不弘，信道不笃，二意三心，或作或辍，斯亦自绝于天，不能上与天通，天纵有十分仁爱，欲生尔等于休养安恬之天，而无如其不能承接天休何也？生等近已见道明，体道力，自家确有把持，惟有一言一动，息息与天相流通，天自爱之重之，保抱之而不置也。夫以道在即天在，重道即重天，爱道即爱天，如此默契潜孚，自臻休祥，天道原与人道通也。试观古今来，只有悖道而为天厌者，未有遵道而不获天休也，生等可恍然悟矣。总之各行其是，各尽其诚，那以外之是非祸福，概有天作主张，生等切勿作越俎代庖之忧可也。夫大道之要，不过神气二者而已，但有先后天之别，修士不可不知。古经云：「先天元神，体也；后天识神，用也。无先天元神，大道无主；无后天识神，大道无用。」尔等用工修炼，必要于混混沌沌、无知无觉时，养得先天元神以为主宰，然后一惊而醒，一觉而动，发为后天识神。此个识神，非朋从尔思、憧憧往来之私识，乃是正等正觉之元神，因其发动而有知觉，故曰识神。只怕此识一起，即纷纷扰扰，恶妄杂念，纷至沓来而不已者，就堕于私，流于欲，而不可以炼丹也。惟有一心了照，矢志靡他，如此用志不纷，乃凝于神，神凝而息可调，息调即丹可结，故曰：「一心只在丝纶上，不见芦花对岸红。」如此一心，虽曰识神，其实即元神也。所以古云「天心为主，元神为用」，巧使盗机，返还造化，何患不立跻圣神？尔等亦明之否？总要于天心发动之后，常常稳蓄，不许一念游移，一息杂妄，庶几天心常在，道心常凝，虽有识亦若无识也。学者修真，下手之际，贵乎一心制服两眼，并口耳身意之妄识，于是集神于丹扃，调息于丹田，务使凡息断灭，然后元气始来归命。既得元气来归，氤氲活泼，婉转悠扬，如活龙动转，十分爽健，此元气之充壮，可以运行河车矣。苟气机大动，不行河车化精为气、化气为神之工，仍然凝聚丹鼎，奈未经火化，阴精难固，不能长留于后天鼎中，一霎时，凡火一起，必动淫根，生淫事而倾矣。即或强制死守，不使他动，奈后天精气，皆属纯阴，未经煅炼，不强制他必泄，即强制他亦必泄也。夫以此诀一行，即可以夺天地鬼神之权，参造化阴阳之法，而自主自夺，我命由我不由天矣，实为长生不老之仙，所谓「阎罗老子亦无奈我何」者此也，所以不许匪人得门而入，使神天无善恶报应之权。尔生属知道者，谅亦深明厥旨，切须稳口闭舌，莫妄泄天机密钥可也。既有元气于丹田，而行河车工法，尤须假后天凡气为阳火阴符，逼迫而催促之，使之上升下降，往来无穷，鼓舞而煅炼之，使之化凡成真，变化莫测。苟徒有元气之发生、活子之现象，而无后天凡气，则先天元气，岂能自上自下、自煅自化？此金丹，虽先天之元气为本，然亦必需后天气为之功用也。至于金丹始终，全仗火候。古人临炉，十分慎重，惟恐一气偶乖，有干阴阳造化，故曰进火行符，犹之煮饭，火缓则生，故贵惺惺常存；火急则焦，故贵绵绵不绝。生于此二语，可知用火之微矣。到得地下雷鸣，火逼金行，此时若非武火，金气安能上升？然必善于用武，任他烈焰万丈，光芒四射，我则以一滴清凉水，遍洒十方足矣。此即「气壮而心亨」之道也，亦即清净恬淡为本之妙术也。故曰：「龙虎相逢上战场，霎时顷刻定兴亡。劝君逢恶须行善，若要争强必损伤。」诚哉其势可畏，其机甚危，而此心不可不临炉审慎也。生既明得此旨，永无倾泄之患焉。虽然，此行河车之法，当如是耳，若一概施之于守中，气机未畅，心神未宁，一以纯任自然之法行之，则神气安能打成一片，有何药物可采哉？此必于玄关初现之时，肾气上升，心液下降，用起数息之武火，不许一念走作，一息纷驰。如此紧催慢鼓，鼓动橐籥机关，然后凡息方停，真息始见，人心乃死，道心乃生。否则，漫说自然，必无自然也。故曰虽有生知之圣人，亦必下困知勉行工夫始得。古云：「西山白虎正猖狂，东海青龙不可当。两手捉来令死斗，化成一块紫金霜。」又曰：「降龙须要志如天，伏虎心雄气似烟。痴蠢愚人能会得，管教立地作神仙。」此种武火，施之于龙虎不交、水火不济之时则可，若行河车，则已龙吟虎啸，夫唱妇随，于此仍用此个法则，又恐迫逐真气散乱，孟子云：「如追放豚，既入其苙，又从而招之」，此大错矣。吾将全功毕露，生等须努力修持，以慰为师之望焉。切勿妄泄，自干罪咎。

Having finished explaining and commenting on the whole Daodejing, Huang Yuanji called all his disciples together and earnestly charged them:

"Today the Great Way is in decline, as precarious as a stack of eggs, and everything depends on you, this group of people sincerely studying the Way, to uphold the upright Breath of heaven and earth and rescue the course of the world. Even though there are rumors outside of bandits and traitors rising up and calamities of war about to break out, Heaven has its own arrangements, and it will never let you meet with unforeseen danger. My only worry is that you yourselves will not hold to virtue broadly enough or believe in the Way firmly enough, being of two minds, practicing for a while and then stopping. That would be cutting yourselves off from the Way of Heaven, unable to reach up to the heart of Heaven. Even if Heaven has the fullest love and wishes to keep you in a blessed land of peaceful rest, if you yourselves cannot receive this grace of Heaven, what can Heaven do?

"Lately your seeing of the Way has become clear and your embodying of the Way has become effective; you truly have a firm grip within. As long as from now on every word and deed, every breath, is in communication with the Way of Heaven, Heaven will naturally love and value you and hold you close in its protection without letting go. For where the Way is, Heaven is; to honor the Way is to honor Heaven; to love the Way is to love Heaven. Through such hidden accord and silent response, auspicious blessings come of themselves; the Way of Heaven is originally in constant communication with the way of humans! Look throughout past and present: there have only ever been those who turned against the Great Way and were rejected by Heaven; when has anyone kept to the Great Way and failed to receive Heaven's protection? You should now see this clearly. In short, each of you walk your own upright path well and each fulfill your own sincerity; as for the right and wrong, fortune and misfortune outside yourselves, leave them all for Heaven to decide and arrange. Never worry on Heaven's behalf or fret about doing others' jobs for them!"

Having given these instructions on how to live in the world with a settled heart, Huang Yuanji went on to impart, holding nothing back, the most essential crucial junctures of the whole practice of internal alchemy:

"The crux of the Great Way, in the end, is nothing more than two things, Spirit and Breath; but within them there is a strict distinction between Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven, which practitioners absolutely must know. An ancient classic says: 'The Original Spirit of Prior Heaven is the substance; the cognitive spirit of Posterior Heaven is the function. Without the Original Spirit of Prior Heaven, the Great Way has no master; without the cognitive spirit of Posterior Heaven, the Great Way has no handle by which to act.' When you set to work cultivating, you must first, in the utter stillness of an undivided state without knowing or awareness, nurture the Original Spirit of Prior Heaven as master. Then, when stillness reaches its extreme and turns to movement, with a start one wakes, with an awakening one stirs, and from this comes forth the wondrous function of clear awareness: this is what the ancient classic calls the 'cognitive spirit.' But see clearly: the cognitive spirit here is by no means the selfish, deluded cognition of the Book of Changes, with its wild thoughts 'coming and going restlessly,' led about by passion and desire. It is the clear awareness that appears when the Original Spirit, originally of perfect and complete enlightenment, stirs into action, provisionally called the 'cognitive spirit.'

"What is most to be feared is that as soon as this awareness emerges, a chaotic flood of evil and stray thoughts surges in like a landslide and will not stop; then in an instant one falls into selfishness and drifts into passion and desire, and can no longer refine the elixir! The only way is to keep a single, brightly illuminating heart, setting one's resolve without the slightest wavering. The Zhuangzi says, 'Using the will undivided, one concentrates the spirit': only when the will is single and undivided can the spirit truly gather. Once the spirit is concentrated, the breath can be regulated evenly (regulating the breath); once the breath is regulated, the Golden Elixir can form. As an old poem describes a fisherman: 'His whole heart is on the line and hook; he does not see the red reed flowers on the far bank.' Such a single, undivided heart, though in words we call it the cognitive spirit, is at bottom the Original Spirit itself! The ancients said, 'The heart of Heaven is the master, the Original Spirit the function.' If you skillfully use this pivot that steals creation from Heaven, why worry that you cannot enter straight into the realm of the sages? Have you understood? The key lies entirely in this: after the heart of Heaven first stirs, store and nurture it steadily at every moment, allowing not a single thought to drift and not a single breath to become confused; only then can the heart of Heaven always be present and the heart of the Way always be concentrated. Though there is clear awareness, it is as pure as if there were no ordinary cognitive thoughts.

"When those who cultivate the true first set to work, what is most precious is using a single heart to subdue the two eyes (not looking about at random), and to gather in along with them all the outward-running deluded thoughts of mouth, ears, body and mind. Then settle the heart and spirit in the lower Cinnabar Field (the bolt of the elixir), regulate the breath evenly within the Cinnabar Field, and wait patiently in stillness until the ordinary breathing of mouth and nose naturally grows fine and comes to rest; only then does the Original Breath of Prior Heaven truly return and restore life.

"Once the Original Breath of Prior Heaven has returned and is dense and lively in the Cinnabar Field, winding and melodious, like a living dragon nimbly turning, refreshed and vigorous, this shows that the Original Breath is already full and strong, and you can follow its momentum to turn the River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit)! If the movement of Breath is already strong but you are unwilling to turn the River Chariot to do the transforming work of 'transforming Essence into Breath and Breath into Spirit' (the three transmutations), and still hold it stubbornly stockpiled in the Cinnabar Field without moving it, this Essence and Breath, not yet tempered and purified by fire phasing and not yet freed of its yin substance, cannot stay long in the cauldron of Posterior Heaven. In the blink of an eye, as soon as a spark of ordinary Posterior-Heaven fire flares up, it will at once stir up lustful thoughts and even lead to the outpouring of Essence and Breath! Even if you grit your teeth and forcibly hold it, pressing it down so that it cannot move, the untempered Essence and Breath of Posterior Heaven are by nature yin substance: if you do not press them down, they will of course leak; if you press them down, they will leak just the same!

"But as long as you know the formula of turning the River Chariot in time, you can transform Essence and Breath and take part in the creative transformations of yin and yang in heaven and earth. The ancient Daoist schools have always regarded this as the secret formula for seizing creation, 'my life is up to me, not to Heaven,' so that even 'old King Yama can do nothing to me.' That is why the patriarchs of every generation never allowed people of crooked character to glimpse the way in, lest they disturb the heavenly order of good and evil. Since all of you are disciples who understand the Way and keep to what is upright, you surely know the proper measure in this; you must guard your tongues and never show off or leak it carelessly to the wrong people.

"Let me add one more subtle point. Once there is Original Breath of Prior Heaven in the Cinnabar Field, when turning the River Chariot you must also borrow the gentle ordinary Breath of Posterior Heaven (the rhythm of breathing) as yang fire and yin tally to urge it on and push it lightly, letting it circulate, rising and descending, back and forth without ceasing, stirring and tempering it again and again, so that it can transform the ordinary into the true, with inexhaustible wondrous functions. If there is only the sprouting of the Original Breath of Prior Heaven and the appearance of the Living Zi Hour, but no use at all of the fire phasing of Posterior-Heaven breathing to coordinate and move it, how could the Original Breath of Prior Heaven obediently rise and descend by itself and temper and transform itself? Clearly, although the Golden Elixir takes the Original Breath of Prior Heaven as its root, it also cannot do without the workings of Posterior-Heaven Breath as an auxiliary condition.

"As for the success or failure of the Golden Elixir from beginning to end, it depends entirely on the two words fire phasing. The ancients were extremely careful when approaching the furnace, fearing that the slightest deviation of the Breath would go against the creative transformations of yin and yang. So they used a comparison: advancing the yang fire and withdrawing the yin tally is like lighting a fire to cook rice. If the fire is too slow, the rice will not cook (it stays raw), so what is valued is clear, bright wakefulness, always alert; if the fire is too fierce, the rice burns, so what is valued is gentle continuity, unbroken and unceasing! Savor these two sentences carefully, and you will understand the finest measure in the use of fire.

"When deep in the Cinnabar Field the single yang stirs mightily like 'thunder rumbling beneath the earth,' and the fire presses the metal to move, if at this moment there were no moderate martial fire to drive it, how could the true Breath rise? But what matters most in using martial fire is 'using it well.' Let the movement of Breath in the body surge like flames ten thousand feet high, radiating light in every direction; in my heart I remain as clear, cool and composed as if holding a single drop of cool sweet dew and sprinkling it over the ten directions! This is what the Book of Changes calls 'the Breath vigorous and the heart unobstructed,' and it is precisely the marvelous heart-method of the whole book, which takes pure stillness and placid calm as its root! An old alchemical poem says: 'When dragon and tiger meet, they go to the battlefield; in a single instant rise or ruin is decided. I urge you, when you meet evil, to do good; if you strive to be the stronger, you are sure to be harmed.' Truly, that momentum is awe-inspiring and that juncture extremely delicate and perilous; when approaching the furnace, the heart and spirit must be exceptionally careful! Once you have thoroughly grasped this principle, you will never again have to fear leakage or loss of control.

"That said, the cool composure described above is the rule for when the River Chariot is turning. If you apply it indiscriminately to the beginning stage of guarding the center and embracing the One, when the movement of Breath is not yet flowing freely and the heart and spirit are not yet at peace, and simply talk idly of 'relying purely on naturalness,' how could spirit and Breath possibly become one? And where would there be any true medicine to gather?

"So it must be at the moment when the Mysterious Pass first appears, the kidney Breath first rises and the heart's fluid first descends, that you decisively take up the focused martial fire of counting breaths, allowing not a single thought to wander and not a single breath to become disordered. Urging tightly and fanning slowly in this way, you set the inner mechanism working like pumping a bellows (the tuoyue); only then does the ordinary breathing of mouth and nose naturally come to rest and the inner True Breath appear; the ordinary human heart dies away and the pure heart of the Way is born! Without going through this solid, focused work, merely talking about naturalness will never bring true naturalness! That is why the Confucians also say that even a sage born with knowledge must still put in the hard work of 'knowing through difficulty and practicing with effort.' An old alchemical formula says: 'The White Tiger of the western mountain is raging wild; the Azure Dragon of the eastern sea cannot be withstood. Seize them with both hands and make them fight to the death; they turn into a lump of purple-gold frost.' And again: 'To subdue the dragon, your resolve must be like Heaven; to tame the tiger, your heart must be bold and your Breath like smoke. If even a foolish, dull person can grasp this, they are sure to become an immortal on the spot.' This kind of fierce, decisive martial-fire formula, applied at the beginning when dragon and tiger have not yet joined, water and fire have not yet completed each other and stray thoughts are flying, is exactly the right medicine for the illness!

"But once this pass has been crossed and you have truly entered the turning of the River Chariot, when 'the dragon sings and the tiger roars, the husband leads and the wife follows,' and spirit and Breath blend naturally, if you still cling to this method of fierce martial pressure, you will instead drive the true Breath you worked so hard to gather to scatter and flee in all directions. That would be committing the great mistake Mencius spoke of: 'It is like chasing a runaway piglet: it has already obediently gone back into its pen, and you go after it and tie up its feet!' That would be absurdly wrong!

"Today I have laid out the whole work of internal alchemy in full, holding nothing back. You must all practice diligently and honestly, so as not to disappoint your teacher's hopes! Remember to guard your words, and do not leak these things carelessly, lest you bring blame upon yourselves."

Key Terms

  • The Original Spirit as substance, the cognitive spirit as function: the harmonizing conclusion Huang Yuanji gives in the final chapter. In an undivided state without knowing, nurture the Original Spirit of Prior Heaven as substance; after an awakening stirs, the single-minded awareness, untainted by selfish desire, that regulates the breath and concentrates the spirit is the "cognitive spirit" of true awareness, serving as function.
  • Fire phasing is like cooking rice: if the fire is too slow, the rice stays raw (so one must stay always alert to guard against torpor); if too fierce, it burns (so one must be continuous and unbroken to guard against agitated force). A vivid image revealing the middle way in the use of fire.
  • The Breath vigorous and the heart unobstructed: from the hexagram Kan of the Book of Changes, "only the heart is unobstructed." It describes how, when the true Breath surges like flames ten thousand feet high, the state of mind stays composed and clear like a single drop of cool water, never following the agitation of the Breath into striving to be stronger.
  • The boundary between martial fire and civil fire: when the Mysterious Pass first appears, water and fire have not yet joined and the heart and spirit are scattered, one must use the martial fire of focused counting of breaths, "urging tightly and fanning slowly." Once the River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit) is turning and spirit and Breath accompany each other, one must at once let go of forcing and switch to the civil fire of warm nurturing that follows its natural course.
  • Thunder rumbling beneath the earth: the alchemical classics borrow the hexagram Fu (Return) of the Book of Changes (Zhen thunder beneath Kun earth) to describe the single yang stirring in the lower Cinnabar Field, vitality bursting forth with a roar.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Value true words over flashy ones, integration over showy erudition. Whether studying or judging people you work with, do not be dazzled by "beautiful words," "clever arguments" or "showy erudition." Plain principles that go straight to the core with one thread running through them are usually the ones that best stand the test of time.
  2. "The more one serves others, the more one has" is the antidote to a sense of scarcity. Like a bright mirror, hoard no private interest and be glad to share and help others; knowledge, good relationships and inner confidence grow the more you give, while the more you guard your wealth, the more anxious you become.
  3. "If the fire is too slow, the rice stays raw; if too fierce, it burns" applies to all long-term growth. Whether in quiet sitting, reading or work, being too lax and perfunctory never brings anything to ripeness (raw), while charging ahead anxiously can easily burn you out (burnt). Staying awake (always alert) and steady over time (continuous and unbroken) takes you furthest.
  4. Know how to switch between "applying effort" and "letting go" according to the stage. When a new habit has not yet taken hold and stray thoughts are flying, you need the "martial fire" of focused breath-counting to hold your ground. Once you enter a smooth, focused "flow" state, stop over-monitoring yourself and pushing hard, lest you disrupt a state that was flowing well (like chasing a piglet already back in its pen).

Translator's Notes

  1. "My Way has one thread running through it" and "The Way is one, and that is all": From the Analects ("Li Ren") and the Mencius ("Teng Wen Gong I") respectively.
  2. "Coming and going restlessly, your companions follow your thoughts": From the fourth line of the hexagram Xian in the Book of Changes, "Coming and going restlessly, your companions follow your thoughts," an image of the confused, scattered thinking of Posterior Heaven.
  3. "Using the will undivided, one concentrates the spirit": From the Zhuangzi ("Mastering Life"), the words about the hunchback who catches cicadas, meaning that only when the will is single and undivided can the spirit be highly concentrated.
  4. "His whole heart is on the line and hook; he does not see the red reed flowers on the far bank": A traditional Chan and Daoist fisherman's verse, an image of not being moved by outer sights and sounds while concentrating on regulating the breath and concentrating the spirit.
  5. "Like chasing a runaway piglet: it has already gone into its pen, and you go after it and tie it up": From the Mencius ("Jin Xin II"); li (苙) is a pigpen and zhao (招) means to tie up its feet. Huang Yuanji cleverly cites this line to warn that once spirit and Breath have blended, one must never again force and interfere with fierce martial fire.
  6. Note on the base text and its sources: At the end of this chapter the original book is headed "End of Huang Yuanji's Commentary on the Daodejing" and carries the editor's note: "Edited from the Daozang jiyao edition (in Zangwai daoshu, vol. 10), collated with Ding Fubao's Daozang jinghua lu edition (in Xiao Tianshi, Daozang jinghua, series 1, part 2)." The words "bandits and traitors ... calamities of war about to break out" reflect the turmoil of the times when Huang Yuanji lectured on this classic at the Leyu Hall in Sichuan during the Xianfeng reign of the Qing.
  7. Textual note: The 已 in 已愈有 and 已愈多 in the classic text and in 在一已 and 而已愈多其德 in the commentary has in each case been corrected to 己; 无安吐 to 无妄吐; 询旨风流 to 词旨风流; 聋耳日 to 聋耳目; 孟子日 to 孟子曰; 香茫之际 to 杳茫之际; 救正乾押 to 救正乾坤; 信道不乌 to 信道不笃; the superfluous comma in 欲生尔等于，休养安恬之天 is deleted; 一党而动 is corrected to 一觉而动; 不可以炼开也 to 不可以炼丹也; 虽白识神，其是即元神也 to 虽曰识神，其实即元神也; 氤氤活泼，绝转悠扬 to 氤氲活泼，婉转悠扬; the 几 in 几火一起 and 后天几气 is in both places corrected to 凡; 必池 to 必泄; 包能自上自下 to 岂能自上自下; 用火之徽 to 用火之微; 地下雷吗 to 地下雷鸣; 气壮而心享 to 气壮而心亨; and 水无倾泄之患 to 永无倾泄之患. In addition, at the quotation in §3, the original printed edition mistakenly reads "Breath-spirit of Prior Heaven" (先天气神) for "Original Spirit of Prior Heaven is the substance" (先天元神，体也); the rendering has been made directly according to 先天元神.

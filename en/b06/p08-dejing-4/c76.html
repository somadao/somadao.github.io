VIII · Classic of Virtue (Ch. 71–81) · 德经

Chapter 76 · The Soft and Weak Dwell Above

Where warm, harmonious yang Breath gathers, softness and life keep growing; where cold yin Breath congeals, things turn hard and wither. In internal alchemy, every stage from the first Breath of the Mysterious Pass through gathering the medicine, running the fire and warm nurturing relies on this one soft, tender and lively Breath of Prior Heaven

About This Chapter

Chapter 76 of the Daodejing uses an everyday observation, that people and plants are soft while alive and hard when dead, to state an iron law: "the hard and strong are companions of death; the soft and weak are companions of life." Huang Yuanji first makes the principle of yin and yang, of giving life and taking it, fully clear through a steaming pot, the four seasons and battles from the Spring and Autumn period. Then, in §3, he carries the two words "soft and weak" straight through the fire phasing of every stage of internal alchemy:

  1. Softness is warm yang harmony; hardness is cold yin (§2): People commonly mistake stiffness and aggression for "yang vigor." Huang Yuanji turns this around in a single sentence: a living body is supple and warm because warm, harmonious yang Breath has gathered in it; a corpse is stiff and cold, and plants in autumn and winter dry out and snap, because cold yin Breath has congealed. It is like a steamer: while the steam billows, everything in it softens and melts, and as soon as it cools, everything sets hard. So the "softness and weakness" that teems with life is, at bottom, abundant yang harmony.
  2. The first Breath of the Mysterious Pass: supremely soft yet firm, seeming to be there and not there (§3, first half): The Mysterious Pass, the most important opening in cultivation, gives rise to this very first Breath of all life in heaven, earth, humans and things. Within the dim and obscure, yang is held inside yin and life lies hidden inside decline; it is neither rigid nor dead and still.
  3. Gathering the medicine, running the fire, withdrawing and warm nurturing: no step can do without softness (§3, second half): Gathering the Medicine must be done like an infant whose bones are soft and body weak, yet whose fist holds a firm grip. When running the fire, the whole body turns limp and soft as cotton, lively and gently harmonious: this is the sign that true yang is circulating densely. During withdrawal and warm nurturing one must be even more continuous and fine-grained, the spirit too lazy to think and the mouth too lazy to speak. From the ten months of gestation (the Holy Embryo) to the three years of nursing and the nine years facing the wall, everything relies on this soft, weak Breath of Prior Heaven; otherwise it is nothing but empty burning and empty refining.

Safety note: §3 of this chapter mentions traditional stage terms of internal alchemy such as "gathering the medicine in the second phase," "running the fire in the fourth phase," "withdrawing the tally and returning to the furnace," and "ten months of gestation, three years of nursing, nine years facing the wall." These are traditional metaphors and stage descriptions for the natural transformation of body and mind in deep stillness. Do not deliberately chase sensations of the "whole body melting into delight" while sitting, and do not mechanically apply fire-phasing steps. Simply keep the breath soft and the body and mind relaxed and quiet; never force breath-holding or throw yourself into blind, harsh practice.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：人之生也柔弱，其死也坚强。万物草木之生也柔脆，其死也枯槁。故坚强者死之徒，柔弱者生之徒。是以兵强则不胜，木强则拱。强大处下，柔弱处上。

Lord Lao says: While alive, a person's body is soft and supple; after death it becomes stiff and cold. While alive, the myriad plants and trees have branches and leaves that are tender and fragile; after death they become dry and hard. Therefore the hard and strong belong to the company of death, and the soft and gentle belong to the company of life. For this reason an army that relies only on its strength and bravado fails to win, and a tree that grows stiff and old stops at the size of an armspan and readily invites the axe. The forcefully strong in fact occupy the losing, declining position below; the soft and gentle instead occupy the thriving position above.

§2
Original (Chinese)

人禀阳和之气则生，阴寒之气则死。一当阳和气聚，则四体柔顺，一身苏绵，而生机不息矣；一当阴寒气结，则肌肤燥熯，皮毛槁脱，而死气将临矣。试观釜甑之间，蒸蒸浮浮，则阳气氤氲，物融而化。到寒凉时候，物冷而坚。又观天地，春夏之交，阳气炽而万物畅茂，无不发荣滋长。迨至秋冬之会，阴气盛而万物飘零，无不枯槁难荣。是知人之生也，逢阳气之温和则柔；人之死也，遇阴寒之凝固则刚。其生也柔脆，其死也枯槁，人物一源，无分彼此。是知天下万事万物，无不以坚强为死之徒，柔弱为生之徒也。譬诸用兵，在在强者取败，弱者取胜，如子玉过刚败绩，伯比羸师胜随是也。其故何耶？盖以强者衰之渐，弱者兴之几，宜其不胜矣。再观诸木，木至坚也，阴气盛而阳气衰，宜其大止拱把而无由滋育焉。夫强大者，生气尽而死气临，诚物之至下者也；柔弱者，阴气消而阳气盛，乃物之至上者也。人奈何不自弱而自强，不处上而处下哉？

A person who receives warm, harmonious yang Breath lives and grows; one who falls under cold, stagnant yin Breath moves toward death. As long as warm yang Breath fills and gathers in the body, the four limbs and hundred joints are supple and free, the whole body is relaxed and soft as cotton, and vitality flows on unceasingly. Once cold yin Breath congeals and stagnates, the skin turns dry and parched, the hair withers and falls out, and the breath of death draws near.

If you doubt it, look at the steamer used in everyday cooking. While the steam rises and swirls inside, yang Breath is dense and abundant, and even the hardest food is steamed soft and tender; once the fire goes out and it cools completely, the food at once turns cold and hard. Now look up at the natural world. Between spring and summer, yang Breath is strong and all things flourish lushly; there is nothing that does not sprout, unfold and thrive. When autumn meets winter, yin Breath reaches its peak and all things wither and fall; there is nothing that does not dry out, grow brittle and struggle to grow. From this we see that a living person, meeting warm yang Breath, is soft, and a dead person, meeting congealing cold yin, becomes rigid. Plants are tender and moist in life and dry and brittle in death. Humans and all things come from the same single source of creation and are no different. Clearly, among all the affairs and things under heaven, there is none for which it does not hold that "the hard and rigid belong to death, and the soft and gentle belong to life."

Take warfare as an analogy. History is full of cases where the arrogant and aggressive suffered crushing defeats while those who drew back and showed weakness won. In the Spring and Autumn period, Ziyu, chief minister of Chu, was far too headstrong and self-willed, and he returned in great defeat from the battle of Chengpu. By contrast, the Chu grandee Dou Bobi deliberately sent out troops that looked thin and worn out to lure the enemy, and won a great victory over Sui. Why is this so? Because outward strength pushed to the limit is precisely the beginning of decline, and inward restraint and humility, gathering strength, is precisely the first opening of rise. Naturally, then, an army bent on showing its strength finds it hard to win. Look again at trees: once a trunk has grown too hard, dry and old, it shows that stagnant yin Breath inside is strong and the warm yang life-force has weakened. No wonder it stops growing at the thickness of an armspan and can no longer be nourished into flourishing.

In short, what is called "strong and great" is really life-force exhausted and death at the door, the very lowest grade; what is called "soft and weak" is really stagnant yin melting away and warm yang abundant, which truly occupies the highest grade. Why, then, do people in the world refuse to rest content in softness and instead strain desperately to be strong, abandoning the high ground where life flourishes to burrow down into the low ground of rigidity and decay?

§3
Original (Chinese)

修炼之道，最重玄关一窍，是为天地人物，生生之始气。此气至柔而刚，至弱而强，且刚柔强弱，俱无所见，惟恍惚杳冥中，忽焉阴里含阳，杀里寓生，似有似无，若虚若实，此真无声无臭，上天之载之始机也。人能盗此虚无元始之气，则先天生生之本已得，而位证天仙不难矣。既盗得玄关始气，以为金丹之宝，然二候采药，亦当专气致柔，如稚子骨柔体弱而握固，始得初气以为丹本。四候行火，又要知一身苏软如绵，美快无比，方是先天絪缊蓬勃之机，冲和活泼之象。有此阳气，可炼仙丹。再于退符之候，归炉封固，入鼎温烹，犹当绵绵密密，了了如如，无怠无荒，如痴如醉，神懒于思，口懒于语，所谓「天上春云如我懒，谁知我更懒于春」。如此之柔之弱，方是先天阳气，可以长存而不敝。总之，十月怀胎，三年乳哺，九年面壁，无非先天柔弱之气，为之丹成而仙就耳。修士当寻此柔脆之气，始不空烧空炼、枉劳精神也。

In the way of internal alchemy, what matters most is the single opening of the Mysterious Pass. It is the original first Breath by which heaven, earth and all things are born and keep being born (the One Breath of Prior Heaven). On the surface this Breath is supremely soft, yet inside it holds supreme firmness; it seems supremely delicate, yet hides supreme strength. Moreover, at the moment it first stirs, not even the distinguishing marks of firm and soft, strong and weak can be seen. Only in the utter stillness of the dim and obscure does yang suddenly appear within yin and life within decline, seeming to be there and not there, as if empty and as if full. This is the first opening of creation that the Classic of Poetry calls "the workings of high Heaven, without sound and without scent." If a person can recognize and lay hold of this empty, primordial Breath, they have obtained the root of Prior Heaven's unceasing life, and realizing the Great Way will then not be hard.

Even once you have recognized this first Breath in the Mysterious Pass and taken it as the treasure from which the Golden Elixir is refined, the later step-by-step work of fire phasing still depends from start to finish on the two words "soft and weak":

  • At the stage of "gathering the Medicine in the second phase," you must still concentrate the Breath and reach utmost softness, like a newborn whose sinews and bones are soft yet whose fist naturally holds a firm grip: neither rigid nor tense. Only then can you calmly gather this newly born true Breath as the foundation for forming the elixir.
  • At the stage of "running the fire in the fourth phase" (turning the fire phasing), you must also know how to recognize the genuine signs: the whole body must be limp and soft as cotton, more comfortable and at ease than anything. That is the true face of the vital, billowing circulation of the Breath of Prior Heaven, gently harmonious and lively. Only yang Breath of this warm, harmonious kind is fit for refining the elixir (not a dry, tense heat like a blaze in dry kindling).
  • When you reach the stage of "withdrawing the yin tally," closing the work by returning to the furnace, sealing it firmly and nurturing warmly in the cauldron, you should be all the more continuous and fine-grained, clear and distinct, calm and at ease, neither slack and scattered nor neglectful and wandering, your whole being relaxed and tranquil as if slightly tipsy. The spirit is too lazy to think idle thoughts and the mouth too lazy for idle talk, just as an alchemical poem describes it: "The spring clouds in the sky are lazy like me; who knew I am lazier still than spring!"

Only when one reaches such utmost relaxed softness and gentle continuity is it true yang Breath of Prior Heaven, which can be preserved and nourished for a long time without ever wearing out. In the end, from what the alchemical classics call "ten months of gestation" (warmly nurturing the Holy Embryo) to "three years of nursing" and then "nine years facing the wall" (refining the form through true emptiness), which step does not rely entirely on this soft, weak, warm and moist Breath of Prior Heaven to accomplish the divine elixir and the Great Way? Practitioners must recognize and savor this tender, lively vitality; only then will they avoid the wrong path of clenching their teeth and forcing, burning and refining in vain, and wasting their spirit for nothing.

Key Terms

  • Warm yang Breath and cold yin Breath: Huang Yuanji uses these to redefine "soft and weak" and "hard and strong." The relaxed warmth of a living body is a sign of gathered yang harmony; the rigid dryness of dead things is a sign of congealed cold yin. This overturns the worldly misconception that "forcing it is yang vigor."
  • Firm grip: From Chapter 55 of the Daodejing, "its bones are weak and sinews soft, yet its grip is firm," describing a state like an infant's, in which the body is supremely soft while Essence and Breath are kept within and not leaked.
  • Gathering the medicine in the second phase, running the fire in the fourth phase, withdrawal and warm nurturing: the alchemists' names for the stages of fire phasing (gathering the Medicine, running the fire, withdrawing the tally and sealing). Huang Yuanji stresses here that throughout, the true sign is a soft, compliant movement of Breath that is "limp and soft as cotton, continuous and fine-grained."
  • Ten months of gestation, three years of nursing, nine years facing the wall: traditional alchemical classics borrow the images of pregnancy, nursing and Bodhidharma facing the wall to describe the long process of stillness in which the Holy Embryo is warmly nurtured and the yang spirit is fostered until it is perfectly whole.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Break the illusion that "tense = powerful." Whether in exercise, work or sitting meditation, rigid teeth-gritting and grim endurance are expressions of "cold yin, the breath of death." Truly skilled effort and focus always rest on a body and mind that are loose, lively and soft, with the breath flowing freely.
  2. Use "melting softness" to check how relaxed you really are. Each time you sit, breathe or take a midday rest, notice whether your shoulders, jaw, brow and belly are quietly tightening. The softer and more at ease you are, the more readily warm yang vitality rises.
  3. Allow yourself a little "spring-cloud laziness." "The spirit too lazy to think, the mouth too lazy to speak" is not passive giving up; after intense activity it means deliberately switching off excess thinking and pointless chatter, so that spirit and Breath can recharge in fine, quiet continuity.
  4. Leave room in how you deal with things; do not use up every word and every ounce of strength. "A strong tree stops at an armspan," "a strong army does not win": once you take an overbearing, rigid stance in relationships or work, you have often reached the tipping point toward decline.

Translator's Notes

  1. "A strong tree stops at an armspan" (mu qiang ze gong): Received editions of the Daodejing vary greatly at this line. The Wang Bi edition reads mu qiang ze bing (木强则兵), the Heshang Gong edition and the silk manuscripts read mu qiang ze gong (木强则共), and some Ming and Qing commentaries read ze zhe (则折, "then it breaks") or ze gong (则拱). In §2 Huang Yuanji explains it as "it stops at the size of an armspan and cannot be nourished further" (its girth stops at what two arms can encircle and grows no more), so both the classic text and the rendering follow the base text's 拱.
  2. "Ziyu was too unyielding and was defeated; Bobi's feeble troops beat Sui": Both from the Zuo Commentary. Cheng Dechen of Chu (style name Ziyu), chief minister, was headstrong and underestimated his enemy, and was defeated by Jin at the battle of Chengpu in 632 BCE (Duke Xi, year 28). The Chu grandee Dou Bobi proposed luring Sui into complacency with troops that looked weak and worn, and thereby won a great victory (Duke Huan, year 6).
  3. "The spring clouds in the sky are lazy like me; who knew I am lazier still than spring": A famous line often cited in Song, Yuan and Ming alchemical texts and in Chan and Daoist poetry talks, describing the relaxed stillness of withdrawal and warm nurturing, in which the mind is at leisure and free of thought yet remains alert and unclouded.
  4. Textual note: In the title, the comma in 第七十六章，柔弱处上 has been corrected to a middle dot; 皮毛福脱 is corrected to 皮毛槁脱 on the basis of the context (肌肤燥熯, 其死也枯槁) and the shape of the character; 譬请用兵 to 譬诸用兵; 胜隨 is normalized to 胜随; 再观诸术，不至坚也 is corrected to 再观诸木，木至坚也 on the basis of the classic's 木强则拱 and the sense; 全弱而强 to 至弱而强 following the preceding 至柔而刚; 恍惚香冥 to 恍惚杳冥; 稚子肯柔体弱 to 稚子骨柔体弱; and 先天纲缰 to 先天絪缊.

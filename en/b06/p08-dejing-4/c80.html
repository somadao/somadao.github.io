Chapter 80 · A Small Country with Few People

Waning Essence and old age are no dead end for cultivating the Way; the paths of pure stillness that refines emptiness and of yin-yang return are both made clear: first use the martial fire of counting breaths to drive out turbid Breath, then, once the Breath is full and the spirit at peace, return at last to natural non-action

About This Chapter

"A small country with few people, where the sounds of cocks and dogs are heard from one to the next, and the people grow old and die without ever visiting each other" is the most celebrated ideal picture in the Daodejing. Huang Yuanji first sets out its original meaning for governing the world, giving the people peace and contentment (§2); then, in §3 and §4, he unfolds one of the most practically instructive discourses in the whole book: he reads "a small country with few people" as "the method of cultivation for those who are old and whose Essence has waned," and systematically compares the two great gateways of the alchemical Way, "cultivating through pure stillness" and "replenishing through yin and yang":

  1. A small country with few people: even the old, whose Essence has waned, can return to the elixir (§3): The Golden Elixir relies on the Lead and Mercury of Prior Heaven, not on the blood and Breath of Posterior Heaven, so old and young alike can set to work. "Having tools worth ten or a hundred people yet not using them" means gathering in the six sense roots, restraining the outside to nourish the center. "Having no boats or carriages, no armor or weapons" means there is no need to mobilize great forces for everything; through quiet nurturing of centrality and harmony one returns to the wordless accord with Heaven of "knotted cords." "Neighboring countries within sight, yet never visiting one another" means that though one sees and hears, the mind does not stir in the face of objects and is not stained by sounds and colors.
  2. "Cultivating through pure stillness": pointing directly at refining emptiness (§4, first half): Without going through the elaborate procedure of refining Essence into Breath, one simply sets the Original Spirit upright, without thinking or worrying, and the water and fire, sun and moon within the body rise and descend of themselves, Spirit the father and Breath the mother joining of themselves to form true Mercury. This method relies purely on naturalness, but only those of the highest capacity can attune to it, and its results come very slowly.
  3. "Replenishing through yin and yang": a down-to-earth return (§4, second half): Ordinary people, fallen into Posterior Heaven with their spirit and Breath dissipated, are like travelers already far from home who have no choice but to spend some effort walking back. First recognize the true seed of the single yang with which the Supreme Ultimate lays its foundation; in quiet sitting, close the six gates and follow and count the breaths. When the outer breath pauses and the True Breath stirs, let the spirit lead the Breath, opening the passes to rise and descend by way of the Caudal Funnel, using the martial fire ("battle in the field") to drive out turbid Breath. Once the Breath in the Cinnabar Field is full and the heart and spirit are at peace, return once more to the natural way of refining emptiness through pure stillness.

Safety note: The "replenishing through yin and yang" of §4 in this chapter refers to the harmonizing and returning of yin and yang within oneself, of the Kan water and Li fire, of spirit and Breath; it is not the paired sexual cultivation of man and woman as popularly misunderstood. The text's descriptions of advanced methods such as "closing the six gates tightly," "the outer breath pauses and the True Breath begins to stir," "opening the passes and leading the Breath up the Caudal Funnel," and "fierce cooking and rapid refining with martial fire" all require long foundation-laying and timely guidance from a qualified teacher. Never forcibly hold the breath on your own or use willpower to drive hot Breath forcefully up the spine (the Caudal Funnel and Governing vessel); this can very easily cause a swollen or aching head, chest tightness, stagnant Breath or nervous tension. In daily practice, focus on natural, unhurried breathing and a calm heart and spirit, and stop immediately at any discomfort.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：小国寡民，使有什伯人之器而不用，使民重死而不远徙。虽有舟车，无所用之；虽有甲兵，无所陈之。使民复结绳而用之。甘其食，美其服，安其居，乐其俗。邻国相望，鸡犬之声相闻，民至老死不相往来。

Lord Lao says: Even in a country with little territory and few people, though it has tools that do the work of ten or a hundred people, it does not show them off or misuse them; it lets the people value their lives, cherish their homeland and not have to leave their native place and move far away to make a living. Though there are boats and carriages, there is no need to ride them on far journeys; though there are armor and weapons, there is no need to draw them up for battle. It lets the customs of the people become so simple that they return to the plain sincerity of the ancient practice of "keeping records with knotted cords." The people find their food sweet, their clothes beautiful, their dwellings secure and their customs joyful. Neighboring countries can see each other, and the sounds of cocks crowing and dogs barking can be heard from one to the other, yet to the end of their days the people are each content with their own lot and feel no need to bustle back and forth visiting one another.

§2
Original (Chinese)

小国寡民，地僻人稀，欲成丰大之邦，敦上礼之俗，似亦难矣。然能省其虚费，裁其繁文，使有什伯人之器而不用，则糜费少而器物多，国家之富可致也。且不纵欲而轻生，营私而罹死，远游他乡，贸居人国，而惟父子相依，兄弟是恋，重死而不远徙，则康乐和亲之世可臻也。以故媚我君王，念兹土宇，虽有舟舆，不肯远适异国以离父母邦焉。朝廷深仁厚泽，沦肌浃髓，恩同父子，谊若弟昆，是以叛乱顽徒，悉化为良善，虽有甲兵，亦无所陈之矣。如此上恬下熙，民安国泰，使复行结绳之政，乐太和之风，亲亲长长，宅宅田田，甘其饮食，美其衣服，于以安居而乐俗，敦厚以成风，又何患国小民寡，难以惇大成裕，仁厚可风哉？第见民爱君如父母，君视民如子弟，中心耿耿，系念殷殷，纵顷刻之别离，亦不忍也。虽邻国在即，举目能窥，鸡犬相闻，倾耳可听，而民则自少至壮，自生及死，不与邻国一相往来，此盖民之感恩戴德，沐化涵情于君上者深矣。是以安无为之治，享有道之天，而不肯一步稍离。如此，则国岂犹患小，民岂犹患寡哉？势必声教四讫，风声远播，而天下归仁，万国来同也。

A small country with little territory and few people, in a remote place, would seem to find it hard to become a rich and great state and to foster customs of honest, deep-rooted respect for ritual. But if it can cut out showy waste and trim away elaborate formalities, so that even with efficient tools at hand it does not lightly misuse them, then with less waste its stores will naturally grow abundant, and the country's prosperity can be achieved. Moreover, if the people no longer recklessly risk their lives out of indulgence and greed, leaving their native place to drift in other countries, but wish only for fathers and sons to stay together and brothers to remain close, valuing their lives and not lightly moving far away, then a peaceful age of health, joy, kinship and harmony will come about as naturally as water flowing into a channel.

Precisely for this reason, the people sincerely love their ruler and are attached to this native land; though they have boats and carriages, they do not wish to go far off to foreign countries and leave the land of their parents. The court's deep humaneness and abundant favor soak into the very marrow of the people; the bond between ruler and people is like that of father and son, their affection like that of brothers. Even those who were once stubborn and rebellious are transformed into good citizens, so that although armor and weapons are kept ready, there is nowhere to deploy them.

When those above are tranquil and non-acting and those below are joyful and at peace, the country secure and the people content, it is as if the plain governance of the ancient age of knotted cords had returned, and everyone bathes in the clear breeze of supreme harmony: respecting and loving parents and elders, each settled in their own home and tilling their own fields, finding simple food sweet and plain clothing handsome, living in peace and working happily, with pure customs and a prevailing honesty. Why then worry that the country is small and the people few, so that it is hard to attain the humane bearing of "honest greatness and abundant fulfillment"? One sees only that the people love their ruler as their father and mother and the ruler regards the people as his children and younger brothers; their inner attachment is so sincere that they cannot bear to be parted even for a moment. Even though the neighboring country is right before their eyes, visible at a glance, and the crowing of its cocks and barking of its dogs can be heard by cocking an ear, the people, from youth to prime, from birth to death, do not wish to go to the neighboring country to seek connections. This is entirely because the people are grateful for the ruler's virtue and deeply content in an age of the Way, pure and non-acting, so that they will not leave it by a single step! When it has come to this, is there still any worry that the country is small or the people few? Its virtuous teaching will surely spread in all directions and its fame travel far, until at last all under heaven turns to humaneness and the ten thousand states come of their own accord, gladly and sincerely submitting.

§3
Original (Chinese)

此喻年老精衰者修炼之法。夫人到老来，精气耗散，铅汞减少，欲修金丹大道，亦似难乎其难。不知金丹一事，非属后天精气，乃是先天铅汞。苟得其至一之道，采而取之，饵而服之，不论年老年少，皆可得药于一时半刻，成功于十年三月。特患不闻先天真一之气，徒取服于后天有形之精，不惟老大无成，即少壮之士，亦终无得也。惟下手之初，勉强支持，使手不妄动，足不轻行，目不外视，耳不他听，口绝闲言，心无妄想，自朝至暮，涤虑洗心，制外养中，退藏于密，不使一些之牵，不令半毫之累，积之久久，诚至明生，自然目光内照，耳灵内凝，舌神内蕴，心灵内存，四肢舒徐，头头合道。此喻什伯人之器而不用，然后用之无不足也。民比身也。人到老来，莫不畏死情极，好生心深，然畏死而不知求生，徒畏亦无益耳。惟谨慎幽独，时时内观，刻刻返照，不离方寸之中，久则致中致和，虽天地可位，万物可育矣，何况近在一身，而有不位不育者乎？此立玄牝，养谷神，绵绵若存，用之不勤，惺惺常在，守之不败，寂而常照，照而常寂，即常应常静，无文无武，所谓「动观自在，静养中和」者此也。固不事河车运转，斗柄推迁，又无须戡乱以武，野战则宜，守城以文，沐浴为尚，取喻于临炉进火，用师克敌也。此清净而修之法，非阴阳补益之工，不但老人行持，可以得药还丹，即少年照此修持，亦可绵绵密密，不贰不息，上合夫於穆之天。第躁进无近功，急成非大器，惟优游餍饫，如水之浸润，火之薰蒸，久则义精仁熟，而道有成矣。故虽有舟舆，无所乘之，虽有甲兵，无所陈之也。且夫进退升降、朝屯暮蒙之法，太上前已喻言：「兵者不祥之器，圣人不得已而用之。」「师之所处，荆棘生焉。大兵之后，必有凶年。」足见临炉采药行火，特为后天气拘物蔽之深者立一法程，倘不如此，则凡气无由化，真金不可还也。若能静养为功，不施烹煎之术，惟守虚静之中，则不知不觉，无为无思，自然浑浑沦沦，纯乎以正，默然合天，不待言思拟议，而与天地流行无间。此即使民复结绳而用之，不立文字，不假言诠，而善记不用筹策也。甘其食，美其服，即精贯于中，气环于外，内甘而外美，有不可名言者。安其居，乐其俗，则中心安仁，随其所之，无不宜也。修炼至此，了了常明，如如自在，对境可以无心，遇物何能相染？虽有所见所闻，亦若无见无闻，绝不因色声而生其心，故曰：「邻国相望，不相往来。」此无上上乘，无下下乘，玄之又玄、妙而又妙之功。呜呼！学至于此，与道大适矣。

In the actual practice of internal alchemy, "a small country with few people" is an image of the method by which those who are getting old and whose Essence and Breath have waned should cultivate. Once a person reaches old age, the Essence and Breath of the physical body have dissipated, as if little were left of the Lead and Mercury within, and cultivating the Great Way of the Golden Elixir seems harder than hard. What people do not know is that the matter of the Golden Elixir does not depend at all on the limited Essence and Breath of the Posterior-Heaven body; it is drawn from the true Lead and true Mercury of Prior Heaven (the One Breath of Prior Heaven)! As long as one truly understands this Way of the utmost One and knows how to gather and nurture it, whether old or young, one can obtain the true medicine in a moment of stillness and succeed step by step through accumulation over days and months, like the "ten months and three years." What is most to be feared is never hearing of the true unified Breath of Prior Heaven and knowing only how to cling to the formed Essence and blood of Posterior Heaven. In that case not only will the old fail in their cultivation, but even young people strong in body will in the end gain nothing.

How, then, does one set to work concretely? At the very start, some conscious restraint and support are still needed: the hands do not move about at random, the feet are not restless, the eyes do not look outward greedily, the ears do not listen outward indiscriminately, the mouth says little idle talk, and the mind entertains no deluded thoughts. From morning to night, wash away stray worries and purify the original mind; by gathering in outer involvements, nurture the central palace (restraining the outside to nourish the center); withdraw the heart and spirit into a deep, hidden, quiet place, letting not the slightest outside matter tug at you and not a hair's breadth of worldly burden weigh on your mind. Accumulating this day after day, when sincerity reaches its utmost, numinous clarity naturally shines forth: the gaze naturally turns back inward, hearing naturally gathers inward, the spirit of the fluids beneath the tongue is naturally stored within, the heart and spirit naturally abide within, the four limbs are relaxed and at ease, and every movement accords with the Way. This is just like the classic's "though it has tools worth ten or a hundred people, it does not use them": gather back all the senses and talents of the body and do not misuse them, and when the time truly comes to use them, they are inexhaustible and in no way lacking!

The "people" of the classic are an image of this one body's nature and life. When people reach old age, who is not terribly afraid of dying and desperately eager to live? But fearing death without understanding the true way of seeking life, what use is fear in vain? Only by carefully keeping watch in the hidden moments of solitude, contemplating inwardly at every hour and turning the light back at every moment, never leaving this Square Inch of the heart and spirit. Over time one achieves the centrality before joy, anger, sorrow and pleasure arise and the harmony when they arise in due measure; even heaven, earth and all things can each settle in their places and be nurtured, so how could this body and mind right here not be settled and nurtured? This is establishing the Mysterious Pass (the Mysterious Female) and nourishing the valley spirit: "continuous, as if ever present; used, yet never exhausted"; clear and awake, always present, kept without loss; within stillness there is always bright illumination, and within illumination there is always a return to stillness; one can respond to things as they come and yet always remain pure and still, with no question of civil fire or martial fire. This is exactly what the ancients called "in motion, contemplate freely; in stillness, nourish centrality and harmony."

On this path there is in any case no need to deliberately move the River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit) or shift the Handle of the Dipper, nor any need, as in quelling a rebellion, to talk of "martial fire for battle in the field, civil fire for guarding the city, and ablution for warm nurturing." Those are all methods of deliberate action that use military campaigns as an image for approaching the furnace, advancing the fire and eliminating yin evils. What is spoken of here is the method of cultivating purely through pure stillness, not the work of replenishing through vigorous operation and refining. Not only can old people who practice accordingly obtain the medicine and return to the elixir; young people who practice accordingly can also be continuous and fine-grained, single-minded and unceasing, matching above the profound, unceasing Way of Heaven. There is just one iron rule: rash haste has no shortcut, and trying to grow fat in one mouthful will never make a great vessel. One must nurture at leisure and soak oneself fully, like spring water moistening things and a gentle fire steaming them; over time principle naturally ripens and the Great Way completes itself. That is why it says, "Though there are boats and carriages, there is no need to ride them; though there are armor and weapons, there is no need to draw them up": the boats and carriages of the River Chariot need not be deliberately driven, and the armor and weapons of the martial fire need not be deliberately deployed.

Understand that the fire phasing of deliberate action, with its advancing and withdrawing, rising and descending, and its morning and evening operations ("Zhun in the morning, Meng in the evening"), was long ago compared by Lord Lao to warfare: "Weapons are instruments of ill omen; the sage uses them only when there is no alternative." "Where armies have camped, thorns and brambles grow; after a great war, there are sure to be years of famine." Clearly the fierce means of approaching the furnace, gathering the medicine and running the fire is a procedure established, out of necessity, specifically for those deeply bound by the temperament of Posterior Heaven and thickly clouded by desire for things; without tempering with fire in this way, the heavy ordinary Breath cannot be dissolved and the true gold cannot be returned. If a person is from the outset able to take empty, still nurturing as their work, applying no art of boiling and cooking, simply abiding steadily in empty purity and stillness, then, without knowing or noticing, without thinking or doing, they become naturally undivided and whole, pure and upright without bias, silently in accord with Heaven, and without needing words or thoughts to deduce or gesture, they are already flowing with the creative process of heaven and earth without gap. This is precisely "letting the people return to knotted cords and use them": clear and bright of themselves without setting up elaborate writing or relying on conceptual calculation.

At this stage, "finding their food sweet and their clothes beautiful" is like the true Essence within pervading and filling, and the harmonious Breath without circulating all around; sweet within and lustrous without, there is an indescribable sense of peace and benefit. "Their dwellings secure and their customs joyful" means the heart abides in the Way of humaneness, and whatever situation one is in and wherever one goes, everything is fitting and right. When cultivation reaches this state, the heart and spirit are clear and distinct, calm and open, at ease in suchness; facing a profusion of outer objects, no deluded mind arises, so how could contact with the myriad affairs and things stain it? Though the eyes see and the ears hear, it is as if nothing were seen and nothing heard; outer sounds and colors never stir selfish craving. This is what the classic means by "neighboring countries can see each other, the sounds of cocks and dogs can be heard from one to the other, yet the people grow old and die without ever visiting each other!" This is the unsurpassed, wondrous work beyond all distinctions of high and low, mysterious and yet more mysterious, wondrous and yet more wondrous. Ah, when study reaches this ground, one is truly fused with the Great Way, free and at ease!

Original (Chinese)

若论修道，古人有两等修法，有清净而修者，有阴阳而补者。清净而修，即炼虚一著，不必炼精、炼气为也。然非上等根器，不能语此。若果根蒂不凡，从此一步做去，俱是顺天地自然之道，不似吾师今日之教，尚多作为也。盖人身之中，原有阴阳坎离，乾坤阖辟，日月水火，升降进退之机，犹天之运行，皆自然而然，无须为之推迁，但只一正其元神，使之不知不觉，无思无虑，那清空一气，浩浩荡荡，自然一呼一吸，上下往来，如乾坤之阖辟，日月之往来，水火之升降，阴阳之否泰进退，如此而已矣。虽有火候，不过清心寡欲，主静内观，使真气运行不息而已；虽有进退升降，不过以真水常升，真火常降而已；纵道沐浴，亦不过惩忿窒欲，涤虑洗心，令太和在抱而已；虽有得药成丹，亦不过以神为父，以气为母，两两扭结一团，融通无间，生出天地生我之初一点真灵，即所谓离宫之真精，又谓人身之真汞；以我神气炼此一个真汞，结胎成婴，日后生出阳神，官骸血脉，五脏六腑，毛发肌肤，灵明知觉，无一件不与人肖，分之可化为万身，合之仍归于一气，要皆自神父气母两两交媾而煅出这个真汞之精，以为阳神者也。然此真汞，须有生发之候，盖心为五脏之中气，中气一升，五脏之气随升，中气一降，五脏之气随降，其生也由于真汞之动，其息也由于真汞之静。要之，动静升降，皆属自然之道，惟顺其自然之运用可矣。但此步工法，自古神仙少有从此一著下手者。盖以清静之道，听其自然，顺之不逆，非上等根器不能，且亦见效最迟，不若阴阳而补为较易也。何谓阴阳而补？必先识得太极开基，先天一阳发生，然后将我这点真阳之气，投入丹田之中，犹父母交媾，精血合做一团，入于胞胎之内，此为先天真种，种在乾家交感宫，日运铅汞，渐生渐长，他日出胎，方成脱壳神仙。若无此个真种，是空炼也，虽有所得，亦不过保固色身，不能生出法象也。知之否？有此一点真阳之气，入于胞胎，然后加以神光下照，久之真阳有动机，不妨将坎中之水，引之上升，离宫之火，导之下降，直将色身所有阴滓尸气炼化，只取得一味真气，配我灵阳，合而为丹，养之为神，可以飞升变化，然此亦自然之道也。凡人落在后天，神气多耗，年华又老，犹走路之人，离家已远，不得不从远处回来，所以必要费力也。夫以神气两分，不能合而为一，日间打坐，必用一点意思，几分气力，将我神气两两入于丹田之中，不许一丝外走，一息出，一息入，我惟顺其呼吸之息，自一而十，自十而百，而千而万，在所不拘。如此紧闭六门，存神丹扃，作一阵，然后外息暂停，真息始动。我于此又温养一阵，然后真阳之气，蓬蓬勃勃，真如风涌云腾一般，我急忙开关，引之上升，其升也，以神不以气，但须凝神了照尾闾一路之上足矣。到得真气冲冲，温养片刻，然后下降。总之，真阳初动，必须用点气力，然后可升可降。盖以凡身浊气太重，必十分鼓荡，乃能祛其尘垢，而后有清清白白之神气，为我炼成丹本。所以古人云：「始而采药，非用武火猛烹急炼，则真金不能出矿」，此武火所以名为野战也。至于升降已毕，丹田气满，心神安泰，然后以炼虚之法，顺其气机而为之足矣。此虽勉强，亦是自然当如此勉强者，生须照此行持可也。

If we survey the ancients' paths of cultivating the Way as a whole, they can broadly be divided into two kinds of method: one is "cultivating through pure stillness," the other "replenishing through yin and yang" (here meaning the reverse cultivation and replenishment of spirit and Breath, water and fire, yin and yang within the body).

First, cultivating through pure stillness: this points directly at the step of "refining emptiness," without needing to go step by step through the deliberate procedure of refining Essence into Breath and Breath into Spirit. But this is by no means within reach of ordinary capacity; one must have the highest capacity even to speak of it. If a person's root truly is extraordinary and they set to work directly from the single step of refining emptiness through pure stillness, the whole way accords with the natural Way of heaven and earth, unlike the method I am teaching you now, which still involves a good deal of deliberate work.

For the human body already contains in itself the complete mechanism of yin and yang, Kan and Li, the opening and closing of Qian and Kun, and the rising and descending, advancing and withdrawing of sun and moon, water and fire. Like the movements of the heavenly bodies, it all happens of itself; there is no need at all for a person to push it around with their head. You need only set the Original Spirit upright and steady, letting it dwell in a pure state without knowing or noticing, without thinking or worrying, and the clear, empty Original Breath in the body will naturally flow vast and mighty, with each inhalation and exhalation going up and down, back and forth: Qian and Kun open and close naturally, sun and moon revolve naturally, water and fire rise and descend naturally, yin and yang wax and wane, advance and withdraw naturally. That is all there is to it!

On this path, even when one speaks of fire phasing, it is no more than clearing the mind and lessening desire, centering on stillness and contemplating inwardly, letting the true Breath circulate unceasingly of itself. Even when one speaks of advancing and withdrawing, rising and descending, it is no more than the true water naturally rising and the true fire naturally descending. Even when one speaks of ablution, it is no more than curbing anger and restraining desire, washing away stray worries, so that one always embraces the Breath of supreme harmony. Even when one speaks of obtaining the Medicine and forming the elixir, it is no more than taking Spirit as father and Breath as mother, the two fusing into one undivided whole without the slightest separation, from which is born that single spark of true numinosity from the moment heaven and earth first gave me life: the true Essence of the Li palace, the true Mercury of the human body. Refining this spark of true Mercury warmly and for a long time with one's own spirit and Breath, one forms a numinous embryo (the Holy Embryo), and in time the yang spirit (the spontaneous Dharma Body) is brought forth complete, its whole being of numinous clarity and awareness just like a real person; divided, it transforms in a thousand ways, and united, it returns to a single true Breath. In the final analysis, all of this comes from Spirit the father and Breath the mother naturally joining and tempering this spark of the Essence of true Mercury.

And this spark of true Mercury also has its own natural season of arising. The heart's Breath is the central, pivotal Breath of the five viscera: when the central Breath rises, the Breath of the five viscera rises with it; when the central Breath descends, the Breath of the five viscera descends with it. Its arising comes from the natural motion of true Mercury, and its subsiding from the natural stillness of true Mercury. In short, motion and stillness, rising and descending all belong to the natural Way; one need only follow its natural workings.

Yet since ancient times, very few cultivators have been able to set to work directly from the step of "refining emptiness through pure stillness." Why? Because the way of pure stillness relies entirely on letting things take their natural course, following without going against them; without the very highest capacity one cannot hold steady in it, and its results come the slowest of all. It is not as close at hand and easy to begin as the path of replenishing through yin and yang.

What, then, is "replenishing through yin and yang"? It first requires you to recognize clearly the moment when "the Supreme Ultimate lays its foundation," when the single yang of Prior Heaven first stirs (the Living Zi Hour), and then to gather this newly born spark of true yang Breath steadily back into the lower Cinnabar Field. Just as when the parents conceive, Essence and blood combine into one whole and settle within the womb, this is the "true seed of Prior Heaven," planted in the central palace of interaction within the body. Day after day one warmly nurtures the Lead and Mercury with spirit and Breath, letting it gradually grow, until in time, as a ripe melon falls from its stalk, one can truly leave the ordinary and enter sagehood. Without this true seed of Prior Heaven it is all refining in vain; even if there is some effect, at most it barely preserves the ordinary embryo of the physical body and cannot give rise to numinous, wondrous Dharma forms. Have you understood this point?

Once this spark of true yang, the true seed, has fallen into the womb of the Cinnabar Field, next let the pure light of the spirit shine steadily down upon it and nurture it warmly. Over time, when the true yang finds its moment to stir vigorously, one can follow its momentum, leading the true water in Kan to rise and guiding the true fire of the Li palace to descend (the conjunction of Kan and Li), so as to refine away completely the yin turbidity and dross accumulated in the physical body, extracting only a single, supremely clear true Breath, which, combined with my own numinous, luminous true yang, is refined together into the elixir and warmly nurtured into spirit. In the end this, too, returns to the natural Way.

It is just that ordinary people, having long fallen into Posterior Heaven, have dissipated too much of their spirit and Breath and are getting on in years. They are like someone who has set out on a long journey and is already too far from home, who has no choice but to spend some effort walking back step by step; that is why at first a certain degree of effort is indispensable. Since spirit and Breath are now separated in two places and cannot come together, during daily quiet sitting one must first borrow a little focused True Intention and a measure of gentle effort to settle spirit and Breath together back in the Cinnabar Field, not letting the slightest bit run off and scatter outward. With each inhalation and exhalation, I simply follow the breath quietly and count it (regulating the breath), from one to ten, from ten to a hundred, even to a thousand or ten thousand, without being bound to a fixed number.

In this way, close the six gates of eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body and mind cleanly, keep the spirit concentrated at the gate of the Cinnabar Field, and hold steady in quiet for a good while. When the outer breath of mouth and nose (ordinary breathing) gradually becomes fine and pauses, the inner True Breath naturally begins to stir. At this point, nurture warmly and quietly for a while longer, and the true yang Breath in the body will surge up vigorously, truly like wind rising and clouds billowing. At this moment, follow its momentum and open the passes, leading it up along the spine. But be sure to remember: the rising relies on the light illumination of the spirit and intention, never on forcing it up with coarse breath. Simply concentrate the spirit and illuminate clearly the way up from the Caudal Funnel, and that is enough! When the true Breath is full and surging, nurture it warmly for a moment, then let it descend naturally along the front back to the Cinnabar Field.

To sum up: when the true yang first stirs, one must borrow a moderate amount of effort to move it smoothly up and down. Because the yin turbidity and stagnant Breath of an ordinary person's body are too heavy, they must be thoroughly stirred up before the accumulated dross of years can be washed clean, leaving a clear, pure spirit and Breath as the foundation for forming the elixir. That is why the ancients said: "At the beginning of gathering the Medicine, without fierce cooking and rapid refining by martial fire, the true gold cannot be extracted from the ore." This is exactly why the alchemical classics call the martial fire "battle in the field." Once this round of rising and descending is complete, the true Breath in the Cinnabar Field is full, and the heart and spirit are thoroughly at peace, then turn back to the method of "refining emptiness through pure stillness" described above, and simply nurture warmly, following the natural movement of the Breath; that is enough! This first step of deliberate work may look a little forced on the surface, but for ordinary people who have dissipated themselves in Posterior Heaven, it is precisely a necessary forcing that accords with the laws of nature. All you disciples must practice steadily in exactly this order.

Key Terms

  • A small country with few people (an image of cultivation for those who are old and whose Essence has waned): Huang Yuanji turns this whole chapter into an alchemical guide for the elderly and those with waning Essence. The Golden Elixir lies in the One Breath of Prior Heaven, not in the blood and Breath of Posterior Heaven, so old and young alike can succeed.
  • Cultivating through pure stillness vs. replenishing through yin and yang: the central distinction of this chapter. The former points directly at "refining emptiness," relying purely on naturalness, but requires the highest capacity and is slow to show results; the latter uses counting breaths with one's own inner Breath, the light of the spirit shining downward, the rising and descending of Kan and Li, and the martial fire to drive out turbidity, first acting deliberately and then returning to non-action. It suits ordinary people dissipated by Posterior Heaven better.
  • Restraining the outside to nourish the center: gathering in the outward running of the six gates (hands and feet, ears and eyes, mouth and heart) so as to nurture the true Breath of the central palace and the Original Spirit.
  • Opening the passes and "by the spirit, not by breath": the key to following the momentum when the true yang surges, leading the Breath up from the Caudal Funnel. Huang Yuanji sternly warns that it relies entirely on the light illumination of pure spirit and intention; never force it through with brute breath.
  • Battle in the field (martial fire) and guarding the city (civil fire): when first gathering the true yang and driving out the turbid Breath of the ordinary body, one must borrow focused breath-counting and a moderate degree of effort to stir it up; this is called martial fire or battle in the field. Once the Breath is full and the spirit at peace, one turns to empty, still, warm nurturing, called civil fire or guarding the city, and refining emptiness.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Do not sentence yourself to failure with "I'm too old, my constitution is too poor." Through "a small country with few people," Huang Yuanji tells us plainly that cultivating body and mind relies on gathering the mind back into stillness and reconnecting with the vitality of Prior Heaven. As long as you are willing to reduce outward drain from today on (restraining the outside to nourish the center), it is never too late to begin.
  2. Honestly admit that you are an ordinary person "already far from home." Constantly talking big about "I just need pure stillness and non-action, I don't need to practice anything" is often laziness dressed up as lofty vision. Start with the most down-to-earth practice of gathering in the senses and quietly counting breaths; once the heart and spirit are truly steady, you will naturally enter non-action.
  3. Keep firmly in mind the five words "by the spirit, not by breath." Whenever you practice quiet sitting or breathing, your attention should only illuminate lightly, like a soft lamplight; never grit your teeth and hold your breath, or use brute force to push and press at some place in the body.
  4. True "never visiting each other" means the mind not stirring before objects, not hiding in the deep mountains. Even in the middle of a bustling city, where "the sounds of cocks and dogs are heard," as long as your eyes see and your ears hear without being drawn by sounds and colors into craving, anger or comparison, you are right now in the pure peach-blossom haven of the small country with few people.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Honest greatness and abundant fulfillment" (dun da cheng yu): From the Book of Documents, "Announcement Concerning Luo": "Be honest and great and bring about abundance, and you will forever have renown," meaning to be generous and broad and to achieve an abundant, flourishing state.
  2. "Ten years and three months": Probably a transposition in printing of the alchemical idiom "ten months and three years" (ten months of gestation, three years of nursing). The original wording is provisionally kept, and the rendering takes both readings into account.
  3. "Restraining the outside to nourish the center": From the preface to the "Four Admonitions" of Cheng Yi of the Northern Song: "It proceeds from within and responds to the outside; restraining the outside is the means of nourishing the within."
  4. "In stillness always illuminating, in illumination always still": A famous saying on the unity of concentration and wisdom valued by both Chan Buddhism and internal alchemy, meaning to keep clear, awake awareness in utter stillness, and in awareness not to lose unmoving stillness.
  5. "Zhun in the morning, Meng in the evening": A term of alchemical fire phasing, using the images of the hexagrams Zhun and Meng of the Book of Changes as a figure for the rules of advancing and withdrawing when raising and lowering and running the fire each morning and evening.
  6. "Planted in the palace of interaction of the house of Qian": An alchemical idiom, meaning to settle the newly born single yang true Breath of Prior Heaven steadily in the central palace, the Cinnabar Field.
  7. Textual note: 媚我君玉 is corrected to 媚我君王; 朝延 to 朝廷; 沦肌浹髓 is normalized to 沦肌浃髓; 惊大成裕 is corrected to 惇大成裕 following the Book of Documents; the 永 in 铅永减少 and in 由于真永之动 is in both places corrected to 汞; 制外乔中 to 制外养中; 舌神内蘊 is normalized to 舌神内蕴; 叙而常照，照而常叙 is corrected to 寂而常照，照而常寂; 舟與 to 舟舆; 荆辣生焉 to 荆棘生焉; 气枸物蔽 to 气拘物蔽; 无见无阐 to 无见无闻; and 上等根嚣 to 上等根器.

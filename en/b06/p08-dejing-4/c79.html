Chapter 79 · Always on the Side of the Good

Only by holding oneself to account, like holding the left half of a tally without pressing claims on others, can resentment be dissolved at the root; inner cultivation begins with guarding the center and is completed in embryonic breathing, refining a pure, true and unified Breath out of the Essence and Breath of the ordinary body to form the Holy Embryo

About This Chapter

Chapter 79 of the Daodejing speaks of how "reconciling a great resentment always leaves some resentment behind," of "holding the left half of the tally without making demands on others," and of how "the Way of Heaven has no favorites; it is always on the side of the good." Huang Yuanji first shows, from the standpoint of moral self-examination, that "simply cultivating oneself without being harsh on others" is the fundamental way to dissolve all grievances; then, in §3, he turns straight to the most profound practical essentials of internal alchemy: guarding the center and embryonic breathing (True Breath):

  1. Settling things outwardly is not as good as dissolving them inwardly; keep the tally, do not collect the levy (§2): If you force a reconciliation of a great resentment while still intent on blaming the other, you may make up on the surface, yet lingering bitterness remains in the hidden recesses of the heart. Only when, like someone who holds the "left half of the tally" yet never uses it to call in debts, you put your whole focus on turning back to reproach yourself, curbing anger and restraining desire, does your virtue become complete, and the Way of Heaven naturally always protects the good.
  2. Beginning with guarding the center, completed in embryonic breathing (§3, first half): The sage cleanses the mind and withdraws into the hidden depths, setting aside all outer judgments of right and wrong, like and dislike. The work starts from guarding the center and embracing the One and is finally completed in "embryonic breathing." Huang Yuanji strenuously dispels a misunderstanding: embryonic breathing is by no means the outer air passing in and out of mouth and nose (ordinary breathing), but the vitality of Prior Heaven that, at the moment ordinary breathing comes naturally to rest, opens and closes in the Cinnabar Field, where true yin and true yang, Original Spirit and Original Breath, have melted into one.
  3. The three transmutations of reverse cultivation and "the embryo forms within the hidden Breath" (§3, second half): Generation in the forward direction runs "from principle comes Breath, from Breath comes form"; return by reversal transforms form into Breath, transforms Breath into Spirit and returns to emptiness (the three transmutations). One cannot do without the Essence and Breath of the Posterior-Heaven body as raw material, yet one must never take the coarse, heavy ordinary Essence for the immortal elixir. Only by burning and refining it a thousand and ten thousand times to extract a pure, dustless, true and unified Breath can one, as the Classic of Embryonic Breathing says, have "the embryo form within the hidden Breath, and the Breath rest within the embryo," Spirit and Breath cleaving to each other like mother and child, husband and wife, gathered and never scattered.

Safety note: §3 of this chapter explains in detail the principles of advanced alchemical methods such as "ordinary breathing stops and embryonic breathing stirs" and "when the form is complete after ten months, one sheds the shell and the spirit goes out." Please take careful note: embryonic breathing must never be imitated by deliberately holding or suppressing the breath (forcibly stopping breathing). Forced breath-holding can easily cause oxygen deprivation, dizziness, chest tightness or heart-rhythm disturbances. The "stopping of ordinary breathing" in the text refers to the phenomenon of breathing naturally becoming extremely fine and long in a state of deep relaxation and stillness. In daily practice, simply guard the center and embrace the One gently and let the breath follow its natural course; never chase any visions of "shedding the shell and sending out the spirit."

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：和大怨，必有余怨，安可以为善？是以圣人执左契，而不责于人。故有德司契，无德司彻。天道无亲，常与善人。

Lord Lao says: When a deep resentment has already formed and you then force a reconciliation, some resentment is bound to linger in the heart; how can that count as complete and proper? Therefore the sage is like someone who holds the creditor's proof (the left half of the tally) yet never goes to others to press their claims or find fault. So the person of virtue simply keeps to their own duty and good faith, as if keeping the left half of the tally, while the person without virtue keeps an eye on others at every turn to pick holes and assign blame, as if a tax official auditing the levy (si che). The Way of Heaven itself has no partiality or favorites, yet it always naturally protects and draws near to those who truly do good and cultivate virtue.

§2
Original (Chinese)

修身之道，惟善为宝。为善之道，自治为先。盖道在内而不在外，修在己而不在人。惟事事内观，时时返照，过则改之，善则加勉，庶明善诚身，永为天地之肖子，圣贤之完人，而不至有所缺矣。是见为善者，只问己之修省，不问人之从违。如责人而不自责，观外而不观内，虽一时小忿，积而至于大怨，纵能十分解散而不至于成仇，然内无返躬自责之道、惩忿窒欲之功，虽能解之于外，而不能释之于隐微，安能清净无尘，潇洒自乐，而复乎本然至善之天也哉？故「和大怨必有余怨，安可以为善」？惟圣人，持身接物，处己待人，一以修己为主，而人之是非好恶，概不计较。譬如合同契约，分左右而执之，永以为凭，明尔无我虞、我无尔诈之意。圣人执德如执左契，只修诸己，不责诸人，此所以与天地同其大也，是谓「有德者司契」。无德之人，重外轻内，常以察察为明，而人之恩怨必较，此为「无德者司彻」。夫司彻者，以考过为事，全不自省，而民弗从，何如司契者，责己重，责人轻，而人无不相予以信。可知责人者轻己，己之善难完；责己者轻人，己之善克复也。人底于至善，而天心眷顾，自亿万年而不朽。《书》曰：「皇天无亲，惟德是辅」，即太上「天道无亲，常与善人」之谓欤？

In the way of cultivating oneself, only "goodness" is the most precious treasure; and in the way of doing good, the first priority is to govern oneself. For the Great Way lies within one's own heart and not outside the body; cultivation relies on correcting oneself, not on remaking others. Only by contemplating inwardly in every matter and turning the light back on one's heart at every moment, correcting faults at once and redoubling one's effort upon seeing goodness, can one truly understand goodness and make oneself sincere, forever a worthy child of heaven and earth and a complete person in the eyes of the sages and worthies, leaving behind no deficiency or regret.

From this we see that one who truly does good asks only whether their own self-examination is sufficient, not whether others comply. If you only know how to blame others and never turn back to examine yourself, if you watch only the outside and never look into your own heart, then even what begins as a little irritation will, accumulating day by day, brew into deep hatred and great resentment. Even if afterward an outside party mediates, and on the surface the conflict is fully untangled and never turns into mortal enmity, still, since the heart lacks the breadth to turn back and reproach itself and lacks the work of curbing anger and restraining desire, the resentment, though resolved on the surface, cannot be dissolved in the most hidden knot at the bottom of the heart. How then could the mind become pure and dustless, free and joyful in itself, and return to its original nature of utmost goodness? That is why it says: "Reconciling a great resentment always leaves some resentment behind; how can that count as good?"

Only the sage, whether cultivating themselves or dealing with people and things, puts "cultivating oneself well" first in everything, and does not haggle at all over others' gossip, judgments of right and wrong, likes and dislikes. It is like drawing up a contract in ancient times: the wooden tally was split in two, and each party held the left or right half as proof, signifying the good faith of "you need not guard against me, and I will never deceive you." The sage holds to virtue just as one holds the "left half of the tally," using it only to restrain and cultivate themselves, never taking it to others to call in debts or find fault. This is exactly why the sage can be as vast as heaven and earth, and it is what the classic means by "the person of virtue keeps the tally."

By contrast, the person without virtue values outer calculation and neglects the cultivation of the heart. All day long they take hair-splitting scrutiny for shrewdness, haggling over every grievance and favor in human relations, never satisfied until they have paid them back. This is called "the person without virtue collects the levy" (like a harsh official who does nothing but audit accounts and extort taxes). Those who spend all day scrutinizing and blaming others for their faults without examining themselves in the least can never win the people's hearts. How could they compare with the noble person who keeps the left half of the tally, strict with themselves and generous to others, so that everyone naturally gives them their trust? From this we know: those who love to blame others in fact slight themselves, and their own goodness will never be complete; those who know how to examine themselves strictly and treat others with tolerance are able instead to restore their own original goodness fully. When a person's cultivation reaches utmost goodness, Heaven naturally looks after and protects them, and their virtue, work and spirit only grow firmer with time. The Book of Documents says, "August Heaven has no favorites; it aids only virtue." Is this not the very same meaning as Lord Lao's "the Way of Heaven has no favorites; it is always on the side of the good"?

§3
Original (Chinese)

圣人之学，惟洗心退藏于密，以外之善恶好丑、是非从违，一概不计，所以汰虑沉思，凝神默照，以至于心明性见，欲净理纯，上与天合德，历万古而不磨。其功始于守中，其成由于胎息，人亦知之乎？古人言胎息，学人莫看是外气，是凡息停时，那丹田中真阴真阳、元神元气，融会一团，混成一气，氤氤氲氲，蓬蓬勃勃，若开若阖，若有若无，视不见，听不闻，想象之而有迹，恍惚之而有形者，此殆人生之始气，心得之而有体，性得之而有用，人非此气不能生，欲成上品之仙，亦离不得此气为之主。古云：「人生之始，因理有气，因气有形」，此天地生人之顺道也。返还逆修者，实从形形色色中，慢慢的运起阳火阴符，收归五明宫内，而以太乙和气、天然神火烹之，即可化形而为一气，又由此气一炼，即可化气成神，于此固守虚无，保养灵阳，即还于无极之初，可以出则成形，入则无迹。道有何异于人哉？总之，此个胎息，即返到父母媾精、一团气血之候。人能养此胎息，日夜以无为有为、无思有思之真意，保守之，团聚之，即结成灵胎，而为元神。迨至十月形全，脱壳而出，上透顶门，直冲霄汉，可以骖鸾鹤，上云霄，遨游天外，飞升玉京，直顷刻间事耳。然此胎息，虽从凡人色身中炼出，却又不是凡精凡气凡神结成。炼丹者，虽离不得后天有形有色之精气以为之本，却亦不全仗于此也。盖后天精气，皆有形质，便有气数，生死轮回，势所不免，又况粗精粗气，尽属蠢钝之物，乌能有灵？要不过借此凡色身中所有之顽物，千烧万炼，取出那一点清净无尘、至灵至神之精气神，以为真一之气，而返之于我，以成仙胎神丹耳。所谓抽铅添汞之说，不过如此。其余著形著色，皆非道之正宗。古人云：「胎从伏气中结，气从有胎中息。」是知欲结神丹，成就不老之躯，非养胎息不能；欲得胎息凝结于虚无丹田中，非结得有胎，他亦不肯来归，而纯纯乎动静与俱。若有一点凡气夹杂，凡神外驰，则神必外游，气必外泄，不能如子母夫妇，聚而不散也，知否？

The learning of the sage comes down, in the end, to what the Book of Changes calls "cleansing the mind and withdrawing into the hidden depths": washing the mind perfectly clean and withdrawing into the deepest, subtlest place of stillness and concentration, not reckoning with or clinging to any outer good and evil, beauty and ugliness, right and wrong, praise and blame. Precisely because of this, one can wash away stray thoughts and drifting ideas, concentrate the spirit and contemplate in silence, cultivating all the way to the point where the mind is clear and nature is seen, selfish desires are cleansed and heavenly principle is pure and whole, one's virtue matching that of Heaven above, enduring through ten thousand ages without wearing away. And this whole work of inner cultivation begins with "guarding the center and embracing the One" and is completed in "embryonic breathing" (True Breath). Does the world know this?

When the ancients speak of "embryonic breathing," students must never mistake it for the outer air going in and out of nose and mouth (ordinary breathing)! True embryonic breathing is this: in utter stillness, when the ordinary breathing of mouth and nose has naturally come to rest and grown so fine as to be almost imperceptible, true yin and true yang, Original Spirit and Original Breath, merge in the lower Cinnabar Field into one undivided whole, becoming a single Breath of Prior Heaven. It is dense and swirling, vital and billowing, as if opening and closing, as if there and not there. The eye cannot see it and the ear cannot hear it, yet when the mind senses it subtly it seems to leave real traces, faintly taking shape within the dim and obscure. This is the very primal true Breath of the beginning of life (the One Breath of Prior Heaven): the mind, obtaining it, gains its true substance and support; nature, obtaining it, gains its real and wondrous function. Without this true Breath a person cannot live, and to accomplish the highest grade of the Great Way one likewise cannot do without this true Breath as master.

The ancients said: "At the beginning of life, from principle there comes Breath, and from Breath there comes form." This is the forward way by which heaven and earth bring forth human beings. The return of internal alchemy by reversal, however, goes back the other way. From within this body of forms and colors, one slowly applies the fire phasing of yang fire and yin tally, gathering the scattered spirit and Breath back into the central palace (the Palace of Fivefold Brightness), and cooking them gently with the harmonious Original Breath of the Supreme One and the natural, pure fire of the spirit. Thus one can first "transform form into one Breath," then further refine this true Breath so as to "transform Breath into Spirit" (the three transmutations). After that, by steadily guarding emptiness and carefully preserving this single spark of numinous, luminous true yang, one can return completely to the primal beginning of the Ultimateless, reaching the free and wondrous state of "coming out, it takes form; going in, it leaves no trace." The Great Way is hidden within every person; how could it be some heterodox, bizarre art that defies common principle?

In short, this "embryonic breathing" means returning one's state of life to the moment when the parents first conceived, to the embryo that was one undivided whole of Original Breath of Prior Heaven. If a practitioner can nurture this embryonic breathing continuously and finely, guarding it carefully day and night and gathering it together with a True Intention that neither clings to deliberate action nor sinks into dull thoughtlessness, then over time a numinous embryo (the Holy Embryo) forms and nurtures the Original Spirit to fullness. The alchemical classics describe it in almost mythical language: when "after ten months the form is complete" and the fire phasing is fulfilled, one can, as if breaking out of a shell, pass up through the crown of the head (sending out the spirit), soar straight into the clouds and roam the Great Void. That is the natural manifestation once the work is ripe, as a melon falls from its stalk.

But one point must be distinguished with perfect clarity: although this embryonic breathing is refined out of an ordinary person's physical body, it is by no means formed directly by kneading together the ordinary Essence, Breath and Spirit of Posterior Heaven. Those who refine the elixir cannot do without borrowing the Essence and Breath of Posterior Heaven, with their form and color, as the raw material to begin with, yet they must never rely entirely on these themselves. For all Essence and Breath of Posterior Heaven have the limitations of form and substance, and whatever has form and substance cannot escape the cosmic reckoning of formation, abiding, decay and emptiness, nor the cycle of birth and death. Moreover, what spiritual quality could that coarse, turbid, dull ordinary Essence and ordinary Breath possibly have in themselves? Refining the elixir is simply borrowing the crude "raw stuff" already present in this body of flesh, burning and refining it a thousand and ten thousand times, purifying and extracting the single spark of pure, dustless, supremely numinous and spiritual Essence, Breath and Spirit of Prior Heaven hidden within, combining it into "the true and unified Breath," and returning it to the original heart, thereby accomplishing the immortal embryo and the divine elixir. What the ancients called "drawing out Lead and adding Mercury" (Lead and Mercury) is no more than this; all other side paths that cling to the forms and colors of the physical body are not the orthodox lineage of the Great Way.

The ancient Classic of Embryonic Breathing says: "The embryo forms within the hidden Breath; the Breath rests within the embryo that has formed." Clearly, if you want to form the divine elixir and build the foundation of an unaging body, you must nurture embryonic breathing; and if you want this True Breath to settle obediently in the empty Cinnabar Field without wandering off, then unless the true Breath is deeply hidden and the numinous embryo has begun to form, it will not be willing to return and stay at peace, accompanying you in motion and stillness from start to finish. As long as even a trace of the agitation of ordinary Posterior-Heaven Breath is mixed in, or the ordinary spirit of Posterior Heaven (the cognitive spirit) still chases outward, the spirit is bound to wander off and the Breath is bound to leak out after it, and they can never cleave to each other closely like mother and child, husband and wife, gathered and never scattered. Have you understood this crucial point?

Key Terms

  • Keeping the tally and collecting the levy: "Keeping the tally" (si qi) means holding the proof yet using it only to restrain oneself, making no harsh demands on others; "collecting the levy" (si che) originally referred to the official in charge of assessing taxes, and here stands for the virtueless attitude of hair-splitting scrutiny that only blames others for their faults.
  • Guarding the center and embracing the One and True Breath (embryonic breathing): Huang Yuanji sums up the whole course of the sages' inner cultivation as "the work begins with guarding the center and is completed in embryonic breathing." Embryonic breathing is not the outer air of mouth and nose (ordinary breathing) but the inner True Breath of the Original Spirit and Original Breath of Prior Heaven, opening and closing densely in the Cinnabar Field.
  • The three transmutations (transforming form into one Breath, transforming Breath into Spirit, returning to the Ultimateless): the reverse sequence of return corresponding to the way heaven and earth bring forth people, "from principle comes Breath, from Breath comes form."
  • The embryo forms within the hidden Breath; the Breath rests within the embryo: quoted from the Classic of Embryonic Breathing. When the true Breath is deeply hidden and still, the True Intention and the numinous embryo condense; once the numinous embryo is stable, ordinary breathing that chases outward rests of itself and the True Breath is always present.

Putting It into Practice

  1. To resolve accumulated grievances with others, first tear up the "account book" in your heart. If you say "never mind, we've made up," while inwardly still tallying item by item how much the other owes you (collecting the levy), the lingering resentment will never disperse. Bring your energy back to cultivating yourself, as if holding the left half of the tally, and only then can the knot in your heart truly loosen.
  2. "Cleansing the mind and withdrawing into the hidden depths" is the best psychological protection. When judgments of right and wrong, beauty and ugliness are flying around you, there is no need to rush to rebut and justify yourself point by point. First draw your attention back to the quiet inner center (guarding the center); once your emotions are steady, right and wrong will naturally become clear.
  3. When practicing breathing, first understand that "True Breath is not holding the breath." When regulating the breath in quiet sitting, never deliberately hold the breath to imitate "embryonic breathing." As long as stray thoughts are few and the body is relaxed, the breath will naturally and gradually become light, fine, even and long; it is enough to sense a warm, moist opening and closing in the lower belly.
  4. With less "ordinary spirit chasing outward," the Breath naturally stays gathered. The moment you are distracted into chasing outer scenes or scrolling through emotions, the spirit scatters and the Breath leaks. Setting aside a period each day of solitude with no phone at all and no judging of right and wrong is the most practical way of "guarding the center and nourishing the Breath."

Translator's Notes

  1. "August Heaven has no favorites; it aids only virtue": From the Book of Documents ("Charge to Cai Zhong"), also quoted from the "Book of Zhou" in the Zuo Commentary (Duke Xi, year 5).
  2. "Cleansing the mind and withdrawing into the hidden depths": From the Book of Changes, "Great Commentary," Part I: "With this the sage cleanses the mind and withdraws into the hidden depths."
  3. "The embryo forms within the hidden Breath; the Breath rests within the embryo that has formed": The opening line of the famous short Tang-dynasty internal-alchemy scripture Gaoshang Yuhuang taixi jing (The Most High Jade Emperor's Classic of Embryonic Breathing, known for short as the Classic of Embryonic Breathing).
  4. "Palace of Fivefold Brightness" (wuming gong): An occasional name in Daoist texts for the Cinnabar Field or central palace, meaning the empty realm of the central palace where the Essence and Breath of the five viscera gather toward the Origin, bright and fully transparent.
  5. Textual note: In the title, the 一 in 第七十九章 一 常与善人 has been corrected to a middle dot; the 日 in 太上日 in the classic text and in 《书》日 in the commentary has in each case been corrected to 曰; the 已 in 修在已, 只修请已, 责已重, and 责人者轻已，已之善难完；责已者轻人，已之善克复也 has in each case been corrected to 己, and 请 to 诸; 惩忿室欲 is corrected to 惩忿窒欲; 潇酒自乐 to 潇洒自乐; 相子以信 to 相予以信; 听不闸 to 听不闻; a superfluous quotation mark after 因气有形 has been deleted; 太乙租气 is corrected to 太乙和气 on the basis of the character shape and sense; 异于人散 to 异于人哉; 邀游天外 to 遨游天外; 所有之颜物 to 所有之顽物 on the basis of the preceding 蠢钝之物 and the character shape; and 几神外驰 to 凡神外驰.

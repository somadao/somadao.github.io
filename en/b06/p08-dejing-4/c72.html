Chapter 72 · When the People Do Not Fear Authority

Only by revering heavenly principle and knowing oneself without showing off can one dwell in peace and live long; the most crucial point of inner cultivation is to distinguish the formless Prior-Heaven Original Spirit from the traceable Posterior-Heaven cognitive spirit

About This Chapter

On the surface this chapter discusses the principles of conduct and governance: "fearing Heaven's authority, broadening one's dwelling, cherishing one's life." In its second half, however, Huang Yuanji cuts straight into the most fundamental watershed of internal alchemy, the distinction between the Original Spirit and the cognitive spirit:

  1. Revering Heaven, watching oneself when alone, knowing oneself and cherishing oneself (§2): The majesty of heavenly principle lies in what is hidden and subtle. If people do not know reverence and vigilance in solitude, and abandon the peaceful dwelling of humaneness and rightness, they will surely bring disaster on themselves. The sage likewise "knows himself" and "cherishes himself," but never, as ordinary people do, "shows off as soon as he knows, and prizes himself as soon as he cherishes"; instead he holds fast inwardly, removing desire and preserving principle.
  2. The different sources of the Original Spirit and the cognitive spirit (§3, first half): At the first moment when the parents' essences unite and an embryo forms, there is only an undivided mass of supremely harmonious breath, without knowledge or consciousness yet capable of endless creation; this is the Prior-Heaven Original Spirit bestowed by Heaven and hidden in the Cave of Emptiness. Only when the ten months of gestation are complete and the baby is born with a first cry do the thinking and scheming mind, which arises in dependence on flesh, blood and the five sense organs, appear: this is the Posterior-Heaven cognitive spirit.
  3. Transforming the cognitive into the Original, turning yin into yang (§3, second half): After birth the Original and the cognitive spirit are intermingled. When the Original Spirit is in charge, joy, anger, sorrow and delight all accord with heavenly principle, and the cognitive spirit too is transformed into the Original Spirit; when the cognitive spirit is in charge, even humaneness, rightness, propriety and wisdom degenerate into private favors and private grudges, and the Original Spirit is buried. The starting point of practice lies in recognizing, within a still state free of knowing and perceiving, that single thought of pure heavenly principle in which the Mysterious Pass first stirs, rectifying the root and clearing the source, so that over time the yin, self-serving cognitive spirit is thoroughly transformed into the pure Original Spirit.

Safety note: §3 of this chapter speaks of "making sure the outer and inner Three Treasures are sealed" and of "seeking the single opening of the Mysterious Pass at the moment of no knowing and no perceiving." "Sealing" here means not letting the ears, eyes, mouth, tongue and wandering thoughts run outward and squander the spirit; it does not mean forcibly holding the breath, suppressing thoughts by brute force, or withdrawing from real life. In daily practice keep the breathing smooth and natural and the body and mind relaxed and quiet; if you feel tightness in the chest, breath-holding, or mental strain, relax at once and stop practicing.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：民不畏威，大威至矣。无狭其所居，无厌其所生。夫惟不厌，是以不厌。是以圣人自知不自见，自爱不自贵，故去彼取此。

Lord Lao says: If the people no longer revere even the most basic authority and law, a far greater calamity and punishment is about to descend. Do not keep narrowing the place where you dwell in peace, and do not despise and spoil the vitality you were born with. Precisely because one does not despise or spoil one's own original vitality, life is not despised by heaven and earth and remains long and stable. Therefore the sage knows his own station and nature deeply, yet never shows them off to others; he cherishes his virtue and his life, yet never grows arrogant or prizes himself. Thus the sage discards that path of ostentation and boasting and devotes himself wholly to this path of steady inner cultivation.

§2
Original (Chinese)

所谓威者，纲常名教之大，天理所最难犯者。使知慎独于衾影，畏天威于隐微，自然天锡纯嘏，眉寿无疆。《诗》曰：「畏天之威，于时保之。」若天威俨在咫尺，而戒慎弗懔且明，致令伦常澌灭，礼义消亡，则天良无存，天罚不贷，而凶灾不免，性命难全，是民之不畏威，而大威至矣。若是者，皆由不知仁为安宅，旷安宅而弗居，义以生气，舍生气而自丧也。呜呼！彼民不幸，未生太古之世，以德威惟畏，德明为怀，故愚昧多愆，天显罔顾，而旱干水溢，疫病灾荒，种种祸患兴矣。惟在上者，导以天下之广居，使游心于太和之宇，无狭隘为居而日蹈于危亡也；引以浩然之正气，使直养于清虚之天，无厌弃其生而自罹于断绝也。夫惟自爱其生之理，自保其天之良，而不稍厌斁，即《诗》云：「敬天之怒，无敢戏豫；敬天之渝，无敢驰驱」也。「天监厥德」，「俾尔炽而昌，俾尔寿而臧」，实有与天地同为悠久者焉，是以不厌。非圣人，其孰能之？古帝王，恭己无为，懋昭大德，日就月将，洗心涤虑，精参造化之妙，洞晰本来之天，惟自知之耳。至若德业文章，外之所著，圣人绝不以之表见于人，且朝乾夕惕，重道守身，一息不肯离乎仁，天下无有加于己，其自爱为何如哉？他如名位声华，人之所尊重者，圣人绝不以之足贵。虽圣人自知自爱之端，亦凡人共知共爱之端，特凡人知之而必见之，爱之而必贵之，圣人自知不自见，自爱不自贵，其慎幽独而不敢炫耀于人，重保养而不敢矜尚于世，岂凡人所可同日语乎？夫亦曰去欲取理，尽人合天，以至超凡入圣，绝类离群，而成亿万年不朽之神者，皆由此自知广居之安，自爱长生之乐，一于此，不二于彼，而民自迁善而不知为之耳。舍此，乌能若是哉？

The "authority" spoken of here refers to the solemn laws of the ethical bonds and of the great Way of heavenly principle that must least of all be violated. If a person knows how to examine himself carefully when alone in a dark room, with nothing to be ashamed of before his own shadow and coverlet, and constantly holds reverence for heavenly principle in the subtlest stirrings of the mind, Heaven will naturally bestow pure and abundant blessings and grant a long and lasting life. The Classic of Poetry says: "Revere the authority of Heaven, and so preserve [its mandate]." Conversely, if the majesty of heavenly principle stands right before one's eyes, a mere foot away, yet a person has no caution or vigilance at all and indulges himself until the ethical bonds are utterly gone and propriety and rightness have wholly perished, then, with conscience lost, the laws of the Way of Heaven will grant no pardon; disaster is unavoidable and life is hard to preserve. This is "when the people do not fear authority, a great authority arrives."

The reason one falls to such a state is entirely that one does not know that "humaneness" is the most peaceful dwelling of the human heart, yet leaves this fine peaceful dwelling empty and does not live in it; and does not know that "rightness" is the true road for nourishing the flood-like vital breath, yet insists on abandoning that vitality and bringing ruin upon oneself. Alas! The people of later ages had the misfortune not to be born in the simple age of high antiquity; they did not know how to admonish themselves with the authority of virtue or to keep bright virtue in their hearts, and so, ignorant and full of faults, they disregarded the manifest Way of Heaven, and droughts and floods, plagues and famines, and every kind of calamity followed one after another. Only those in high positions can guide people's hearts into humaneness, the broadest dwelling under heaven, letting the mind and spirit roam freely in the cosmos of supremely harmonious primordial breath, so that they no longer cling to narrowness and slide step by step toward peril and ruin; and only they can enlighten and guide people with the flood-like upright breath, letting them nourish themselves uprightly in a realm pure, empty and unstained, so that they no longer despise and spoil their original vitality and cut off their own path of life.

Precisely because a person knows how to cherish the fundamental principle of life and to preserve the pure, good original heart bestowed by Heaven, without the slightest weariness or slackness, it is just as the Classic of Poetry says: "Revere the anger of Heaven, and dare not play or idle; revere the changes of Heaven, and dare not gallop recklessly." Heaven examines a person's virtue clearly and will "make you flourish and prosper, make you long-lived and good." This vitality can truly endure as long as heaven and earth; this is "only because one does not despise it, is one not despised." Who but the sage can do this?

The sage kings of antiquity rectified themselves and governed through non-action, diligently made their great virtue shine, and, accumulating day by day and month by month, cleansed their hearts and purified their thoughts, penetrated deeply into the mysteries of the creative transformations of heaven and earth, and thoroughly illumined their own original nature. Yet all of this they simply knew clearly in their own hearts (knowing themselves); as for the outwardly visible merits and achievements of government, or literary talent, the sage never used them to show off or display himself before the world (not displaying himself). At the same time, they stayed alert and diligent from morning to night, valued the great Way and guarded body and mind carefully, unwilling to depart from the humane heart for even a moment, holding that nothing under heaven was more precious than preserving this original heart. How deep this "cherishing of oneself" was! As for fame, rank and glittering favor, which the world values most, the sage never treated them as capital worth being proud of or boasting about (not prizing himself).

In fact, the first stirrings of the sage's "knowing himself and cherishing himself" are present in ordinary people too; the only difference is that ordinary people, as soon as they understand a little, rush to show it off, and as soon as they cherish themselves a little, grow arrogant and self-important. The sage, by contrast, knows himself without displaying himself and cherishes himself without prizing himself; when alone he is cautious and attentive and dares not make a show, and in cultivating his nature and life he is earnest and solid and dares not put on airs before the world. How could ordinary worldly people be mentioned in the same breath? In the end, it comes down to removing private desire and following heavenly principle, fulfilling human effort so as to accord with the Way of Heaven, and so transcending the ordinary to enter sagehood, rising above the common crowd and achieving an enduring, imperishable numinous spirit. All of this springs from knowing deeply the peace of the broad dwelling of humane virtue and cherishing the joy of long life in the great Way, holding single-mindedly to this side and never wavering to chase after that side of ostentation. Meanwhile the people, influenced imperceptibly, naturally turn toward the good without even noticing that anyone is deliberately teaching them. Departing from this road, how could one reach such a state?

§3
Original (Chinese)

此言无狭所居，其所居者必大；无厌所生，其所生者必长。虽然，用工之际，元神、识神，不可不知。夫人受气之初，从父母媾精时，结成一点泰珠，此时絪絪缊缊，只有一团太和之气，并无一点知识，然而至神至妙，极奇尽变，作出天下无穷事业出来，都由此一点含灵之气之神，从无知无识而有知有识，从无作无为而有作有为，莫非由此而始。此时天人一理，物我同源，体用兼赅，显微无间，故曰元神，此是天所赋畀。到得血肉之躯既成，十月胎圆，呱地一声，婴儿落生，此时识神始具。夫元神者，先天之元气，天地人物一样，都藏于太虚中，一到人身，则隐伏于人身虚无窟子之内，此是天所赋者。修行人，欲成大道，夫岂可著空著色以求之哉？惟有一无所知，一无所有，扫却一切尘氛，而个中消息自现，灵妙自生。至若识神，乃人身精灵之鬼，劫劫轮回种子。必要五官具备，百骸毓成，将降生落地时，然后这精灵之魂魄，方有依附。古人谓「后天识神，因有形魄而生」者此也。此元神、识神之大分别处也。但有生之后，元、识两神，交合一处，有时元神用事，识神退听，则后天之意气虽动，要皆由仁义礼智而发为喜怒哀乐，识神亦化为元神者此也；有时识神用事，元神隐没不见，虽仁义礼智之见端，亦皆变为私恩私爱、私憎私嫌，元神亦化为识神者此也。总之，为口耳一身起见者，皆是识神。一到识神用事，焉有光明正大、可以对天地、质鬼神的事业出来？惟混混沌沌中，忽焉一感而动，此时天理纯全，毫不挟后天识见，如能稳立脚根，端然行去，即纯乎天理，而无一毫人欲之私。吾故教人于无知无觉时，寻玄关一窍，良以此时，与天地一体，与虚空一致，能从此处把握行将去，则天地之生生，不难自我而为生生，虚空之变化，不难自我而神变化。此时一觉，诚为天地人之根源。修士不从此下手，又从何处以为仙圣之阶哉？要之，无思无虑而出者，元神也；有作为见解，自色身而出者，识神也。元神无形，识神有迹，一自虚无中来，一从色身中出，二者大不相侔。既明得元神生于虚无，识神生于色身，我于是正本清源，务令内外三宝闭塞，不许一知一见从有形有象、有思有虑而出，如此操持，如此涵养，久久尸魄之灵，皆化为清净元神，八万四千毫毛，亦转为护法灵神，所谓「化识为元，转阴成阳」者此也。此在人实力于虚无一边，不要为色身起见著想得矣。

The preceding passage says: if you do not let the dwelling of your heart become narrow, the realm in which you dwell will naturally be wide and vast; if you do not despise and spoil your original vitality, life will naturally extend long. Nevertheless, when it truly comes to the work of internal alchemy, the distinction between the Original Spirit and the cognitive spirit must by no means be left unclear.

When a person first receives the breath of heaven and earth, at the instant the parents unite and conception occurs, a first, perfect numinous pearl is formed. At this time, undivided and swirling, dense and mingling, there is only a mass of rich, supremely harmonious primordial breath, without the slightest Posterior-Heaven discriminating knowledge. Yet it is utterly divine and wondrous, endless in its transformations: all the marvelous creations and boundless deeds of a person's whole future life unfold from this first point of true breath and true spirit containing numinous brightness, from no knowledge and no consciousness to knowledge and consciousness, from no doing and no acting to doing and acting. There is not one thing that does not begin here. At this initial stage the Way of Heaven and human nature are one principle, external things and the self come from one source, substance and function are both complete, and the hidden and the manifest are without separation; therefore it is called the "Original Spirit." This is the original true nature bestowed on us directly by Heaven.

When the body of flesh and blood has grown in the mother's womb and the ten months of pregnancy are complete, with a "wah!" of crying the infant is born, and only then is the Posterior-Heaven "cognitive spirit" formed along with it. Understand that the Original Spirit is that Prior-Heaven primordial breath of Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven (the One Breath of Prior Heaven); heaven, earth, humans and all things are the same in this. Ordinarily it is stored in the Great Void; once it descends into a human body, it lies hidden in the Cave of Emptiness between the heart and the kidneys. It is the true treasure bestowed by Heaven. If practitioners wish to achieve the Great Way, how could they seek it by clinging to dead emptiness or by clinging to the physical body with its forms? Only by emptying body and mind into a state of knowing nothing and having nothing, sweeping away all worldly dust and defilement, will the Prior-Heaven tidings inherent within appear naturally, and numinous, wondrous vitality arise of itself.

As for the cognitive spirit, it is nothing but the shrewd, clever yin awareness attached to the flesh, the seed that draws beings into the cycle of rebirth life after life. Only when the five sense organs are complete, the hundred bones have developed and taken shape, and birth is imminent does this clever ethereal soul and corporeal soul have a shell to attach itself to. The ancients said, "the Posterior-Heaven cognitive spirit is born because of the physical corporeal soul"; this is exactly the principle. This is the most fundamental dividing line between the Original Spirit and the cognitive spirit.

After a person is born, the Original Spirit and the cognitive spirit become intermingled. Sometimes the Original Spirit is master of the house and the cognitive spirit stands aside and obediently follows orders; then, although Posterior-Heaven intentions and emotions also stir, they are all proper joy, anger, sorrow and delight flowing from the humaneness, rightness, propriety and wisdom of the original heart. This is "the cognitive spirit transformed into the Original Spirit." Sometimes, conversely, the cognitive spirit jumps out to be master of the house, and the Original Spirit is obscured and buried without a trace; then even what outwardly looks like the first stirrings of humaneness, rightness, propriety and wisdom has gone bad to the core and turned into private favors and private affections, private hatreds and private dislikes. This is "the Original Spirit transformed back into the cognitive spirit." In a word: everything calculated only for the private benefit of one's own mouth and belly, ears and eyes, this stinking bag of skin, is the cognitive spirit. Once the cognitive spirit is in charge, how could one produce upright, honorable undertakings that can face heaven and earth above and be tested before ghosts and spirits below?

Only within the utter stillness of undivided chaos, when suddenly a thread of natural responsiveness stirs and sprouts, is heavenly principle in that instant pure and complete, without the slightest admixture of Posterior-Heaven scheming or opinion. If you can stand firmly on this ground and carry it forward upright and correct, it is purely heavenly principle in operation, without a trace of selfish human desire. That is why I repeatedly teach people to recognize the single opening of the Mysterious Pass at the moment of utter stillness free of knowing and perceiving: it is truly because at that moment a person becomes one body with heaven and earth and merges into one with the void. If you can hold on to this firmly and go forward from here, then the endlessly generative creation of heaven and earth can be continued through me, and the unfathomable numinous transformations of the void can also manifest freely through me. This single instant of true awareness is truly the common root of heaven, earth and humanity. If practitioners do not begin here, where else could they step onto the ladder leading to immortals and sages?

To sum up: what flows out naturally, without deliberation or thought, is the Original Spirit; what springs up from the desires of the flesh, with deliberate action and discriminating calculation, is the cognitive spirit. The Original Spirit has no form or image; the cognitive spirit leaves traces to be found. One comes from emptiness, the other emerges from the physical body; the two are utterly different. Having seen clearly that the Original Spirit is born of emptiness and the cognitive spirit of the physical body, we must rectify the root and clear the source, gathering in the outer ears, eyes and mouth and the inner Essence, Breath and Spirit (sealing the outer and inner Three Treasures so nothing leaks), and allowing no one-sided knowledge or opinion to spring up recklessly from the physical body or from scattered thoughts. Keeping hold and nourishing oneself like this for a long time, eventually the yin, turbid corporeal awareness that clung to the flesh will all be transformed into pure Original Spirit, and even the eighty-four thousand hairs of the body will become numinous spirits protecting the true Way. This is what the alchemists call "transforming the cognitive into the Original, turning yin into yang." The crux of all this lies in honestly applying one's effort on the side of "emptiness," and never letting one's thoughts revolve around the Posterior-Heaven physical body.

Key Terms

  • Original Spirit and cognitive spirit: The Prior-Heaven true spirit that creates naturally, without thought or deliberation, at the first moment of receiving breath and forming the embryo is the Original Spirit; the discriminating, calculating mind that arises in dependence on the five sense organs and the hundred bones after the infant is born is the cognitive spirit.
  • The Cave of Emptiness: The empty, numinous central palace in the human body where the Prior-Heaven Original Spirit lies hidden; it is not a fixed bodily organ, and appears of itself when all worldly dust is swept away.
  • Mysterious Pass: The instant of vitality that, at the moment of utter stillness free of knowing and perceiving, suddenly stirs in response, purely heavenly principle and unmixed with private cognition.
  • The inner and outer Three Treasures: The inner Three Treasures are Essence, Breath and Spirit; the outer Three Treasures are the ears, eyes and mouth. "Sealing the inner and outer Three Treasures" means withdrawing sight and turning hearing inward, silently guarding the true origin, so that spirit and breath do not leak outward.
  • Transforming the cognitive into the Original, turning yin into yang: This does not mean destroying the everyday mind, but letting the Posterior-Heaven cognitive spirit submit to the mastery of the Prior-Heaven Original Spirit, so that joy, anger, sorrow and delight all issue from impartial heavenly principle and are no longer driven by selfish desire and yin breath.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Use "for whom does this thought arise?" to tell the Original Spirit from the cognitive spirit at a stroke. Huang Yuanji gives the most down-to-earth test: "Everything calculated for the mouth, the ears and one's own body is the cognitive spirit." Before deciding something, pause for half a second and ask yourself: does this thought come from a public-spirited intuition, or from face, self-interest and calculation?
  2. Practice "knowing oneself without displaying oneself, cherishing oneself without prizing oneself." People with real inner confidence know their strengths without rushing to prove them everywhere, and value their own body and mind without putting on airs or looking down on others; the more eager you are to declare and show off, the more easily your spirit and energy are squandered outward.
  3. Broadening your heart is "not narrowing your dwelling." If you lock your energy for a long time into weighing gains and losses and into the narrow cracks of human relations, life only grows narrower; keep a generous, humane heart, and your inner space will naturally grow wider as you go.
  4. In sitting, lean toward "pure emptiness"; don't wrestle with your thoughts. The cognitive spirit is best at arguing with itself. In sitting you need not fight stray thoughts; simply do not follow bodily sensations and deliberations, and gradually you will come to sense that clear background free of thought and worry.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Watching oneself when alone, before shadow and coverlet": Draws on the Doctrine of the Mean's "the noble person is watchful over himself when alone" and the Song-dynasty saying attributed to Cai Yuanding, "walking alone, unashamed before one's shadow; sleeping alone, unashamed before one's coverlet," describing someone who does not deceive their own heart even alone in a dark room.
  2. "Revere the authority of Heaven, and so preserve it": From the Classic of Poetry ("Hymns of Zhou," Wo jiang); "Heaven bestows pure blessings" and "make you flourish and prosper, make you long-lived and good" are from the Classic of Poetry ("Hymns of Lu," Bi gong); "revere the anger of Heaven, and dare not play or idle; revere the changes of Heaven, and dare not gallop recklessly" is from the Classic of Poetry ("Major Odes," Ban); "accumulating day by day and month by month" is from the Classic of Poetry ("Hymns of Zhou," Jing zhi).
  3. "Leaving the peaceful dwelling empty and not living in it," "the broadest dwelling under heaven," "the flood-like upright breath": From Mencius, "Lilou I," "Tengwengong II" and "Gongsun Chou I" respectively.
  4. "Alert from morning to night" (zhaoqian xiti): From the line statement of the Nine in the Third Place of the Qian hexagram in the Book of Changes: "The noble person is active all day long, and in the evening remains watchful as if in danger; no blame."
  5. Textual note: In the Classic, 太上日, and in the commentary 《诗》日 and 故日元神, every 日 is corrected to 曰; 凶灾不兔 is corrected to 凶灾不免; 愚味多您 to 愚昧多愆; 早干水溢 to 旱干水溢; 太和之字 to 太和之宇; 散天之渝 is corrected to 敬天之渝 according to the Classic of Poetry; 超凡人圣 to 超凡入圣; 一手此，不二于彼 to 一于此，不二于彼; 絪絪缰缰 to 絪絪缊缊; 因地一声 is corrected to 呱地一声 according to sense and Huang's usual idiom; 夫岜可 to 夫岂可; 忽馬 to 忽焉; 大不相件 to 大不相侔; and the variant characters 狹 and 挾 have been standardized to their simplified forms.

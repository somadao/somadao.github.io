Chapter 73 · It Comes Without Being Summoned

Courage in daring means resolutely cutting off the deluded mind; courage in not daring means gently nourishing the Original Spirit; when killing and giving life complement each other and one follows the Way of Heaven, the true mechanism comes without being summoned

About This Chapter

"Courage in daring leads to death; courage in not daring leads to life" has traditionally been read as a lesson in worldly self-preservation. Here Huang Yuanji leaps beyond such surface shrewdness and approaches it from the dialectic of Heaven's mandate and creative transformation, and of "letting the human mind die and the Dao mind live" in inner cultivation:

  1. Firmness to kill the deluded mind, gentleness to give life to the true mind (§2): Stealing the pure, empty breath of heaven and earth to cultivate the elixir has two essentials: cutting off the selfish, deluded mind demands vigorous resolve, while giving birth to and nourishing the original true mind demands mildness and pliancy. Worldly benefit and harm are often the reverse of the Way of Heaven: death is lodged within life and life within death. Even the sage always holds this in awe, does not compete with others for supremacy, and simply looks back into himself.
  2. Killing the human mind is giving life to the Original Spirit (§3, first half): Restraining desire depends on resolve: "courage in daring leads to killing," and what is killed is the indulgent, clinging ordinary human mind. Preserving heavenly principle depends on gentleness: "courage in not daring leads to life," and what comes to life is the pure, unstained Prior-Heaven Original Spirit. Only when the mind dies does the spirit come alive, and only when the spirit is alive can the mind die. They seem to be "one beneficial, one harmful," but in fact they complete each other.
  3. Heaven and humanity are one breath; resonance comes without being summoned (§3, second half): The Way of Heaven never argues right and wrong with people, yet in silence it nurtures all things and is clear in reward and punishment. When practitioners silently operate the mechanism of killing and giving life with a true mind free of thought and deliberation, they come into resonance and accord with heaven and earth, like children running to their mother or husband and wife drawing close: without forced summoning, the true breath comes of itself. And since the void is the Way and the Way is Heaven, those who follow the Way naturally enjoy the protection of Heaven's net and the gathering of auspicious blessings.

Safety note: This chapter uses traditional terms of mind-cultivation such as "killing the deluded mind," "killing the human mind," and "cutting off seeing and hearing." Their original meaning is resolutely letting go of clinging and selfish desire and gently nourishing clear awareness; they in no way advocate harsh self-punishment, suppression of normal emotions, or compulsive, isolating retreat. Daily practice should combine firmness with gentleness and follow what is natural.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：勇于敢则杀，勇于不敢则活。此两者，或利或害，天之所恶，孰知其故？是以圣人犹难之。天之道，不争而善胜，不言而善应，不召而自来，坦然而善谋。天网恢恢，疏而不漏。

Lord Lao says: Those who rely only on reckless daring, pushing ahead and striving to dominate, often bring death upon themselves; those who have the courage to restrain themselves and dare not act rashly preserve their vitality instead. Of these two kinds of courage, one brings benefit and the other brings harm; yet what Heaven truly detests, and the deeper reasons for it, who can fully fathom? That is why even the sage, facing this question, still finds it profound and hard to gauge and dares not treat it lightly.

The workings of the Way of Heaven are like this: it never contends with the myriad things, yet it is best at prevailing; it never lectures at length, yet it is best at responding precisely; no one needs to call or urge it, yet when the occasion is ripe it arrives of itself; it looks relaxed, open and unhurried, yet in secret it has long since arranged everything thoroughly and fittingly. The net of Heaven is vast and boundless; its meshes seem wide, yet it never lets the slightest thing slip through.

§2
Original (Chinese)

《诗》曰：「维天之命，於穆不已。」人盗天地之气以为丹，即盗於穆不已之天命。此命在天即清虚一气，在人即太和一气。惟由平旦直养，至于浩然充塞乎两大，即反本复命，上下与天地同流矣。养之维何？一在于死妄心，死妄心，贵于刚，刚则不屈于物而令正气常伸；一在于生真心，生真心，贵于柔，柔则能悦诸心而令浩气常凝。此两者，一往无前，奋其果敢之力者，死机也；逡巡不进，甘为懦弱之材者，生气也。勇于敢则杀，勇于不敢则活，此进为退基，负为胜本。《易》曰：「日中则昃，月盈则食」，「天地盈虚，与时偕行」，或利或害，往往与世相反，故人之所喜，天之所恶也。且夫天，亦何所恶哉？好生者彼苍之心，有时不用生而用杀；尚德者上帝之意，有时不以德而以刑，此间生中寓杀，杀中有生，其意深微，有非人所能测度者。天之所恶，孰能知其故耶？是以修道之圣人，知福为祸基，柔为刚体，酌经权而用其中，忘利钝而守其正，不与凡人争利害，惟于一己辨从违。至于降灾锡福，惠吉逆凶，虽圣人犹难测其微，况下焉者乎？夫圣之道，亦天之道也。圣人纯任自然，而进退升降，自运转于一身之中。天道无为自然，而生长收藏，常流行于太虚之表。所以不与万物争强，而修短频临，究无一夫之能傲，是不争而善胜矣；不与下民言理，而祸福所及，卒无一地之或逃，是不言而善应矣。虽其中或迟或速、或重或轻，暗中自有权衡，有不由人谋者在，故曰：「不召而自来，坦然而善谋」。任他才智过人，奸巧绝世，而肺肝洞见，虽张皇掩饰，有何益乎？「天网恢恢，疏而不漏」，洵不诬也。

The Classic of Poetry says: "The mandate of Heaven, how profound and unceasing!" When practitioners speak of "stealing the breath of heaven and earth" to refine the Golden Elixir, what they steal is precisely this profound and unceasing vitality of Heaven's mandate. In heaven and earth this mandate is the pure, empty, numinous One Breath of Prior Heaven; in a human being it is the rich, mild primordial breath of supreme harmony. If a person simply nourishes it uprightly from the clear moment of early dawn (the breath of daybreak) onward, until the flood-like upright breath fills all between heaven and earth, this is called returning to the root and restoring the mandate, flowing together with heaven and earth above and below.

How, concretely, should one nourish it? There are two essentials: one is "killing the deluded mind," the other is "giving birth to the true mind." Cutting off the selfish, deluded mind depends on firm vigor: only if one is firm and strong enough will one not yield to the lure of external things, and one's upright breath will always stand tall and extended. Cultivating the original true mind depends on mildness: only if one is pliant enough can the mind and spirit be at ease and content, and the flood-like primordial breath remain gathered and undispersed. Judged by appearances alone, the drive to forge ahead with bold daring hides a mechanism of death leading to ruin, while the attitude of yielding, humble and content with softness hides, on the contrary, a burgeoning vitality. Hence "courage in daring leads to killing; courage in not daring leads to life": hasty advance is often the beginning of retreat, and willingness to take the lower position is in fact the root of victory.

The Book of Changes says: "When the sun reaches its zenith it declines; when the moon is full it wanes," and "the fullness and emptiness of heaven and earth proceed in step with the seasons." Worldly benefit and harm are often exactly the opposite of how ordinary people see them; so the showing off and bravado that the world loves most are precisely what the Way of Heaven detests. Then again, does Heaven really detest anyone out of partiality? The original heart of blue Heaven is to love life, yet sometimes it uses not nurture but severe killing (as with leaves falling in autumn and winter); the original intent of the Way of Heaven is to honor humane virtue, yet sometimes it uses not kindness but punishment. Here, a mechanism of killing is hidden within vitality, and vitality is gestating within the mechanism of killing; the intent is profound and subtle, by no means something the ordinary human mind can easily gauge. Why Heaven detests certain things, who can fully explain?

Therefore the sage who cultivates the Way knows deeply that good fortune is the prelude to misfortune and that softness is the substance of firmness. He knows how to weigh constant principle against expedient adaptation and hold to the middle way, forgets immediate gain and loss, sharpness and bluntness, and holds firm to the upright path. He never contends with the world over momentary advantage, victory or defeat, but only distinguishes in his own heart what to follow and what to avoid. As for the subtle causality by which Heaven sends down calamity or bestows blessing, making those who follow the Way fortunate and those who oppose it unfortunate, even the sage finds it unfathomable and holds it in constant awe; how much more so ordinary people?

In fact, the Way of the sage is the Way of Heaven. The sage entrusts himself wholly to the natural, and the advancing and withdrawing, rising and sinking of the breath within (fire phasing) circulate of themselves through his whole body; the Way of Heaven is non-acting and natural, and the rhythm of spring birth, summer growth, autumn harvest and winter storage likewise runs unceasingly through the cosmos of the Great Void. So the Way of Heaven never competes with the myriad things in strength, yet when the length of a life or the rise and fall of fortune comes due, no arrogant person can ultimately withstand it: this is "not contending, yet excelling at prevailing." The Way of Heaven never opens its mouth to lecture the world on great principles, yet when the causality of fortune and misfortune is fulfilled, no corner under heaven can escape by luck: this is "not speaking, yet excelling at responding." Although retribution comes sooner or later, heavier or lighter, there is in secret a precise weighing that no human scheming can sway; hence "it comes without being summoned; relaxed, yet excellent at planning." However exceptional a person's talent and intelligence, however unmatched their cunning and craft, in the eyes of the Way of Heaven their lungs and liver are laid bare and perfectly clear; even if they scramble frantically to cover up, what good would it do? "The net of Heaven is vast; its meshes are wide, yet nothing slips through": this saying is truly no lie!

§3
Original (Chinese)

遏欲贵果，不果则人心放纵，人欲缠绵，故勇于敢则杀，所以杀人心也；存理贵柔，不柔则凡气躁暴，元气动摇，故勇于不敢则活，所以活元神也。然死心所以活神，害中有利；活神方能死心，利中有害；或利或害，两者相济，人心易死，道心易生。顾其中有天道焉。天有好恶，刑与德并施，生与杀共用，人或知之矣。而具生机于杀机之中，伏活机于死机之内，世人未易窥测焉。天之所恶，孰知其故哉？圣人身同天地，知恶之正所以好之，且非恶无以成好，此中循环妙用，虽圣人犹难知之。然而圣人之道，亦即天之道也。天不与凡民争是非，而发育万物，无有不荷其煦妪而驾而上之者；不与凡民言感孚，而阴阳迭运，无有不相为默契而悖而驰之者。盖天人一道，寂然不动，感而遂通，化何神也？物我同源，廓然大公，物来顺应，措何当欤？至人以无思无虑之真，默运神功于生杀之舍，暗袭天机于造化之宫，入水府，造金乡，踵希夷，绝视听，杀者生之，生者杀之，初不知其何以相胜相应，如子母夫妇，不召自来，不谋自合，如此其感孚之捷而神耶？至灾祥予夺，祸福贞淫，天网恢恢，诚无有逃而脱之者。以虚空即道，道即天，不能逃虚空，即不能逃天。人不违道，即不违天，天休不于以滋至哉？

Restraining selfish desire depends on resolve: without enough resolve, the ordinary human mind indulges itself freely and greed clings without end. Hence "courage in daring leads to killing": what is killed here is exactly that restless, deluded ordinary human mind! Preserving heavenly principle depends on gentleness: without enough gentleness, the agitated breath of the Posterior Heaven surges violently and drags the Prior-Heaven primordial breath into unrest. Hence "courage in not daring leads to life": what comes to life here is exactly the pure, numinous Prior-Heaven Original Spirit!

The wonder lies in this: killing the restless human mind is precisely in order to awaken the pure Original Spirit; it looks like a damaging loss ("harm"), yet it brings great benefit. Nourishing and awakening the Original Spirit is what truly dissolves the ordinary human mind; it looks like soft preservation ("benefit"), yet it quietly melts away arrogant, deluded breath. This one benefit and one harm complete and assist each other, so the ordinary human mind naturally subsides with ease and the pure Dao mind naturally grows.

Yet behind this lies a very deep Way of Heaven. That Heaven has likes and dislikes, that punishment and kindness go together, and severe killing and nurture are used side by side, perhaps some people know. But that Heaven hides vitality within the mechanism of killing and buries a road to life within a deadly situation, such winding, subtle arrangements are very hard for the world to see through. "What Heaven detests, who knows the reason?" The sage, being one body with heaven and earth, knows deeply that Heaven's detestation and punishment are precisely meant to care for and complete; without that severe paring away, true vitality could not be brought to completion. The wondrous function of the cycle of yin and yang in this is something even the sage marvels at as profound and inexhaustible.

Nevertheless, the Way of the sage is in the end the Way of Heaven. Heaven never argues with the world over right and wrong, crooked and straight; it simply nourishes all things in silence. There is no living thing that does not receive its warm covering and nurture, so how could anyone rise above it? Heaven never talks to ordinary people about sincere resonance; it simply lets yin and yang alternate in their courses, and all things in heaven and earth accord with it secretly, so who could run counter to it? For Heaven and humanity are originally one Way: ordinarily "still and unmoving," at the first touch of occasion "it responds and penetrates." How wondrous this transforming nurture is! External things and the self come from the same source; when the heart is "vast and impartial," it responds naturally to whatever comes. How fitting this response is!

The perfected person, with precisely this original mind free of thought and deliberation, silently operates the divine work at the pivot where death and life meet, and secretly accords with the hinge of heaven and earth's creative transformation: the spirit enters the bureau of Kan water and is nourished in the homeland of Dui metal, follows the Great Way of the "faint and inaudible," which can be neither seen nor heard, and gathers in the craving of sight and hearing that runs outward. The deluded thoughts that ought to be cut off are cut off and transformed into life; the restless breath that ought to be restrained is gathered in and returns to stillness. At first one cannot even say why they prevail over and respond to each other so naturally, yet true yang and true spirit, like mother and child or husband and wife, return of themselves without deliberate calling and come into harmony of themselves without anxious calculation. How swift and wondrous their mutual resonance and accord!

As for the calamities and auspicious signs, the giving and taking away in the human world, all fortune and misfortune, good and evil: Heaven's net is vast and boundless, and truly no one can escape from it. Why? Because the all-pervading void is the Way, and the Way is Heaven. Since a person cannot step outside the void for even a moment, neither can they step outside the Way of Heaven for even a moment. As long as one's conduct and intentions do not violate the great Way, one does not violate Heaven; how then could Heaven's harmony and blessed protection fail to descend in an endless stream?

Key Terms

  • Killing the deluded mind, giving birth to the true mind (killing the human mind, giving life to the Original Spirit): Huang Yuanji's inner-cultivation reading of "courage in daring leads to killing; courage in not daring leads to life": cutting off selfish desire requires firm, resolute courage, while nourishing the Original Spirit and the One Breath of Prior Heaven requires gentle, pliant work.
  • Upright nourishing from daybreak (pingdan zhiyang): From Mencius; it refers to the clear breath of early morning, before the mind and spirit are disturbed by worldly affairs. Nourishing it uprightly and continuously from there lets the upright breath grow abundant.
  • The faint and inaudible (xiyi): From Chapter 14 of the Daodejing, "Looked at but not seen, it is called faint (yi); listened to but not heard, it is called inaudible (xi)"; it refers to the soundless, formless substance of the Great Way.
  • Resonance (ganfu): The genuine resonance and accord that arise naturally with the inner true breath and with the creative transformations of heaven and earth once the mind and spirit are pure and single; this is what is meant by "coming without being summoned."

Putting It into Practice

  1. Use "firmness" and "gentleness" in the right places. Many people use them exactly the wrong way round: with their own bad habits they dither and cannot bring themselves to act (failing to be firm where firmness is needed), while with family, colleagues, or in sitting practice they are impatient, domineering and forceful (failing to be gentle where gentleness is needed). Be decisive in breaking bad habits; be gentle in nourishing the mind and spirit.
  2. Setbacks and losses often hide a turning point. In the Way of Heaven, "death is lodged within life, and life within death." When life stalls, when you lose something, or when the world pours cold water on you, there is no need to rush to blame Heaven and others; often it is forcing you to stop your hasty, superficial steps and set your real foundations firmly.
  3. Argue less for the moment; build more steadily. "Not contending, yet excelling at prevailing; not speaking, yet excelling at responding." Character and ability that truly stand firm need not win every quarrel; hold to the upright path and do things solidly, and time will naturally give the fairest answer.
  4. "One cannot escape the void, and so one cannot escape Heaven." Heavenly principle is not a judge hanging high in the clouds but the ecology of cause and effect you inhabit at every moment; act openly and do not deceive even in a dark room, and your heart will naturally be steady and unafraid.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Upright nourishing from daybreak, until the flood-like breath fills the two great ones": Draws on "the breath of daybreak" in Mencius, "Gaozi I," and on "nourished uprightly and without harm, it fills all between heaven and earth" in Mencius, "Gongsun Chou I"; "the two great ones" are heaven and earth.
  2. "When the sun reaches its zenith it declines; when the moon is full it wanes" and "the fullness and emptiness of heaven and earth proceed in step with the seasons": From the Book of Changes, the Commentary on the Judgment of the Feng hexagram and of the Sun (Decrease) hexagram respectively.
  3. "Following brings fortune, opposing brings misfortune" (hui ji ni xiong): Draws on the Book of Documents ("Counsels of the Great Yu"): "Following the right path brings good fortune, following opposition brings misfortune, as shadow follows form and echo follows sound"; that is, according with the Way is fortunate and opposing it is unfortunate.
  4. "Still and unmoving, it responds and penetrates" and "vast and impartial, responding naturally to things as they come": The former is from the Book of Changes ("Great Commentary," Part I); the latter from the Northern Song thinker Cheng Hao's Letter on Calming Nature (Dingxing shu).
  5. Textual note: In the Classic, the superfluous comma in 不召而，自来 has been deleted; 《诗》日 is corrected to 《诗》曰; both instances of 死安心 are corrected to 死妄心 in light of the parallel with 生真心 and 杀人心 below; the superfluous character 糈 between 勇于敢则杀 and 勇于不敢则活 has been deleted; 与时借行 is corrected to 与时偕行; 一已 to 一己; 惠吉迪凶 to 惠吉逆凶; 狗不诬也 to 洵不诬也; 過欲贵果 to 遏欲贵果; 存理贵来，不来则凡气躁綦 is corrected to 存理贵柔，不柔则凡气躁暴 according to sense and parallelism; 感乎 and 感子 are both corrected to 感孚; 阴阳选运 to 阴阳迭运; and 天网憐恢 to 天网恢恢.

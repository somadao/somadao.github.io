VIII · Classic of Virtue (Ch. 71–81) · 德经

Chapter 78 · Bearing the Country's Disgrace

Water is supremely soft yet can overcome the hard; the true Essence of the first stirring of yang is likewise soft as water, and only by nurturing it with a supremely soft, non-acting spirit and turning back to examine oneself at every turn does it grow into a vast, supremely firm power

About This Chapter

Following directly on Chapter 76's "the soft and weak dwell above," Chapter 78 raises "water" to the foremost example of the soft and weak overcoming the hard and strong, and leads into the saying "straight words that sound like their opposite": "One who bears the country's disgrace is called lord of the altars of soil and grain; one who bears the country's misfortunes becomes king of all under heaven." Huang Yuanji moves from contemplating water and cultivating the mind to the broad sense of responsibility of Tang the Accomplished and King Wu, and then, with a single stroke, reveals what "water" really refers to in the alchemical Way:

  1. The root of the problem: knowing softness without practicing it (§2, first half): A single drop of water is tiny, yet gathered into a sea it breaks down everything hard. Everyone under heaven knows that the soft and weak overcome the hard and strong, yet almost no one is willing to act on it. The root of this is that the moment a thought stirs, it is stained by passions, desires and social habits; people turn combative and aggressive, the "guest Breath" of bluster swells and the upright Breath fades.
  2. True responsibility versus "sleeping on brushwood and tasting gall" (§2, second half): Huang Yuanji draws a careful distinction. Tang's "If the people of the myriad regions have sinned, the sin lies with my own person" and King Wu's self-reproach "It is only that I, the little one, am not good" came from a genuine largeness of spirit, from utmost sincerity in seeking the fault in oneself, so that stepping back became stepping forward. This is utterly different from the later "sleeping on brushwood and tasting gall, swallowing disgrace and enduring filth," endured reluctantly with a scheming heart bent on revenge.
  3. Water as an image of the true Essence of the first stirring of yang (§3): In inner cultivation, the first spark of true Essence and true yang (the Living Zi Hour) is at first extremely soft, weak and subtle, just like a drop of water. Only when it is nurtured day after day by the supremely soft and weak, pure Original Spirit, with a gentle fire phasing of "taking non-action as action and non-merit as merit," can it be gradually gathered and gradually condensed, growing into the vast, supremely firm Breath that fills heaven and earth.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：天下柔弱莫过于水，而攻坚强者莫之能胜，其无以易之。故弱胜强，柔胜刚，天下莫不知，莫能行。是以圣人云：「受国之垢，是谓社稷主。受国之不祥，是为天下王。」正言若反。

Lord Lao says: Nothing under heaven is softer and weaker than water, yet when it comes to striking and overcoming the hard and strong, nothing can surpass it, and nothing can change this nature of it. So "the weak overcome the strong and the soft overcome the hard": there is no one under heaven who does not know this principle, yet no one who can actually practice it. Therefore the sage says: "Only one who dares to bear all the country's humiliation and disgrace is fit to be lord of the altars of soil and grain; only one who dares to shoulder all the country's calamities and misfortunes is fit to be king of all under heaven." True principles that accord with the Great Way often sound like the very opposite of worldly common sense.

§2
Original (Chinese)

太上前章言，「柔弱者生之徒，坚强者死之徒，是以柔弱处上，坚强处下」，可知至柔而至刚，至弱而至强，人当日夜行习，在在以柔弱为重，而不以刚强自用矣。不知人身，试观诸水。夫水至柔而至弱，善利万物而不争，常处污下而不厌，虽一滴之微，人得侮之，一勺之多，人得轻之，及其积而为渊，汇而为海，则汪洋浩瀚，能载舟亦能覆舟，能成物亦能戕物，不惟天下无以胜之，即善攻坚强者，无坚不破，无强不摧，亦莫与之抗衡。是知天下之至柔，能御天下之至刚；天下之至弱，能驱天下之至强。水哉水哉！何其柔弱如此，而刚强如彼哉？且天下之事，无有易于攻水者，而坚强卒莫能胜，人何以不居柔而居刚、不为弱而为强者，随在皆是也？岂不知柔之胜刚、弱之胜强乎？盖以天良之动，莫不有知，而一动之后，顿为情欲所染，习俗所移，故悻悻自雄，不肯安于柔弱，是以机巧熟而义理生，嗜好偏而天真没，致令道心离，人心起，客气盛，正气消，生理无存，生机已灭，欲其生生不息也难矣。圣人云：「受国之垢，是为社稷主。」如成汤言：「朕躬有罪，无以万方；万方有罪，罪在朕躬。」退步即为进步，所以受天命于无穷也。「受国之不祥，是为天下王。」如武王曰：「受克予，非朕文考有罪，惟予小子无良。」自后即为自前，所以荷天休于勿替也。岂同后世之卧薪尝胆、蒙垢纳污者，所得而拟议哉？此真常不易之理，万古不磨之经，是为天下正言，而圣人则反求诸己，又何尝以此苛求于人哉？

In the earlier Chapter 76, Lord Lao already said: "The soft and weak belong to the company of life, the hard and strong to the company of death; therefore the soft and weak dwell above and the hard and strong dwell below." We can see from this that utmost firmness is hidden within utmost softness, and utmost strength within utmost delicacy. People ought to put this into practice from morning to night, making softness and humility the priority in every matter and never forcing things through strength. If looking only at your own body you still cannot grasp it, go and look at water.

Water is the softest and weakest thing under heaven. It is good at nourishing all things yet never contends for credit; it readily dwells in low, dirty places without any distaste. A single drop of water is so tiny that anyone can slight it; a single ladleful is so little that anyone can belittle it. Yet once it has gathered drop by drop into a deep pool and rushed together into a great sea, it becomes a boundless expanse that can steadily bear up huge ships and can capsize vessels in an instant, that can moisten and bring all things to completion and can wash away and destroy all things. Not only can nothing in the world defeat it; even the fiercest things most skilled at attacking the hard and overcoming the strong, said to break every hardness and crush every strength, have no power at all to stand against the surging force of water. Clearly the utmost softness under heaven can master the utmost hardness, and the utmost weakness can drive the utmost strength. Water, oh water! How can you be so soft and weak outwardly, and yet so firm and strong in your inner power?

By rights, nothing in the world is easier to cut or strike than water, yet no hard or strong force can ever defeat it. That being so, why is the world full of people who refuse to keep to softness and insist on being hard, who refuse to show weakness and insist on contending for strength? Do people really not understand that "the soft overcomes the hard and the weak overcomes the strong"? It is not that they do not understand. It is that when innate conscience first stirs, everyone understands perfectly well, but the instant a thought sets off, it is stained by private passions and desires and led astray by worldly fashions, and at once they turn indignant and think themselves heroes, refusing to their dying breath to rest content in softness. Over time, calculation and cunning grow ever more practiced while humaneness, righteousness and true principle grow ever more unfamiliar; private desires and cravings grow ever more fixed while original innocence is gradually buried. The result is that the mind of the Way departs and the ordinary human mind rises high, restless "guest Breath" runs rampant and upright Breath dissipates from the whole body, the healthy workings of life vanish and vitality gradually dies out. To hope for life that goes on producing life is then very hard indeed!

The sage says: "Only one willing to bear the shame and disgrace of a whole country is fit to be lord of the altars of soil and grain." So Tang of Shang, reproaching himself when praying for rain, said: "If I myself have sinned, do not involve the people of the myriad regions; if the people of the myriad regions have sinned, the guilt lies wholly on me alone!" Being willing to step back before the people and take responsibility became precisely the ladder by which his virtue advanced, and so he could receive Heaven's mandate without end. It also says: "Only one who dares to shoulder the misfortunes of a whole country is fit to be king of all under heaven." So King Wu of Zhou, addressing his troops before attacking Zhou of Shang, said: "If Shou defeats me, it will not be because my late father King Wen was at fault, but entirely because I, his unworthy descendant, am not good!" Being willing to place himself last, examining himself and taking responsibility, became precisely the great stature by which he led all under heaven, and so he could receive Heaven's blessing, never failing.

How could this open, upright breadth of responsibility be compared with the scheming strategists of later ages, who harbored resentment and calculation and outwardly "slept on brushwood and tasted gall, swallowing disgrace and enduring filth," while in fact waiting for the chance to take revenge? This is an eternal, unchanging truth, a constant classic that ten thousand ages cannot wear away; it is in itself the most upright "straight word" under heaven. It is only because the sage always uses it to seek strictly within themselves that it sounds "like its opposite" to ordinary people used to shifting blame outward. And when has the sage ever used this lofty standard to make harsh demands of others?

§3
Original (Chinese)

水喻一阳初动，真精始生，其机至弱，其势至柔，而渐采渐结，日益月增，以至于浩然之气，至大至刚，塞乎两大，统乎万汇，而无坚不入，无强不破者焉。《悟真》云：「白虎首经至宝，华池神水真经。上善若水利源深，不比寻常药品。」顾气之柔弱，有似于水，至柔而寓至刚，至弱而兼至强，实有擎天顶地、捧日举月、呼风唤雨、驱雷掣电之威，是天下之坚强者，虽曰浩气，其实真精。须以至柔至弱之神养之，而无为为为、无功为功，庶几得矣。其曰「受国之垢，是为社稷主；受国之不祥，是为天下王」者何？即古人反躬自责，「朕实不德，民有何辜」之意也。学者求之于人，何若反修诸身之为得耶？

In the actual practice of internal alchemy, the "water" of the classic is an image of that tiny spark of vitality when, in utter stillness, the first yang stirs (the Living Zi Hour) and the true Essence of Prior Heaven has just been born. When it first emerges, its opening is extremely delicate and its momentum extremely soft. Yet as long as you know how to follow its momentum, gathering and refining it bit by bit and letting it condense bit by bit, growing day by day and month by month, it can in the end be nurtured into what Mencius called the "vast, flowing Breath": supremely great and supremely firm, filling the space between heaven and earth, embracing all phenomena, truly able to enter every hardness and break every strength!

Zhang Boduan's Awakening to Reality says: "The first menses of the White Tiger is the supreme treasure, the divine water of the Flowery Pool is the true classic (the original reads 'true gold'). Highest good is like water, its beneficial source is deep; it is not to be compared with ordinary medicines." (Here "the first menses of the White Tiger" and "the divine water of the Flowery Pool" are alchemical metaphors for the true Essence and true Breath of Prior Heaven and for the numinous fluid of Sweet Dew.) When this true Breath of Prior Heaven is first born, it is as soft and weak as clear water, yet within its utmost softness it hides utmost firmness, and within its utmost weakness it also holds utmost strength. Once fully nurtured, it truly has the overflowing upright force to prop up heaven and support the earth, to turn Qian and Kun around, with the might of ten thousand thunderbolts. So the "vast, flowing Breath" that can truly attack the hard and overcome the strong is, traced to its source, really nurtured from this single spark of true Essence of Prior Heaven.

Since its nature is as soft and tender as water, when we nurture it we must likewise bring forth a supremely soft, supremely weak, pure and undefiled Original Spirit to warm and protect it, acting in the manner of "non-action" and accumulating merit with a mind that "does not covet merit." Only then can we come close to truly attuning to it and obtaining it. And what does the classic's "one who bears the country's disgrace is lord of the altars of soil and grain; one who bears the country's misfortunes is king of all under heaven" mean for cultivating oneself and dealing with the world? It is the humble, sincere heart of the ancient sages, who on meeting a problem first turned back to reproach themselves: "Truly it is I who lack virtue; what fault have the people?" For those who cultivate the Way, rather than spending all day finding fault with others and blaming them, how much more rewarding it is to turn back in every matter and cultivate oneself!

Key Terms

  • The true Essence of the first stirring of yang: the vitality of Prior Heaven first sprouting in utter stillness (the Living Zi Hour, Essence). At birth it is supremely soft and weak like a trickle of water; accumulated day by day it becomes the supremely great and supremely firm vast, upright Breath.
  • Divine water of the Flowery Pool: An alchemical metaphor for the clear, moistening fluids generated in the mouth and within the body (Sweet Dew) and for the true Breath of Prior Heaven, quoted from Awakening to Reality: "highest good is like water, its beneficial source is deep."
  • Guest Breath and upright Breath: "Guest Breath" means the restless temper, empty arrogance and urge to dominate stirred up by passions, desires and social habits; "upright Breath" means the vast, steady power nurtured from pure true Essence and innate conscience.
  • Seeking within oneself: Huang Yuanji's distillation of the core method behind "bearing the country's disgrace, bearing the country's misfortunes." True responsibility comes from sincere self-examination, as distinct from the strategic forbearance of those who nurse venom and wait to strike back.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Tell "true resilience" from "false forbearance." Huang Yuanji deliberately separates the self-reproach of Tang and King Wu from "sleeping on brushwood and tasting gall, swallowing disgrace and enduring filth." Gritting your teeth and swallowing your anger with a sense of grievance and resentment stores up hostility in the heart; openly taking responsibility and turning your attention back to your own growth makes upright Breath grow in the heart.
  2. Between "understanding the principle" and "being able to do it" lies the first second in which a thought stirs. Everyone knows that the soft and weak overcome the hard and strong, yet in a conflict they "turn indignant and think themselves heroes." Next time anger first flares up, see through the "guest Breath" first and wait three seconds before speaking; you will already have won more than half the battle.
  3. New good habits and the sensations of quiet sitting both need "soft nurturing," not "forced ripening." "Its opening is extremely delicate and its momentum extremely soft": the sense of calm and the good state that come at the start are like a drop of water and are most harmed by impatient, forceful meddling. Protect them steadily over time with the ordinary mind of "taking non-action as action and non-merit as merit," and streams will gather into a sea.
  4. When something happens, first ask what you can cultivate in yourself; it saves the most inner wear. "Rather than seeking it in others, why not turn back and cultivate oneself?" Complaining about the outside world will not change the situation; bring your energy back to adjusting your own rhythm and thoughts, and the initiative is immediately back in your hands.

Translator's Notes

  1. Quotations from Tang and King Wu: Tang's "If I myself have sinned, do not involve the myriad regions; if the myriad regions have sinned, the sin lies with my own person" appears in the Analects ("Yao Yue") and in the Book of Documents ("Announcement of Tang"). King Wu's "If Shou defeats me, it is not that my late father was at fault, but only that I, the little one, am not good" appears in the Book of Rites ("Records on Dykes," Fangji) (and also in the "Great Declaration" of the spurious old-text Book of Documents); "Shou" is King Zhou of Shang, Di Xin.
  2. The quotation from Awakening to Reality: From Zhang Boduan of the Northern Song, Awakening to Reality, "West River Moon." The received text reads: "The first menses of the White Tiger is the supreme treasure, the divine water of the Flowery Pool is true gold. Thus we know highest good's beneficial source is deep; it is not to be compared with ordinary medicines." The base text has "true classic" and "highest good is like water, its beneficial source is deep," a free adaptation made by Huang Yuanji while lecturing; the original wording is kept here and noted.
  3. "The first menses of the White Tiger": An alchemical metaphor, not referring to any impure worldly substance; it actually stands for the pure, undefiled true Breath of the first yang just born from Prior Heaven (true Lead).
  4. Textual note: In the classic text, 太上日 is corrected to 太上曰; the superfluous comma in 坚强处，下 is deleted; 尤强不摧 is corrected to 无强不摧; 惟子小子无良 to 惟予小子无良; 其势至来 to 其势至柔 on the basis of the context (其机至弱, 至柔而寓至刚); 呼风喚雨 is normalized to 呼风唤雨; and 反修请身 is corrected to 反修诸身.

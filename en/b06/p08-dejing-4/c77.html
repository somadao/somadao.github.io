VIII · Classic of Virtue (Ch. 71–81) · 德经

Chapter 77 · Acting Without Relying on It

Like a drawn bow, the Way of Heaven presses down what is high, lifts what is low, takes from surplus and makes up what is lacking; inner cultivation means letting the fire above shine downward and the water below moisten upward, returning the water and fire of Posterior Heaven to the balance of Prior Heaven

About This Chapter

Chapter 77 of the Daodejing uses the image of "drawing a bow" to contrast "the Way of Heaven, which takes from what has surplus and makes up what is lacking," with "the way of humans, which takes from those who lack in order to serve those who have surplus." It is the chapter of the whole classic with the strongest sense of balance and the widest public-mindedness. Huang Yuanji first speaks of Heaven's impartiality and the sage's broad-heartedness in claiming no credit; then, in §3, he turns the "drawn bow" into a picture that can be grasped at a glance: the mutual completion of water and fire within the body:

  1. The Way of Heaven keeps things level; the sage acts without relying on it (§2): As the Way of Heaven turns the waxing and waning of yin and yang, it is like drawing a bow and taking aim: what is too high is pressed down, what is too low is raised, surplus is reduced and lack is made up, always returning to the great, central and perfectly upright. The way of humans, driven by selfishness and cunning, does the very opposite. Only the sage who has the Way offers their surplus to the world, does things without claiming credit and accomplishes things without displaying worthiness, at one with the spontaneous transforming work of the Way of Heaven.
  2. The imbalance of Posterior Heaven: fire above, water below, yang water scorched by yin fire (§3, first half): At birth a person is pure yin and pure yang, perfectly level and upright. Once fallen into Posterior Heaven, the heart's fire tends to blaze upward and the kidneys' water tends to sink downward; water and fire no longer meet. As a result, the restless, ordinary yin fire is constantly in surplus and the water of true yang constantly lacking; the heart of Heaven within is gradually clouded over and the true Breath gradually dissipates.
  3. The drawn bow as an image of Kan and Li completing each other (§3, second half): Cultivating in accord with the Way of Heaven means letting the fire that sits above shine warmly downward, and the water that sits below rise to moisten upward: using the surplus fire of the heart to warm and replenish the deficient water of the kidneys, while the true water rising upward in turn restrains the restless yin fire. This is the natural balance of the conjunction of Kan and Li: turning the unbalanced yin and yang of Posterior Heaven back into the yin and yang of Prior Heaven, which flow unceasingly, free and effortless.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：天之道，其犹张弓乎？高者抑之，下者举之，有余者损之，不足者补之。天之道，损有余而补不足；人之道则不然，损不足以奉有余。孰能以有余奉天下？惟有道者。是以圣人为而不恃，功成而不处，其不欲见贤耶？

Lord Lao says: Is not the way Heaven works just like drawing a bow to shoot? If the tip of the bow is too high, press it down a little; if too low, raise it a little; if the pull is excessive, ease it slightly; if the strength is insufficient, make it up as needed. The Way of Heaven always takes from what has surplus to make up what is lacking. The worldly way of humans is just the opposite: it always docks those who already lack in order to serve the strong who already have more than enough. Who in the world can take their own surplus of blessings, virtue and wealth and offer it to the whole world? Only one who has truly realized the Great Way. Therefore the sage acts without relying on it or boasting, and when the work is accomplished does not claim or dwell in it. Is this not because they have no wish at all to put their worthiness on display?

§2
Original (Chinese)

天道流行，发育万物，无非一阴一阳，往来迭运，大中至正，无党无偏而已，故阴极生阳，阳极生阴，阴盛阳衰则抑阴扶阳，阳盛阴衰则抑阳扶阴，消息盈虚，与时偕行，庶生生化化，以成自在无为、万年不敝之天。何异张弓者然？持弓审固，内志既正，外体复直，务令前后手臂平正通达，高者抑之，下者举之，有余者损之，不足者益之，然后顺手而发，随机自中，不患其或失。况天之道，亏盈而益谦，损有余以补不足。人则多奸多诈，不若天道之自然，取民脂膏，饱其囊橐，往往损不足以奉有余。孰能以君上之有余，而奉天下之不足哉？惟有道之圣人，法天道而顺人情，损者损之，补者补之，不使小民有怨咨之叹也。虽为者自为，亦顺承天道而已，绝不矜所为焉；成者自成，亦至诚尽性而已，绝不居其功焉。斯人也，殆与天道无为而化成，同归自然运度，不欲见有为之迹、成物之功，赫赫照人耳目，非贤而不欲以贤见耶？此所以「为天无极，惟圣合天」也。

The Way of Heaven moves and flows through the universe, nurturing all things, and in the end it is nothing but one yin and one yang alternating back and forth, great, central and perfectly upright, partial to none and leaning to no side. That is why yin at its extreme gives rise to yang, and yang at its extreme gives rise to yin. When yin is strong and yang weak, yin is restrained and yang supported; when yang is strong and yin weak, yang is restrained and yin supported. Waxing and waning, filling and emptying move along naturally with the seasons, and so all things are born and transformed without end, forming this heaven and earth that are free, effortless and never decay through ten thousand years.

How is this any different from drawing a bow? The archer holds bow and arrow steadily and aims with precision, the will within upright and the body without straight, making sure both arms, front and rear, are level and free: if the bow is too high, it is lowered; if too low, it is raised; if the force is too great, it is eased a little; if the strength is lacking, a little is added. Once everything is evened out and centered, the arrow is released smoothly and, in response to the moment, hits the bullseye naturally, with no fear of missing.

What is more, the very nature of the Way of Heaven is to diminish what is full and enrich what is humble, taking from surplus to make up what is lacking. Ordinary people of the world, however, are full of cunning and deceit, far from the naturalness and fairness of the Way of Heaven. They often wring the sweat and blood of the common people to fill their own pockets, and specialize in "docking those who lack in order to serve those who have surplus." Who in the world can take the surplus of those on high to relieve the lack of the people under heaven? Only the sage who has truly attained the Way, who models themselves on the Way of Heaven while according with human feelings: reducing what should be reduced and replenishing what should be replenished, never letting ordinary people utter sighs of resentment.

Though the sage quietly does a great many things, they simply follow and carry out the Way of Heaven as it naturally moves, and never boast of their own ability. When things are accomplished of themselves, this too is simply the duty of utmost sincerity and fulfilling one's nature, and they never take the credit for themselves. Such a person is just like the Way of Heaven, which transforms and completes all things without acting, returning together with it to the natural measure of cosmic movement. They do not wish to leave traces of deliberate action, nor to parade the merit of having completed all things dazzlingly before the eyes and ears of the world. Is this not exactly "possessing great worthiness yet not wishing to display it"? The ancients said, "Heaven is boundless and without limit; only the sage accords with Heaven"; this is precisely the state they meant.

§3
Original (Chinese)

人生之初，原是纯阴纯阳，至平至正，无有胜负参差，故日征月迈，骨柔体弱而滋长焉。迨有生后，火常居上，水常居下，水火不交，是以阴常有余，阳常不足，阳水每为阴火所灼，故人心益多，凡气愈炽，而天心所以日汩，真气所以渐亡，生生之机无有存焉者矣。惟天之道，火居上而必照下，水居下而必润上，如张弓者之高者抑、下者举，则水火平矣。使阴火之有余，下补阳水之不足，既补阳水之不足，仍制阴火之有余，如张弓者然，有余者损，不足者补，则阴阳正矣。此皆水火自运，阴阳自交，而天亦不知其为之也。夫人道以有为而累，天道以无为而尊。修炼岂有他哉？惟以后天阴阳，返还先天阴阳，流行不息，自在无为得矣。

Now look at it from the standpoint of a person's own nature and life. When a person is first born, they are pure yin and pure yang, perfectly level and upright, with no imbalance or unevenness between yin and yang. So, as the days and months go by, like an infant with soft sinews and bones, they keep growing, full of vitality.

Once they grow up and fall into the life of Posterior Heaven (see Prior Heaven / Posterior Heaven), the situation changes. The heart's fire often blazes restlessly upward and the kidneys' water often sinks coldly downward; water and fire are cut off from each other and cannot blend. As a result, the restless, draining "yin fire" of Posterior Heaven (scattered and deluded thoughts, an agitated and pent-up Breath) is constantly in surplus, while the "yang water" that moistens and nourishes life (the vitality hidden in true yin and true Essence) is constantly lacking; and the precious yang water below is again and again boiled and scorched dry by the agitated yin fire above. The result is that selfish human desires multiply, restless ordinary Breath burns ever hotter, the originally pure heart of Heaven is submerged a little more each day, the true Breath of Prior Heaven dwindles away bit by bit, and little is left of the pivot by which life goes on producing life.

Yet look at how the Way of Heaven works in nature. The fire of the sun, though it hangs high in the sky, inevitably shines down over the earth; the water of rivers and seas, though it lies low on the ground, inevitably steams up into clouds and moistens the sky above! This is exactly like drawing a bow, "pressing down what is high and lifting what is low": as soon as water and fire blend, all at once there is balance and harmony. In inner cultivation within the body the principle is the same (the conjunction of Kan and Li): gather in the excess, flying fire of the heart above and let it warm and replenish the deficient true water below; once the true water below has been warmed and replenished, it steams upward to moisten, and in turn naturally restrains and calms the excess restless fire above. Just as in drawing a bow, "taking from surplus and making up what is lacking," the yin and yang of the whole body thereby return to central, upright balance and harmony!

Moreover, when the work goes deep, all of this is simply water and fire moving of themselves, yin and yang joining of themselves, and even heaven and earth do not notice any deliberate doing. The worldly "way of humans" exhausts itself by forcing action everywhere, while the "Way of Heaven" is honored and endures by following without acting. What mysterious tricks could true internal alchemy possibly involve? Put plainly, it is just a matter of turning the unbalanced, draining yin and yang of Posterior Heaven back, by reversal, into the balanced, blended yin and yang of Prior Heaven, and letting them circulate unceasingly through the body, free and effortless. That is all it takes.

Key Terms

  • The way of drawing a bow: The central image of this chapter. Pressing down the high, lifting the low, taking from surplus and making up what is lacking when drawing a bow is both the law of Heaven's impartiality and a perfect picture of balancing water and fire, yin and yang, within the body.
  • Water and fire not meeting, and the conjunction of Kan and Li: In the ordinary state of Posterior Heaven, the heart's fire blazes upward (anxious thinking and restlessness) and the kidneys' water sinks downward (cold and dissipated); this is called water and fire not meeting. Gathering in the heart-spirit and letting it shine down on the lower Cinnabar Field, so that water and fire rise and descend and blend, and yin and yang return to level uprightness, is called the conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Yang water and yin fire: Here "yin fire" means the floating fire of deluded, restless thoughts of Posterior Heaven, which drains and harms Essence and Breath; "yang water" means the true Essence and true water within Kan that hold the vitality of true yang of Prior Heaven.
  • Returning to the yin and yang of Prior Heaven (Prior Heaven / Posterior Heaven): Bringing oneself back from the lopsided, depleted state of Posterior Heaven to the balanced condition of birth, perfectly level and upright, flowing naturally around its circuit.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Notice whether you are in a state of "fire above, water below." Overthinking, anxiety and insomnia, a dry mouth and irritability, cold hands and feet are typical signs of "the heart's fire blazing upward while the kidneys' water fails to moisten." At such times the best thing is not to keep forcing your mind on the problem, but to bring your attention from your head back to your breath and lower belly, cooling the "overheated tip of the bow."
  2. Balance the rhythm of your life with the mindset of "adjusting the bow." When your energy is overdrawn, dare to lighten the load (taking from surplus); actively make up the rest, exercise and quiet sitting you have long neglected (making up what is lacking). This will carry you further than any extreme self-discipline.
  3. Sharing your "surplus" is accumulating blessings in accord with the Way of Heaven. When you really have time, experience or resources to spare, be glad to give those around you a hand. It does not leave you with less; instead it dissolves the narrow anxiety of the "way of humans."
  4. "Not relying, not dwelling" after you succeed spares you ninety percent of the trouble. When something is done well, turn the page; do not keep asking for credit or flaunt your seniority everywhere. Your mind stays clear and unburdened, and others are genuinely won over.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Holding the bow steadily and aiming, the will within upright and the body without straight": Adapted from the Book of Rites, "The Meaning of Archery": "Thus the archer, in advancing, retiring and turning about, must accord with ritual. Only when the will within is upright and the body without is straight can one hold bow and arrow steadily and aim; only when one holds bow and arrow steadily and aims can one speak of hitting the mark."
  2. "Diminishing what is full and enriching what is humble": From the Book of Changes, the Tuan commentary to the hexagram Qian (Modesty): "The Way of Heaven diminishes the full and enriches the humble; the Way of Earth overturns the full and flows toward the humble."
  3. "As the days and months go by" (ri zheng yue mai): Adapted from the Classic of Poetry, "Lesser Odes," Xiaowan: "Day by day I go on, month by month I march," describing the flow of time and the continuous growth and unfolding of life.
  4. Textual note: In the title, the 一 in 第七十七章 一 为而不恃 has been corrected to a middle dot; in the classic text, 太上日 is corrected to 太上曰 and 敦能以有余奉天下 to 孰能以有余奉天下; 饱其囊囊 to 饱其囊橐; 不便小民 to 不使小民; and 日征月近 to 日征月迈.

Chapter 71 · To Know That One Does Not Know Is Highest

Letting go of forced cleverness and conjecture is true knowing free of sickness; in sitting practice one must first settle in the Palace of Li and see one's nature, then send the fire of Spirit into the Water Bureau to forge true yang and turn the River Chariot

About This Chapter

Chapter 71 of the Daodejing is only twenty-five characters long, yet it draws the boundary between "knowing" and "not knowing" with piercing clarity. Huang Yuanji begins with the difference between the sage's mind and the ordinary mind, then abruptly turns the chapter into a weighty practical lesson on the dual cultivation of nature and life and on the fire phasing of the River Chariot:

  1. The self-illumination of the light of Nature versus the sickness of conjecture (§2): The sage's knowing does not come from calculating and guessing; it is the natural luminosity of a mind empty as a mirror that reflects whatever comes before it. Ordinary people force themselves to pass off not-knowing as knowing, and when a lucky guess hits the mark they grow pleased with themselves; in fact this is a grave sickness that exhausts the mind and drains the breath. Only by recognizing deeply that "forced cleverness" is a sickness can one keep to one's proper place, gather the mind back into the Opening, and truly be "free of sickness."
  2. First settle in the Palace of Li, then seek the mystery in the Water Bureau (§3, first half): Huang states plainly that sitting practice must begin by cultivating stillness in the heart-spirit (the Palace of Li), until nothing at all remains in the breast and the mind is illumined and its nature seen, establishing the pure source of the Original Spirit; only then may one turn downward to the lower Cinnabar Field (the Water Bureau) to seek the spark of life. Otherwise, "without nature one cannot gather in the feelings," and blindly guarding the breath down below only sinks one into turbidity.
  3. Forging the blood with the fire of Spirit, the ripeness of the medicine, and the River Chariot of the three passes (§3, second half): The chapter carefully distinguishes the different levels of the conjunction of Kan and Li and of the union of Qian and Kun that forms the elixir; the fire-phasing moment when the Medicine is gathered "neither too old nor too young"; and the three levels of the Celestial Circuit (River Chariot), the initial, middle and upper passes, together with the method of ablution. It concludes with the natural Way: "once the practice is fully mature, no method is needed."

Safety note: §3 of this chapter describes advanced internal-alchemy practices such as "Spirit entering the blood," "the whole body leaping and throbbing," "the Zi–Wu River Chariot," and "advancing the yang fire, withdrawing the yin tally, and ablution at Mao and You." Such bodily signs may only arise naturally on the basis of long-cultivated stillness; never deliberately force the breath around the body, strain to produce trembling or heat, or experiment on your own with fierce fire to push things along. If during sitting you experience a swollen feeling in the head, palpitations, feverish heat, muscle twitching that will not stop, or agitation, end the session at once, get up and take a walk, and return to a calm, ordinary daily routine.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：知不知，上；不知知，病。夫惟病病，是以不病。圣人不病，以其病病，是以不病。

Lord Lao says: To possess true knowledge yet stay modest and keep to simplicity, not regarding oneself as knowing, is the highest state; to not understand yet pretend to understand and force one's guesses is a sickness. Only because one truly regards this sickness of "passing off not-knowing as knowing" as a sickness, guarding against it and rooting it out, does one not fall sick. The sage is free of this sickness precisely because, at every moment, he treats this sickness as a sickness and guards against its first stirrings; that is why he remains free of sickness.

§2
Original (Chinese)

睿智所照，自如明镜无尘，止水无波，物来毕照，毫无遁情。此神明洞彻，自然而知，因物为缘，如心而出，非亿度以为明、悬揣以为知者。其知也，由于性光之自照，而不是有前知之明，却能知人所不知，此上哲之士，非凡人所能及也。凡人智不能烛理，明不能照物，往往拟议其人之诚伪，逆料乎事之兴衰，幸而偶中，人谓其明如镜，自亦诩其烛如神。此等揣摩之知，非神灵之了照，乃强不知以为知，虽有所知，其劳心苦虑，病已甚矣，是自作聪明者，自耗神气者也。夫惟以强知为病，于是病其所病，而穷理以尽性，修命以俟天，慧而不用，智实若愚，自然心空似水，性朗如冰，一灵炯炯，照彻三千，又何营图之苦、机巧之劳，以为患也哉？是以不病。圣人明烛事机，智周物理，自有先觉之明，绝无卜度之臆。故凡人有病，而圣人不病焉者，以其能病所不知，病所不明，而于是一心皈命，五体投忱，尽收罗于玄玄一窍之中，久之灵光焕发，烛照无遗，固随在皆宜，亦无往不利也。以其病病，是以不病。

When true wisdom contemplates the myriad things, it is naturally like a bright mirror without a speck of dust, or still water without a ripple: whatever comes before it is reflected with perfect clarity, and not the slightest hidden circumstance escapes its reflection. This is the natural awareness that comes once the spirit is thoroughly clear: it follows the occasions offered by things and flows directly out of the pure original mind. It is never a "cleverness" manufactured by racking one's brains with conjecture and calculation. Such knowing springs from the natural shining of the original mind's light of Nature; it does not deliberately parade some uncanny gift of foresight, yet it sees into realities ordinary people cannot penetrate. This is the realm of the wise of the highest order, beyond the reach of ordinary people.

Ordinary people's wisdom cannot illuminate the principles of affairs, and their eyes cannot see through the nature of things, yet they often love to speculate privately on whether others are sincere or false and to predict whether affairs will rise or fall. If by luck they hit the mark once or twice, others praise their keen discernment, and they themselves grow smug and boast that they foresee events like a god. But this kind of knowledge, pieced together by guesswork, is not the clear illumination of a pure, numinous spirit at all; it is forcing not-knowing to pass as knowing. Even if they occasionally guess a little correctly, the heart's blood and anxious brooding spent behind it have long since become a grave sickness. Such self-made cleverness, to put it bluntly, is a pointless squandering of one's own Spirit and Breath.

Only when a person truly recognizes that "forced cleverness and reckless conjecture" is a great sickness, resolves firmly to cure its root, and honestly investigates principle to the end so as to fulfill their nature, and cultivates life steadily so as to accord with the Way of Heaven (inner nature / life); only when they have wisdom without rashly flaunting it, and great intelligence that yields as if it were dullness; then, over time, the mind naturally becomes empty and bright as water, the nature clear as ice, and a single point of spiritual brightness shines forth, able to see through the great thousandfold world. Where then would there be the torment of anxious scheming or the fatigue of exhausting one's ingenuity to do oneself harm? This is what the Classic means by "therefore one is free of sickness."

The sage can see clearly the first signs of affairs and comprehends the patterns of all things; he naturally has the foresight that issues from the pure original mind and never relies on blind guessing and conjecture to try his luck. So ordinary people are riddled with sickness while the sage is free of it, because the sage is willing to treat honestly as a sickness whatever he cannot see through or does not understand, and never forces a pretense of understanding. Thereupon he gathers in body and mind, turns wholeheartedly toward the Great Way, and with the utmost devotion collects all his thoughts into that single wondrous opening, the Mysterious Pass. As the days accumulate, the inner numinous light naturally blazes forth, illuminating all affairs without omission, so that in whatever circumstances he finds himself he is at ease and fitting, and nothing he undertakes fails. This is the deep meaning of "because he treats sickness as sickness, he is free of sickness."

§3
Original (Chinese)

此言慧照之知，是为上等。若矫情之知，实为大患。惟以强知之患为患，是以无患。圣人之得免于患者，常以此患为患，所以无患。大旨已明，兹不复赘。今再将道妙详言之。大凡打坐，必先从离宫修定，做一晌，而后自考自证，果然空空无物，于是始向水府求玄。夫离宫修定，是修性也，心空无物，即明心见性矣。所以吾尝云：静坐之初，此心悬之太虚，待身心安定，意气和平，然后徐徐以意收摄，回照本宫。到得了无一物介于胸间，从此一觉一照，即十方三界，无在而不入我觉照之中，然而觉性不生，觉性不灭，不过了了自了、如如自如而已。以此求玄，则水源至清，自可为我结丹之本。一霎时间，自然性光发现。何以见之？即吾前日所示「恍恍惚惚中，忽然一觉而动」是。修道之要，始而以性摄情，若不先讨出性真本来，突地下水府中求玄，不知既无性矣，何以摄得起情来？夫既有虚灵之性，能招实有之情，由此一阳萌动，自然肾间微痒，有氤氲蓬勃之机。要知离非属心也，凡凝耳韵、含眼光，戒香味触法，皆是神火主事，故曰属离；坎非在肾也，一身血肉团子，无非是精，凡精所有，无非是气，精气所在，即是属坎。我以神入血中，火热水里，未必即有气机发动，务须左提右挈，摄起海底之波，上入丹田，久久烹炼，火功既足，忽然天机发动，周身踊跃，从十指以至一身，跳动不止，身如壁立，意若寒灰，丹田气暖，此即血之不老不嫩，合中之时。若非有此效验，尚是微嫩，不可行火；若久见此景，而不知起火，气已散矣，始行用火，是为药老无用。学者审之辨之。然微阳初动，未必即有此盛气，只要心安意适，气息融和，亦可行子午河车。盖人身有形有质之血，不经火煅，尚是污污浊浊一团死血。惟用神火之照，血中自生出一点真气出来，即佛所云「我于五浊恶世修行而得成道果」是，又古谓「鬼窝中取宝，黑山下求铅」是，皆不外浊精败血内，以神火煅出此一点真气来。气既动，阳即生，又当知子进阳火、午退阴符、卯酉沐浴诸法，方能采得此真阳，运行流通，内以驱除脏腑之阴私，外以招摄天地灵阳之真气，久久用功，气质亦变。此河车一法，有无穷妙义也。古有云「气明子午抽添」，抽即抽取水府之铅，添即添离宫之汞。汞即心中灵液，后天中先天，从色中浊精败血中，以神火煅出，而成甘露者是；铅即血中之气，气即古人谓水中之金，此为后天中先天，只可以固凡体，不可以生法身。此是坎离交而生出来之药物，犹不可以作神丹。必要以性摄情，以情归性，性情和合，同煅于坤炉之中，忽地真阳发动，此为乾坤交而结丹，始可炼神丹，为真仙子。总之，修炼别无他法，只是一个河车运转。初关河车，犹须勉强；中关河车，天人合发；到得上关河车，纯乎自然之天，不失其时而已。至于卯酉沐浴诸法，不过恐初学人，心烦火起，行工不得不然，若到纯熟，不须法矣。总在学人，神而明之可也。

What was said above is this: true knowledge that comes from the wise illumination of the original mind is of the highest order; false knowledge that comes from affectation is truly a great calamity. Only by truly treating the calamity of forced cleverness as a calamity does one avoid calamity; the sage escapes calamity because he is always alert to it. The main meaning of the Classic has now been made clear, so I will not belabor it. Next, let me explain in detail the hidden secrets of internal-alchemy practice concealed within it.

In general, when you sit in stillness and apply yourself, you must first begin by cultivating stillness in the "Palace of Li" (the heart-spirit). Sit solidly for a stretch of time, then examine and verify it yourself: is the mind truly empty and open, clinging to nothing? Only once you have confirmed this may you turn downward to the "Water Bureau" (the kidneys and the lower Cinnabar Field) to seek the mystery. Why? Because cultivating stillness in the Palace of Li is cultivating nature: only when the mind is empty and clean of things have you begun to illumine the mind and see your nature. That is why I often say: at the very start of sitting, let this mind hang loosely in the boundless Great Void; wait until the body is settled and the heart and breath are calm and even, then slowly use the True Intention to gather the heart-spirit back and turn its light upon its own palace (the place of spiritual brightness within the heart). When not the slightest stray matter lingers in your breast, then from that moment a single thought of awareness and illumination holds even the ten directions and three realms within its clear contemplation. Yet this awareness itself neither arises by deliberate effort nor falls into dull extinction; it is simply clear and distinct, open and at ease, thus-as-it-is and unmoving.

When you seek the mystery from such a pure state of stillness, the source of life's waters is supremely limpid (the clear source), and it can naturally serve as the foundation for forming the elixir. In that moment of utmost stillness, the light of Nature of the original mind will appear of itself. How do you recognize it? It is what I pointed out to you repeatedly in recent days: within the dim and obscure, a point of clear awareness suddenly stirs into motion of its own accord (the Living Zi Hour). The most crucial secret of cultivating the Way is, at the outset, "using nature to gather in the feelings," that is, using the pure, empty and numinous true nature to govern the surging feeling-breath of the Posterior Heaven. If you have not yet recognized your own original true nature and rashly grope downward in the Water Bureau for the mystery, think about it: if you do not even have true nature in hand, what will you use to hold the feeling-breath that stirs below? Conversely, once the empty, numinous nature is established, it can naturally attract the substantial feeling-breath; a single yang then sprouts and stirs, and between the kidneys in the lower abdomen there naturally arises a faint itching, warmth, and a dense, burgeoning vitality.

You must also understand that the "Li" of the alchemical classics does not refer only to the anatomical heart. Whatever gathers in the hearing ("concentrating the ear's resonance"), holds the gaze within (lowering the curtains and containing the light), and refrains from the outward-running senses of sound, color, smell, taste, touch and mental objects, all of this is the pure fire of Spirit taking charge, and so it is all called "Li." Likewise the "Kan" of the alchemical classics does not refer only to the two kidneys: this whole body of flesh and blood is nothing but Essence, and wherever there is Essence there is nothing but Breath; the root place where Essence and Breath gather is all called "Kan." When I shine the pure Spirit into the blood, vessels, Essence and Breath, and use the gentle fire of Spirit to warm and nourish the Water Bureau, the breath may not stir right away at first. One must keep it company patiently, lifting and supporting it above and below, gently gathering the faint stirring of vitality from the Perineum (the Sea Bottom) back into the lower Cinnabar Field, and cook and refine it gently for a long time. When the work of fire phasing has accumulated sufficiently, suddenly the heavenly mechanism within stirs, vitality surges and leaps throughout the body, spreading from the ten fingers through the whole body, as if trembling and welling up without cease; at this moment the back is upright like a standing wall, while thought is calm as cold ashes, and the Cinnabar Field is warm and melting. This is precisely the perfectly balanced moment when the Essence, blood and breath are "neither too old nor too young." If this sign of the fire's effect has not yet appeared, the Medicine is still too young, and you must not rashly kindle the fire and refine it. Conversely, if this scene appeared long ago but you did not know to seize it in time and kindle the fire, then by the time you try to apply the fire after the breath has scattered in vain, it has become "the medicine is too old and of no use." Students of the Way must observe this carefully here and distinguish it clearly.

That said, when a single yang has only just begun to stir, the breath may not be so abundant right away. As long as the mind is settled and comfortable in the moment and the breathing is harmonious and natural, you may also go with it and turn the Zi–Wu River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit). Understand that the blood in the human body, which has shape and substance, if not warmed, nourished and forged for a long time by the pure fire of Spirit, remains in the end only a lump of heavy, turbid Posterior-Heaven blood. Only by the warm illumination of the fire of Spirit can one draw forth and refine, out of the Posterior-Heaven Essence and blood, that single point of clear, numinous true breath. It is just as the Buddhist scripture says, "practicing in this evil world of the five turbidities, one attains the fruit of the Way," and as the ancient alchemical classics say, "take the treasure from the ghosts' den; seek the lead beneath the black mountain." In the end, it all means refining that single point of Prior-Heaven true breath out of turbid Posterior-Heaven Essence and ordinary blood by means of the pure fire of Spirit.

Once the true breath stirs and true yang is born, one must next understand the rules of fire phasing: advancing the yang fire at the Zi hour, withdrawing the yin tally at the Wu hour, and warm nourishing ablution at the Mao and You hours. Only then can one truly gather this point of true yang and let it circulate through the body: inwardly sweeping away the yin turbidity accumulated in the organs, outwardly attracting the clear, numinous, harmonious yang breath of heaven and earth. Applying oneself like this over many days, even a person's physical and mental temperament will be thoroughly transformed. The single method of the River Chariot truly holds inexhaustible subtleties.

The ancients spoke of "understanding the drawing-off and adding of Zi and Wu." "Drawing off" means drawing off the true-yang lead hidden in the Water Bureau (Kan); "adding" means replenishing the numinous mercury of the Palace of Li (the heart-spirit) (Lead and Mercury). "Mercury" is the numinous fluid produced in the heart, the Prior Heaven emerging within the Posterior Heaven, the Sweet Dew produced by forging the Essence and blood of the physical body with the fire of Spirit. "Lead" is the true breath contained within Essence and blood, which the ancients called "the gold within water." Yet these two medicines, "the Prior within the Posterior," can only strengthen and fill out the ordinary mortal body; they cannot yet give birth to the spontaneous Dharma Body that transcends the physical frame. They are only the preliminary medicines produced by the conjunction of Kan and Li and do not yet count as the ultimate divine elixir. One must go a step further, using true nature to gather in the feeling-breath and making the feeling-breath return completely to true nature, so that nature and feeling blend in deep harmony and are warmed and forged together in the Kun furnace (within empty, still concentration). When suddenly a surge of the purest true yang stirs, that is what is called the union of Qian and Kun forming the Great Elixir; only at this stage can one refine the divine elixir and become a true immortal.

In sum, however much is said about internal alchemy, its core is nothing other than the progressive stages of turning the River Chariot. In the River Chariot of the initial pass, because Posterior-Heaven habits are heavy, one still needs a little deliberate intention to guide it with some effort; in the River Chariot of the middle pass, the inner breath responds to the heavenly mechanism, which is "heaven and the human acting together"; and in the River Chariot of the upper pass, one follows entirely the spontaneous mechanism of heaven and earth, neither early nor late, simply not missing its moment. As for the detailed rules such as ablution at Mao and You, they are merely protective methods set up out of concern that beginners may become restless and agitated and let the fire grow too fierce; once the practice is truly mature, one need not cling even to the methods themselves. All of this depends on practitioners themselves understanding it with a sensitive mind in actual practice and grasping it with spiritual clarity.

Key Terms

  • Light of Nature: The numinous awareness and illumination that appears naturally when the original mind is empty, bright and free of dust, as distinct from the "light of cognition" produced by Posterior-Heaven thinking and guesswork.
  • Conjunction of Kan and Li and Qian and Kun: This chapter strictly distinguishes two levels. Warming and nourishing Essence and blood with the fire of Spirit, drawing off lead and adding mercury, is the "conjunction of Kan and Li," a return to the Prior Heaven from within the Posterior Heaven that mainly strengthens the mortal body. Only gathering the feelings back into nature with the empty, numinous true nature, blending them and forming the elixir, belongs to the "union of Qian and Kun," which can form the divine elixir and achieve the spontaneous Dharma Body.
  • Lead and Mercury (drawing off lead and adding mercury): The true breath within Kan is "lead" (the gold within water); the numinous fluid and true Spirit within Li are "mercury." Taking the true-yang breath of the Water Bureau upward to aid the heart-spirit, so that water and fire complete each other, is called the drawing-off and adding of Zi and Wu.
  • Fire phasing and old or young medicine: Forcing the fire before the breath is sufficient is "young medicine"; applying the fire only after the breath has scattered is "old medicine." Warming and nourishing just at the moment of still, melting warmth when vitality is perfectly balanced is called hitting the center.
  • The River Chariot of the three passes (Celestial Circuit): The three stages in which the inner breath circulates along the vessels and is refined: the initial pass is guided with a slight intention, at the middle pass heaven and the human act together, and at the upper pass one follows pure spontaneity.
  • Ablution (ablution at Mao and You): A regulating method in which, when the fire reaches its point of balance and harmony, one pauses all thought of advancing or withdrawing and rests in warm stillness, so as to prevent restlessness of mind and dry, agitated fire.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Break the habit of "seeing through" other people. Constantly speculating about others' motives and predicting success or failure in daily life may seem clever, but it is exactly the "sickness that exhausts the mind and drains the breath" that Huang Yuanji exposes. A little less self-made calculation and a little more clear, matter-of-fact awareness will spare you more than half of your spirit and energy at once.
  2. Admitting "I don't know" is the mind's umbrella. "Treating sickness as sickness, one is free of sickness": only by facing your blind spots openly will you avoid drifting ever further into arrogance and prejudice, and only then can you truly hear honest advice and see things as they are.
  3. In sitting, settle the "mind" first; don't rush to work on the "breath." Huang Yuanji warns again and again, "first cultivate stillness in the Palace of Li, then seek the mystery in the Water Bureau." Even in ordinary relaxed sitting, first let your thoughts settle; above all, do not force yourself to guard the Cinnabar Field or chase sensations of breath while restless and agitated.
  4. Methods are crutches; set them down once you are fluent. Whether counting breaths or regulating fire, these are all provisional devices for steadying the mind; do not lock yourself into shackles of rules and frameworks.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Conjecture passed off as brightness" (yiduo yi wei ming): Yiduo means guessing or speculating; the allusion is to the Analects ("Xianjin"): "he speculates, and often hits the mark."
  2. "Practicing in this evil world of the five turbidities, one attains the fruit of the Way": Draws on the sense of the Amitābha Sūtra and the Lotus Sūtra: Śākyamuni Buddha attained supreme perfect awakening in an evil world of five turbidities (the turbidity of the age, of views, of afflictions, of beings, and of lifespan). Huang Yuanji uses it as a metaphor for refining Prior-Heaven true breath out of heavy, turbid Posterior-Heaven flesh and blood.
  3. "Take the treasure from the ghosts' den; seek the lead beneath the black mountain": A common metaphor in the alchemical classics: from the Essence and blood of the lower Cinnabar Field, which ordinary people regard as a place of yin turbidity, one refines Prior-Heaven true yang (lead, the gold within water) by means of true Spirit.
  4. "Concentrating the ear's resonance, containing the eyes' light": Key words of the alchemists for gathering in external entanglements: not letting the ears and eyes chase outward after things, but turning the power of hearing and seeing back to shine within.
  5. Textual note: In the original title "第七十一章 一 知不知上," the "一" is a misread dash and has been changed to a middle dot. In the Classic, 太上日 is corrected to 太上曰; 逆料平事 to 逆料乎事; 照彻三干 to 照彻三千; 营回之苦 is corrected to 营图之苦 according to sense and graphic form; in "惟以强知之惠为惠……所以无惠," all five instances of 惠 are corrected to 患 in light of the surrounding 实为大患 and 免于患; 大旨巴明 is corrected to 大旨已明; 一阳荫动 to 一阳萌动; 徽阳初动 to 微阳初动; both instances of 卵酉沐浴 to 卯酉沐浴; 脏腐之阴私 to 脏腑之阴私; 甘簬 to 甘露; and 炼神舟 to 炼神丹.

Chapter 10 · Concentrating Breath to Attain Suppleness

This chapter traces the complete nine-stage arc from laying the foundation, refining Essence, turning the Celestial Circuit, attaining suppleness, washing the heart, and nurturing the embryo through sending out the Yang Spirit—all anchored in dissolving action into non-action within the dim and obscure

About This Chapter

If Chapter 1 is the master outline of the entire classic, then Chapter 10 is the first complete end-to-end roadmap of internal alchemy in Huang Yuanji's Commentary on the Daodejing—unpacking just fifty-six classical characters line by line into nine full stages from laying the foundation and refining Essence all the way to liberating the embryo and sending out the spirit:

  1. Bearing the corporeal soul and embracing the One (§2): Initiating "refining Essence into Breath" (the first pass of the three transmutations)—illuminating the Cinnabar Field with Spirit-fire so heart-yang enters kidney-yin; catching the first subtle stirring of yang at the Mysterious Pass to gather it back into the central field; then turning the River Chariot (Celestial Circuit) from the Caudal Funnel up to the Muddy Pellet, transforming it into Sweet Dew, and guiding it down the Twelve-Storied Tower into the Crimson Palace.
  2. Concentrating Breath to attain suppleness (§2): Advancing yang fire calls for firm vigor, while retreating yin tallies calls for utter gentleness—sending the descending breath quietly back into the central furnace until breathing seems to vanish and the limbs grow soft as a newborn infant's, the authentic sign of the Elixir congealing.
  3. Cleansing the mysterious mirror; loving the people and governing the state (§2): After water and fire unite, one must wash the mind clean of stray thoughts and tend the single true lead alone—"no mind in the mind, emptiness itself emptied"—then nurture the Holy Embryo effortlessly, where steady Essence is "peace for the people" and abundant Breath is "wealth for the state."
  4. Opening and closing the Gate of Heaven, clear penetration in all directions, and Mysterious Virtue (§2, §3): When maturity opens the Gate of Heaven, posterior cognitive spirit transforms wholly into open, luminous Original Spirit, mirroring every phenomenon without raising any self-conscious thought of "knowing." Though the nine stages advance step by step, the overarching key never leaves "guarding the center and embracing the One, abiding in the dim and obscure, and dissolving action into non-action."

Safety note: This chapter presents the full classical internal alchemy map—including advanced imagery and terminology such as "starting fire at the Caudal Funnel, turning the River Chariot to the Muddy Pellet, descending the Twelve-Storied Tower to the Crimson Palace, and the Yang Spirit exiting through the crown." Such texts belong to classical alchemical theory; never attempt to force energy up the spine into the head or chase "spirit-exit" or "crown-opening" experiences, which can easily trigger headaches, insomnia, blood-pressure fluctuations, or emotional instability. For everyday cultivation, draw solely on its mental keys: softening the breath, clearing the heart of clutter, and following nature.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：载营魄抱一，能无离乎？专气致柔，能如婴儿乎？涤除玄览，能无疵乎？爱国治民，能无为乎？天门开阖，能无雌乎？明白四达，能无知乎？生之畜之，生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰，是谓玄德。

Lord Lao says: Bearing the blood-vitality and corporeal soul while embracing the One—can you keep them from ever parting? Concentrating Breath until it reaches utmost suppleness—can you be like a newborn infant? Cleansing and scouring the mysterious mirror of inner vision—can you leave not a single blemish? Cherishing the state and ordering the people (in self-cultivation, conserving Essence and enriching Breath)—can you do so through effortless non-action? As the Gate of Heaven opens and closes—can you go beyond merely holding to the passive feminine (or harmonize stillness and power)? Clear and penetrating in all four directions—can you abide free of calculating knowledge? Giving birth to them and rearing them—giving birth without possessing, acting without presuming, fostering growth without dominating: this is called Mysterious Virtue.

§2
Original (Chinese)

此章开口，即说炼精化气之道。既得精气有于身，即要一心一德而不使偶离，离则精气神三宝，各分其途，不能会归有极，以为炼丹之本。故太上曰：「载营魄抱一，能无离乎？」夫营者，血也，血生于心，魄藏于肺，其必了照丹田，一心不动，日魂方注于月魄之中，月乃返而为纯乾。此由心阳，入于肾阴，神火照夫血水，虽水冷金寒，却被神火烹煎，而油然上升，自蓬勃之不可遏。至人知此玄牝为天地人物之根，于是一呼一吸间，微阳偶动，即一眼觑定，一手拿住，运一点己汞以迎之，左旋右抽，提回中田，凝聚不散，即载魄而返，抱一而居，不片刻间，而真阳大生，真气大动矣。由是运行河车，自虚危穴起火，引至尾闾，敲九重铁鼓，运三足金蟾，上升于顶，俱要一心专注，不贰不息。及至升上泥丸，牟尼宝珠已得，若不于此温养片时，则泥丸阴精不化，怎得铅汞融和，化成甘露神水，以润一身百脉？既温养泥丸矣，复引之下重楼，入绛宫，即午退阴符也。但进火之时，法取其刚，非用乾健之力，真金不能自升；退符之候，法用其柔，非以柔顺之德，阳铅依然散漫，不能伏汞成丹。故曰：「专气致柔，能如婴儿乎？」其意教人，于阴生午后，一心朗照，任其气机下降，如如自如，了了自了，却不加一意，用一力，此即坤卦柔顺利贞，君子攸行之道也。至绛宫温养，送归土釜，牢牢封固，惟以恬淡处之，冲和安之，一霎时间，气息如无，神机似绝，此致柔也。温养片晌，神气归根，自如炉中火种。久久凝注，不令纷驰，自然真气流行，还转周身，一心安和，四肢苏软，不啻婴儿之体，如絮如缕，有柔弱不堪任物之状，此是征丹凝之象。从此铅汞相投，水火既济，又当洗心涤虑，独修一味真铅。有心一走作，丹即奔驰，不惟丹无由就，即前取水乡之铅，亦不为我有。《清静经》云：「心无其心，物无其物，空无所空，无无亦无，湛然常寂。」又何瑕疵之有？故曰：「涤除玄览，能无疵乎？」倘外丹虽得，内照不严，则人欲未净，天理未纯，安得一粒黍珠虚而成象？到得丹有于身，犹须保精裕气以成圣胎。虽然，其保精也，要顺自然；其裕气也，须随自在，此不保之保胜于保，不裕之裕胜于裕。否则，矜持宝贵，鲜不危焉。夫以丹为先天元气，无有形状，何须作为？若执迹象以求，未免火动后天，而先天大道亡矣。故曰：「爱民治国，能无为乎？」民，比精也；国，喻气也。治世之要，推恩以爱民，立法以治国，霸者之驩虞小补，大远乎王者之无为而治。重熙累洽，气象所争，在有为、无为间耳。治身之道，以精定为民安，以气足为国富。炼己则精定，直养则气足，极之浩然刚大，充塞两间，亦若视为固有之物，平常之端，不矜功能，不逞才智，浑浑沌沌，若并忘为盈满者然，无为也而大为出焉矣。学人到此，精盈气足，养之久久，自然裂顶而出，可以高驾云霞，遨游海岛，视昔之恪守规中，专气致柔者，大有间矣。故曰：「天门开阖，能无雌乎？」此言前日调神养胎，不能不守雌也；而今则阳神充壮，脱离凡体，冲开天门，上薄霄汉，诚足乐也，气何壮乎！到此心如明镜，性若止水，明朗朗天，活泼泼地，举凡知觉之识神，化为空洞之元神矣，前知后晓，烛照靡遗，此明明白白，所以四达而不悖也。然常寂而常照，绝无寂照心，常明而常觉，绝无明觉想，殆物来毕照，不啻明镜高悬，无一物能匿者焉，而要皆以无为为本，有为为用。当其阳未生，则积精累气以生之；及其阳已生，则宝精裕气以畜之。迨其后留形住世，积功累仁，虽生而不夸辅育之功，为而不恃矜持之力，长而不假制伏之劳，一劫此心，万劫此心，真可为天上主宰分司造化之权，是以谓之「玄德」。

This chapter opens directly with the Way of "refining Essence into Breath" (the first pass of the three transmutations). Once Essence and Breath are gathered in the body, one must hold them with single-minded steadiness without letting them part for a single instant; should they part, the Three Treasures—Essence, Breath, and Spirit—go their separate ways and can no longer converge at the central pivot to form the root of the Elixir. Hence Lord Lao asks first: "Bearing the blood-vitality and corporeal soul while embracing the One—can you keep them from parting?"

What is meant by "blood-vitality and corporeal soul" (yingpo)? Ying is nutritive blood born in the heart, while po is the corporeal soul stored in the lungs (see ethereal and corporeal souls). One must illuminate the Cinnabar Field steadily with an unmoving heart so that the solar hun pours into the lunar po, turning the waning moon back into pure Qian. This is heart-yang descending into kidney-yin, Spirit-fire gently warming the blood and water below: however cold the water and chill the metal may originally be, softly heated by true Spirit-fire they rise spontaneously in an unstoppable surge of vitality!

Knowing that this Mysterious Female is the root of heaven, earth, humanity, and all creatures, the realized person watches between one exhalation and one inhalation: the instant a faint spark of yang stirs, he fixes one steady eye upon it, takes firm hold with one sure hand, meets it with his own quiet True Intention ("self-mercury"), gently draws it back into the central field, and holds it together without scattering—that is "bearing the corporeal soul back and abiding in the One." Within moments, true yang arises mightily and true Breath stirs in full!

Next comes turning the River Chariot (Celestial Circuit): lighting the fire from the Xu-Wei cavity in the lower back and drawing vitality up through the Caudal Funnel—in traditional metaphor, "striking the nine-tiered iron drum and moving the three-legged golden toad"—ascending along the spine to the crown, all with single-minded, unbroken concentration. Upon reaching the Muddy Pellet at the crown, the radiant Maṇi pearl is obtained; yet one must pause to warm and nurture it there briefly, for otherwise the yin essence in the Muddy Pellet will not transform, and how could Lead and Mercury blend into fragrant Sweet Dew to irrigate all hundred channels of the body? Once warmly nurtured at the Muddy Pellet, it is guided gently down the Twelve-Storied Tower of the throat into the Crimson Palace—this is what alchemists call "retreating the yin tally at the Wu hour."

Here fire phasing makes a decisive distinction: while advancing yang fire upward at the Zi phase, the method demands firm vigor—without the steadfast strength of Qian, heavy true gold cannot ascend; yet when retreating the yin tally downward at the Wu phase, the method demands utmost gentleness—without yielding suppleness, the newly risen yang lead scatters instead of subduing mercury to form the Elixir. Thus the classic immediately asks: "Concentrating Breath to attain suppleness—can you be like a newborn infant?"

This instructs the practitioner, during the afternoon Wu retreat as yin arises, to simply hold a bright, serene awareness and let the vital flow descend of itself—utterly natural, utterly clear—without adding a single deliberate thought or ounce of force. This is the yielding, auspicious steadiness of Hexagram Kun along which the noble person walks. Warmed in the Crimson Palace and returned to the central "earthen cauldron," it is gently sealed and tended in plain tranquility and soft harmony: in an instant, outer breathing seems to cease and mental machinery falls silent—this is true "attaining suppleness"! Nurtured thus for a short while, Spirit and Breath return to their root like live embers banked in a furnace. Held softly without scattering, true Breath naturally circulates throughout the entire body: the heart rests in deep peace and all four limbs turn warmly supple like a newborn infant's—soft as cotton floss or silk threads, too yielding even to lift a weight. This is the authentic sign that the Elixir has congealed!

From then on, with Lead and Mercury united and water and fire harmonized, one must thoroughly wash the heart and clear away stray worries, cultivating solely this single taste of "true lead." Let the mind wander for a moment and the Elixir scatters—not only will the Elixir fail to mature, even the true lead gathered from the watery realm of Kan will slip away! As the Classic of Purity and Stillness says: "No mind in the mind, no thing in things, emptiness itself emptied, non-non-being also gone—limpid and ever still." How could the slightest blemish remain? Hence the classic asks: "Cleansing the mysterious mirror—can you leave no blemish?" If outward energetic signs appear while inward vigilance grows slack, so human cravings remain unwashed and heavenly principle impure, how could that luminous millet-pearl ever take form out of emptiness?

Once the Elixir is present within, one must still conserve Essence and enrich Breath to mature the Holy Embryo. Yet conserving Essence must follow nature, and enriching Breath must move in complete ease—this "conserving without tensely conserving" far surpasses anxious guarding, and "enriching without straining" far surpasses forced effort! Otherwise, hovering nervously over it like a fragile treasure almost always invites danger. For the Elixir is the formless One Breath of Prior Heaven—what need has it for artificial meddling? Clinging to tangible signs only stirs posterior mundane fire and destroys the prior-heaven Way!

Hence the classic asks: "Loving the people and governing the state—can you practice non-action?" In alchemical metaphor, "the people" stands for Essence and "the state" stands for Breath. In governing a realm, dispensing petty favors to "love the people" and piling up harsh regulations to "govern the state" are merely the short-lived patches of a hegemon, worlds apart from the sage-king's effortless rule—the divide between enduring golden-age peace and restless turmoil lies solely between forced action and non-action. Ordering one's own body obeys the exact same rule: settled Essence is "peace among the people," and ample Breath is "wealth of the state." Faithful refining of the self settles Essence; gentle direct nurturing fills Breath. Even when flood-like vitality grows vast enough to fill heaven and earth, regard it as ordinary and innate—never boasting of attainment, never parading cleverness, resting in undivided simplicity as though forgetting fullness altogether: utterly non-acting, yet giving birth to the greatest achievement!

At this height, with Essence brimming and Breath full, long quiet nurturing brings the Yang Spirit to maturity so that it naturally breaks out through the crown, soaring free above the clouds and roaming over cosmic seas—a world apart from the earlier stage of tightly guarding the center and softly concentrating Breath! Hence the classic asks: "As the Gate of Heaven opens and closes—can you go beyond keeping to the feminine?" Earlier, while settling Spirit and nursing the embryo, one had to stay yielding and "keep to the feminine"; now, with the Yang Spirit robust and free of mortal bounds, bursting through the Gate of Heaven (sending out the spirit) into the high heavens—what boundless joy, what majestic vigor!

Here the heart is a spotless mirror and inner nature still water—bright sky above, lively earth below—and every trace of calculating cognitive spirit transforms into open, vacuous Original Spirit, clearly perceiving past and future and illuminating all things without omission: crystal clear, penetrating all four quarters without obstruction! Yet ever still and ever illuminating, there is no thought of "I am still and illuminating"; ever clear and ever aware, there is no notion of "I am clear and aware"—like a bright mirror hung high, reflecting whatever comes without letting a single thing hide, grounded always in non-action as substance and action as function.

Before yang arises, one accumulates Essence and Breath to give birth to it ("giving birth"); once yang has arisen, one treasures Essence and Breath to nourish it ("rearing"). Later, staying in the world to build merit and kindness, one gives life without boasting of fostering, acts without presuming on effort, and guides growth without dominating—the very same pure heart across one kalpa and ten thousand kalpas, truly fit to share in Heaven's work of cosmic creation: therefore it is named "Mysterious Virtue."

§3
Original (Chinese)

此将筑基得药、炼己还丹、脱胎得珠，九节工夫，一一说出，要不外虚极静笃，含三抱一，恍惚杳冥为主；自守中以至还丹，皆离不得浑有知于无知，化有为于无为。夫以先天一元真气，隐于虚无，不在见见闻闻之地。人能泯其知觉，去其作为，则一元真气常在。故太上曰：「惚兮恍，其中有象；恍兮惚，其中有物；杳兮冥，其中有精。」此可知，道生天地，原是浑浑沌沌，无可拟议。惟浑其神知，没其见闻，道即在其中矣。倘起大明觉心，则后天识神应念而起，已非先天元神，故必恍惚中求，杳冥中得，修士其亦知所从事矣。

This chapter lays out one by one the full nine stages of internal alchemy—from laying the foundation, harvesting the Medicine, refining the self, and forming the Returned Elixir, all the way to liberating the embryo and attaining the luminous pearl. Yet when ten thousand threads are gathered back to one point, the master key remains nothing other than "utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, uniting the Three Treasures to embrace the One, and abiding in the dim and obscure"! From the first step of guarding the center all the way to completing the Returned Elixir, not a single step can depart from two maxims: dissolve deliberate knowing into undivided unknowing, and transform contrived action into natural non-action.

Why? Because the One Primordial True Breath hides deep within emptiness and non-being, never in the surface realm of what ordinary eyes see and ears hear. Whenever a person lets go of discursive sensing and sets aside forced contrivance, that One Primordial True Breath stands ever present. Thus Lord Lao declares later in Chapter 21: "Dim and vague, yet within it are images; vague and dim, yet within it is reality; obscure and dark, yet within it is Essence."

From this we know that the Way engendering heaven and earth has always been undivided and whole, never captured by calculating concepts. Simply soften restless cleverness and let outward sights and sounds sink away, and the Way is right there in the midst! Conversely, if during quiet sitting you insist on raising a tense, hyper-alert mind ("I must stay sharply aware and monitor every single sensation!"), posterior cognitive spirit immediately leaps out on cue, and it is no longer prior-heaven Original Spirit. Therefore, throughout the entire alchemical path, one must seek within the dim and vague and attain within the obscure and deep—earnest cultivators now know clearly how to set about their work!

Key Terms

  • Bearing the corporeal soul and embracing the One (zai yingpo baoyi): Huang Yuanji reads ying as heart-blood and po as lung-soul, together representing bodily vitality and soul; "embracing the One" means gathering Essence, Breath, and Spirit in the central palace without parting—the start of refining Essence into Breath.
  • Advancing yang fire / retreating yin tally (fire phasing): While turning the Celestial Circuit, guiding vitality up the Governing Vessel past the Caudal Funnel to the Muddy Pellet calls for firm focus ("advancing yang fire"); after nurturing and generating Sweet Dew, letting it descend the Twelve-Storied Tower into the Crimson Palace and back to the center calls for effortless softness ("retreating yin tally").
  • Cleansing the mysterious mirror (dichu xuanlan): Xuanlan refers to the clear inner mirror of awareness; "cleansing" means washing away human cravings and wandering thoughts to hold single-minded purity without blemish.
  • Opening and closing the Gate of Heaven and keeping to the feminine: During early embryo nurturing one stays gentle and still like a brooding bird ("keeping to the feminine"); once maturity allows the Yang Spirit to exit the crown ("Gate of Heaven") beyond mortal limitation, one is no longer confined to passivity and shines forth as unobstructed Original Spirit.
  • Dim and obscure: The undivided, softly open state transcending tense self-monitoring (dominated by cognitive spirit)—the single unbroken heart-method running through all nine stages of this chapter.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Practice "rhythmic switching between firmness and softness," never white-knuckling nonstop. Like "firm when advancing fire, gentle when retreating the tally": give clean focus when daytime work demands effort, then immediately shift into infant-like "concentrated suppleness" once the task wraps up so nerves and breath truly recover.
  2. "Conserving without tensely conserving" beats anxious control. Whether caring for your health, raising children, or leading a team, gripping tightly out of anxiety backfires fastest; lay a sound foundation, then give natural growth room to breathe.
  3. Set aside a few minutes daily to "cleanse the mysterious mirror." The mind gathers dust each day from emotions, feeds, and comparison; sitting quietly for five minutes before sleep—no phone, no scorecard—lets the mirror clear again.
  4. Watch out for "hyper-monitoring" during quiet sitting. While relaxing or meditating, avoid constantly interrogating yourself—"Am I calm yet? Where is the breath now?" That tense scrutiny pulls you straight back into the calculating mind; allowing a gentle, unhurried ease is far more restorative.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Cherishing the state and ordering the people" vs. "loving the people and governing the state": Early editions of the Daodejing (such as Heshang Gong's) read 爱国治民, while Wang Bi's standard edition reads 爱民治国. Huang retains 爱国治民 in the classic text and uses the idiomatic 爱民治国 in his commentary, reading "people" as Essence and "state" as Breath.
  2. "Xu-Wei cavity," "nine-tiered iron drum," "three-legged golden toad": Classical internal alchemy imagery—Xu and Wei are the two northern lunar mansions marking the Zi position (lower abdomen/kidney vitality), while striking the iron drum and moving the golden toad depict vitality surging past the Caudal Funnel up to the Muddy Pellet.
  3. "Lead of the watery realm" and "single millet-pearl": Allusions to Zhang Boduan's Awakening to Reality (Wuzhen pian) and classic alchemical imagery of the luminous Returned Elixir ("mysterious pearl like a grain of millet").
  4. "Short-lived patches of a hegemon" and "enduring golden-age peace": The first adapts Mencius ("Jin Xin I": "Under a hegemon the people are pleased for a moment; under a true king they are vast and at ease"); chongxi leiqia describes deep, lasting peace and moral harmony.
  5. "As the Gate of Heaven opens and closes, can you be without the feminine?": Editions divide between 能为雌乎 ("be the feminine," Heshang Gong) and 能无雌乎 ("be without the feminine," Wang Bi). Following 能无雌乎, Huang ingeniously harmonizes both phases: keep to the feminine while nurturing the embryo, then transcend passivity when the Yang Spirit matures.
  6. Textual note: Base-edition errors corrected: stray 一 in title removed, 日 → 曰, 人于/人绛宫 → 入, 已汞 → 己汞, 尾间 → 尾闾, 军牢 → 牢牢, 一篓 → 一霎, 真错/水乡之错 → 铅 (following Wuzhen pian), 泰珠 → 黍珠, 邀游/视晋 → 遨游/视昔, 宵汉 → 霄汉, 尤一物能置 → 无一物能匿, and 香冥/今 → 杳冥/兮.

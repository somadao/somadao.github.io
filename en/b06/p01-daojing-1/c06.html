Chapter 6 · The Valley Spirit Never Dies

The Valley Spirit is the vacuous, luminous principle; the Mysterious Female is the opening-and-closing Breath of yin and yang; tending them gently at the threshold of stillness and motion—neither forgetting nor forcing—brings forth True Embryonic Breathing and the Golden Elixir

About This Chapter

Chapter 6 is only twenty-six characters long, yet generations of internal alchemists have prized it as a core oral formula. Huang Yuanji dissects "Valley Spirit" and "Mysterious Female" with crystalline precision—neither floating into empty abstraction nor getting stuck on physical anatomy:

  1. Valley Spirit and Mysterious Female—one principle, one Breath (§2, §3): "Valley" conveys mountain-hollow emptiness; "Spirit" conveys unfettered, responsive liveliness; "never dies" means wakefully clear and never obscured—this is the principle of the Supreme Ultimate. "Mysterious" (xuan) is Heaven (yang), "Female" (pin) is Earth (yin); the vacuous gateway where heaven and earth unite in opening and closing is the Gate of the Mysterious Female—this is the Breath of yin and yang. Using the hollow Mysterious Female to nurture the luminous Valley Spirit is the practical marvel of drawing forth the formless through the perceptible.
  2. "Used downward by ordinary folk, it is the door of death; inverted by the sage, it is the gate of life" (§2): At this very same root of heaven and earth, letting vitality leak outward along posterior cravings exhausts life and hastens decay; reversing and gathering it inward replenishes Essence and Breath and extends years.
  3. Never cling to a fixed bodily spot; watch the "threshold of stillness and movement" (§2, §3): When Embryonic Breathing first stirs, never clutch rigidly at the Cinnabar Field, but observe its natural rise and fall "neither inside nor outside." Stillness alone is not the root, nor is movement alone; solely the exact threshold where stillness and movement meet is the true Root of Heaven and Cavern of Earth.
  4. The fire-phasing of "continuous as if abiding, used without strain" (§2, §3): Nurturing cannot be rushed and cannot be neglected—neither clinging nor abandoning, neither forgetting nor forcing. With steady, patient tending, true Embryonic Breathing stirs of itself; once stirred, proper fire phasing avoids all reckless forcing or meddling.

Safety note: This chapter discusses "stirring of Embryonic Breathing," "going out the Mysterious and entering the Female," and "neither forgetting nor forcing." When practicing quiet seated breath observation, heed Huang Yuanji's repeated caution: "never rigidly cling to the Cinnabar Field" and "admit no anxious impatience." Never intentionally hold your breath to chase "breath suspension" or "Embryonic Breathing"; let soft, unhurried breathing and ease of body and mind be your measure.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：谷神不死，是谓玄牝。玄牝之门，是谓天地根。绵绵若存，用之不勤。

Lord Lao says: The vacuous, luminous, ever-wakeful "Valley Spirit" never dies; the pivot where it operates and manifests is called the "Mysterious Female." The gateway through which the Mysterious Female opens and closes is called the root of heaven and earth. Continuously, gossamer-fine, it seems ever present; draw upon it calmly, and it never strains or runs dry.

§2
Original (Chinese)

修炼一事，只缘人自有身后，气质拘于前，物欲蔽于后，犹精金良玉，原无瑕疵，因陷于污泥之中，而金之精者不精，玉之良者不良，所以欲复原形，非用淘汰之力、琢磨之功，不能还乎初质也。太上示人下手之工曰：「谷神不死。」何以为谷神？山穴曰谷，言其虚也；变动不拘曰神，言其灵也。不死，即惺惺不昧之谓也。人能养得虚灵不昧之体，以为丹头，则修炼自易。然而无形无影，不可捉摸，必于有声有色者，而始得其端倪。古云：「要得谷神长不死，须从玄牝立根基。」何以谓之玄？玄，即天也。何以谓之牝？牝，即地也。天地合而玄牝出，玄牝出而阖辟成，其间一上一下，一往一来，旋循于虚无窟子，即玄牝之门也。孔子曰：「乾坤，其易之门。」不诚然乎？第此门也，阴阳往来之路，天地造化之乡，人物发生之地，得之则生，失之则死，凡人顺用之则为死户，圣人颠倒之则为生门。人欲炼丹，以成长生久视之道，舍此玄牝之门，别无他径也。非天地之根而何？修士垂帘观照，混沌无知时，死凡心也；忽焉一觉而动，生道心也，所谓「静则为元神，动则为真意」。是其中胎息一动，不要死死执著丹田，必于不内不外之间，观其升降往来，悠扬活泼，即得真正胎息矣。古人云「出玄入牝」，是出非我本来面目，入亦非我本来面目，惟此一出一入间，中含妙谛，即虚灵也，所谓「真阴真阳形而为真一之气」是也。天地之根，岂外此乎？要之，谷神者，太极之理；玄牝者，阴阳之气。其在先天，理气原是合一；其在后天，理气不可并言。修道人，欲寻此妙窍，著不得一躁切心，起不得一忽略念，惟借空洞之玄牝，养虚灵之谷神，不即不离，勿忘勿助，斯得之矣。故曰：「绵绵若存，用之不勤。」

Why must human beings cultivate at all? Simply because once we take on a physical body, temperament hems us in from the start and material cravings cloud us afterward—like fine gold and pure jade that originally had not a single flaw, yet fell into mud and mire until the gold lost its luster and the jade lost its glow. To restore their original state requires washing away the grime and patient polishing before their initial purity returns.

Pointing out where to begin practice, Lord Lao opens with: "The Valley Spirit never dies." What is the "Valley Spirit"? A hollow mountain cleft is a "valley," expressing unobstructed emptiness; unrestrained, responsive liveliness is "Spirit," expressing bright awareness; "never dies" means clear, wakeful alertness that never grows dim. If one can nurture this vacuous, luminous, unclouded substance as the Elixir-seed, cultivation becomes effortless.

Yet because it has neither shape nor shadow and cannot be grabbed directly, we must catch its first clue through what has tangible sound and movement. Thus an old alchemical saying teaches: "To keep the Valley Spirit forever undying, you must lay its foundation in the Mysterious Female." What is meant by "Mysterious" (xuan)? Heaven (yang). What is meant by "Female" (pin)? Earth (yin). When heaven and earth unite, the Mysterious Female appears; once it appears, opening and closing take place—vitality rising and falling, coming and going, revolving gently inside the Cave of Emptiness: this is the Gate of the Mysterious Female! Confucius says in the Book of Changes, "Qian and Kun—are they not the gateway of Change?" Is that not the very same truth?

Understand well that this gateway is the thoroughfare of yin and yang, the native ground of cosmic creation, the birthplace of humanity and all things: gain it and you live, lose it and you decline. Ordinary people let it run outward along posterior cravings, turning it into the "door of death"; realized sages invert and gather it back, making it the "gate of life." Anyone wishing to refine the Elixir and realize the Way of longevity and clear vision has no other path apart from this Gate of the Mysterious Female—what else could be the "root of heaven and earth"?

When a practitioner sits quietly, lowering the curtains of the eyes to look inward, entering undivided unknowing where scattered thoughts cease is letting the restless mundane mind die away; then, when a single spark of clear awareness suddenly stirs out of stillness, that is giving birth to the mind of the Way—what alchemists describe as "in stillness it is Original Spirit, in movement it is True Intention."

At that moment, the instant Embryonic Breathing gently stirs within, never cling rigidly to a fixed physical spot in the Cinnabar Field; rather, in that open space "neither inside nor outside," watch its natural rising and falling, unhurried and effortlessly alive—that is attaining genuine Embryonic Breathing! The ancients spoke of "going out the Mysterious and entering the Female": exhalation alone is not my original face, nor is inhalation alone my original face; right in that subtle transition between going out and coming in lies the wondrous truth—vacuous luminosity itself, what the classics call "true yin and true yang coalescing into the True One Breath." How could the root of heaven and earth lie anywhere else?

In sum, the "Valley Spirit" is the principle of the Supreme Ultimate, and the "Mysterious Female" is the Breath of yin and yang. In Prior Heaven, principle and Breath are seamlessly one; in Posterior Heaven, the two cannot simply be conflated. Whoever seeks this wondrous opening must admit not a shred of anxious haste and entertain not a single careless thought—simply use the hollow opening-and-closing Mysterious Female to nurture the luminous Valley Spirit, neither clinging nor letting go, neither forgetting nor forcing ("neither forgetting nor helping it grow"). Thus it is naturally realized—hence the classic says: "Continuously, as if abiding; draw upon it without strain."

§3
Original (Chinese)

大道无形，生育天地；大道无名，发育万物。圣人以有而形无、实而形虚，显呈此至隐至微之一物，曰谷神。谷神者，空谷之神，问之若答，应焉如响，即不死也。其在人身，总一虚灵不昧之真。自人丧厥天良，谷神之汩没者久矣！后之修士，欲得谷神长存，虚灵不昧，以为金丹之本，仙道之根，从空际盘旋，无有把柄，惟从无欲观妙、有欲观窍下手，有无一立，妙窍齐开，而玄牝立焉。故曰：「此窍非凡窍，乾坤共合成。名为神气穴，内有坎离精。」总要精气神三者，打成一片，方名得有无窍、生死门。否则，为凡窍，而无先天一元真气存乎其中。虚则落顽空，实则拘形迹，皆非虚灵不昧之体。惟此玄牝之门，不虚不实，即虚即实，真有不可名言者。静则无形，动则有象，静不是天地之根，动亦非人物之本，惟动静交关处，乃坎离颠倒之所，日月交光之乡，真所谓「天根地窟」也。学人到得真玄真牝，一升一降，此间之气，凝而为性，发而为情，所由虚极静笃中，生出法象来。知得此窍，神仙大道，尽于此矣。其曰「绵绵若存」者，明调养必久，而胎息乃能发动也；曰「用之不勤」者，言抽添有时，而符火不妄加减也。人能顺天地自然之道，则金丹得矣。

The Great Way is formless, yet it engenders heaven and earth; the Great Way is nameless, yet it rears all creatures. The sage uses "being" to silhouette "non-being" and "solidity" to silhouette "emptiness," gently displaying this most hidden, subtlest reality and naming it the "Valley Spirit." The Valley Spirit is like the echo-spirit of an empty mountain valley: call out and it answers at once, responding clearly without ever failing—that is what "never dies" means. Within a human being, it is simply that vacuous, luminous, unclouded authenticity. Alas, since people lost touch with their innate conscience, this Valley Spirit has long lain buried!

When later practitioners wish to keep the Valley Spirit ever present and unclouded as the root of the Golden Elixir and foundation of the immortal Way, spinning around in bare empty space gives no handhold; one can only begin from Chapter 1's "observing marvels without desire and observing the opening with desire." Once non-being and being stand balanced, the wondrous opening swings wide, and the Mysterious Female is established!

Thus an ancient immortal verse proclaims: "This opening is no ordinary bodily opening—Qian and Kun join together to form it; it is called the Cavity of Spirit and Breath, holding within it the true essence of Kan and Li." In short, Essence, Breath, and Spirit must meld completely into one before it deserves the name "Opening of Being and Non-Being" or "Gate of Life and Death." Otherwise it is merely a dead physical spot devoid of the One Primordial True Breath: leaning toward emptiness drops you into stagnant "stubborn vacuity," while leaning toward solidity traps you in physical fixation—neither is the vacuous, luminous true substance.

Only this Gate of the Mysterious Female is neither empty nor solid, yet at once empty and solid—truly beyond words. In stillness it has no form; in movement it shows living signs. Stillness alone is not the root of heaven and earth, nor is movement alone the origin of living beings; solely the exact junction where stillness and movement turn into one another is the place where Kan and Li invert and unite, the home where sun and moon mingle their light—truly worthy of the name "Root of Heaven and Cavern of Earth"!

Once a student reaches this genuine Mysterious Female rising and descending, the primordial Breath moving there condenses as inner nature and radiates outward as true feeling; from utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, authentic alchemical realities naturally arise. Truly recognize this single opening, and the entire Great Way of the immortals lies right here.

As for the closing lines: "continuously, as if abiding" makes clear that gentle tending must be sustained patiently over time before genuine Embryonic Breathing stirs of itself; "use it without strain" warns that every adjustment of fire phasing has its natural timing and must never be forced by impatient adding or subtracting. Simply accord with the natural Way of heaven and earth, and the Golden Elixir is attained.

Key Terms

  • Valley Spirit (gushen): "Valley" denotes unobstructed emptiness, "Spirit" denotes responsive liveliness, and "never dies" denotes wakeful, unclouded clarity—our innate vacuous-luminous substance (principle of the Supreme Ultimate).
  • Gate of the Mysterious Female (Mysterious Female, Cavity of Spirit and Breath, Root of Heaven and Cavern of Earth): Xuan is heavenly yang and pin is earthly yin; not a fixed physical organ, but the living gateway appearing when Essence, Breath, and Spirit merge at the threshold of stillness and movement.
  • Embryonic Breathing (True Breath): Fine, continuous inner breathing that manifests naturally after deep stillness without forceful mouth-or-nose manipulation, observed effortlessly "neither inside nor outside."
  • Neither forgetting nor forcing (wuwang wuzhu): From Mencius ("Gongsun Chou I"), adopted by internal alchemists as the supreme rule of breath and Spirit cultivation—neither letting awareness drift away nor anxiously trying to force results.
  • Stubborn vacuity (wankong): A lifeless, dull emptiness devoid of vitality and clear awareness—the exact opposite of the alchemical ideal of utmost emptiness that remains vividly luminous.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Notice the "tiny pause" between breaths. The quiet hinge where an exhalation finishes and the next inhalation has not yet begun is the easiest everyday glimpse of the "junction of stillness and movement"—simply sense it for a few breaths without controlling it, and the mind settles swiftly.
  2. Replace "pushing hard" with "neither forgetting nor forcing." Whether in quiet sitting, study, or recovery, slacking off lets focus scatter ("forgetting"), while anxious pushing spoils the work ("forcing"). Keeping a light, steady, "gossamer-continuous" rhythm carries you farthest.
  3. Never grip a rigid spot in the body. If focusing on the navel or brow during relaxation creates tightness or pressure, widen your awareness immediately into an open sense of space "neither inside nor outside" so body and mind breathe freely again.
  4. Protect clear wakefulness. Real rest is neither numbing yourself with endless scrolling nor sinking into foggy stupor, but giving yourself ten quiet minutes now and then to let the dust-covered "fine gold and pure jade" breathe.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Qian and Kun—are they not the gateway of Change?": From the Book of Changes, "Appended Remarks II."
  2. "This opening is no ordinary bodily opening...": A famous Southern-lineage stanza widely attributed to Bai Yuchan and quoted in Xingming guizhi and Xiuzhen shishu, warning against equating the Mysterious Pass with a fixed anatomical spot.
  3. "Neither clinging nor abandoning, neither forgetting nor forcing": Wuwang wuzhu comes from Mencius ("Gongsun Chou I," on nourishing flood-like qi), taken by Ming–Qing alchemists—especially Huang Yuanji—as the golden standard for tending the Mysterious Pass and Embryonic Breathing.
  4. "Use it without strain" (yong zhi bu qin): Traditional commentators read qin either as "exhausted" or as "laborious / forced"; Huang Yuanji ties it tightly to alchemical fire-phasing—never recklessly adding or subtracting effort.
  5. Textual note: Base-edition errors corrected include a stray 一 in the heading, 日 → 曰 (four times), 面何 → 而何, 人 → 入 (three times in 出玄入牝), 至徽 → 至微, 泪没 → 汩没, and 静鸟 → 静笃.

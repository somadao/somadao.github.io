Commentary on the Daodejing

道德经注释 · By Huang Yuanji (Qing) · Huaxia Publishing House, Essentials of the Daoist Gate (Gao Cheng punctuated edition) · 2016

The Commentary on the Daodejing (also called Lectures on the Daodejing) was written chapter by chapter, at his students’ request, by the great inner-alchemy master Huang Yuanji (style name Shang, from Fengcheng in Jiangxi) while he was teaching at the Leyu Hall in Sichuan in the Guangxu reign of the Qing dynasty. It is a practitioner’s commentary on the Daodejing, and it forms a pair with Book Three, Huang Yuanji’s Heart-Method of Quiet Practice for Nourishing Life, Annotated (drawn from his Sayings from the Leyu Hall). Most commentators on Laozi discuss doctrine; Huang Yuanji brings every line down to body and mind, nature and life, the Mysterious Pass, gathering the spirit and regulating the breath, and keeping reverence in daily life, while showing that “cultivating oneself and governing the world are one and the same thread: nothing but reverence.” Following the Essentials of the Daoist Gate (punctuated by Gao Cheng, Huaxia Publishing House, 2016), this site presents Huang Yuanji’s Preface, his General Purport, and all eighty-one chapters of the Classic of the Way and the Classic of Virtue.

How to read

  1. Every chapter follows the same order: About This Chapter → Original Text and Translation (first the classic text with a direct rendering, then Huang Yuanji’s commentary paragraph by paragraph) → Key Terms → Putting It into Practice → Translator's Notes. The classical Chinese original is folded away; you can read the translation alone and follow everything.
  2. Huang Yuanji wrote at the end of the Qing and quotes the Book of Odes, the Book of Changes, the Great Learning, the Doctrine of the Mean, Mencius and the Song Confucians at length. The translation aims to bring out the full meaning of the classical text and its pointers for inner-alchemy practice, rather than echoing its wording.
  3. The chapter titles are the four-character headings added by Mei Ziqiang in the base edition. The Classic of the Way (Chapters 1–37) and the Classic of Virtue (Chapters 38–81) are each divided into four groups for easier reference.
  4. Descriptions of advanced stages, such as the first stirring of the Mysterious Pass, embryonic breathing, turning the River Chariot, and the fire phasing of gathering and refining, are for understanding only; do not imitate them on your own. If you feel unwell during quiet sitting, stop at once.

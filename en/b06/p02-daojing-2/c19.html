II · Classic of the Way (Ch. 11–20) · 道经

Chapter 19 · Little Selfishness and Few Desires

Abandoning sageliness and discarding cleverness does not reject virtue or wisdom, but trims showy polish to thicken substance; displaying plainness, embracing simplicity, and curbing desire restore the Prior Heaven mind from which the true Elixir forms

About This Chapter

Continuing directly from the previous chapter's lament over declining morals, Laozi prescribes what sounds at first like a shocking cure: "Abandon sageliness and discard cleverness, and the people will benefit a hundredfold; abandon humaneness and discard righteousness, and the people will return to filial piety and parental kindness; abandon craft and discard profit, and thieves and bandits will vanish."

Readers across centuries have been puzzled: does Laozi actually want to destroy wisdom and virtue? Huang Yuanji's reading is remarkably deft—neither turning Laozi into an anti-intellectual nor watering down his edge—peeling back three rich layers:

  1. The essence of "abandoning and discarding" is "trimming showy polish to enrich inner substance." Huang Yuanji divides each pair into "substance" (zhi) and "adornment" (wen): sageliness, humaneness, and skill are inner substance, whereas showy cleverness, rigid righteousness, and grasping profit are outward adornment. True supreme sageliness looks simple, supreme humaneness looks unassuming, supreme skill looks clumsy; once display, calculation, and greed are drawn back into quiet non-action, genuine substance overflows and the people naturally "display plainness, embrace uncarved simplicity, have little selfishness, and hold few desires."
  2. Preserving the mind and nourishing inner nature come first in refining the Elixir. Applied to internal alchemy, if one does not first restore one's mind and nature to unforced simplicity and works solely with the cognitive, calculating mind, Essence remains ordinary essence and Breath remains ordinary breath—never capable of fusing into the Prior Heaven Elixir. Only when the true sovereign head is established at the root does every step of refining Essence, Breath, and Spirit ring true.
  3. Open-minded scholarship: recording an alternative Confucian–Daoist reading. At the end of the chapter, Huang Yuanji also cites and praises another commentary that reads the first two clauses as ironic warnings (abandoning what ought *not* to be abandoned breeds harm) and the third as direct counsel (abandoning craft and profit eliminates banditry), showing his generous breadth of learning.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：绝圣弃智，民利百倍；绝仁弃义，民复孝慈；绝巧弃利，盗贼无有。此三者，以为文不足，故令有所属。见素抱璞，少私寡欲。

Laozi says: Abandon showy sageliness and discard sharp cleverness, and the people will gain a hundredfold benefit. Abandon ostentatious humaneness and discard rigid righteousness, and the people will return to natural filial piety and parental kindness. Abandon scheming craft and discard greedy profit, and thieves and bandits will cease to exist. These three sayings alone seem too plain and sparing as outward directives, so give the people's hearts something clear to hold to: display raw silk's plainness, embrace uncarved jade's simplicity, harbor little selfishness, and hold few desires.

§2
Original (Chinese)

天下人物之众，贤愚贵贱不等，总不外理气贯通而已。其所以扞隔不通，情暌意阻者，皆由上之人无以为感，下之人无以为化耳。古来至圣之君，顺自然之道，行无为之政，不好事以喜功，不厌事而废政，虽有聪明睿智，一齐收入无为国里、清净乡中。而下观而化，自然亲其亲，长其长，安其居，乐其业，无一民不复其性，无一物不遂其生者。此上古之世，人皆敦厚，物亦繁衍，其利不诚百倍哉？若至仁之主，素抱慈良之性、恻怛之心，一以济人利物为事，浩浩荡荡，浑浑沦沦，不言是非，不言曲直，而任天以动，率性以行，自然无党无偏，归于大中至正之域。斯民之观感而化者，为子自孝其亲，为父自慈其子，虽有不孝不慈之人，相习成风，旋且与之俱化，此何如之隆盛也耶！

Countless are the people and creatures under heaven—wise and dull, noble and humble—yet all are governed by nothing more than the unimpeded flow of heavenly principle and vital breath. Whenever barriers and friction arise, estranging hearts and blocking goodwill, it is always because those above offer no genuine inspiration and those below have nothing to transform them.

The supremely sagely rulers of antiquity followed the natural Way and practiced effortless governance: never meddling to chase flashy exploits, yet never growing lazy and neglecting public duties. Even though they possessed keen intelligence and deep foresight, they gathered it all quietly into the realm of non-action and the country of purity. Watching them, the people were naturally transformed: cherishing their own kin, honoring their elders, dwelling securely in their homes, and finding joy in their work—not a single person failing to recover their good nature, not a single creature failing to flourish. In that ancient age, people were warm and generous and harvests abundant—was the people's benefit not truly a hundredfold?

And look at the supremely humane ruler: steadily harboring a gentle nature and compassionate heart, wholly devoted to aiding humanity and benefiting all creatures—spacious and undivided, never harping on rights and wrongs or debating crooked and straight, moving simply with Heaven and acting from innate nature. Free of cliques or bias, he abides in the great centered uprightness. Moved by his quiet example, children naturally honor their parents and parents naturally cherish their children; even the occasional unfilial or harsh soul is soon gently transformed by the wholesome climate around them. What a flourishing era that is!

§3
Original (Chinese)

后世聪明绝顶、敏捷超群之君出，其宰物治世，不知道本无为，顺而导之则易，逆而施之则难，故或喜纷更而扰民，设法兴条，卒至国家多难，民不聊生。或好功烈而荒政，穷兵黩武，卒至府库空虚，民不堪命，无怪乎民穷国病，攘窃劫夺之风起，而盗贼公行天下。若是者，皆由至巧之君，不知用巧于无为之天、自在之地，欲富国而贪利，以至国势不振，民风不靖如此也。苟能至巧无巧，如其心以出之，顺其势以导之，「正其谊不谋其利，明其道不计其功」，「君子之德风，小人之德草」，自然如水之趋下，火之炎上，有不可遏抑者焉。斯时之民，犹有不顾廉耻，作盗贼，好非为者乎？无有也。

In later ages, dazzlingly brilliant, hyper-nimble rulers appeared. Managing affairs and ruling the realm, they failed to grasp that the Way is rooted in non-action—easy when guiding along the natural grain, grueling when forcing against it. Thus some delighted in constant upheaval that harassed the people, piling on clever statutes and nitpicking regulations until calamities multiplied and citizens could barely scrape by. Others craved military glory while neglecting domestic care, exhausting armies in endless wars until treasuries stood empty and the populace collapsed under the strain. Small wonder the people grew impoverished, the state sickened, theft and pillage flared up, and bandits roamed openly across the land!

All of this happened because overly clever rulers did not know how to place their skill within effortless ease and natural freedom; eager to enrich the realm yet driven by greed for profit, they left the state enfeebled and society turbulent. Were a ruler truly able to practice "supreme skill that appears artless"—acting from a sincere heart, guiding along the natural momentum, embodying Dong Zhongshu's maxim "Uphold righteousness without scheming for profit; clarify the Way without calculating private credit," and Confucius's saying "The virtue of the noble ruler is wind, the virtue of the common folk is grass; when wind blows over grass, it surely bends"—then public goodness would flow as irresistibly as water rushing downward or fire rising upward. At such a time, would anyone still cast aside shame to turn thief or commit outrages? Not a single soul!

§4
Original (Chinese)

此大圣若愚，大仁若忍，大巧若拙，后人视之，若有不堪为君、不足为政者然。然而圣德之涵濡，仁恩之感被，知巧之裁成，虽文采不足于外，而质实则多于内也。理欲原不相谋，足于外，自歉于中，减其文，自饶其实。圣之所以弃智，仁之所以弃义，巧之所以弃利，无非自敦其实，自去其文而已。虽然，下民至愚，恒视上之所为以为去就。如此黜华崇实，自使小民一其心于本原之地，而不彫不琢。盖所见者，为质实无文之政；斯所抱者，皆太璞不凿之真。如此浑完自然，衣服饮食，各安其常，酬酢往来，各率其分，虽气禀有限，难保无私欲之偶萌，然亦少矣寡矣。总之，圣也、仁也、巧也，皆质也；智也、义也、利也，皆文也。绝圣弃智，绝仁弃义，绝巧弃利，皆令文不足，质有余，而各有专属也。民之食德饮和于其中者，又乌有不利益无穷，孝慈日盛，盗贼化为良善耶？此隆盛之治，吾久不得而见之矣。

This is why "supreme sageliness looks simple, supreme humaneness looks unsentimental, and supreme skill looks clumsy." Short-sighted observers of later ages glance at such a ruler and imagine him unfit to wear the crown or run a government. Yet the quiet soaking of sagely virtue, the wide embrace of humane grace, and the apt shaping of true skill—however plain and unadorned on the outside—overflow with rich substance within!

Heavenly principle and selfish desire never pull in the same direction: lavish polish outside always leaves emptiness inside, whereas trimming outward show naturally thickens inner substance. Hence why true sageliness discards showy cleverness, true humaneness discards stiff labels of righteousness, and true skill discards greedy profit: solely to deepen substance and shed empty ornament. Moreover, ordinary folk are plain-hearted and always take their cue from what those above actually do. When leaders reject flash and honor substance, the people naturally settle their hearts at the quiet root, uncarved and unspoiled. Because what their eyes behold every day is plain, honest governance, what they embrace in their hearts is unmarred primal truth. Whole and natural in this way—content with ordinary clothing and food, keeping proper measure in everyday dealings—though mortal temperament has its limits and a flicker of private desire may still stir now and then, it becomes very little and very rare indeed.

In summary, sageliness, humaneness, and skill are all inner substance (zhi); cleverness, rigid righteousness, and profit-seeking are all outward show (wen). "Abandoning sageliness and discarding cleverness, abandoning humaneness and discarding righteousness, abandoning craft and discarding profit" all serve to pare down empty show and enrich solid substance so that every heart finds its proper anchor. Living amid such grace and drinking in such harmony, how could the people fail to reap boundless benefit, grow daily in filial love and kindness, and see even bandits turn into good citizens? Alas, how long it has been since I saw such a flourishing age!

§5
Original (Chinese)

此喻修养之道，先要存心养性。心性一返于自然，斯后天之精气，亦返于先天之精气。倘未见性明心，徒以后天气质之性、知觉之心为用，则精属凡精，气属凡气，安得有真一之精、真一之气，合而成丹乎？修行人，须从本原上，寻出一个大本领、真头脑出来作主，于是炼精、炼气、炼神，在在皆是矣。悟得此旨，不但知太上之经，治世修身，处处一串，即四书五经，无在非丹经矣。

Applied as an analogy for self-cultivation, this chapter teaches that one must begin by preserving the mind and nourishing inner nature. The moment mind and nature return to natural plainness, Posterior Heaven essence and breath also return to Prior Heaven essence and breath (Prior Heaven / Posterior Heaven). If one has not yet clarified the mind and seen inner nature—fussing instead with habitual temperament and the calculating cognitive mind—then Essence remains ordinary essence and Breath remains ordinary breath: how could the Essence of True Unity and the Breath of True Unity (One Breath of Prior Heaven) ever emerge and fuse into the Elixir?

A practitioner must first uncover at the very source a grand foundation and true sovereign head to preside over all practice. Once that inner anchor stands firm, every stage of refining Essence, refining Breath, and refining Spirit rings completely true. Whoever penetrates this purport sees not only that governance and cultivation thread seamlessly through every line of Laozi's classic, but that even the Confucian Four Books and Five Classics are, from cover to cover, manuals of the Golden Elixir!

§6
Original (Chinese)

他注言，在上之人，绝弃圣智，而民只知有利，故趋利者百倍；绝弃仁义，而民不知爱亲，故大反乎孝慈，此不当绝弃者而绝弃之，其弊如此。至于巧利，与圣智仁义相悖，能绝之弃之，盗贼何有？此当绝弃者，而绝弃之，其效如此。此讲甚高。三者以下，谓治民不必以令，但命令必本于躬行所系属者为要。见素则识定，抱璞则神全，少私寡欲，所谓「有天下而不与也」，非裕无为之化者，曷克臻此？

Another commentary offers a different reading: if those in power actually threw away sageliness and wisdom, the populace would care for nothing but gain, so profit-seekers would multiply a hundredfold; if they threw away humaneness and righteousness, people would forget how to love their kin and would turn completely against (fu read as "rebel against") filial piety and kindness—showing the dire harm of discarding what ought *not* to be discarded. Craft and profit-seeking, by contrast, run directly counter to sageliness, wisdom, humaneness, and righteousness; thoroughly discarding them leaves no room for thieves or bandits—showing the clear benefit of discarding what *ought* to be discarded.

This interpretation is remarkably lofty! As for the lines beginning "These three...", they mean that ruling the people does not require a flood of decrees—any instruction issued must be rooted in what the leader personally embodies. Displaying plainness settles cognitive restlessness; embracing uncarved simplicity keeps Original Spirit whole; holding little selfishness and few desires reaches what Confucius praised in Shun and Yu as "possessing the entire world yet having no personal attachment to it." Who but one steeped in the quiet transformation of non-action could ever reach such heights?

Key Terms

  • Prior Heaven / Posterior Heaven: this chapter states explicitly that only when mind and nature return to natural plainness do Posterior Heaven ordinary essence and breath transform into Prior Heaven true essence and breath; working solely with the Posterior Heaven cognitive mind can never form the Elixir.
  • Essence (jing), Breath / Pneuma (qi), and Spirit (shen): the Three Treasures of internal alchemy; this chapter emphasizes establishing the "true sovereign head" of preserving mind and nourishing nature first, after which refining Essence, Breath, and Spirit aligns at every step.
  • One Breath of Prior Heaven: called here "Essence of True Unity and Breath of True Unity," naturally transformed and fused only after mind and nature return to plain simplicity.
  • Displaying plainness and embracing simplicity (jiansu baopu): su is undyed raw silk and pu is uncarved jade (or timber). Huang Yuanji applies both directly to inner cultivation: "displaying plainness settles cognition; embracing simplicity keeps Spirit whole."
  • Trimming polish to enrich substance (jian qi wen, rao qi shi): Huang Yuanji's master key to the chapter's three "abandon and discard" clauses—not destroying true sageliness or kindness, but paring away flashy outward display to thicken genuine substance within.

Putting It into Practice

  1. "Trim outward show and thicken substance" in daily work and life. Periodically audit your schedule and energy: what goes toward appearances, empty formalities, and optics ("polish"), and what actually nourishes life and creates real value ("substance")? Cut the empty polish decisively and return your energy to what matters.
  2. Steady your mind before chasing techniques. Whether in health cultivation or learning any craft, if your mind is churning with anxiety, comparison, and impatience ("Posterior Heaven cognitive mind"), even the finest method breeds inner friction. Quiet your heart and lower your grasping first, and real skill will take root.
  3. Lead by plain example, not endless rulebooks. Never expect ever-longer regulations to win hearts ("piling on clever statutes"). The honesty, restraint, and steadiness people witness in you every day is the most persuasive wind ("the virtue of the noble person is wind").
  4. Allow yourself to be human—aim for "little" and "few." Laozi is deeply realistic: he does not demand that ordinary people become "zero-ego, zero-desire" overnight, but advises "little selfishness and few desires." Slightly less clinging and slightly less grasping than yesterday is solid, genuine progress.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Uphold righteousness without scheming for profit; clarify the Way without calculating private credit": Famous saying of Han scholar Dong Zhongshu recorded in the Book of Han ("Biography of Dong Zhongshu").
  2. "The virtue of the noble person is wind, the virtue of the common folk is grass": From the Analects ("Yan Yuan"), illustrating how moral leadership shapes society as naturally as wind bending grass.
  3. "Possessing the entire world yet having no personal attachment to it": From the Analects ("Taibo"), praising Sage-Kings Shun and Yu for holding supreme authority without a shred of private clinging.
  4. "Another commentary" (tazhu): The alternative reading Huang Yuanji cites and praises—reading the first two clauses as warnings against discarding virtue and reading fu as "rebel against"—stems from Ming–Qing Confucian–Daoist harmonizing commentators (echoing lines of thought found in Wang Fuzhi and Qing Neo-Confucians), illustrating Huang's open-minded approach to exegesis.
  5. Textual collation: Several base-text misprints were corrected in the Chinese source, including 日 → 曰, 容智 → 睿智, 收人 → 收入, missing 居 restored in 安其居，乐其业, 华至 → 卒至, 不请 → 不靖, 德章 → 德草, and an extraneous comma removed from 民之食德饮和.

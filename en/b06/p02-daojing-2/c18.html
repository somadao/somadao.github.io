II · Classic of the Way (Ch. 11–20) · 道经

Chapter 18 · The Decline of the Great Way

Humaneness, righteousness, filial devotion, and loyal ministers stand out only after the world loses its natural truth; internal alchemy reads them as the remedial work of refining and restoring wholeness once primal innocence is broken

About This Chapter

"When the Great Way declines, there arise humaneness and righteousness; when cleverness emerges, there arises great hypocrisy; when the six family ties are out of harmony, there arise filial piety and parental kindness; when the state falls into dark chaos, there arise loyal ministers." This is one of the most startling chapters in the Daodejing. Many first-time readers assume Laozi is attacking humaneness, filial devotion, and loyalty—yet the exact opposite is true.

Tracing two parallel threads—the decline of public morals and the inner work of self-cultivation—Huang Yuanji brings Laozi's deep sorrow into crisp focus:

  1. Laozi does not belittle virtue; he grieves the loss of the Great Way's natural truth. Just as nobody sings praises of emergency medicine while in robust health, loving parents and filial children ought to be an unspoken norm. Waiting until families fracture to award prizes for filial piety, or until a nation collapses to celebrate lonely heroes, is itself a tragedy. Huang Yuanji's lament cuts deep: "One person wins the fame of a loyal minister, while the whole realm suffers the calamity of dark chaos."
  2. Humaneness and righteousness have no fault; the ruin comes from pedantry and hypocrisy. Social decline descends from unselfconscious wholeness, to consciously promoting humaneness and righteousness, to hijacking their banner for fraud, and finally to outright deceit driven by clever calculation.
  3. Alchemical application: deliberate remedies become necessary only once primal innocence is broken. On the cultivation plane, high antiquity mirrors an intact, unspent body that naturally aligns with the Way without artifice. Only after Posterior Heaven leakage disrupts that wholeness must one invoke harmonizing yin and yang ("humaneness and righteousness"), reconciling the six sense-roots ("filial piety and kindness"), and vigorous refining ("loyal ministers") to restore order and return to seamless simplicity.

Safety note: Traditional alchemical metaphors near the end of this chapter—such as "harvesting yin to replenish yang" and "vigorous cooking and rapid refining"—refer strictly to internal balancing of yin and yang within one's own body and quiet discipline that clears away mental turbidity. They must never be misread as sexual dual cultivation or violent physical exertion. In everyday life, focus on returning to plain honesty and dropping scheming calculation.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：大道废，有仁义。智慧出，有大伪。六亲不和，有孝慈。国家昏乱，有忠臣。

Laozi says: When the natural Great Way falls into neglect, humaneness and righteousness come to be preached. When shrewd cleverness emerges, great hypocrisy arises alongside it. When the six close family relations fall out of harmony, filial piety and parental kindness stand out. When a state sinks into dark turmoil, loyal ministers appear.

§2
Original (Chinese)

尝观上古之世，俗尚敦庞，人皆浑朴，各正其性，定其命，安其俗，乐其业，一如物之任天而动，率性以行，无事假借，不待安排，顺其性之当然，有不知其所以然者。庄子谓臃肿鞅掌之徒，蠢朴劳瘁，动与天随，饶有真意，此所以「不识不知，顺帝之则」。是何如之化理哉？要不过浑浑沦沦，无思无虑，与大道为一而已矣。无如皇风日降，大道愈衰，为上者于是有仁义之说，兢兢业业，无敢或荒。夫由义居仁，亦圣贤美事，未可厚非，而特拟诸古昔盛时，大道昌明，人心浑噩，不言仁义而仁义自在个中者，固大有间矣。故太上为之叹曰：「大道废，有仁义。」由是上与下，慕仁义者窃其名，假仁义者行其诈，虽仁义犹是，而作为坏矣。此岂仁义之不良耶？殆由穿凿日甚，拘于仁、狃于义者之为害耳。然犹曰仁义也，虽不及大道之真，尚未至于大伪也。

Looking back upon high antiquity, customs were generous and plain and people lived in unvarnished simplicity. Each kept inner nature upright and life settled (inner nature and life), content with simple ways and joyful in daily work—just like natural creatures moving with Heaven and acting according to their innate nature, borrowing no external props and needing no artificial contrivance, simply living as nature prompts without even analyzing why. Like the plain, toil-worn folk Zhuangzi admired—ungainly in appearance and busy with honest labor, yet moving effortlessly with Heaven and brimming with True Intention—they embodied the Classic of Poetry's line: "Without cunning or calculation, naturally following Heaven's pattern." What a sublime order that was! In essence, it was simply living undivided and care-free, at one with the Great Way.

Alas, as the primal spirit declined day by day and the Great Way waned, rulers began propagating doctrines of "humaneness and righteousness," working earnestly lest they be neglected. In truth, dwelling in humaneness and walking in righteousness are noble ideals of sages and worthies, beyond reproach in themselves. Yet compare that with the flourishing age of antiquity—when the Great Way shone brightly, hearts were wholesome, and nobody needed to preach "humaneness and righteousness" because both were naturally present within—and the gap is vast indeed! That is why Laozi sighed: "When the Great Way declines, there arise humaneness and righteousness."

From then on, both above and below, those enamored of reputation stole the name of humaneness and righteousness, while those wearing their mask practiced fraud. The words remained unchanged, yet the motives and conduct behind them turned corrupt. Was that any fault of humaneness and righteousness themselves? Surely not—the harm came from ever heavier hair-splitting, rigid fixation on the outward label of humaneness, and stubborn posturing over righteousness. Even so, while humaneness and righteousness were still spoken of, though inferior to the Great Way's natural truth, things had not yet sunk into sheer, naked hypocrisy.

§3
Original (Chinese)

自此以后，世俗愈乖，人心弥坏，即仁义之传，其所存者亦几希。但见朝野内外，上下君臣，一以智而炫其才，一以慧而施其伎，此来彼往之内，大都尔诈我虞矣。不能一道同风，安望齐家治国？所以父子生嫌，兄弟启衅，甚至夫妇朋友、亲戚乡邻，人各一心，心各一见，几如胡越之不相亲也，何况其他？万一有子能孝，朝廷特为奖之；有父能慈，乡里共为称之。噫！父慈子孝，原属天地之常经，家庭之正轨，又何足表扬哉？乃自三党六亲不和，而忤逆之风日炽，阋墙之衅时闻，所以有能孝能慈者，固不胜郑重而表其里居，以风天下焉。不诚远逊大道隆盛之期，子有孝而不知其为孝，父克慈而并忘其为慈者哉！虽然，即此能孝能慈，亦是因不和而返为和之道。但今之世，好为粉饰，徒事铺张，言慈孝而袭取夫慈孝之名者，殊难枚举。

Afterward, customs grew even more perverse and hearts decayed further, until scarcely a remnant of genuine humaneness and righteousness survived. Across court and countryside, between ruler and minister, everyone flaunted cleverness to show off talent and wielded smart tricks to gain advantage; back and forth, almost every exchange became mutual deception. Unable to share one Way and one moral climate, how could anyone hope to harmonize households or govern states?

Consequently, resentment grew between fathers and sons, quarrels broke out among brothers, and even husbands and wives, friends, relatives, and neighbors each harbored separate agendas and private views—estranged as northern nomads from southern Yue, let alone wider society! At that point, should a single son happen to be dutiful, the court rushes to confer special honors; should a father show true kindness, the village gathers to sing his praises. Alas! Parental love and filial devotion are the most ordinary laws of heaven and earth, the natural course of every home—what is there to parade with fanfare? Precisely because extended kin and close relatives fell into discord, disrespect flared ever fiercer, and sibling feuds became commonplace, communities now solemnly erect honorary arches to hold up the rare filial son or kind father as an example to the realm. How far that falls below the flourishing age of the Great Way, when sons were naturally filial without labeling it "filial piety," and fathers were naturally loving while forgetting the very word "kindness"! Even so, genuine kindness and filial devotion amid discord still offer a path back toward harmony—except that in today's world, obsessed with cosmetic show and loud display, those who mouth filial piety simply to steal its reputation are too numerous to count.

§4
Original (Chinese)

又况五朝而后，骨肉相摧，君臣交质，无怪乎上有昏庸之主，下有跋扈之臣，而国家自此不靖矣。赖有忠肝义胆者，出而安邦定国，虽成败利钝，未可预知，而尽瘁鞠躬，一片孤忠可表，数不可回，以力挽，势不可救，以心全，如诸葛武侯之六出祁山，姜伯约之九伐中原是也。况人臣事主，愿为良臣，不愿为忠臣。幸而国祚承平，同襄补衮之职。不幸而强梁迭起，各展济世之才。世有昏乱，天所以显忠臣也；世有忠臣，天所以维昏乱也。然忠臣出矣，即使昏乱能除，一洗干戈之气，化为礼义之邦，亦不及皇古之无事远矣。呜呼！忠清之臣，愿终身埋没而不彰。不然，一人获忠臣之名，天下蒙昏乱之祸，不大可痛哉！

Worse still, in turbulent later ages since the Five Dynasties and eras of division, blood kin slaughtered one another and rulers and subjects even traded hostages out of mutual distrust. Small wonder that dim-witted lords sat above while arrogant warlords schemed below, leaving the state without a day of peace! Survival then depended on men of iron loyalty stepping forward to steady the realm. Though success or failure could never be guaranteed, they spent themselves to the last breath with solitary devotion—straining every sinew even when fate could not be turned, keeping integrity whole even when the tide could not be saved, like Zhuge Liang leading six campaigns from Mount Qi or Jiang Wei launching nine expeditions into the Central Plains.

Yet any true minister serving a sovereign would far rather be a "good minister" in peaceful times than a "loyal martyr" in chaos. In fortunate ages of peace, ruler and minister quietly mend small flaws together like stitching a royal robe; only in tragic times, when violent usurpers surge up one after another, must heroes deploy emergency measures to salvage the realm. Dark turmoil is what makes a loyal minister visible; loyal ministers are what Heaven uses to hold a crumbling world together. Still, even when a loyal minister steps forth, quells the chaos, washes away the stench of war, and restores civility, it remains worlds below the untroubled peace of high antiquity. Alas! A truly upright, devoted minister would gladly live out his entire life in obscurity rather than win fame through crisis—for otherwise one person gains the glorious title of "loyal minister," while the entire world suffers the catastrophe of dark turmoil. Is that not heartbreaking beyond words?

§5
Original (Chinese)

此太上感慨世道，伤今思古，欲人返朴还真，上与下同于无知，其德不离，同乎无欲，其道常足，熙熙皞皞，大家相安于无事而不知其所之者。即有仁义智慧，孝子忠臣，一概视为固然，不知其为有，且羞称其为有，此何如之浑朴乎？虽然，此为治世之论，推之修身之法，亦不外是。首句喻言浑沦之俗，太朴未彫，犹童贞之体，不假作为，自成道妙。若一丧其本来之天，则不得不借先天阴阳以返补之。夫阴阳，一仁义也，即「大道废，有仁义」之说也。至于审取一身内外两个真消息，凭空以智慧采之取之，温之养之，此中即不纯正，多杂后天，不能不有伪妄。此又「智慧出，有大伪」之意也。他如采阴补阳，所以和六根之不和，使归于大定，即孝慈之喻也。猛烹急炼，所以靖一身之昏乱，使跻于清明，即忠臣之旨也。知此，则道不远矣。此太上明复命归根之学，究有何道哉？不过率其浑然粹然之天而已，修之者，亦修此而已。

This chapter is Laozi's heartfelt lament over the course of the world—grieving the present, longing for antiquity, and urging humanity to return to uncarved simplicity and truth: where high and low alike abide in guileless "unknowing" so virtue never departs, and in tranquil "desirelessness" so the Way is ever sufficient, living joyfully in untroubled peace without knowing who pulls the strings. Even when humaneness, wisdom, filial children, and loyal ministers genuinely exist, everyone takes them completely for granted—unaware of anything special, even embarrassed to boast of them. How richly wholesome and simple!

Yet though all this speaks of governing the world, applied to the inner method of self-cultivation, every single line holds true without a hair's discrepancy: - The opening image of primal, uncarved simplicity mirrors an intact, unspoiled body of innocence that naturally embodies the wonder of the Way without deliberate contrivance. Once that innate wholeness is lost, one has no choice but to borrow Prior Heaven yin and yang to replenish and restore it—those paired forces of yin and yang correspond to "humaneness and righteousness," the inner meaning of "when the Great Way declines, there arise humaneness and righteousness." - Once one senses the subtle interplay of yin and yang within and without, if one tries to grasp, harvest, and nurture them using clever analytical scheming, the work loses purity, mixes in Prior Heaven / Posterior Heaven Posterior Heaven calculation, and inevitably breeds delusion—that is the inner meaning of "when cleverness emerges, there arises great hypocrisy." - Likewise, harmonizing inner true yin to replenish true yang so that the restless six sense-roots settle back into deep stillness corresponds to "when the six family ties are out of harmony, there arise filial piety and kindness." And applying vigorous fire phasing ("fierce cooking and rapid refining") to clear bodily and mental turmoil so clarity returns corresponds to "when the state falls into dark chaos, there arise loyal ministers."

Understand this layer, and the Way is close at hand. In the end, what secret does Laozi's teaching of returning to the root and restoring life come down to? Nothing more than living in accord with that seamless, pure innate Heaven—all cultivation, from first step to last, cultivates nothing other than this.

Key Terms

  • inner nature (xing) / existence, life (ming): this chapter portrays people of antiquity as "each keeping inner nature upright and life settled," living naturally without artificial contrivance.
  • True Intention (zhenyi): invoking Zhuangzi's plain, toil-worn folk who "move with Heaven and brim with True Intention," pointing to unfeigned sincerity unspoiled by calculation.
  • Prior Heaven / Posterior Heaven: reading "when cleverness emerges, there arises great hypocrisy" as a caution in internal alchemy—approaching practice with Posterior Heaven scheming contaminates Prior Heaven purity.
  • fire phasing (huohou): "vigorous cooking and rapid refining" at the chapter's close refers to the temporary martial-fire phase used to clear inner turbidity and turmoil, likened to a "loyal minister" in crisis—a remedial measure that must ultimately give way to effortless ease.
  • Good minister vs. loyal minister (liangchen vs. zhongchen): a famous saying of early Tang statesman Wei Zheng to Emperor Taizong ("Let me be a good minister, not a loyal minister"): a good minister quietly prevents trouble in peaceful times, whereas a loyal minister shines only amid national catastrophe.

Putting It into Practice

  1. See through the pattern: what gets loudly preached is often what is missing. In an organization or in one's own life, when slogans grow deafening and rulebooks cover every wall, underlying trust and harmony have usually cracked. Don't just slap on more labels—repair the quiet sincerity underneath.
  2. Be a "good minister" at work and home, not a crisis-addicted "loyal hero." Quietly preventing small problems and mending small gaps so things run smoothly without drama is far wiser than letting a mess explode and pulling heroic all-nighters to rescue it.
  3. Beware the exhaustion and phoniness of over-cleverness. Whether dealing with people or managing yourself, calculating angles at every turn ("when cleverness emerges") breeds wariness in others and leaves your own heart weary and artificial.
  4. Every technique is a remedy, not the final home. Sitting practice, breath regulation, and disciplined effort are crutches to bring us back from imbalance to balance. Once steadiness returns, dare to release strained effort and live naturally again.

Translator's Notes

  1. Plain, toil-worn folk (yongzhong yangzhang zhi tu): From Zhuangzi ("Gengsang Chu"), describing unpretentious, hard-working people free of cosmetic polish who move in quiet harmony with nature.
  2. "Without cunning or calculation, naturally following Heaven's pattern": From the Classic of Poetry ("Major Odes," Huangyi), praising guileless alignment with the Way of Heaven.
  3. "Estranged as northern Hu and southern Yue": The Hu peoples of the far north and Yue of the far south were a classical metaphor for extreme distance and mutual alienation.
  4. Three clans and six close ties (sandang liuqin): The three clans refer to paternal, maternal, and spousal lineages; the six close ties generally denote father and son, elder and younger brothers, and husband and wife (or parents, siblings, and spouse), standing broadly for immediate family.
  5. Quarrels within the walls (xiqiang): From the Classic of Poetry ("Minor Odes," Changdi), "Brothers may quarrel inside the walls, yet unite against insults from outside," denoting domestic sibling strife.
  6. Zhuge Liang's six campaigns from Mount Qi and Jiang Wei's nine expeditions: Celebrated northern campaigns of Shu-Han statesmen Zhuge Liang and Jiang Wei during the Three Kingdoms era, classic emblems of steadfast loyalty even against impossible odds.
  7. "Rather a good minister than a loyal minister": Recorded in the Essentials of Governance of the Zhenguan Reign (Zhenguan zhengyao), where Wei Zheng urged Emperor Taizong to create an age of peace where ministers could serve quietly rather than die heroically in turmoil.
  8. Textual collation: Several base-text misprints were corrected in the Chinese source, including 日 → 曰, 闲矣 → 间矣, 筹凿 → 穿凿, 面 → 而 in two places, 肯肉 → 骨肉, 祝 → 况, 思臣 → 忠臣 in two places, 忠请 → 忠清, 来 → 采 in two places, 冤有何道战 → 究有何道哉, and 而巴 → 而已.

II · Classic of the Way (Ch. 11–20) · 道经

Chapter 14 · The Image Without Image

Unseen, unheard, and ungraspable, blending into One as the Prior Heaven Breath of True Unity; internal alchemy nurtures it in the dim and obscure without stirring forced awareness

About This Chapter

This chapter presents Laozi's most direct portrait of the substance of the Way: unseen (yi), unheard (xi), and ungraspable (wei), three aspects blending into One—neither glaring above nor dark below, continuous yet unnameable, returning at last to the dim and obscure realm of "the shape without shape, the image without image."

Huang Yuanji brings this seemingly abstract ontology straight down to the decisive practical pivot of internal alchemy:

  1. Distinguishing Posterior Heaven from Prior Heaven Essence, Breath, and Spirit. Whatever eyes can see, ears can hear, or hands can seize belongs entirely to tangible Posterior Heaven Essence, Breath, and Spirit; it can only sustain a mortal frame, never accomplish the Way. Only when Prior Heaven Wood-nature, Metal-affection, and Intention-earth merge into One Breath beyond the senses do we touch the root of True Unity.
  2. "Chaotic dimness" is the guiding method from start to finish. Many assume cultivation means keeping the mind razor-sharp and consciously observing every thought. Huang Yuanji cuts right through this misconception: stirring up a forced "bright observing mind" prevents deep accumulation and slips into side-paths. Before true yang stirs, as it emerges, and as it returns to the furnace for nurturing, one abides throughout in the dim and obscure with effortless naturalness.
  3. "Hold fast to the Way of antiquity to govern the beings of today." The "Way of antiquity" is the primordial One Breath of Prior Heaven prior to heaven and earth. Grasping this unseen guiding thread to harmonize the present five senses, limbs, and myriad affairs is the straight path where substance stands firm and function flows naturally.

Safety note: Alchemical metaphors in this chapter—such as "refining Essence, Breath, and Spirit," "fetal infant," "Yang Spirit," and "returning to the furnace and tripod"—point to relaxing the mind and dropping grasping thoughts. Never attempt to force internal visualizations or willfully push breath through the body. In daily practice, simply rest in natural ease, neither dull and drowsy nor tense and strained.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：视之不见名曰夷，听之不闻名曰希，搏之不得名曰微。此三者，不可致诘，故混而为一。其上不曒，其下不昧，绳绳兮不可名，复归于无物。是谓无状之状，无象之象，是谓恍惚。迎之不见其首，随之不见其后。执古之道，以御今之有，能知古始，是名道纪。

Laozi says: Looked at, it cannot be seen—this is called yi (level, invisible). Listened for, it cannot be heard—this is called xi (rarefied, silent). Grasped for, it cannot be caught—this is called wei (minute, intangible). These three cannot be probed apart, and therefore blend into One. Above, it is not glaring; below, it is not dim. Unbroken and continuous, it cannot be named, returning once more to no-thing. This is called the shape without shape, the image without image; this is called the dim and obscure (huanghu). Meet it and you see no head; follow it and you see no tail. Hold fast to the Way of antiquity to govern the existent things of today; to know the ancient beginning is called the guiding thread of the Way.

§2
Original (Chinese)

大凡天下事，俱要有个统绪，始能提纲挈领，有条不紊，况修道乎哉？且夫大道之源，即真一之气也；真一之气，即大道之根也。何谓真一之气？《诗》曰：「维天之命，於穆不已」是。何谓大道之根？《诗》曰：「上天之载，无声无臭」是。理气合一，即道也。修士若认得这个纲纪，寻出这个端倪，以理节情，以义定性，以虚无一气为本根，长生之道得矣。如以清清朗朗、明明白白为修，吾知道无真际，修亦徒劳也。太上所以状先天大道曰：「视之不见曰夷，听之不闻曰希，搏之不得曰微。」夫心通窍于目也，目藏神，肾通窍于耳也，耳藏精，脾通窍于四肢也，四肢属脾，脾属土，土生万物，真气凝焉，即精神寓焉。若目有所见，耳有所闻，手有所把捉，皆后天有形有色、有声有臭之精气神，只可以成形，不可以成道。惟视无所见，则先天木性也；听无所闻，则先天金情也；搏无所得，则先天意土也。故曰：后天之水火土，生形者也；先天之金木土，成仙者也。其曰夷、曰希、曰微者，皆幽深玄远，不可捉摸之谓，真有不可穷诘者焉。能合五气为一气，混三元为一元，则真元一气在是，天然主宰亦在是。所以《悟真》云：「女子著青衣（火生木），郎君披素练（水生金）。见之不可用（后天水火土），用之不可见（先天木金土）。恍惚里相逢（混而为一），杳冥中有变。霎时火焰飞，真人自出现。」修士知此，即知大道之源、修道之要矣。

In all affairs under heaven, one must grasp a single guiding thread to pull the net by its lead rope and keep everything orderly—how much more so in cultivating the Way! Now the source of the Great Way is the Breath of True Unity, and the Breath of True Unity is the root of the Great Way. What is the Breath of True Unity? It is what the Classic of Poetry describes: "The mandate of Heaven—how deep, solemn, and unceasing!" And what is the root of the Great Way? As the Classic of Poetry says: "The workings of High Heaven have neither sound nor scent." Principle and Breath fused into one—that is the Way. If a cultivator recognizes this guiding thread and traces this clue, tempering emotions with principle, steadying inner nature with righteousness, and grounding oneself in the empty One Breath of Prior Heaven, the Way of longevity is secured. By contrast, if one assumes cultivation means keeping the mind crisp, analytical, and brightly inspecting everything, I know for certain one will never touch the true frontier of the Way, and all one's labor will be in vain.

That is why Laozi describes the Prior Heaven Great Way as: "Looked at and unseen, called yi; listened for and unheard, called xi; grasped for and ungraspable, called wei." In the human body, the heart opens to the eyes, and the eyes harbor Spirit; the kidneys open to the ears, and the ears harbor Essence; the spleen connects to the four limbs, and the spleen belongs to Earth, which generates the myriad things—here true Breath condenses, and here Essence and Spirit alike dwell. If the eyes see some vision, the ears hear some sound, or the hands grasp something tangible, all of these belong to Posterior Heaven Essence, Breath, and Spirit—bound to form, color, sound, and scent; they can build a physical body, but they can never accomplish the Way. Only looking without seeing accords with Prior Heaven Wood-nature; listening without hearing accords with Prior Heaven Metal-affection; grasping without seizing accords with Prior Heaven Intention-earth (True Intention). Hence it is said: Posterior Heaven Water, Fire, and Earth generate physical form; Prior Heaven Metal, Wood, and Earth accomplish immortality.

Calling them yi, xi, and wei expresses their deep, elusive mystery—truly beyond analytical dissection. When one unites the five breaths into One Breath and blends the Three Origins into One Origin, the Breath of True Origin abides right here, and so does the body's natural sovereign. Therefore Zhang Boduan's Wuzhen pian says: "The maiden wears a green robe (Fire generating Wood); the young gentleman dons white silk (Water generating Metal). What can be seen cannot be used (Posterior Heaven Water, Fire, and Earth); what is truly used cannot be seen (Prior Heaven Wood, Metal, and Earth). They meet in the dim and indistinct (blending into One), and transform within the deep obscure. In an instant flames soar upward, and the Perfected One spontaneously appears." A cultivator who grasps these lines knows both the source of the Great Way and the essential secret of cultivation.

§3
Original (Chinese)

若不知始于虚无，执着一身尸秽之气、杂妄之神，生明觉心，作了照想，吾恐藏蓄不深，发皇安畅？此炼精、炼气、炼神之功，所以不离乎混沌焉。既混沌，久之则胎婴长，阳神生，而其间毓胎养神之法，又不可不知，即前章「爱民治国，行无为道」是。阳神出入，运行自然，时而神朝于上，则不知其所自，上所以不曒也；时而神敛于下，则不忽其所藏，下所以不昧也。由此绵绵密密，继继绳绳，无可名状，亦无有作为，仍还当年父母未生之初，浑然无一物事。《易》曰：「洗心退藏于密」，是其旨矣。故云：「复归于无物。」

If one does not know to begin from emptiness, clinging instead to this frame's stale, turbid breath and scattered, deluded spirit—rousing a forced "bright observing mind" and deliberately striving for crystal-clear awareness—I fear one's inner reserve will never deepen; how could a luminous, unobstructed flowering ever unfold? That is why the entire work of refining Essence, Breath, and Spirit never departs from primordial chaos (hundun). Abiding long in this undivided chaos, the Holy Embryo grows and the Yang Spirit comes to birth; and one must also know how to nurture the embryo and tend the spirit throughout—precisely what an earlier chapter called "loving the people and governing the state by practicing the Way of non-action."

At this stage, the Yang Spirit emerges and withdraws in complete spontaneity: at times spirit ascends toward the crown without consciously plotting its rise, free of glaring self-display—this is "above, it is not glaring"; at times spirit gathers and rests below without slipping into careless stupor, still quietly aware of where it abides—this is "below, it is not dim." Continuing thus in fine, unbroken, seamless threads, beyond any name or deliberate contrivance, one returns step by step to the original state before one's parents were born, wholly undivided and empty of any fixed thing. As the Book of Changes says, "Wash the heart clean and withdraw into the hidden secret"—that is the exact purport. Hence Laozi says: "Returning once more to no-thing."

§4
Original (Chinese)

虽然，无物也，而天下万事万物，皆自此无中生来，太上所以有「无状之状，无象之象」之谓也。然究有何状、何象哉？不过恍恍惚惚中，偶得之耳。果能恍惚，真阳即生，迎其机而导之，殆不见其从何而起，是前不见其首也；随其气而引之，亦不见其从何而终，是后不见其尾也。道之浩浩如此。此不亦「大周沙界，细入毫芒」者乎？是道也，何道也？乃元始一气，人身官骸之真宰也。得之则生，失之则死，完则为人，歉则为物，所争只毫厘间耳。学人得此元始之气，调摄乎五官百骸，则毛发精莹，肌肤细腻，是谓「执古之道，以御今之有」者此也。人能认得此开天辟地，太古未有之元始一气，以为一身纲纪，万事主脑，斯体立而用自行，本正而末自端矣。倘学人，不以元始一气为本，欲修正觉，反堕旁门，可悲也夫！

Even so, while it is "no-thing," all affairs and all myriad creatures under heaven are born out of this very non-being; hence Laozi calls it "the shape without shape, the image without image." Yet what shape or image does it actually have? None save what is encountered effortlessly in the midst of the dim and obscure. When one truly enters this dim, ungrasping stillness, a spark of true yang naturally arises. Meeting its impulse and gently guiding it, one can scarcely tell where it begins—this is "meeting it, you see no head"; following its natural flow, one cannot tell where it ends—this is "following it, you see no tail." So vast and boundless is the Way! Is this not what the ancients called "vast enough to encompass worlds numerous as the sands of the Ganges, subtle enough to enter the tip of an autumn hair"?

What Way is this? It is the Primordial One Breath, the true hidden master of all our sense organs and bones. Gain it and you live; lose it and you die. Complete, it forms a human being; partial, it becomes other creatures—the margin between them is no wider than a hair's breadth. When a practitioner realizes and receives this Primordial Breath and lets it harmonize the five senses and hundred bones, hair grows lustrous and skin fine and supple: this is precisely what Laozi means by "holding fast to the Way of antiquity to govern the beings of today." If one clearly recognizes this Primordial Breath—already present before heaven and earth split apart—and establishes it as the guiding framework of one's body and the sovereign head of all affairs, then once substance stands firm its function moves of itself, and once the root is upright every branch straightens naturally. Conversely, if a student fails to take the Primordial One Breath as the root and tries to manufacture some contrived "awakened awareness," falling instead into heterodox side-paths—how tragic that is!

§5
Original (Chinese)

此状道之体。学道人，会得此体，方有下手工夫。若真一之气，是先天性命之源，非后天精气神可比。欲见真气，必将性命融成一片，始得真一之气。第此气，浑浑沦沦，浩浩荡荡，虽无可象可形，而天下之有象有形者，皆从此无形无象中出，诚为大道纲维，天地人物之根本也。道曰守中，佛曰观空，儒曰慎独，要皆同一功用。故自人视之，若无睹无闻，而自家了照，却又至虚至实，至无至有。所以子思曰：「莫见乎隐，莫显乎微。」君子慎独之功，诚无息也。要之，隐微幽独之地，虽有见显可据，而大道根源，只是希夷微妙，无可状而状，无可象而象，极其浑穆。学道人，总要于阳之未生，恍惚以待之；于阳之既产，恍惚以迎之。于阳之归炉入鼎，恍惚以保之、养之。绝不起大明觉，庶几无时无处，而不得大道归源焉。前言阳神出现，明天察地，通玄达微，及了悟之候，光明景界，纯任自然，有知若无知，有觉若无觉。况下手之初，可不恍恍惚惚，死人心以生道心乎？

This chapter depicts the substance of the Way. Only after grasping this substance does a student of the Way truly know where to begin practice. The Breath of True Unity is the shared Prior Heaven source of inner nature and life, utterly incomparable to Posterior Heaven physical essence, breath, and spirit. To encounter this true breath, one must fuse inner nature and life into one seamless whole; only then is the Breath of True Unity revealed. Undivided and vast, though it has no image or form one can point to, everything in the world possessing shape and image issues from this formless mystery. Truly it is the guiding framework of the Great Way, the common root of heaven, earth, humanity, and all creatures.

Daoism calls this "guarding the center and embracing the One"; Buddhism calls it "contemplating emptiness"; Confucianism calls it "vigilance in solitude" (shendu). At bottom, all three are the exact same work. To onlookers from outside, it seems as if nothing is seen or heard; yet in the practitioner's own quiet awareness, it is at once utterly empty and utterly real, utterly nothing and utterly everything. Thus Zisi writes in the Doctrine of the Mean: "Nothing is more visible than what is hidden; nothing is more manifest than what is subtle." The noble person's quiet watchfulness in solitude truly never ceases.

Still, when all is said and done, though real experiential evidence appears in that subtle, solitary depth, the root of the Great Way itself remains rarefied, invisible, and minute—shapeless yet shaped, imageless yet imaged, profoundly undivided and solemn. Students of the Way must remember this above all: before true yang has arisen, await it in the dim and obscure; once true yang has emerged, welcome it in the dim and obscure; when true yang returns to the furnace and tripod for warm nurturing, guard and nourish it in the dim and obscure. Never stir up a strained, over-bright analytical awareness—then at every moment and in every place you will return to the source of the Great Way. Earlier we spoke of the Yang Spirit appearing, illuminating heaven and earth and fathoming every subtlety; yet even at the stage of thorough awakening, that luminous realm is completely natural—knowing as if not knowing, aware as if unaware. If even final realization is so quietly unassuming, how much more at the very outset must one abide in the dim and obscure, letting the restless human mind die away so that the tranquil mind of the Way may be born?

Key Terms

  • One Breath of Prior Heaven: called here "Breath of True Unity," "Primordial One Breath," and "guiding thread of the Way"—the Prior Heaven root of nature and life before heaven and earth divided, transcending sensory form.
  • the dim and obscure: a deep, undivided state neither rigidly hyper-alert nor dull and sleepy; Huang Yuanji stresses meeting true yang before it arises, when it arises, and during warm nurturing all within this state.
  • Prior Heaven / Posterior Heaven: this chapter sharply distinguishes Posterior Heaven Water, Fire, and Earth (tangible essence, breath, and spirit that form the physical body) from Prior Heaven Metal, Wood, and Earth (formless true nature, true affection, and true intention that accomplish immortality).
  • True Intention (zhenyi): called here "Prior Heaven Intention-earth," the pure, unified master corresponding to the spleen-Earth that harmonizes the five breaths and three origins.
  • guarding the center and embracing the One: a central Daoist practice placed here alongside Buddhist "contemplating emptiness" and Confucian "vigilance in solitude," showing that all three traditions converge in quiet work at the subtle origin.
  • Yi, Xi, and Wei: Laozi's three names for the Way beyond sight (unseen = yi), beyond hearing (unheard = xi), and beyond touch (ungraspable = wei), inseparably blended into One.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Grasp the "guiding thread" before sorting the details. When swamped by tangled tasks or drained in body and mind, do not plunge headlong into spinning around details. Pause first to recover inner steadiness and your core direction ("hold fast to the Way of antiquity"), then meet today's concrete demands from a calm center ("govern the beings of today").
  2. In quiet practice, stop "staring hard" at your mind. Many people sitting in meditation try so fiercely to monitor thoughts or catch breath sensations that their nerves tighten further—precisely what Huang Yuanji warns against as "rousing a bright observing mind." Soften and widen your attention, allowing a relaxed, lightly aware, unforced ease ("the dim and obscure").
  3. Do not chase sensory phenomena. If lights, ringing in the ears, or subtle bodily sensations appear during quiet rest, neither celebrate nor cling to them. By Huang Yuanji's standard, whatever eyes see or ears hear belongs to Posterior Heaven form; letting them come and go ungrasped lets your stillness deepen.
  4. Practice "vigilance in solitude" in everyday quiet moments. Cultivation does not wait for a mountain retreat. Whenever you are alone and an impulse or emotion first stirs, neither indulging it nor deceiving yourself puts the shared wisdom of "guarding the center" and "vigilance in solitude" into immediate action.

Translator's Notes

  1. "The mandate of Heaven—how deep, solemn, and unceasing": From the Classic of Poetry ("Odes of Zhou," Wei tian zhi ming). Here 於 is pronounced wū (an exclamation of awe) and mu denotes profound solemnity.
  2. "The workings of High Heaven have neither sound nor scent": From the Classic of Poetry ("Major Odes," Wenwang); zai means deeds or workings, and xiu (臭) means scent.
  3. "The maiden wears a green robe... the Perfected One spontaneously appears": A quatrain from Northern Song master Zhang Boduan's Wuzhen pian (Folio on Awakening to Reality). Internal alchemy maps Five Phases generation here: heart-Fire conceals true nature returning to Wood (green robe), and kidney-Water conceals true affection returning to Metal (white silk); Huang Yuanji's parenthetical glosses highlight the contrast between Posterior Heaven Water/Fire/Earth and Prior Heaven Wood/Metal/Earth.
  4. "Wash the heart clean and withdraw into the hidden secret": From the Appended Remarks (Xici shang) of the Book of Changes, referring to rinsing away worldly calculation and resting spirit in deep stillness.
  5. "Nothing is more visible than what is hidden; nothing is more manifest than what is subtle": From the Doctrine of the Mean (Zhongyong, traditionally attributed to Zisi), explaining why the noble person remains vigilant even when unseen and alone.
  6. Textual collation: Several base-text misprints were corrected in the Chinese source, including 日 → 曰, 致请/穷请 → 致诘/穷诘, 其上不嗷 → 其上不曒 (matching the commentary's 不曒 below), 绳绳今 → 绳绳兮, 不可提摸 → 不可捉摸, 香冥 → 杳冥, 无睹无闸 → 无睹无闻, and 希夷徽妙 → 希夷微妙.

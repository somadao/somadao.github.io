Chapter 32 · Knowing Where to Stop Avoids Peril

The Way is forever nameless and the Uncarved Block, though tiny, is sovereign of the world; cultivation clarifies Lead and Mercury ascending and descending, Sweet Dew falling to the center, and resting unmoved once one knows where to stop

About This Chapter

Chapter 32 of the Daodejing begins with "The Way is forever nameless," speaks next of "Though the Uncarved Block is tiny, no one in the world dares make it a servant" and "Heaven and Earth unite to send down Sweet Dew," and concludes with "Knowing where to stop is how one avoids peril"—once names and distinctions arise, knowing the exact point at which to stop and guard the root keeps one safe from danger.

Huang Yuanji first unpacks the overall philosophical meaning, and then in the second half lays bare—with extraordinary candor—the innermost alchemical secrets of Lead and Mercury, the conjunction of Kan and Li, Sweet Dew, and stopping the fire to dwell in stillness:

  1. Doctrinal level: The Great Way is formless and nameless; spread across heaven and earth it is boundless, yet gathered into the human heart it is whole and intact like a tiny "Primordial Uncarved Block." Holding fast to this root—just as lords and kings guard the Way and all nations submit, or mountain streams flow home to rivers and seas—gives everything its proper anchor and resting place.
  2. Alchemical level: Huang Yuanji states frankly that the Supreme Patriarch spoke of "Sweet Dew" and "knowing where to stop" only in broad strokes, so he spells out the full sequence for the reader: first, Spirit-fire shines downward to transform Posterior Heaven mundane essence into luminous fluid (mundane Mercury), which descends to meet True Yang in the Yellow Court, coalescing into True Lead (obtaining the Medicine); next, it ascends from the Caudal Funnel to the Muddy Pellet, where water and fire, Lead and Mercury fuse into cool, fragrant Sweet Dew that drops from the palate into the mouth and is swallowed back into the Yellow Court for warm nourishment and sealing. At that moment the whole body feels blissfully soft and the mind rests unshaken—the direct experiential proof of "knowing where to stop avoids peril."

Safety note: The latter half of this chapter describes advanced internal phenomena including the conjunction of Kan and Li, Breath ascending from the Caudal Funnel to the Muddy Pellet, and Sweet Dew descending. Huang Yuanji himself stresses that "this state is impossible without deep stillness and absorption" and that "harvesting without harvesting surpasses deliberate harvesting." Never use conscious willpower to yank energy upward or force it into the crown of the head, as doing so easily triggers head pressure, dizziness, or mental agitation.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：道常无名。朴虽小，天下不敢臣。侯王若能守，万物将自宾。天地相合，以降甘露，人莫之令而自均。始制有名，名亦既有，夫亦将知止，知止所以不殆。譬道之在天下，犹川谷之于江海也。

The Supreme Patriarch says: The Way is forever nameless. Though the Uncarved Block is tiny, no one under heaven dares treat it as a servant. If lords and kings can guard it, the myriad things will submit of their own accord.

Heaven and Earth unite to send down Sweet Dew; no one commands it, yet of itself it falls evenly everywhere. Once institutions are established, names arise; once names exist, one must also know where to stop—knowing where to stop is how one avoids peril. To liken the Way's presence in the world: it is as mountain streams and valley brooks are to the great rivers and seas.

§2
Original (Chinese)

道本冲漠无朕，而实万象森列，无人不具，无物不有。人物未生以前，此物实为之本；人物既生以后，此物又为之根，虽至隐至微，而要不可一刻离也，离则万事万物皆瓦裂矣，故曰「道常无名」。为学人计，不得不强为之名曰黍珠一粒，阳神三寸，自在玄宫，周通法界，犹之太朴完全，其物虽小，其用则大，天下万事万物，俱赖此以为君，孰得臣而后之耶？即如侯王，操生杀之权，为万民之主，孰敢不奉其命令？人苟得此太朴，拳拳服膺，守而弗失，虽殊方异域，莫不航海梯山而来，况近者乎？可见万国宾服，皆由斯朴之能守也。夫人自有生后，气质拘之，物欲蔽之，斯道之存者几希。若欲抱朴完贞，惟效法天地而已。天气下降，地气上腾，犹人身坎离交媾，水火调和，天地相合，而甘露垂珠，自然降于中宫。此阴阳燮理，日月同宫，谁为为之，孰令听之？皆由以道为之主宰也。然道究有何名哉？或曰「真铅」，或曰「金丹」，古人制此名，皆为后之修士计耳。修士既知其名，即当求其实。彼自阴阳交媾，一点落于黄庭，就当止其所而不迁，安其居而不动，斯大道乃常存矣。既知所止，中有主而不易，又奚至生灭而遭危殆之辱耶？可见道散于外，浩淼无垠，浑沦莫测，及敛之于内，混混沌沌，退藏宥密。学者苟莫知统宗，无从归宿，则散而无纪，即立己犹不能，焉能及人？故曰：「道之在天下，犹川谷之于江海。」惟有主归，所以成其大也。子思谓「君子之道费而隐」，其即此「一本散万殊，万殊归一本」之道也欤？

The Way is inherently vast, quiet, and devoid of any visible trace, yet within it all ten thousand phenomena stand complete; no human being lacks it, and no creature is without it. Before humans and things came into existence, it was their origin; after they are born, it remains their root. Though supremely hidden and subtle, one cannot be separated from it for a single instant—separated from it, all affairs and all things shatter like broken tiles. Thus the classic says, "The Way is forever nameless."

For the sake of students, past masters had no choice but to force names upon it, calling it "a single millet-sized pearl" or "a three-inch Yang Spirit," dwelling freely in the Mysterious Palace yet pervading the entire Dharma-realm. It is like an intact, uncarved block: tiny in seeming bulk, yet boundless in function. All ten thousand things under heaven depend on it as their sovereign—who could ever reduce it to a servant and place it last? Just like lords and kings who hold authority over life and death and rule over the multitudes, who would dare disobey their command? If a person truly attains this Uncarved Block, holds it close to the breast, and guards it without letting go, even distant realms will cross seas and scale mountains to come pay homage—how much more those close at hand! Clearly, the willing submission of all lands springs entirely from guarding this Uncarved Block.

Alas, once born into the world, human beings are hemmed in by constitutional temperament and clouded over by material desires, until scarcely a trace of this Way remains. To embrace the Uncarved Block and restore pure wholeness, there is no method other than modeling oneself on Heaven and Earth. Heaven's breath descends while Earth's breath ascends—just like the conjunction of Kan and Li and the harmonizing of water and fire within the human body. When this inner heaven and earth unite, Sweet Dew forms into hanging pearls and drops naturally into the Central Palace. In this harmonious blending of yin and yang, where sun and moon share one palace, who forces it to happen? Who issues orders for it to obey? Everything is governed silently by the Way itself.

Yet what name does the Way truly possess? Sometimes it is called "True Lead," sometimes the "Golden Elixir"—the ancients coined these terms solely for the convenience of later cultivators. Once a cultivator knows the name, he should seek the reality behind it: the moment yin and yang unite and that single drop of true vitality falls into the Yellow Court, one should stop right where it belongs without shifting, settling calmly in its dwelling without stirring; only then does the Great Way abide forever. Once you know where to stop and hold an unshakable master within, how could you ever drift into endless arising and passing away and suffer the disgrace of peril?

Thus we see that when the Way spreads outward, it is vast, boundless, and unfathomably whole; when gathered inward, it is indistinct and undivided, withdrawing into deep, quiet secrecy. If a student fails to grasp this single unifying source and has no place to come home to, his mind scatters without order—unable even to establish himself, how could he ever benefit others? Therefore the classic says, "The Way in the world is as mountain streams and valley brooks are to the great rivers and seas." Precisely because there is a single sovereign destination, its greatness is achieved. When Zisi said in the Doctrine of the Mean that "the Way of the noble person reaches far and wide yet remains hidden," was he not speaking of this very Way in which "one root scatters into ten thousand particulars, and ten thousand particulars return to one root"?

§3
Original (Chinese)

此章甘露，是铅汞合而始降，知止是神气萃于中宫，太上俱浑言之，吾再详道之。学人欲修性命，先明铅汞。古云：「汞是我家固有之物，铅乃他家不死之方。」若但言心性，无从捉摸，古仙真借名为汞，此个汞非他，乃心中之灵液，从涕唾津精气血液，后天所生阴滓物中，加以神火下照，久久化为至灵之液。此个灵液，元性所寄。盖以本性原来真常清净，不染纤尘，与太空等，非从后天色身所有之精，用起文武火，加以神光了照，则灵液不化，灵性无依，故炼丹之士，必先炼精化气。所谓「其精不是凡精，乃是玉皇口中涎」，玉皇比心也，心中真液即涎也。既得精生汞化，由是灵液下降坎宫，真阳亦复上升，交会于黄庭土釜，我以神气凝住于此，久之，真铅从此蓬勃絪縕而有象，此即所谓「得药」也。然灵液即真水，真水即汞也；真阳即真气，真气即铅也。汞为精，铅为气，二者皆后天有形有象之铅汞，只可顺而生男育女，不可为长生大药。必从此汞之下降，铅之上升，会合中宫，凝神调息，片刻间，兀兀腾腾，如雾如烟，如潮如海，才算是真铅，可为炼丹之本，所谓「坎离交而得药」是也。

In this chapter, "Sweet Dew" descends only after Lead and Mercury have united, and "knowing where to stop" means Spirit and Breath gathering together in the Central Palace. Because the Supreme Patriarch spoke of both only in general terms, I will now explain them in full detail.

A student who wishes to cultivate inner nature and life must first understand Lead and Mercury. An old saying goes: "Mercury is what my own house already possesses; Lead is the deathless recipe sought from the other house." If one spoke only of "mind and nature," it would be too elusive to grasp, so ancient immortals borrowed the name "Mercury." This Mercury is nothing other than the luminous fluid generated in the heart: from among the Posterior Heaven fluids carrying yin dross—mucus, saliva, thin secretions, essence, breath, blood, and thick fluids—when one shines the fire of Spirit downward upon them, over time they are refined into a supremely luminous liquid. This luminous fluid is where Original Spirit and original nature lodge. Because original nature is inherently constant, pure, untouched by a speck of dust, and equal to empty space, unless one takes the Essence present in the Posterior Heaven physical body, applies gentle and vigorous fire, and illuminates it clearly with Spirit-light, the luminous fluid will not transform and luminous nature will have nothing to rest upon. Hence every alchemist must begin with "refining Essence into Breath." As the scripture says, "This Essence is not ordinary mundane essence, but saliva from the mouth of the Jade Emperor"—the "Jade Emperor" is a metaphor for the heart, and the true fluid refined from the heart is that clear saliva.

Once true Essence arises and Mercury transforms, this luminous fluid naturally descends into the Kan palace of the kidneys, while True Yang simultaneously ascends from below, and the two meet in the earthenware cauldron of the Yellow Court. There I hold Spirit and Breath steadily condensed; after a while, True Lead begins to billow, steam, and take palpable shape within—this is what is called "obtaining the Medicine."

Yet one must distinguish carefully: taken separately, that luminous fluid (True Water, Mercury) belongs to Essence, and that True Yang (True Breath, Lead) belongs to Breath. While apart, both are still Posterior Heaven Lead and Mercury with form and appearance—useful in the ordinary forward course for begetting sons and daughters, but incapable of becoming the Great Medicine of longevity. Only when Mercury descends and Lead ascends, uniting in the Central Palace while one condenses Spirit and practices regulating the breath, so that within moments a warm, billowing fullness arises like mist and smoke, like a rising tide and swelling sea—only then does it count as genuine "True Lead," fit to serve as the foundation for refining the Elixir. This is what is known as "the conjunction of Kan and Li yielding the Medicine."

§4
Original (Chinese)

于是运起阳火阴符，逆从尾闾直上泥丸，泥丸久积阴精，与我这点真铅之气，配合为一，即所谓「乾坤交而结丹」是也。阳气上升泥丸，有何景象？觉得头首爽利，非等平日之昏晕，有如风吹云散而天朗气清，另有一番气象，才算是真汞。以前之汞，还是凡汞，不可以养成仙胎。铅汞会于泥丸，斯时之凡精、凡气，合同而化，不见有铅，并不见有汞，只是一清凉恬淡之味，化为甘露神水，香甜可口，不似平日粗精浊气，即古人谓「醍醐灌顶」是，从上腭落下，吞而服之，送入黄庭温养，即封固矣。此个真精一生，浑身酥软如绵，欲睡不睡，欲醒不醒，而平日动荡之身心，至此浑然湛然，不动不摇，自安所止而得所止，又何殆之有哉？此境非大静大定不能。若夫采取之法，即一意凝注，毫不分散，古人谓「不采之采胜于采」是。所谓交媾者，即「神入气中，气包神外，两两不分」是。学人行一步，自有一步之效验。若无真实处，工犹未至。天机毕露，人其自取证为可。

Next, one sets in motion the yang fire and yin tally, following the path of reversal straight up along the Governing Vessel from the Caudal Funnel at the base of the spine to the Muddy Pellet palace at the crown of the head. The yin essence long stored in the Muddy Pellet blends as one with this ascending spark of True Lead breath—this is what is called "the union of Qian and Kun crystallizing the Elixir."

What does it feel like when True Yang breath ascends to the Muddy Pellet? One's whole head feels crisp, light, and lucid—nothing like ordinary dullness or dizziness—as though a cool breeze had swept away thick clouds to leave a bright, clear sky, revealing an altogether different atmosphere. Only the luminous fluid transformed at this stage truly deserves the name "True Mercury"; the Mercury prior to this pass was still mundane Mercury and could not nourish the Holy Embryo.

When Lead and Mercury unite in the Muddy Pellet, all the former mundane essence and mundane breath melt together into one; neither Lead nor Mercury can be told apart any longer. There remains only a cool, serene, delicious flavor turning into divine Sweet Dew, fragrant and sweet on the tongue, utterly unlike ordinary coarse essence and turbid breath—precisely what the ancients called "clarified ghee poured over the crown." This Sweet Dew drips down from the upper palate; one gently swallows it and escorts it back down into the Yellow Court for warm nourishment—this completes "sealing and securing."

The instant this true Essence is born, the entire body turns blissfully soft like cotton, poised in a quiet wonder neither asleep nor ordinarily awake. The body and mind that once tossed and churned every day now become seamlessly whole and crystal-clear, completely still and unshaken, settling effortlessly into their true resting place. Knowing where to stop and attaining that stop, how could peril ever touch one?

Still, let this be plain: such a state cannot arise without deep stillness and profound absorption. As for the "method of harvesting" that so many inquire about, it is nothing more than holding a single True Intention steadily focused without the slightest distraction—what the ancients meant by "harvesting without harvesting surpasses deliberate harvesting." And what is called "conjunction" is nothing strange or arcane: it simply means "Spirit enters into Breath, Breath enfolds Spirit from outside, and the two become indistinguishable."

With every honest step a student takes, a genuine verification naturally follows; if there is no real fruit in body and mind, the work has not yet matured. Here the heavenly mechanism has been laid completely bare—let each person put it to the test and verify it directly.

Key Terms

  • Primordial Uncarved Block (taipu): the uncarved, undivided state of original authenticity, symbolizing the Prior Heaven Great Way inherent in everyone.
  • Lead and Mercury: alchemical terms borrowed from external metallic alchemy to describe the interplay of yin essence and yang breath, Spirit and Breath within the body. This chapter carefully distinguishes mundane Lead and Mercury from True Lead and True Mercury.
  • conjunction of Kan and Li: the practice in which heart-fire (Li) shines downward and kidney-water (Kan) rises to meet it in the Central Palace—the key to "obtaining the Medicine."
  • Yellow Court: the central region of the abdomen, also called the Central Palace or earthenware cauldron, where Spirit and Breath meet and are warmly nourished and sealed.
  • Caudal Funnel and Muddy Pellet: the base of the spine (weilü) and the center of the brain at the crown (niwan), marking respectively the starting point and summit of the Governing Vessel ascent.
  • Sweet Dew: cool, sweet saliva naturally produced in the mouth during deep quiet stillness when Spirit and Breath harmonize, likened to "clarified ghee poured over the crown" and swallowed down to the Central Palace to nourish body and mind.
  • Harvesting without harvesting (bucai zhi cai): refraining from forceful guiding or anxious grabbing, and simply resting a single pure True Intention in quiet illumination so vitality gathers of itself.

Putting It into Practice

  1. "Knowing where to stop" is the rarest skill in modern life. Desires, overthinking, overtime, endless scrolling—when nothing has a stopping point, Essence, Breath, and Spirit leak away continuously. Set a clear daily "shutdown time" so your mind has a home to return to rather than running dry.
  2. Cultivation begins with finding your inner anchor. Like mountain streams flowing to the sea, no matter how many daily tasks pull at you, having one steady inner center of value—unshaken by outside praise or blame—eliminates most mental friction.
  3. In quiet sitting, focus on "Spirit entering Breath," never force sensations. Resting attention gently on breathing and bodily steadiness—a single calm intention without scattering—is far safer and more effective than trying to push energy along meridians by willpower.
  4. Judge your progress by genuine lived steadiness, not impressive terminology. As Huang Yuanji says, "With every step comes a step's verification": a quieter mind, a clearer head, steadier emotions are the real proof. Memorizing lofty jargon while staying agitated inside means the work has not yet begun.

Translator's Notes

  1. "Hold close to the breast and never lose" (quanquan fuying): from the Doctrine of the Mean (Zhongyong): "Whenever he attained one good thing, he clasped it to his breast and never let it go."
  2. "Cross seas and scale mountains" (hanghai tishan): sailing across oceans and setting ladders up steep peaks—a classical idiom for distant peoples braving every hardship to come offer allegiance.
  3. "The Way of the noble person reaches far and wide yet remains hidden" (junzi zhi dao fei er yin): from Chapter 12 of the Doctrine of the Mean. Fei denotes the vast breadth of the Way's function, yin the hidden subtlety of its substance. The phrase that follows—"one root scatters into ten thousand particulars, and ten thousand particulars return to one root"—is a classic Song–Ming Neo-Confucian formulation.
  4. "Mercury is what my own house already possesses; Lead is the deathless recipe from the other house": a celebrated alchemical formula rooted in the Southern Lineage, indicating that one's luminous mind-nature (Mercury) must pair with Prior Heaven vital breath (Lead) to accomplish dual cultivation of nature and life.
  5. "This Essence is not ordinary mundane essence, but saliva from the mouth of the Jade Emperor": quoted from the Song of Knocking on the Lines (Qiaoyao ge), attributed to Lü Dongbin, where the "Jade Emperor" symbolizes the heart and the saliva signifies pure luminous fluid rather than ordinary reproductive essence.
  6. "Clarified ghee poured over the crown" (tihu guanding): originally a Buddhist metaphor—tihu (Skt. maṇḍa) being the purest clarified essence refined from milk, symbolizing supreme Dharma flavor—adopted by internal alchemy for the cool, sweet fluid descending from the crown after True Breath ascends to the Muddy Pellet.
  7. Textual collation: Numerous obvious graphical errors in the base edition have been corrected from context, including 名曰 (base: 日), 守而弗失 (base: 面), 梯山 (base: 样山), 浩淼 (base: 浩森), 口中涎 (base: 延), 蓬勃絪縕 (base: 莲勃锢縕), 泥丸 (base: 泥九), 以前之汞 (base: 乘), 不采之采胜于采 (base: 来), 真实处 (base: 兵实处), and 天机毕露 (base: 毕鎝).

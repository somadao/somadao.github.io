Chapter 34 · Never Claiming Greatness

The Great Way overflows abundantly yet claims no credit; in internal alchemy, this unfolds step by step into the seamless work of refining Essence into Breath, Breath into Spirit, and Spirit into Emptiness

About This Chapter

Chapter 34 of the Daodejing portrays the Great Way as "overflowing everywhere, moving left and right": all ten thousand things rely on it for life and it never refuses; its work succeeds and it claims no ownership; it clothes and nourishes all creatures yet never acts as their master. Precisely because it stays forever without desire, claims no credit, and never considers itself great, it achieves true greatness.

Huang Yuanji again develops his commentary on two levels, and on the alchemical level holds nothing back—making this one of the clearest, most complete chapters on practical beginning methods in the entire book:

  1. The substance of the Way: The Great Way is simultaneously "so small nothing can split it"—permeating every blade of grass and tree without a gap—and "so vast nothing can contain it"—encompassing heaven, earth, past, and present without leaving anything out. United with the Way, the sage practices guarding the center and embracing the One in stillness and responds naturally to circumstances in movement; though his virtue equals Heaven's, he never thinks of himself as "great," and thereby achieves genuine greatness.
  2. Internal alchemy: Huang Yuanji translates "how the sage achieves greatness" directly into concrete practice, walking step by step through how the three transmutations (refining Essence into Breath, Breath into Spirit, and Spirit into Emptiness) flow seamlessly together within a single complete sitting: first condensing Spirit, then regulating the breath, making the mind like an empty vessel and thought like melting ice; shining Spirit-light downward into the lower Cinnabar Field (Cavity of Breath), pairing warm gentle Spirit-fire with the soft Wind of Xun so heart and kidney breaths unite and outer breathing merges with inner True Breath; then letting stillness reach its peak and stir naturally, letting go into pure emptiness, and closing by returning to the furnace and sealing. Crucially, he draws a sharp boundary between orthodox internal alchemy and heterodox side-paths: the Waterwheel circuit opens *naturally* once True Breath is full—never through selfish willpower forcing an empty rotation.

Safety note: This chapter details the sequence of "refining Essence into Breath, Breath into Spirit, and Spirit into Emptiness" along with "the Waterwheel opening naturally," yet Huang Yuanji repeatedly cautions against using subjective willpower to "run it emptily and force it mechanically," which easily causes congestion and imbalance. Daily quiet sitting should rest on relaxation, ease, and following nature—never force energy through passes by intention.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：大道泛兮，其可左右。万物恃之以生而不辞，功成不名有，衣被万物而不为主。常无欲，可名于小。万物归焉而不为主，可名于大。是以圣人终不为大，故能成其大。（泛者，滥也。）

The Supreme Patriarch says: How the Great Way overflows everywhere—it can turn to the left or to the right! The myriad things rely on it for life and it never refuses; when its work is done it claims no credit or possession; it clothes and shelters all things yet never sets itself up as their master.

Ever without desire, it may be called "small"; all things return to it yet it does not act as their master, so it may be called "great." Therefore the sage never considers himself great, and by that very fact achieves his greatness. (Note: fan here means overflowing and all-pervading.)

§2
Original (Chinese)

道本渊涵无极，浩荡无涯。《诗》曰：「左之左之，君子宜之；右之右之，君子有之。」观此可见，道之随时取用，无人不遂，无物不充焉。斯道也，何道也？万物生生之本也。道在天地，万物资以为生，而不辞其纷扰，以道无不足，故其生无不畅也。虽然，生之遂之之道既足，而物赖以成，亦若物之自生自遂，而道不见为有，其成功为奚若乎？虽不名为有，而天地之大，四海之遥，无人不被其涵濡，无物不荷其帡幪，且听物之自生自育，而道若不知其为生为育，普护一切，包涵万有，斯诚「衣被万物而不以为主」焉。道之功成，浩浩乎无可名也，常无欲也，无欲即常清常净，真常之道也。就其小而名之，虽一草一木之微，无有或外，弥纶万有，无隙可寻，浑然一团，纤尘悉化，此「小莫能破」之义也，故曰「常无欲，可名于小」。就其大而名之，铺天匝地，统育群生，亘古及今，包含万汇，而究无一物之不归并，无一夫之或外，此「大莫能载」之旨也。故曰：「万物归焉而不为主，可名于大。」圣人之道，何其费而隐哉？夫圣人，与道合真，静则守中抱一，浑同於穆之天；动则因物随缘，俨寓时行之象。惟天为大，惟圣则之，圣实与天同其大也，然圣终不以为大也。惟不以为大，故能成其大，此所以为大圣人欤？

The Way is inherently unfathomably deep, boundless, and shoreless. The Classic of Poetry sings: "Turning to the left, the noble person fits the occasion; turning to the right, the noble person has it well in hand." From this we see that the Way is available at every moment and in every direction: no human being fails to find fulfillment through it, and no creature lacks its fullness. What Way is this? It is the very root of ceaseless generation for all ten thousand things. Operating throughout heaven and earth, the Way sustains every creature's life without ever shrinking from their tangled multiplicity; because the Way lacks nothing, every life it generates flourishes freely.

Even so, though its power to give birth and bring things to maturity is completely abundant, creatures grow and fulfill themselves as though entirely on their own, while the Way shows not the slightest sign of ownership—what manner of accomplishment is that! Though it claims no title of possession, across the vastness of heaven and earth and the farthest reaches of the four seas, not a single person is outside its gentle nourishment, not a single creature fails to rest beneath its sheltering canopy. Leaving all things free to sprout and grow on their own, the Way acts as though unaware it is giving them birth and raising them—universally sheltering everything and embracing all existence. Truly this is "clothing and sheltering all things without acting as their master."

The Way's achievement is so vast it defies all naming, for it is forever free of desire; to be free of desire is to be eternally pure and clear, the true and constant Way. Speaking of it in terms of "small": even something as tiny as a single blade of grass or twig does not lie outside it; it weaves through all existence without leaving the tiniest seam, seamlessly whole, with every speck of selfish dust dissolved. That is the meaning of "so small nothing in the world can split it"—hence "Ever without desire, it may be called small." Speaking of it in terms of "great": it blankets heaven and wraps the earth, universally nurturing all living beings, spanning past and present and containing all categories of existence, until not a single thing fails to return to it and not a single person stands outside it. That is the meaning of "so vast nothing in the world can contain it"—hence "All things return to it yet it does not act as their master, so it may be called great."

How far-reaching yet deeply hidden is the Way of the sage! Merged with the truth of the Way, in stillness the sage practices guarding the center and embracing the One, seamlessly one with the deep, silent mystery of Heaven; in action he responds to things as conditions unfold, mirroring the seasons moving silently on their course. Heaven alone is truly great, and the sage alone models himself on Heaven: in reality the sage matches Heaven's vastness, yet from start to finish he never regards himself as great. Precisely because he never thinks himself great, he accomplishes true greatness—is this not why he is called a great sage?

§3
Original (Chinese)

此言道之浩浩，生万物而有余，被万物而至足，无小无大，悉包个中。圣人能成其大，皆由修造有本。今特详下手之工：如打坐之时，先凝神，继调息，到得神已凝了，不必有浩然正气，至大至刚，充塞天地，只要心无烦恼，意无牵累，觉得心如空器，一点不有，意若冰融，片念不生，此身耸立，恍如山岳静镇，不动不摇，由是以神光下照于气穴之中，默视吾阴跷之气，与绛宫之气，两相会于丹鼎之中，我即以温温神火细细烹炼，微微巽风缓缓吹嘘，自然精融气化，此即炼精化气也。何以知其炼精化气哉？前此未采外来之气，与吾心内之神，两相配合，会成一家，此个坎离各自分散，全不相依，呼吸亦不相调。到得收回外气，以制内里阴精，气到之时，阴精自化。上下心肾之气，既合为一，自然绛宫安闲，肾府自在，外之呼吸与内之真息，合为一气，浑如夫妇配成，聚而不散，日充月盈，真阳从此现象矣，此即化气之明征也。既已化气，再行向上之事。何谓向上之事？斯时呼吸合，神气交，凝聚丹田，宛转悠扬，几如活龙游泳，一日有无数变化，我惟凝神于中，注息于外，听其天然，自然静极而动，动极而静，此即炼气化神也。到得静定久久，我气益调，前此宛转流行于丹田者，此时烹炼极熟，觉得似有似无，若动若静，粗看不觉，细会始知。此际务将知觉之心，一齐泯去，百想无存，万虑全消，即丹田交会之神气，听他自鼓自调，自温自煅，我惟致虚守静，纯任自然，神入气中而不知，气周神外而不觉。如此烹炼一阵，自有一阵香风上冲百脉，遍体薰蒸，此所谓神生气也。又觉精神日长，智慧日开，一心之内，但觉一气从规中起，清净微妙，晶莹如玉，此所谓气生神也。如此神气交养，两两相生，斯时正宜撒手成空，不粘不脱，若有心，若无意，此炼神还虚之实际也。此三件功夫，一时可行可到。学人须遵道而行，不可但到神气粗交，未至大静，即行下榻；又不可但到神气大交，凝成一片，两不分明，未到虚无清净自在之境，速离坐地。必须照此行持，从炼精起，至于气长神旺，久久化为清净自然，再加归炉封固工法，然后合乎天地盈虚消息，与一年春夏秋冬气象，如此始完全一周工夫。照此修持，自然我气益调，我神益静，中有无穷变化，不尽生机。由是日夜勤工，绵绵密密，寂照同归，自有真气薰蒸，上朝泥丸，下流丹府，透百脉而贯肌肤，勃然有不可遏之状。此河车之路，自然而通，我不过顺其所通而略为引之足矣，非若旁门左道，以自家私意，空空去运，死死去行，不观他自动自静，而为之起止也。久之丹成道立，走雾飞空，与天为徒。圣人之成其大，诚非轻易也已。

This chapter speaks of the boundless vastness of the Way: it generates the myriad things with energy to spare, shelters them completely, and encloses everything great and small alike. The sage is able to achieve such greatness only because his self-cultivation rests on a solid root. Here I will spell out the practical beginning method in full detail:

When you sit down in meditation, first condense Spirit, then gently practice regulating the breath. Once Spirit is settled and gathered, there is no need to chase Mencius's "flood-like upright breath, supremely vast and firm, filling heaven and earth." Simply let the mind be free of vexation and intention be free of entanglements, feeling the heart like an empty vessel holding not a single thing, thoughts dissolving like melting ice so not a stray notion arises, while the body sits upright like a tranquil mountain peak, unmoving and unshaken.

From this state, shine the clear, gentle Light of Spirit softly downward into the lower Cinnabar Field (Cavity of Breath)—this is concentrating Spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath. Quietly watch the breath of the Yinqiao point below (around the Perineum) and the breath of the Crimson Palace above meet each other inside the alchemical cauldron. Using only a gentle, warm Spirit-fire to simmer finely and the soft, fine Wind of Xun (gentle nasal breath) to fan slowly, turbid Essence naturally melts and transforms into pure Breath. This is "refining Essence into Breath."

How do you recognize that Essence is genuinely being refined into Breath? Previously, before gathering the clear outer breath to pair as one household with inner Spirit, heart-fire (Li) and kidney-water (Kan) remained split apart, paying each other no heed, and breathing was uneven and discordant. Once outer breath is drawn back inward to subdue stagnant yin essence inside, the moment True Breath arrives, yin essence dissolves on its own. With upper heart-breath and lower kidney-breath united into one, the Crimson Palace at the chest naturally feels calm and spacious, the kidney region below feels light and at ease, and outer breathing at nose and mouth merges with inner True Breath into a single warm current—like husband and wife joined in harmony, gathered without scattering. Growing fuller day by day and richer month by month, Prior Heaven True Yang manifests from this—that is the unmistakable sign of "transforming into Breath."

Once Breath is transformed, move on to the higher stage. What is that higher stage? Now breathing is unified and Spirit and Breath are joined and condensed in the Cinnabar Field, winding and flowing gracefully like a living dragon swimming through water, undergoing countless subtle transformations in a single day. I simply keep Spirit softly centered within and let breath follow naturally outside, leaving everything to nature: stillness reaches its utmost and naturally stirs; movement reaches its utmost and naturally returns to stillness. This is "refining Breath into Spirit."

After prolonged quiet absorption, my breath grows ever more harmonious; the current that formerly circulated gracefully through the Cinnabar Field is now simmered to complete ripeness, feeling half-present and half-absent, half-moving and half-still—unnoticed by a coarse mind, recognized only through delicate awareness. At this precise juncture, one must let the calculating, discriminating mind fade away completely, until a hundred notions vanish and ten thousand cares dissolve. Even the united Spirit and Breath in the Cinnabar Field are left alone to stir, regulate, warm, and temper themselves; I simply attain emptiness and guard stillness, entrusting all to nature, with Spirit entering Breath unknowingly and Breath encircling Spirit imperceptibly. After simmering quietly like this for a spell, a fragrant warm breeze rises naturally through all the channels, steaming and permeating the entire body—what the ancients called "Spirit generating Breath." Next, one feels vitality growing stronger day by day and wisdom opening wider; within the unified mind, a single stream of breath rises from the central cauldron, pure, subtle, and lustrous as jade—what is called "Breath generating Spirit."

When Spirit and Breath mutually nourish and generate each other like this, it is the exact moment to "let go completely into emptiness": neither clinging tightly nor drifting loose, seemingly attentive yet utterly without artifice—this is the genuine reality of "refining Spirit to return to Emptiness."

In truth, these three stages—refining Essence into Breath, Breath into Spirit, and Spirit into Emptiness (the three transmutations)—can all be practiced and reached sequentially within a single sitting. A student must follow this natural order: never end the session and leave your seat the moment Spirit and Breath have only roughly met without reaching deep stillness; nor should you jump up hastily once Spirit and Breath have deeply fused into one undifferentiated whole without yet entering pure, empty, effortless freedom. Follow the full arc faithfully—from refining Essence, onward to abundant Breath and vibrant Spirit, gradually melting into clear naturalness—and finish with the closing work of "returning to the furnace and sealing," gathering breath calmly back into the Cinnabar Field to store and secure it. Only then does your practice accord with Heaven and Earth's waxing and waning and the full cycle of spring, summer, autumn, and winter, completing one full round of work.

Cultivating day after day in this way, your Breath naturally grows ever more harmonious and your Spirit ever deeper in stillness, holding endless transformation and inexhaustible vitality within. Working diligently day and night, seamlessly and steadily, stillness and lucid awareness merging into one, True Breath naturally steams upward to pay homage to the Muddy Pellet at the crown and flows downward into the Cinnabar palace, penetrating every vessel and permeating flesh and skin with an unstoppable surge of life. This "Waterwheel" path (the Celestial Circuit) opens completely *of its own accord*; I need only follow its already-open flow and lend the lightest guidance—utterly unlike heterodox side-paths that use selfish personal intention to spin emptily and push mechanically, forcing starts and stops without watching whether the breath moves or rests on its own. Over long time, the Elixir forms and the Way stands firm, transcending bodily bounds to walk as a companion of Heaven. The sage's achievement of greatness is truly no easy feat.

Key Terms

  • guarding the center and embracing the One: letting mind and Spirit rest in centered, empty stillness—neither scattered nor lopsided—merged as one with the Great Way.
  • concentrating Spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath: shining lucid awareness (Spirit-light) gently downward into the lower Cinnabar Field (Cavity of Breath) below the navel so mind-Spirit and True Breath naturally unite.
  • Yinqiao: a pivotal point in the lower body in internal alchemy and meridian theory, situated around the Perineum, regarded as the hinge where True Yang arises.
  • Crimson Palace: the energy chamber beneath the heart, regarded in internal alchemy as the seat of heart-fire (Li), corresponding across the torso with lower kidney-water (Kan).
  • Wind of Xun: using the trigram Xun (wind) as an image for the long, fine, gentle rhythm of breathing in quiet sitting that softly fans the warm Spirit-fire.
  • the three transmutations: the three progressive tiers of internal alchemy—refining Essence into Breath, Breath into Spirit, and Spirit into Emptiness. This chapter notes especially that besides spanning a lifetime's path, all three can be experienced sequentially within a single complete sitting.
  • Waterwheel (heche): an alchemical image for True Breath circulating along the Conception and Governing vessels (the Celestial Circuit). Huang Yuanji emphasizes that the Waterwheel opens naturally only once True Breath is abundant, rejecting forced empty rotation by willpower.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Accomplish the work without grabbing the credit, and your stature grows largest. Those who truly hold a team or family together act like air and clothing—nourishing quietly without fanfare; rushing to stamp your name on every success actually shrinks your own measure.
  2. Begin quiet sitting with emptiness and ease, not heroic intensity. When you sit down, you do not need to imagine towering cosmic energy; simply letting go of vexation, sitting steadily like a mountain, and letting thoughts melt like ice already puts you right inside the true gate.
  3. Close every session completely—never jump up abruptly. Before finishing meditation or relaxation practice, leave a few minutes to "return to the furnace and seal": draw attention softly back to the lower abdomen, rest there quietly for a moment, and only then stretch and rise slowly.
  4. Guide lightly along the natural current; never force energy around. Whether regulating breath or resting quietly, respect the body's own rhythm—neither fabricating movement when breath is still nor blocking it when it flows naturally, steering clear of "running it emptily and forcing it mechanically."

Translator's Notes

  1. "Turning to the left, the noble person fits the occasion; turning to the right, the noble person has it well in hand": from the Classic of Poetry ("Minor Odes," Changchang zhe hua), praising a noble person's all-around grace and competence; Huang Yuanji borrows it to illustrate the Way's unhindered availability in every situation.
  2. "Gentle nourishment" (hanru) and "sheltering canopy" (pingmeng): hanru means soaking and nurturing; pingmeng originally refers to a tent or awning stretched overhead, hence protective shelter.
  3. "So small nothing can split it," "so vast nothing can contain it," and "far-reaching yet hidden": all drawn from the Doctrine of the Mean ("The Way of the noble person reaches far and wide yet remains hidden... Spoken of in its greatness, nothing under heaven can contain it; spoken of in its smallness, nothing under heaven can split it").
  4. "Deep, silent mystery of Heaven" (wumu) and "the seasons moving on their course" (shixing): wumu comes from the Classic of Poetry ("Odes of Zhou," Wei tian zhi ming); shixing echoes Analects 17.19 ("Does Heaven speak? The four seasons run their course and the hundred things are born").
  5. "Central cauldron" (guizhong): a frequent alchemical term for the centered, empty midpoint of the body (the Yellow Court or lower Cinnabar Field), from which the One Breath spontaneously arises.
  6. "Companion of Heaven" (yu tian wei tu): from the Zhuangzi ("The Great and Venerable Teacher"), meaning to roam in harmony with Heaven's Way.
  7. Textual collation: In the classic text, "rely on it for life" (shizhi, 恃之) was misprinted as 视之 in the base edition and is corrected from standard editions and Huang's commentary below. Several obvious misprints in the commentary (纷扰, 一草一木, 牵累, 阴跷, 绛宫, 真阳, 守静, 晶莹, 不可遏) have likewise been emended from context.

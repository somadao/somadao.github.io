IV · Classic of the Way (Ch. 31–37) · 道经

Chapter 36 · Sharp Instruments of the State

What the world mistakes for political cunning—"to take, first give"—Huang Yuanji reveals as the alchemical secret of reversing cosmic decline, overcoming hardness through softness, and safeguarding transmission for worthy recipients

About This Chapter

Chapter 36 has long been the most controversial chapter of the Daodejing: "What is to be contracted must first be expanded... what is to be taken away must first be given" has frequently been read by political strategists as a cynical ploy of luring an opponent into overreach. Yet Laozi immediately reveals his real point: "the soft overcomes the hard, the weak overcomes the strong; a fish must not leave the deep waters, and the sharp instruments of the state must not be shown to others"—pointing to the subtle law of natural waxing and waning and the wisdom of concealing what is vital.

Huang Yuanji breaks completely out of the political-intrigue reading and interprets the entire chapter through the alchemical lens of "reversing creation":

  1. Reversing cosmic transformation: Ordinary mortals simply slide downhill with the cycles of spring, summer, autumn, and winter, birth, aging, sickness, and death; the sage sees straight through the subtle pivot of waxing and waning and reverses yin and yang amid autumn and winter's decline to renew vitality—not through brute force, but through utmost softness and yielding ("the soft overcomes the hard, the weak overcomes the strong"). Just as a fish cannot leave deep water for an instant, a human being cannot lose the Prior Heaven true Essence hidden within.
  2. Stealing the pivot and choosing worthy recipients: "Stealing life from the midst of killing" means drawing Prior Heaven true Essence from the empty clarity of heaven and earth and the empty stillness of one's own body—never the tangible mundane essence toyed with by heterodox side-paths. Because this reversal of returning to the root bears on life's ultimate foundation and touches heaven's secret mechanism, patriarchs across the ages strictly upheld the precept that "the sharp instruments of the state must not be shown to others"—not out of selfishness, but because someone lacking inner virtue and inner mastery could never put the formula to work even if told, and reckless transmission only invites calamity.

Safety note: This chapter cites traditional alchemical metaphors such as "drawing yang from the Dark Mountain Cavern, seizing treasure from the Ghosts' Den" and "stealing the pivot," yet Huang Yuanji draws a clear boundary: what is drawn is the Prior Heaven true Essence born in "empty space and empty stillness," utterly distinct from the physical fluids or sexual-harvesting heterodoxies of side-paths. Never read alchemical metaphors literally or stray onto crooked paths.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：将欲噏之，必固张之；将欲弱之，必固强之；将欲废之，必固兴之；将欲夺之，必固与之。是谓微明。柔胜刚，弱胜强，鱼不可脱于渊，国之利器不可以示人。

The Supreme Patriarch says: What is about to contract must first be expanded; what is about to weaken must first be strengthened; what is about to decline must first be raised up; what is about to be taken away must first be given. This is called subtle clarity.

The soft overcomes the hard; the weak overcomes the strong. A fish must never leave the deep pool; the sharp instruments of the state must never be displayed to others.

§2
Original (Chinese)

天有盈虚消长，人有寿夭穷通，此亦气数之常，然只可以概凡夫，而不可以律圣人。圣人则有挽回天地之能，扭转乾坤之德，要不外颠倒阴阳，逆施造化而已。即如时至秋也，万物将收，而欲噏弱而难整，圣人则有张天地之气运，强血气之功能焉；时至冬也，万物皆废，而欲槁夺而难生，圣人则有气象之重兴，岁月之我与者。此至微而至明，实常而实异，非圣人莫喻也。易危为安，反乱为治，非神勇者，不能臻此神化。然究其所为返还之术，不过曰柔、曰弱。惟其柔也，故能胜刚；惟其弱也，故能胜强。所用者何？人无精则绝，鱼无水则死，一旦脱之于渊，则水涸而生机息矣，亦犹人无真一之精，则所存者几希。人之与鱼，同一不离乎水，但非天露之水，乃造道渊深而一元之水，汩汩乎来频相灌溉也。昔庄子谓「相濡以沫，相呴以湿，不若相忘于江湖」，是其旨也。后世旁门，以有形有质之精，为修炼长生之本，殆不知，道之为物，刚健中正，纯粹以精，都从恍惚杳冥、虚无自然而生者。其间火药之密机、烹调之旨，非圣师不授，非至诚不几，非有功有德、虚心访道、竭诚求师者，未易仙缘凑合。盖天机秘密，天地至重，鬼神最钦，妄传匪人，殃遗九祖，犹国家利用之密器，不可以轻示人，是以君子慎密而不出也。学者亦见及此乎？

Heaven has its cycles of waxing and waning, fullness and emptiness; human life has longevity and early death, hardship and success. That is indeed the ordinary course of fate—yet it applies only to ordinary mortals and cannot bind a sage. A sage possesses the capacity to turn back heaven and earth and the virtue to reverse Qian and Kun; at bottom, this is nothing other than inverting yin and yang and applying creation in reversal.

For instance, when autumn arrives, the myriad things begin to draw inward, contracting and weakening until they can scarcely rally; yet the sage has the means to expand heaven and earth's vital current and strengthen the vitality of blood and breath. When winter arrives, everything lies dormant and withered, stripped bare as if life could no longer sprout; yet the sage can rekindle inner vitality and reclaim the years of life into his own hands. This law is supremely subtle yet supremely clear, seemingly ordinary yet truly extraordinary—none but a sage can fathom it.

Turning peril into safety and reversing disorder into order—without divine courage and steadiness, no one can reach such wondrous transformation. Yet when we ask what method the sage actually uses to return and reverse decline, it comes down to just two words: "soft" and "weak." Precisely through utmost softness, he dissolves and overcomes the hard; precisely through yielding weakness, he bears and overcomes the strong.

And what substance does this art depend upon? Without Essence, human life ceases; without water, a fish dies. Once a fish leaves the deep pool, the water dries up and its spark of life goes out—just as when a person drains away the pure, unified Prior Heaven Essence within, scarcely any vitality remains. Both human beings and fish cannot part from water for a moment; yet for humans it is not ordinary rainwater or dew from the sky, but the single primordial spring welling up from the deep abyss of the Way, bubbling continuously to irrigate the whole frame. As Zhuangzi once said, "When the springs dry up and fish lie stranded on land, spitting foam and breathing moisture on one another to stay alive, how much better to forget each other swimming freely in the rivers and lakes!" That captures the very essence of this teaching.

Later heterodox side-paths mistakenly take tangible, physical mundane essence as the basis for cultivating longevity. How little they understand that what the Way brings forth—though firm, upright, pure, and quintessential—arises wholly out of the dim and obscure state of empty naturalness! Within it, the hidden pivot of fire phasing and Medicine and the delicate knack of simmering and nourishment are never transmitted except by a sage teacher, never approached without utmost sincerity, and rarely encountered except by one who accumulates merit and virtue, seeks the Way with an open heart, and asks a master with total devotion. For this is heaven's deepest secret—most treasured by heaven and earth and held in highest awe by spirits and deities. Recklessly passing it to unworthy people brings grave calamity upon generations—just like the secret decisive instruments a state depends upon, which must never be casually shown to others. Thus the noble person guards it in strict discretion and does not blurt it out. Do students see deeply enough into this?

§3
Original (Chinese)

此言修道之士，真有宇宙在手、万物生心之妙，然亦不过「观天之道、执天之行」，顺而取之、逆而施之足矣。其寓生机于杀机之中，即所谓「至阴肃肃，至阳赫赫，肃肃出乎天，赫赫出乎地」；由至阴而取至阳，所谓「盗机」者此也。人能于「黑山窟取阳，鬼窝里取宝」，即是盗生机于杀机之内，要皆在天地虚空中取，人身虚静处夺，此精才是真精，非世之凡精可拟。人能盗之，不失其时，用一度工，自有一度之进益。劝学者，以柔以弱，立德立功，庶得神天之佑，自有仙人传授口诀。否则，最大事情，惊天地而动鬼神，纵是神仙，要皆不传者多。盖天机至密，天律最严，不可违也。庄子曰：「使道可献人，则人莫不献之于君。使道可进人，则人莫不进之于亲。使道可与人，则人莫不与之于弟兄。使道可传人，则人莫不传之于子孙。而皆不可者何？诚以中无德而道不立，中无主而道不行也。」合数圣之言观之，则知国之利器，不可轻以示人矣。后世修士，切勿以大道为公，不择人而授，以致自遭天谴，悔之无及。斯殆有公而不公、不公而公之旨，非下学所能参其微也。尚其懔之。

This tells us that a genuine adept of the Way truly enjoys the marvel of "holding the universe in his hand and giving rise to ten thousand transformations from his mind"—yet at heart it is simply what the Classic of the Hidden Tally says: "Observe the Way of Heaven, lay hold of the movement of Heaven," welcoming it in accord with timing and applying it in reversal.

That he lodges vibrant life inside the very impulse of winter's killing echoes Zhuangzi's words: "Utmost yin is crisp and solemn; utmost yang is blazing and radiant. The crisp solemnity issues from Heaven, while the radiant blaze rises from Earth." Extracting utmost yang right out of utmost yin is what the ancients meant by "stealing the pivot." When a practitioner fulfills the old alchemical saying—"drawing yang from the Dark Mountain Cavern, seizing treasure from the Ghosts' Den"—he is stealing the spark of life from within the breath of decay. Yet remember above all: all of this is drawn within the pure emptiness of heaven and earth and seized within the empty stillness of one's own body. Only Essence born thus is genuine Prior Heaven Essence, beyond comparison with ordinary physical essence. Whoever captures it without missing its exact timing reaps a measure of real progress for every measure of honest effort.

Therefore I earnestly urge all students: conduct yourself in softness and yielding, and build solid virtue and merit; thus you win the blessing of Heaven and will naturally meet an enlightened teacher who transmits the true oral instructions. Otherwise, since this is the weightiest matter under heaven—startling heaven and earth and moving spirits—even if you meet a true immortal, most will still refrain from passing it on, because heaven's secret is supremely confidential and heaven's law is strict beyond violation.

Zhuangzi once said: "If the Way could be offered like a gift, everyone would offer it to his ruler. If it could be presented like tribute, everyone would present it to his parents. If it could be shared out, everyone would share it with his brothers. If it could be bequeathed, everyone would hand it down to his children and grandchildren. Yet none of this can be done—why? Truly because where there is no virtue within, the Way cannot stand; where there is no inner master, the Way cannot move."

Reading Laozi and Zhuangzi side by side, we understand completely why "the sharp instruments of the state must not be lightly shown to others." Let cultivators of later ages never invoke "the Great Way belongs to all" as an excuse to teach indiscriminately without examining the recipient, thereby bringing heaven's reprimand upon themselves when regret comes too late. Here lies a subtle principle: what looks impartial can actually betray the public good, while what looks withheld actually safeguards the Great Way for all—a nuance not easily fathomed by beginners. May later generations hold this in solemn awe!

Key Terms

  • Subtle clarity (weiming): a hidden yet crystal-clear natural law—seeing straight through the reversal point where fullness turns to contraction and utmost decline turns back to renewal.
  • reversal: following the ordinary course produces offspring and leads to aging and depletion; reversing the course restores primordial vitality and rekindles life. This chapter uses generating yang amid autumn and winter's decline to illustrate alchemical reversal.
  • Pure unified Essence (zhenyi zhi jing): also called Prior Heaven true Essence or the single primordial water—innate vitality born spontaneously from the dim and obscure state of empty stillness, distinct from tangible Posterior Heaven physical essence.
  • Stealing the pivot (daoji): a phrase from the Classic of the Hidden Tally (Yinfujing), referring to seizing the subtle timing of heaven, earth, and body to capture and refine the first spark of Prior Heaven yang arising at the depth of yin stillness.
  • Seizing treasure from the Ghosts' Den (guiwo qubao): also paired with "drawing yang from the Dark Mountain Cavern," a traditional alchemical metaphor for refining pure yang vitality right out of the dark, seemingly lifeless yin stillness of the lower region.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Read "subtle clarity": stay humble in good times and steady in low times. Peak expansion already carries the seed of contraction, and deep troughs already carry the turn back upward; knowing this rhythm keeps you from reckless excess in success and despair in adversity.
  2. Dissolve head-on exhaustion with softness. Against heavy pressure, anxiety, or fierce conflict, pushing harder drains your vitality fastest; softening your stance, slowing your breath, and stepping back to view the whole actually returns the initiative to you.
  3. Keep your own "deep pool" full instead of surviving by "spitting foam." Burning yourself out and then leaning on caffeine, stimulation, or outside reassurance to limp along is "a fish leaving the pool"; setting aside quiet daily rest to replenish deep vitality protects the real root of life.
  4. Character and steadiness are the prerequisite for deeper wisdom. As Zhuangzi says, "without virtue within, the Way cannot stand; without an inner master, it cannot move." Before hunting for "secret formulas" or shortcuts, build honesty, restraint, and emotional stability so good methods can actually take root in you.

Translator's Notes

  1. 噏 (xī): a variant of 翕 (xī), meaning to draw in, contract, or close—paired as the opposite of 张 ("expand").
  2. "Spitting foam and breathing moisture on one another... better to forget each other in the rivers and lakes": from the Zhuangzi ("The Great and Venerable Teacher"). Xu (呴) means breathing moisture; ru (濡) means dampening—contrasting desperate mutual survival in distress with returning freely to the vast source.
  3. "Observe the Way of Heaven, lay hold of the movement of Heaven": the opening line of the Yellow Emperor's Classic of the Hidden Tally (Huangdi Yinfujing), from which the term "stealing the pivot" (daoji) also derives ("Heaven and Earth are the thief of the myriad things; the myriad things are the thief of humanity; humanity is the thief of the myriad things").
  4. "Utmost yin is crisp and solemn; utmost yang is blazing and radiant...": from the Zhuangzi ("Tian Zifang"), spoken by Laozi in reply to Confucius's question on the Supreme Way.
  5. Zhuangzi on why the Way cannot be lightly handed over: adapted from the Zhuangzi ("The Turning of Heaven"), which explains that the Way cannot be transferred like a physical possession unless the recipient has "a master within and rectitude without."
  6. "Hold this in solemn awe" (shang qi lin zhi): lin (懔) means awe, reverence, and vigilance.
  7. Textual collation: In the classic text, 是谓微明 was misprinted as 是请微明 in the base edition. Numerous scribal errors in the commentary (寿夭, 槁夺, 鱼无水则死, 汩汩乎来, 相呴以湿, 恍惚杳冥, 妄传, 寓生机, 人能于, 与之于弟兄, 不公而公) have been emended against standard editions and context, and a stray footnote from the previous chapter erroneously inserted after this chapter's classic text has been removed.

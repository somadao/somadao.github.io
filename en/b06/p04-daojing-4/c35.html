IV · Classic of the Way (Ch. 31–37) · 道经

Chapter 35 · Coming Without Harm

Hold fast to the formless Great Image and all under heaven comes in peace and harmony; in internal alchemy, this reveals the turn from utmost movement to stillness, the first stirring of the Mysterious Pass, and the dual Wu-Ji Earth workings of True Intention

About This Chapter

Chapter 35 of the Daodejing symbolizes the Great Way as the "Great Image": whoever holds fast to this formless image that embraces all forms draws the entire world naturally, so all come and go in peace, safety, and harmony without harm. By contrast, sweet music and savory food may briefly halt a passing traveler, yet remain fleeting; the Way, when put into words, seems bland and flavorless, invisible to the eye and inaudible to the ear—yet in actual use it can never be exhausted.

Huang Yuanji links the chapter's "peace, ease, and harmony" (an ping tai) directly with the Book of Changes' hexagram Tai ("Heaven and Earth in harmonious communion"), moving smoothly from outer governance to inner cultivation:

  1. The substance of the Way: The Great Image is the "image without image," and holding it means "holding without clinging"—like Mencius's counsel "neither forget nor force," neither grasping tightly nor letting it slip away. Intense worldly pleasures deliver only a momentary rush, whereas the seemingly flavorless Way hides "the supreme flavor within flavorlessness," bringing lasting contentment and harmony above and below.
  2. Internal alchemy: This chapter is one of Huang Yuanji's classic expositions of True Intention (the two Earths, wu and ji) and the precise timing when the Mysterious Pass first opens. When selfish desires blaze and vital energy begins to wane, one must first move gradually from restless activity toward deep stillness. At the turn from utmost movement to stillness, entering the dim and obscure state, a warm, flowing thread of breath suddenly rises from within deep quiet—that is the first sign of the Mysterious Pass. Waiting quietly before it opens is Yin Earth (ji Earth); gathering it promptly the instant it opens is Yang Earth (wu Earth)—both are simply the wondrous twofold activity of the same lucid True Intention across stillness and movement.

Safety note: This chapter mentions sensations that can appear in deep stillness, such as "faint warmth rising from the lower region, surging through the body as swiftly as thunder," yet Huang Yuanji immediately cautions: "add not a single contrivance, mix in not a single opinion—always let a lucid, aware mind preside." Never treat bodily heat as a goal to chase, and never use forceful intention to urge or amplify physical sensations.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：执大象，天下往，往而不害，安平泰。乐与饵，过客止。道之出口，淡乎无味。视之不可见，听之不可闻，用之不可既。

The Supreme Patriarch says: Hold fast to the Great Image, and all under heaven will come to you. They come without harm—in peace, ease, and harmony.

Music and delicacies will make a passing traveler pause. When the Way is spoken aloud, how bland and flavorless it seems! Look for it, and it cannot be seen; listen for it, and it cannot be heard; yet put it to use, and it can never be exhausted.

§2
Original (Chinese)

何谓大象？即生天生地、生人生物之大道，以其无所不包，故曰大象。究何象哉？殆无极而已矣。顾无象为象，究将何所执乎？亦无执为执，斯于道不悖矣。人能常操常存，勿忘勿助，则大象执焉，大道在焉。昔孔子告颜渊曰：「一日克己复礼，天下归仁。」是知大道所归，即天下所归；无论归人归道，俱是心悦诚服，又何害之有耶？吾知一气相贯通，万物皆默化，融融泄泄，上下相安于泰运之天。此皆自然之依归，非一时所感激。苟徒饰片时之耳目，未始不源源而来，但如世之雅乐可怀，香饵可口，亦足令过客停骖，流连不去，然可暂而不可常也。惟道无味，不似肥浓甘脆，令人咀嚼不已，餍饫无穷，而人之爽口悦心者，自不厌焉。此无味中有至味，非世味之浓所可拟。虽然，道无方体，亦无形状，难想像，亦难捉摸，故曰：视不见，听不闻，而取之靡穷，用之不竭，有如是也，诚「范围天地而不过，曲成万物而不遗」，斯道之所以为大耳。学者其知所向往哉！

What is called the "Great Image"? It is the Great Way that gives birth to heaven and earth, humanity and all creatures; because nothing falls outside its embrace, it is called the Great Image. Yet pressed to the end, what shape does it actually have? Simply the undivided, formless Boundless (wuji). Since its image is precisely the absence of image, how is one supposed to "hold" it? Only by holding without clinging—then one does not run counter to the Way. If a person can preserve and tend it constantly, "neither forgetting it nor forcing it to grow," then the Great Image is held and the Great Way is vibrantly present.

Long ago, Confucius told Yan Yuan: "If for a single day you master yourself and return to ritual propriety, all under heaven will turn toward humaneness." Thus we know that wherever the Great Way gathers, the hearts of all under heaven gather as well; whether people turn toward a sage or toward the Way itself, they do so with heartfelt joy and willing trust—how could any harm possibly arise? In such a state, a single harmonious breath flows freely between above and below, all creatures are quietly transformed, and joy and ease permeate everywhere, with ruler and people alike dwelling safely in a heaven of peaceful harmony. Such allegiance flows purely from nature, never from a passing burst of emotional excitement.

If one merely dazzles eyes and ears with momentary display, crowds may indeed come streaming in—just as refined music delights the ear and fragrant dainties tempt the palate, making passing travelers rein in their horses and linger for a while. Yet such attraction lasts only briefly and can never endure. Only the Way seems flavorless: unlike rich, greasy, sweet, or crisp delicacies that keep people chewing endlessly in greedy indulgence, the crisp refreshment and deep gladness the Way brings never grow tiresome. Within this flavorlessness hides the supreme flavor, beyond comparison with any heavy taste of the world.

Even so, the Way has no fixed location or bodily boundary and no visible shape; it resists mental picturing and eludes physical grasping—which is why the classic says it cannot be seen or heard. Yet when drawn upon it is inexhaustible, and when put to use it never runs dry—wondrous to such a degree! Truly it is as the Book of Changes says: "Encompassing the transformations of heaven and earth without excess, finely completing the myriad things without leaving a single one behind." That is what makes the Way truly great. Should not every student turn his heart eagerly toward it?

§3
Original (Chinese)

此言人必效天地交泰，而后融融泄泄，不啻雅乐可怀，香饵堪味，令人叹赏不置。然其境正非易到也。倘当私欲甚炽，血气将衰之候，不先从极动之处，渐而至于静地，则人心不死，道心不生，凡息不停，真息不见。惟动极而静之际，忽来真意以主持之，此意属阴，谓之己土。少焉恍恍惚惚，阴阳交媾，大入杳冥之境，似梦非梦，似醒非醒。于此定静之中，忽觉一缕热气，混混续续，气畅神融，两两交会于黄房之间，将判未判，未判忽判，此即真铅现象；心花发露，暖气融融，元神跃跃，不由感触，自然发生，斯乃玄关兆象，太极开基也。斯时惟用一点真心发真意以收摄之，此意属阳，为戊土。其实一意，不过以动静之基，分为戊己之土而已。盖玄牝未开，混沌之中，有此真意为主，即无欲观妙之意，谓之阴土；及玄牝开而真机现，即有欲以观其窍，谓之阳土。一为无名天地之始，一为有名万物之母。生天生地、生人生物，皆此一点真意为之贯注。修行人，能以真意主宰运行，庶不至感而有思，动而他驰。所谓「天关由我，地轴由心」，「宇宙在乎身，万化生于心」，皆此时之灵觉，为之运用而主持也。故曰：略先一意，则真机未现，采之无益；略后一息，则凡念已起，采之又多夹杂，不堪为我炼丹大药。此须有大智慧、大力量，方能于此一息中，认得清、把得定，以为成仙证圣之本。虽然，此个玄关，始而其气柔脆，只觉微有热意从下元起，久则踊跃周身，似有不可遏抑之势。学人须于至微处辨得明白，以我真意主持，毫不分散，久之气机大有力量，一任兀兀腾腾，随其所至，不加一意，不参一见，斯得之耳。到得气机壮旺，一静即天机发动，迅速如雷，虽一切喧哗之乡，不能禁止。总要有灵觉之心，为之主持，乃无差也已。

Applied to inner cultivation, this teaches that one must model oneself on Heaven and Earth meeting in harmonious communion; only then does body and mind enter deep, radiant harmony—a quiet joy no less enchanting than refined music or savory dainties, leaving one filled with wonder. Yet such a state is by no means easy to reach. If, at a time when selfish desires rage fiercely and vital blood and breath are beginning to decline, one fails to settle step by step from extreme agitation down into stillness, then the restless human mind will not subside and the lucid mind of the Way will not arise; coarse ordinary breathing will not grow quiet and subtle True Breath will not appear.

Only at the exact turning point where extreme movement settles into stillness does a calm, clear True Intention spontaneously arise to preside over the whole—because nothing has stirred yet, this intention belongs to yin and is called Ji Earth. After a short while, body and mind slip into the dim and obscure state where yin and yang unite, entering profound stillness—like dreaming yet not a dream, like waking yet not ordinary waking. Right in the midst of this deep stillness, one suddenly feels a slender thread of warm breath flowing continuously and seamlessly; breath runs smoothly and Spirit melts in harmony as the two meet in the Yellow Court (Yellow Chamber). At the split second where undivided chaos is about to part—not yet parted, then suddenly parting—Prior Heaven True Lead (the genuine yang vitality within Lead and Mercury) reveals its sign! One feels the flower of the heart quietly blossom, gentle warmth suffusing the frame, and Original Spirit brimming with lively energy—not provoked by any external stimulation, but arising entirely of itself. This is the first manifestation of the Mysterious Pass and the founding moment of the Supreme Ultimate!

At this precise instant, simply use a single spark of pure heart to send forth gentle, steady True Intention to gather and contain it—because the pivot has moved, this intention belongs to yang and is called Wu Earth. In truth, Wu Earth and Ji Earth are one and the same True Intention, given the labels wu (yang) and ji (yin) solely according to whether the moment is still or moving. Before the Mysterious Female (Mysterious Pass) opens, while all rests in undivided chaos, this True Intention quietly presiding is "ever without desire, observing its subtlety" (from Chapter 1)—called Yin Earth. The moment the Mysterious Female opens and the Prior Heaven mechanism appears, lucid awareness beholding and gathering it is "ever with desire, observing its opening"—called Yang Earth. One corresponds to "the Nameless, beginning of heaven and earth"; the other to "the Named, mother of the myriad things." The creative work that births heaven and earth, humanity and all things, is wholly infused by this single spark of True Intention.

If a cultivator lets True Intention preside over the entire process, he will not lapse into idle thoughts at the slightest sensation nor chase off along habitual paths the moment energy stirs. What the ancients meant by "Heaven's pivot rests in my hands, Earth's axis turns by my mind" and "the universe lies within this body, ten thousand transformations are born in the mind" refers wholly to the lucid awareness presiding and guiding at this exact moment.

That is why alchemists say: act a fraction of a thought too soon, and the true mechanism has not yet appeared—harvesting yields nothing; act a single breath too late, and mundane thoughts have already crept in—what you gather is contaminated and unfit to serve as the Great Medicine for refining the Elixir. Great wisdom and great steadiness are required to recognize this hairsbreadth instant unmistakably and hold it cleanly, laying the true foundation for attaining immortality and sagehood.

Still, one must recognize that when this Mysterious Pass first stirs, its breath is tender and delicate—often felt only as a faint trace of warmth quietly rising from the lower region. After long nourishment, it grows lively and abundant, surging through the whole body with a momentum that seems unstoppable. A student must discern it clearly at its tiniest bud, holding True Intention steady as host without the slightest distraction. Over time, as the breath-current gains real strength, simply let it billow and unfold naturally wherever it goes—adding not a single contrivance, mixing in not a single opinion—and you have got it. Once vitality becomes truly robust, even in the midst of a noisy, bustling crowd, the instant the mind grows still, the heavenly mechanism activates swift as thunder and nothing outside can block it. Above all, a clear, unclouded, lucid mind must always preside—only then will there be not the slightest error.

Key Terms

  • Great Image (daxiang): the formless substance of the Great Way that generates and encompasses all images; "holding fast to the Great Image" means abiding in the Way without clinging.
  • True Intention: the lucid presiding awareness radiating naturally from Original Spirit once acquired delusions and grasping are stripped away, symbolized in alchemy as the matchmaker and Central Earth.
  • Wu and Ji Earths (wuji er tu): using the heavenly stems wu (yang Earth) and ji (yin Earth) to denote the twofold action of True Intention in movement and stillness. Resting quietly in undivided stillness before the Mysterious Pass opens is Ji Earth (Yin Earth); gently gathering the true vitality the moment the Pass opens is Wu Earth (Yang Earth)—both arising from one mind.
  • Mysterious Pass: also called the Mysterious Female or Mysterious Opening—not a fixed anatomical spot, but the living, luminous pivot where Prior Heaven vitality suddenly appears at the depth of stillness.
  • the dim and obscure: the profound meditative threshold—neither dreaming nor ordinarily awake—where acquired thoughts temporarily cease and Prior Heaven vitality stands poised to stir.
  • Yellow Chamber (huangfang): another name for the Yellow Court or Central Yellow, the empty center of body and mind where Spirit and Breath meet.
  • Billowing and carefree (wuwu tengteng): a classic Chan and alchemical phrase describing an unforced, uncontrived state in which True Breath swells naturally and Spirit remains lucid and free.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Be wary of "music and delicacies"—short-lived dopamine hits. Excitement, distraction, and instant gratification are like roadside banquets: they hook you for a moment yet leave no lasting peace. Habits that truly nourish life often taste "bland and flavorless" at first, growing sweeter the longer you keep them.
  2. Anchor daily steadiness with "neither forgetting nor forcing." In both focused work and quiet sitting, the danger lies in the two extremes: completely forgetting and drifting slack, or gripping too hard and pushing frantically. Neither dropping nor forcing is the best way to "hold the Great Image."
  3. Practice awareness in the gap where intense activity turns to stillness. Right after finishing a busy stretch or when an emotion first ripples, do not leap straight into the next task—pause for a single minute of quiet breathing. That transition from noise back into clarity is the finest opportunity to sense calm inner mastery.
  4. When pleasant warmth or comfort arises, resist chasing it with excitement. If quiet rest brings gentle warmth and ease, simply let calm, lucid awareness preside as an unruffled witness; the moment you jump in to amplify, judge, or crave the feeling, you fall into "one breath too late, mundane thoughts already creeping in."

Translator's Notes

  1. "If for a single day you master yourself and return to ritual propriety, all under heaven will turn toward humaneness": from Analects 12.1 (Yan Yuan), Confucius's reply to Yan Yuan's question on humaneness.
  2. "Harmonious joy and ease" (rongrong yiyi, 融融泄泄): yiyi (read yì yì) describes warm, relaxed joy, alluding to the Zuo Commentary (1st year of Duke Yin), when Duke Zhuang of Zheng reconciled with his mother Lady Jiang in a tunnel: "Inside the great tunnel, their joy was warm and deep (rongrong); outside the great tunnel, their joy was open and free (yiyi)."
  3. "Rein in their horses" (tingcan) and "greedy indulgence" (yanyu): tingcan means halting one's carriage horses; yanyu means eating to satiation, extended to craving sensory pleasure.
  4. "Encompassing the transformations of heaven and earth without excess, finely completing the myriad things without leaving a single one behind": from the Appended Remarks (Xici I) of the Book of Changes.
  5. "A fraction of a thought too soon" (lüexian yiyi): As the base edition notes, Xiao Tianshi's edition of the Essential Meaning of the Daodejing reads lüexian yixi ("a single breath too soon"), balancing "a single breath too late" and "within this single breath" below; both point to the hairsbreadth timing of harvesting true vitality.
  6. Textual collation: Obvious typographical errors in the base edition have been corrected from context: 融融池池 → 融融泄泄, 荷徒饰 → 苟徒饰, 尚当 → 倘当, 香冥 → 杳冥, 戊已 → 戊己, 观其穷 → 观其窍, 不可過抑 → 不可遏抑, 辩得明白 → 辨得明白, and 您来真意 provisionally emended to 忽来真意 from character shape and context.

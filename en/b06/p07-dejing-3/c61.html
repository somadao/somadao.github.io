Chapter 61 · The Great Should Take the Lower Place

When a great state gladly settles in the low place, a hundred rivers flow into its sea; self-cultivation likewise rests on gentleness and humility—the pivot of forward and reverse flow in which the heart's fire shines down on the Cinnabar Field and Essence, Breath, and Spirit interpenetrate above and below

About This Chapter

Chapter 61 opens with the way a "great state" and a "small state" should deal with each other, and it makes a point that sounds counterintuitive yet carries great weight: the stronger party should be all the more willing to take the lower place and to master movement through stillness. Just as rivers and seas lie at the lowest point, so all the waters under heaven naturally gather into them.

Huang Yuanji unpacks the chapter on two levels, the worldly method and the alchemical method:

  1. The wisdom of "taking the lower place" in governing and in daily life: Whether it is King Tang of Shang treating the Earl of Ge with kindness, or King Goujian of Yue patiently enduring service to the King of Wu, as long as neither the strong nor the weak party is arrogant or confrontational—the great state winning hearts through generosity, the small state preserving itself through humility—each can obtain what it wishes without falling into selfish desire. And the higher one's position, the lower one should carry oneself.
  2. "Communication between heart and kidneys, above and below" in self-cultivation and elixir-refining: Huang likens the "great state" to the Original Spirit and the heart, which dwell above, and the "small state" to the lower Cinnabar Field and the kidneys, which dwell below. Cultivation does not begin by forcing things with harsh vigor; rather, the restless, unyielding temperament of the heart must soften and return to stillness, letting the light of Spirit flow downward like water to warm and illumine the Cinnabar Field. Once the yang breath in the kidneys has been warmed and roused, it moves up along the spine to nourish the head, then transforms into sweet dew and descends back into the Yellow Court. This cycle of descent and ascent is the inner counterpart of "the great state lowering itself to the small state" and "the small state lowering itself to the great state."

Safety note: The latter part of this chapter involves advanced internal-alchemy imagery such as the heart's fire shining down on the Cinnabar Field, the reverse turning of the River Chariot, and the mutual transformation of breath and fluids. For everyday quiet sitting, simply let the heart soften and rest naturally with the breath. Never deliberately steer the breath with your mind or force it upward toward the crown of the head, as this can cause a swollen feeling in the head, chest tightness, or a disordered flow of breath.

Original Text and Translation

§1Classic Text
Original (Chinese)

太上曰：大国者下流，天下之交，天下之牝。牝常以静胜牡，以静为下。故大国以下小国，则取小国；小国以下大国，则取大国。故或下以取，或下而取。大国不过欲兼畜人，小国不过欲入事人。夫两者各得其所欲，故大者宜为下。

Laozi says: A great state should be like the rivers and seas lying downstream, the meeting place where all the waters under heaven converge, and also like the gentle, yielding female of the world. The female constantly overcomes the male through stillness; precisely because she is still, she is content to lie below.

Therefore, when a great state humbles itself before a small state, it wins the small state's allegiance; when a small state humbles itself before a great state, it gains the great state's trust and protection. Thus some, standing high, deliberately lower themselves to win the other; others, standing low, willingly humble themselves to gain the other. A great state wants nothing more than to embrace and nourish more people; a small state wants nothing more than to attach itself to a great state so as to secure its footing. Since both sides can each obtain what they wish, it is all the more fitting that the stronger party should take the initiative in lowering itself.

§2
Original (Chinese)

太上言修道炼丹之学，皆当以柔为主，以静为要。虽曰柔懦无用，孤寂难成，而打坐之初，要必动从静出，刚自柔生，方是真正大道。喻曰「大国者下流」，言水有上有下，上之水必流于下而后已，如大国自谦自抑，毫无满假之思，必为天下所景仰，犹下流之地，为万派所归，其势有必然者，故曰：「天下之交。」夫天下交归，以其能自下也，自下则其气最柔也，非至刚也。彼物之至刚者，孰有过于牡乎？物之至柔也，孰有过于牝乎？牡为阳为刚，牝为阴为柔，宜乎阳刚之牡，常胜阴柔之牝矣，顾何以牝常胜牡耶？夫亦曰牝之能静焉耳。古云「静以制动」，其言不爽，亦同下之承上，其势必然。何况抚兹大国者，卑以自牧，虚以下人，而万国有不来享来王者乎？是以下为高之基，静又为下之本也。古今来，或大国以下小国，如成汤下葛伯，卒取葛之地，抚而有之是也；或小国以下大国，如勾践下吴王，卒取吴之业，兼而有之是也。又或大国不自大而自小，所以取小国如反掌也；亦或小国安于小而事大，所以取大国如拾芥也。论赫赫大邦，实为诸国表率，而抚绥有道，怀柔有方，不欲并吞天下，以山河为一统，乃欲并畜小国，以天下为一家，是非有大过人之德者，不能休休有容也，宜天下归仁，万方奉命矣。区区蕞尔，同属分封藩臣，而贡献频来，奔趋恐后，不欲高人以取辱，莫保宗社之灵长，惟期事人以自全，幸延苍生之残喘，亦非有小过人之智者，不能抑抑自下也，宜人心爱戴，天命来归矣。况乎人必有所志，而后有所欲。今大国欲兼畜人，小国欲入事人，两者所欲，一仁一智，已各得其欲，而不流于人欲之私，是见大小诸邦，各循其理、安其分，而无敢越厥职者焉。虽然，小者自下，其理固然，彼大者尤宜居下，始见一人之端拱，为天下之依归。治世如此，治身又何异乎？

Here Laozi teaches that the whole learning of cultivating the Way and refining the elixir must take gentleness as its principle and stillness as its key. Ordinary people may feel that sheer softness seems useless and that solitary quiet can hardly accomplish anything; yet at the very start of sitting practice, true movement must arise out of stillness and true strength must grow out of softness—only this accords with the genuine Great Way.

Laozi offers the image "a great state is the lower stream." Water lies high and low, and water from on high must flow downward, coming to rest only at the lowest point. If a great state can be modest and restrained, without the slightest trace of complacency or arrogance in its heart, it will surely be revered by all under heaven—just as low-lying rivers and seas naturally become the destination of thousands of tributaries. The trend is inevitable, and so it is called "the meeting place of the world," the point where all under heaven converges.

All under heaven turns toward it because it can place itself below; being able to stay below shows that its breath is supremely gentle rather than stubbornly hard. Among creatures, what is more vigorous than the male? And what is gentler than the female? The male is yang and hard, the female yin and soft; by ordinary reckoning the hard yang male ought always to prevail over the soft yin female—so why is it the female that constantly prevails? In the end, simply because the female can remain still. The ancients said, "Stillness masters movement," and the saying holds true; just as the low place receives what comes from on high, this is a natural, inevitable tendency. How much more so for one who governs a great state: if they can tend themselves with humility and treat others with an open heart, which of the feudal lords in all four directions would fail to come and pay homage? From this we see that humility is the foundation of greatness, and stillness in turn is the root of humility.

From ancient times to the present, there are examples of a great state succeeding by humbling itself before a small one: King Tang of Shang at first treated his neighbor, the Earl of Ge, with courtesy, sending him oxen and sheep and dispatching people to help till his fields, and in the end, in accord with the people's hearts, he pacified and won over the land of Ge. There are also examples of a small state succeeding by humbling itself before a great one: King Goujian of Yue "slept on brushwood and tasted gall," bowing his head to serve King Fuchai of Wu, and in the end turned the tables and annexed the patrimony of Wu. In other words, when a great state does not puff itself up but willingly takes the lower position, winning over a small state is as easy as turning one's palm; and when a small state accepts its circumstances and sincerely serves the great state, winning the great state's trust and favorable opportunity is as effortless as bending down to pick up a blade of grass.

As for an illustrious great state, it is by nature the model for all other states. If it pacifies with the right method and wins people over gently, not intent on swallowing the world by force and seizing its mountains and rivers for itself, but wishing instead to embrace and nurture the smaller states and regard all under heaven as one family—then only a virtue broad and generous far beyond the ordinary could give it such composure and magnanimity, capacious as the sea that takes in a hundred rivers. Such a state naturally draws all under heaven to its benevolence, and the four quarters heed its commands. As for those tiny states, they too were originally enfeoffed vassals. They send tribute on time and hasten to fulfill the rites, afraid only of falling behind; they do not wish to blindly flex their muscles and invite humiliation, endangering the long continuity of their ancestral temples and altars of the soil, but seek only to preserve themselves by sincerely serving the great state, so that their people may recover and rest. This too requires a clear-headed, practical wisdom beyond the ordinary in order to remain humble and self-restrained; such a small state naturally wins the love of the people, and Heaven's mandate looks upon it with favor.

Moreover, a person must first have an aspiration before they have something they seek. Now the great state wishes to embrace and nourish all sides, and the small state wishes to attach itself and serve so as to protect itself. What the two seek is benevolence on one side and wisdom on the other; each obtains its own legitimate wish without slipping into greedy, selfish human desire. Here we see that states great and small each follow their own principle and keep to their own station, never daring to overstep. That said, it is only natural for the weaker party to know how to humble itself; the stronger party should all the more take the initiative in staying low—only then does the ruler's air of governing with folded hands appear, and only then does he become the one on whom all under heaven relies. Such is the principle for governing the world; how is cultivating oneself any different?

§3
Original (Chinese)

大国喻元神也，下流喻以神光下照于丹田，而阴精亦下流入丹田，神火一煅，精化气矣。此个丹田，即玄关也。夫人一身之总持，五气之期会，三花之凝聚，结丹成胎，出神入圣，无不于丹田一穴是炼焉，故曰「天下之交」，犹百川众流之朝宗于海也，炼丹之所在此。而合药之道，又贵以柔顺为主，故取象于天下之牝。牝，柔也，和也，即「太和所谓道」，又曰「专气致柔」。如此至柔至和，则元精溶溶，可以化气而生神，且元精在内，静摄肾气于其中，迨神火一煅，精化为气，于是行逆修之术，运颠倒之工，升而上之，饵而服之，送归土釜，以铅制汞，即以牡制牝，此河车以后之事。若在守中之始，心本外阳而内阴，肾本外阴而内阳。以后天身形而论，心之外阳为牡，肾之外阴为牝。今自离中虚而为阴，坎中满而为阳，即《悟真》云「饶他为主我为宾」，又曰「阳本男身女子身，阴虽女体男儿体」，此颠倒乾坤，离反为牝，坎反为牡矣。修炼之法，务令心之刚者化柔、动者为静，肾之柔者化刚、静者反动，是以离之柔和，温养坎之阳刚，此即「火中生木液，水里发金刚」。以心使气，以性节情，情不妄动，无非以默以柔，谦和忍下，以炼心性，故上田美液，流入元海，液又化气，而入丹田。「大国下小国」，即由上田到下田也。「取小国」者，采取丹田金水之气，逆运河车，上转天谷是也。「小国下大国」，又从下田上昆仑是也。「取大国」者，并合昆仑金液，共入黄庭也。或以上田甘津美液，流入下丹田以生气，则取丹田之气者，是为大国之自下以取也。又或丹田之气，逆上天谷以生液，则吞天谷之气，是为小国之以下而取也。此即金水上升，铅气合髓，精凝气调，片晌间化为甘露神水，流于上腭，滴滴归源，即液化气之候也。待气机充壮，又运河车，送上昆仑，吞脑海髓精，复降下黄庭，是气又化液之时也。然「大国者下流」，以柔以静，休休有容，诚有大过人之度，此即神化气而气化精，于以充满丹田也，故有欲兼畜人之德。小国亦有内朗之智，自知势力不敌，甘愿入觐奉命，诚有小过人之量，此即精生气而气生神，亦以归依黄庭也，故有欲入事人之道。两者所欲，均无外慕，故丹成九转，道高九天，永与乾坤并寿焉，其德之交归为何如哉？修身妙诀，无出于此。得者宝之，勿轻泄焉。

Seen from the standpoint of self-cultivation and elixir-refining, the "great state" stands for one's Original Spirit, and "the lower stream" stands for shining the light of Spirit downward into the lower Cinnabar Field, while the yin essence in the body also flows down into the Cinnabar Field. Once the fire of the Original Spirit warms and tempers it, Essence transforms into Breath. This Cinnabar Field is precisely where the Mysterious Pass lies. It is the master pivot of the whole body, the meeting place of the breaths of the five viscera (see Five Breaths return to the Origin), the place where the three flowers of Essence, Breath, and Spirit condense; forming the elixir, nurturing the Holy Embryo, sending out the spirit, and entering sagehood—not a single step is accomplished anywhere but in this one cavity of the Cinnabar Field. That is why Laozi calls it "the meeting place of the world": just as countless rivers all finally pay court to the sea, the fundamental site of elixir-refining is here.

As for the method of compounding the medicine, it prizes above all gentleness and compliance, and so Laozi again uses the image of "the female of the world." "Female" means gentle and harmonious; it is what the ancients called "Supreme Harmony is what is called the Way," and what the Daodejing calls "concentrating the breath to attain softness." Only when the heart's breath reaches such utmost gentleness and pliancy does the original essence within the body melt and dissolve, transforming into true breath and giving rise to numinous Spirit. Moreover, the original essence, quietly stored within, can gently hold the kidney breath in its embrace; once the fire of Spirit has softly warmed and tempered it and essence has transformed into breath, only then can one speak of practicing reversal and carrying out the work of inverting creation—lifting this true breath upward, swallowing and refining it, and sending it back to the "earthen cauldron" of the central palace (the central palace of spleen-earth), using the "lead" of true yang in the kidneys to subdue the lively "mercury" of the heart (see Lead and Mercury). This is "using the male to govern the female," and it belongs to the stage after the River Chariot has begun to turn.

If we return to the initial stage of guarding the center: the heart is by nature yang outside and yin within, while the kidneys are yin outside and yang within. In terms of the posterior-heaven physical body, the heart shows hard yang on the outside and so resembles the "male," while the kidneys show soft yin on the outside and so resemble the "female." But in terms of the inner structure of the trigrams, the Li trigram corresponding to the heart is empty in the middle (yin), and the Kan trigram corresponding to the kidneys is full in the middle (yang). As the Awakening to Reality says, "Let him be the host while I am the guest," and also, "Yang is at root a male body yet a woman's body; yin, though a female form, is a man's body." This is the mystery of inverting Qian and Kun: Li, reversed, becomes the yielding "female," and Kan, reversed, becomes the hard yang "male."

Therefore the method of cultivation must make the restless, unyielding force of the heart turn soft and make the agitated heart grow still, while making the originally quiescent primal breath of the kidneys turn round to become full, firm, and vigorously active. Using the gentleness of Li, the heart, to warm and nourish the hard yang of Kan, the kidneys—this is what the alchemists mean by "in the midst of fire arises the liquor of Wood; in the midst of water springs forth adamantine Metal." In the end, it comes down to using the mind-spirit to guide the breath and using true nature to regulate the passions, so that emotions do not stir wildly, refining mind and nature through nothing but silence and gentleness, modesty and forbearance. In this way the sweet fluid produced in the upper Cinnabar Field naturally flows downward into the Sea of the Origin in the Cinnabar Field; the fluid transforms into true breath and fills the Cinnabar Field.

What Laozi calls "the great state lowering itself to the small state" refers to the light of Spirit and the fluids descending from the upper Cinnabar Field to the lower; "taking the small state" means gathering the true breath of Metal-and-Water that arises in the lower Cinnabar Field, turning the River Chariot in reverse along the spine, and sending it up to the "Celestial Valley" at the crown of the head (the Muddy Pellet). "The small state lowering itself to the great state" means the true breath of the lower Cinnabar Field rising to pay court to Kunlun at the crown (the Muddy Pellet); "taking the great state" means drawing down the golden liquor generated at the crown and gathering it together into the Yellow Court of the central palace. Sometimes the sweet fluids of the upper Cinnabar Field flow into the lower Cinnabar Field to generate true breath, thereby gathering the breath of the Cinnabar Field—this is the great state deliberately lowering itself to win the small state. At other times the true breath of the lower Cinnabar Field rises in reverse to the Celestial Valley (the Sea of Marrow) to generate fluids, and one then swallows these clear fluids from the Celestial Valley—this is the small state paying court upward and thereby winning the great state.

Concretely, this process is the true breath of the Cinnabar Field rising to the Sea of Marrow, where the breath of lead unites with the brain's marrow; essence and breath condense and harmonize, and in a short while they transform into the divine water of Sweet Dew, which seeps from the upper palate and is swallowed drop by drop, returning to its source—this is the phase in which fluid transforms into breath and nourishes the viscera. When the breath has once again grown full and strong, it is sent once more along the River Chariot up to Kunlun, the Sea of Marrow, where it merges with the marrow-essence and then descends again into the Yellow Court—and this is the time when breath in turn transforms into fluid.

Thus "a great state is the lower stream": embracing all with gentle, quiet composure, it truly has a magnanimity beyond the ordinary—in alchemical terms, this is Spirit transforming into breath and breath into essence, filling the lower Cinnabar Field to the brim, just as a great state wishes to embrace and nourish the multitude. The "small state," too, has an inner, clear-sighted wisdom: knowing its strength is slight, it willingly goes up to pay court and receive commands, and it too has a rare largeness of mind—in alchemical terms, this is essence generating breath and breath generating Spirit, finally taking refuge in the Yellow Court of the central palace, just as a small state wishes to enter and serve the great one. What both above and below seek is free of any stray craving for things outside; it is simply Spirit and breath within the body naturally relying on and nourishing each other. When this cycle continues without cease, the elixir is completed through the nine reversions, the Way rises as high as the ninth heaven, and one lives as long as heaven and earth. How wondrous is this virtue of mutual return between above and below! All the secret keys of self-cultivation go no further than this principle. Those who obtain it should treasure it and not lightly divulge it to those who do not understand.

Key Terms

  • Original Spirit: In this chapter the "great state" stands for the Original Spirit; Huang stresses that in cultivation the Original Spirit must shine downward gently and quietly, rather than letting the restless mind forcibly take charge.
  • Lower Cinnabar Field: Huang regards it as "the meeting place of the world" and "the one cavity of the Mysterious Pass," the fundamental site where Essence, Breath, and Spirit gather and where the elixir forms and the embryo is conceived; see Mysterious Pass.
  • Conjunction of Kan and Li: The heart belongs to Li (yang outside, yin within), the kidneys to Kan (yin outside, yang within); the gentleness of Li's fire warms the hard yang of Kan's water from above, so that water and fire assist each other.
  • Lead and Mercury: The true yang breath in the kidneys is likened to lead, and the numinous spirit-fluid of the heart to mercury; "using lead to subdue mercury" means using full, true yang breath to gather in the drifting, restless consciousness.
  • Yellow Court: The site of the earthen cauldron of the central palace, where breath and fluids finally return to be stored and nurtured after their ascent and descent.
  • Sweet Dew: The sweet saliva that arises naturally in the mouth when one sits in pure stillness and the breath is harmonized; alchemists also call it jade liquor or divine water.
  • River Chariot, Celestial Valley, Kunlun: Traditional figurative terms of internal alchemy. The River Chariot refers to the circulation of true breath along the Governing and Conception vessels; the Celestial Valley and Kunlun both refer to the upper Cinnabar Field at the crown of the head (the Muddy Pellet).

Putting It into Practice

  1. The more strength you have, the steadier you should keep yourself. When you hold the leading role in a team or a family, heavy-handed pressure only provokes pushback; lowering yourself, listening more and arguing less, actually draws people's hearts together.
  2. Use "stillness" and "softness" to dissolve anxiety. When you are agitated and your mind is digging in its heels, stop first and let your breath soften. As this chapter says, "movement arises out of stillness, strength grows out of softness": real capacity for action comes after calm, not in the midst of panic.
  3. When sitting quietly, gently let your attention settle low. Racing thoughts are often a sign of the "heart's fire floating upward." Close your eyes and sit for a few minutes, letting your gaze and breathing naturally sink down toward the abdomen without any effort, so that a tense mind can gradually relax.
  4. Keep to your own role and do not grasp beyond it. Whether you are in a position of strength or weakness, be clear about what you truly need—the great state seeks to embrace, the small state seeks to protect itself—and avoid falling into rivalry and selfish desire; then relationships can stay peaceful for a long time.

Translator's Notes

  1. "King Tang humbled himself before the Earl of Ge": From Mencius ("Teng Wen Gong II"). King Tang lived at Bo, neighboring the state of Ge. When the Earl of Ge neglected his sacrifices, Tang first sent him oxen and sheep, then sent the people of Bo to help Ge till its fields; only after the Earl of Ge killed a boy bringing food to the fieldworkers did Tang send troops to punish him, and the hearts of all under heaven turned to Tang.
  2. "Goujian humbled himself before the King of Wu": After his defeat at Kuaiji, King Goujian of Yue asked to go to Wu as a servant and served King Fuchai of Wu; in the end, "sleeping on brushwood and tasting gall," he destroyed Wu and restored his own state.
  3. "Supreme Harmony is what is called the Way": From Zhang Zai's Correcting the Ignorant ("Supreme Harmony" chapter, Song dynasty), meaning that the balanced, unbiased harmony of the two breaths of yin and yang is itself the Way. "Concentrating the breath to attain softness" comes from Chapter 10 of the Daodejing.
  4. "Let him be the host while I am the guest": From Zhang Boduan's Awakening to Reality (Northern Song), meaning that in elixir cultivation one must not let the cognitive spirit of posterior-heaven deliberation forcibly take charge, but should step back modestly like a guest and follow the arising of prior-heaven true breath.
  5. "Li, reversed, becomes the female; Kan, reversed, becomes the male": The original woodblock edition reads here "Li, reversed, becomes the male; Kan, reversed, becomes the female." Judging from the preceding "Li is empty in the middle and thus yin, Kan is full in the middle and thus yang" and the following "using the gentleness of Li to warm and nourish the hard yang of Kan," this is clearly a printing inversion, and it has been corrected according to the sense of the text.
  6. "In the midst of fire arises the liquor of Wood; in the midst of water springs forth adamantine Metal": An alchemical cipher meaning that once the heart's fire (Li) grows quiet and gentle it can give rise to a numinous fluid, and once the kidney water (Kan) is warmed by the fire of Spirit it can generate the firm, pure-yang true breath.

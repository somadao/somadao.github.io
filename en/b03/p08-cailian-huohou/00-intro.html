Chapter Eight · Gathering, Refining and Fire Phasing · 篇首导读

Introduction to Chapter Eight

What this chapter covers

“Fire phasing” is the hardest part of the elixir way to explain, and the part with the most coded language. Huang Yuanji speaks of it quite plainly in this chapter, and it comes down to a few sentences:

  • Fire phasing is only “effort” and “naturalness”: with intention it is martial fire, without intention it is civil fire. Before the medicine is born one may use a little intention (counting the breaths, for example); once the medicine is born and has returned, let go, and nourish warmly only with the natural fire of the spirit.
  • Ordinary fire harms people: Posterior Heaven breathing is ordinary fire and must not be used; injury from practice is not the fault of effort as such, but of “stirring the Posterior Heaven fire of the triple burner”.
  • Advancing the fire is concentrating the spirit; gathering the medicine is natural breathing wrapping around it — like a blacksmith’s furnace fire and bellows, the two working together.
  • The medicine can be tender or old: gathering before spirit and breath have merged is tender; once awareness has come and the mind runs outward again, it is old.
  • When you see light, do not cling to it: the round light or brightness that appears in stillness becomes an obstruction as soon as you linger over it.
  • Do not chase phenomena: the traditional “six signs” are attached to the turbid Posterior Heaven breath and easily stir ordinary fire by mistake; the real great medicine is marked by mild harmony. Even if no phenomena appear at all, as long as there is one expanse of clear breath, the breath of the true One is there.

The six sections of this chapter follow this order:

How many grades of medicine there are (the outer medicine, the great medicine and the Holy Embryo) → how many kinds of fire there are (civil fire and martial fire) → how advancing the fire and gathering the medicine work together (the method of advancing the fire and gathering the medicine) → when to gather, and how to judge tender and old (gathering the medicine at the Mysterious Pass) → the two moments of yang arising and yin arising (fire phasing at the living zi and living wu) → the signs of the great medicine (the signs of the great medicine arising)

What this chapter is about (in one sentence)

At first use a little intention (martial fire); as soon as something stirs, let go and leave it to nature (civil fire); the spirit concentrated in the elixir field and the breathing natural, gather it back lightly when spirit and breath have merged into one piece; when yang has risen to the head, stop the fire and shine downward, and when yin arises, nourish warmly; do not cling to light or chase phenomena — take mild harmony as the standard.

Reading tips

  1. The four most practical places in this chapter: “first martial, then civil” in Section 2; “what to do when restless breath surges during sitting” in Section 3; “when you see light, do not cling to it” and “once it is obtained, stop the martial fire” in Section 5; “it does not matter if there are no phenomena” in Section 6.
  2. “Ordinary fire” is the root of deviations. Forcing, hurrying, dry heat and holding the breath all stir Posterior Heaven ordinary fire. This is why, as Huang Yuanji says, people are injured by their practice.
  3. Safety note: this chapter speaks in many places of yang energy rising to the Muddy Pellet, concentrating the spirit in the Muddy Pellet, passing the gates and swallowing — these belong to a more advanced stage. Huang Yuanji’s own way is: when yang has risen to the head, become still, stop the fire, and turn to shining down on the elixir field. If a swollen head, headache, flushed face, ringing in the ears, palpitations or insomnia appear, relax at once and rest the intention lightly on the lower belly or the soles of the feet; if it does not subside, consult a doctor.

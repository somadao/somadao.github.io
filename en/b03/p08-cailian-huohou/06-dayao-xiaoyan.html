Chapter Eight · Gathering, Refining and Fire Phasing · 大药发生效验

Signs of the Great Medicine Arising

Signs of the great medicine arising — the six signs are attached to the Posterior Heaven corpse-breath, and ordinary fire must not be stirred by mistake; the fire is the medicine, the medicine is the fire; even without seeing vigorous surging, as long as there is one expanse of clear breath, the heavenly truth is always present

About This Chapter

The last section of this chapter speaks of the signs when the great medicine arises, and of Huang Yuanji’s reserved attitude toward the traditional “six signs”.

  1. The six signs: from the birth of the lesser medicine and the first stirring of the one yang, to the time of gathering, passing the gates, swallowing and warm nourishing, there are six signs — fiery heat in the elixir field, the two kidneys boiling like broth, golden light in the eyes, a strange taste in the mouth, an eagle’s cry in the ears, wind arising in the brain — but all of these are “the form of the formless, made manifest by attaching to the formed corpse-breath of Posterior Heaven”: it is not that there really is a vast, flowing breath, utmost in greatness and strength, set before one’s eyes. It is only that my spirit perceives it and my spirit knows it; others cannot see it.
  2. Why one must know this: only by recognizing this will one not stir ordinary fire by mistake, take the wrong road, and be harmed by the corpse-like, filthy breath of Posterior Heaven.
  3. Gathering and replenishing are very simple: when the one yang stirs, do not run about rashly or leak outward, and in time the one breath steams through — it is the medicine, and it is the fire too. From outside to inside, from above to below, gathering it back against the current — that is gathering; drawing it back into the furnace and tripod — that is replenishing. The fire is the medicine, the medicine is the fire. The one breath of supreme harmony without the slightest idle thought or stray worry, empty, hollow and clearly bright — that is the Way; the steaming of this one breath is the medicine; gathering in this breath of the spirit, not mixed with a scrap of half-understanding, is replenishing.
  4. The sign of the great medicine arising: “Suddenly at midnight a clap of thunder is heard, and ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open one after another” — the one yang returns; golden light in the eyes and sweet dew in the mouth; quickly gather it, pass the gates and swallow. At this time the root of lust has fully retracted and the yang gate is firmly closed; cut off the outer breathing and use the inner spirit-breath; the breath enters the spirit and the spirit wraps around the breath, and in time there is a seamless whole with no coming and going of breath, only a point of spiritual light faintly felt on the Numinous Terrace — the original breath has transformed into the original spirit. Then move the spirit to the upper field and refine for seven days with the non-acting fire of the spirit, and the work of the jade fluid is complete.
  5. The most important passage (§4): if in quiet sitting, with a sudden glance of reverting the light, one suddenly sees something so great it has no outside and so small it has no inside, mingling richly and surging vigorously, expanding vast and all-pervading — this is of course the stirring of the heavenly dynamic, which can be gathered and refined. Even if one has sat a long time without seeing such an all-embracing breath-dynamic, as long as there is one expanse of clear breath, without thought or worry, neither going out nor coming in — the breath of the true One is still stored within; it is only that the Posterior Heaven breath is weak and cannot lift it up. This too is the heavenly truth always present, and it too can be gathered and swallowed. One must never, because there is no vigorous surging and rich mingling, let the mind run off wherever it likes. Huang Yuanji says: “This is the key formula.”

Qiu Ling’s note: the six signs handed down in the world have been followed through the ages, but Huang holds that they are attached to the Posterior Heaven corpse-breath and easily stir ordinary fire by mistake. What Huang means by the great medicine arising is mainly that, with a glance of reverting the light, one suddenly sees a phenomenon of rich mingling and vigorous surging, vast and deep, firm, strong, central and upright, different from the ordinary breath of everyday; Lü Dongbin says “when you reach the point where the water is warm and the body warm, then advance the fire and go forward on your way” — this warmth too differs from the warmth of the breath stirring on ordinary days, and has its own carefree delight. The state Huang describes is far milder and more harmonious than the six phenomena handed down in the world, exactly in keeping with the ancients’ “taking mild harmony as the working of the great medicine”.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

吾教所以名为小药生，又曰一阳初动，及至采取过关、服食、温养之候，虽有丹田火热、两肾汤煎、目有金光、口有异味、耳有鹫鸣、脑有风生六种效验，然亦无形之形，附于后天有形之尸气而昭著，实非有浩然之气至刚至大在于目前，而充塞于两大之间者也。此亦虚拟其状，似有如此之盛。要皆我神觉之，我神知之，非外人所得而窥也。吾教谓之真阳大动，又曰大药发生，以其实有可拟，故曰真阳。以其气机之大，不似以前之微动，故曰大药。生等识此始不错动凡火，错走路头，为后天尸秽之气所害焉。

We Daoists therefore call it “the birth of the lesser medicine”, and also “the first stirring of the one yang”; when it comes to the time of gathering, passing the gates, swallowing and warm nourishing, although there are six signs — fiery heat in the elixir field, the two kidneys boiling like broth (the two kidneys as if broth were cooking in them), golden light in the eyes, a strange taste in the mouth, an eagle’s cry in the ears (a sound behind the ears as if an eagle were crying), wind arising in the brain — these too are only the form of the formless, made manifest by attaching to the formed corpse-breath of Posterior Heaven; it is truly not that there is a vast, flowing breath, utmost in strength and greatness, set before one’s eyes and filling the space between heaven and earth. This too only imitates its appearance, as if it were so grand. In short, it is all perceived by my spirit and known by my spirit, not something others can glimpse. We Daoists call it “true yang stirring greatly”, and also “the great medicine arising”: because there really is something it can be likened to, it is called “true yang”; because its breath-dynamic is great, not a faint stirring like before, it is called “the great medicine”. Only when you recognize this will you not stir ordinary fire by mistake, take the wrong road, and be harmed by the corpse-like, filthy breath of Posterior Heaven.

§2
Original (Chinese)

要之采取先天以补后天，究竟如何采、如何补哉？不过一阳动不妄走作，不外渗漏，久之一气薰蒸。薰蒸之气，药也是他，火也是他。于此外而内之，上而下之，逆而收之，即采取也。于此收回鼎炉中，即返补也。火即是药，药即是火，火与药是二而一者。人知道太和一气无半点闲思杂虑，只见空洞了明，大而无外，小而无内，气机似有非有，似无非无，即道也。有此一气薰蒸即是药也。收敛此神之气，不许参杂一知半解，即补矣！

In short, gathering Prior Heaven to replenish Posterior Heaven — how exactly does one gather, and how replenish? It is no more than this: when the one yang stirs, do not move rashly and run about, do not leak outward, and in time the one breath steams through — this steaming breath is the medicine, and it is the fire too. At this time, drawing it from outside inward, leading it from above downward, gathering it back against the current — that is gathering; drawing it back into the furnace and tripod — that is replenishing. The fire is the medicine, the medicine is the fire; fire and medicine are two and yet one. If one knows the one breath of supreme harmony, without the slightest idle thought or stray worry, seeing only emptiness, hollowness and clear brightness, so great it has no outside, so small it has no inside, the breath-dynamic seeming to be and yet not being, seeming not to be and yet not absent — that is the Way; the steaming of this one breath is the medicine; gathering in this breath of the spirit, not allowing a scrap of half-understanding to be mixed in, is replenishing!

§3
Original (Chinese)

古云：“忽闻夜半一声雷，万户千门次第开。”此即一阳来复之候。眼有金光发见，口有甘露来朝，此大药发生之验也，急忙采取过关、服食。此时淫根缩尽，阳关固闭，绝外呼吸，用内神息，不许一点渗漏，务令息息尽归真，神神齐听命，使此气入神中，神包气外，久之浑然无气息往来，惟觉一点灵光隐约在灵台之上，则元气已化元神矣！自此气合于漠，神凝于虚，似有似无，不内不外，以炼至虚至灵之神，再行向上功夫，迁神于上田，以无为神火炼七日过关服食之功，则玉液功成。

The ancients said: “Suddenly at midnight a clap of thunder is heard, and ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open one after another.” This is the time of the one yang returning. Golden light appears in the eyes and sweet dew comes to the mouth — these are the signs of the great medicine arising, and one quickly gathers it, passes the gates and swallows. At this time the root of lust has fully retracted and the yang gate is firmly closed; cut off the outer breathing and use the inner spirit-breath, allowing not the slightest leak; be sure that every breath returns to the true and every spirit obeys the command; let this breath enter into the spirit and the spirit wrap around the breath, and in time there is a seamless whole with no coming and going of breath, only a point of spiritual light felt faintly upon the Numinous Terrace (the heart) — the original breath has already transformed into the original spirit! From then on, the breath merges with the boundless and the spirit concentrates in emptiness, seeming to be and seeming not to be, neither inside nor outside, refining the utterly empty, utterly spiritual spirit; then do the work of going upward, moving the spirit to the upper field, and with the non-acting fire of the spirit, refine for seven days the work of passing the gates and swallowing — and the work of the jade fluid is complete.

§4
Original (Chinese)

诸子静坐涵养本源，从寂然不动中瞥地回光，忽见其大无内，其小无外，入无积聚，出无分散，细缊蓬勃，扩大宏通之状，固是天机发动，可采可炼，可以服食之大药。即使静坐已久，不见有渊涵一切包罗万象之机，只要一片清气，无思无虑，不出不入，亦是我真一之气蕴蓄其中，只是我后天气弱，不能冲举他壮大耳。此亦是天真常在，亦可采之服食。切不可以无此蓬勃氤氲，而任其心之走作可也。此为要诀。

When you sit quietly and cultivate the original source, out of stillness and non-movement, with a glance of reverting the light, you suddenly see it so great that it has no boundary and so small that it has no inside, entering without accumulating, going out without dispersing, mingling richly, surging vigorously, expanding vast and all-pervading — this is of course the stirring of the heavenly dynamic, which can be gathered and refined: the great medicine that can be swallowed. But even if you have sat quietly a long time without seeing such a breath-dynamic that holds everything in its depths and embraces all phenomena, as long as there is one expanse of clear breath, without thought or worry, neither going out nor coming in — this too is the breath of my true One stored within; it is only that my Posterior Heaven breath is weak and cannot lift it up and make it grow strong. This too is the heavenly truth always present, and it too can be gathered and swallowed. You must never, because there is no such vigorous surging and rich mingling, let your mind run off wherever it likes. This is the key formula.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

世传大药发生之候为丹田火热、两肾汤煎等六种效验，历代遵行久矣！然而，黄氏于此则认为“附于后天有形之尸气而昭著”，易错动凡火为其所害。

黄氏认为，大药发生之候实有可拟，眼有金光发见，口有甘露来朝固然是矣，但主要是瞥地回光忽见氤氲蓬勃包罗万象之清新气机，有浩浩渊渊刚健中正之象，与平日凡气不同。此即真一之气。吕洞宾云：“曲江月淡水澄清，沐浴须当定主宾，若到水温身暖处，便宜进火办前程。”可见水温身暖，亦为大药发生之候，其暖亦与平日气动之暖不同，自有逍遥美快自在之处。黄氏所述大药发生之境界，较之世传六种景象冲和得多，正合古云“以冲和为大药之用”之意。

The signs of the great medicine arising handed down in the world are the six signs — fiery heat in the elixir field, the two kidneys boiling like broth, and so on — and they have been followed through the ages for a long time! However, Huang here holds that these are “made manifest by attaching to the formed corpse-breath of Posterior Heaven”, and easily lead one to stir ordinary fire by mistake and be harmed by it.

Huang holds that when the great medicine arises there really are signs it can be likened to: golden light appearing in the eyes and sweet dew coming to the mouth — that is so; but mainly, with a glance of reverting the light, one suddenly sees a fresh breath-dynamic of rich mingling and vigorous surging that embraces all phenomena, with an air vast and deep, firm, strong, central and upright, different from the ordinary breath of everyday — this is the breath of the true One. Lü Dongbin says: “On the Winding River the moon is pale and the water clear; in ablution one must settle host and guest; when you reach the point where the water is warm and the body warm, then advance the fire and go forward on your way.” (In the lower elixir field the moonlight is pale and the water clear and limpid; in ablution one must distinguish host and guest; when the water is warm and the body warm, one may advance the fire and press on toward what lies ahead.) So “the water warm and the body warm” is also the time of the great medicine arising; and this warmth too differs from the warmth of the breath stirring on ordinary days, having its own carefree delight and ease. The state of the great medicine arising that Huang describes is far milder and more harmonious than the six phenomena handed down in the world, exactly in keeping with what the ancients meant by “taking mild harmony as the working of the great medicine”.

Key Terms

  • The six signs: fiery heat in the elixir field, the two kidneys boiling like broth, golden light in the eyes, a strange taste in the mouth, an eagle’s cry in the ears, wind arising in the brain; see the great medicine passing the gates, the shaking of the six roots.
  • Corpse-breath, corpse-like filthy breath: the formed, turbid breath of Posterior Heaven.
  • True yang stirring greatly, the great medicine arising: a breath-dynamic far greater than the faint stirrings before.
  • Steaming breath: when the one yang stirs and does not leak, in time the whole body is steamed through; the medicine is the fire.
  • From outside to inside, from above to below, gathering it back against the current: gathering.
  • Replenishing: drawing it back into the furnace and tripod.
  • Suddenly at midnight a clap of thunder is heard, and ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open one after another: a metaphor for the return of the one yang.
  • The root of lust fully retracted: the outer kidneys retracting; see the horse-like retraction.
  • Cutting off the outer breathing and using the inner spirit-breath: the mouth-and-nose breathing so fine it seems absent, with the True Breath as the main thing.
  • Numinous Terrace: the heart.
  • Moving the spirit to the upper field: once the practice is deep, the spirit moves to the upper elixir field; a very advanced stage.
  • The heavenly truth always present: the breath of the true One is always there; it is only that the Posterior Heaven breath is weak and has not been able to make it grow strong.
  • Taking mild harmony as the working of the great medicine: the great medicine is marked by mild harmony.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Do not chase “phenomena”. Fiery heat in the elixir field, golden light, ringing in the ears, the sound of wind in the head and so on — Huang Yuanji holds that these are all attached to the turbid Posterior Heaven breath and easily lead one to stir ordinary fire by mistake.
  2. The real standard is mild harmony: fresh, vast, firm, strong, central and upright; the body warm and at ease, carefree and delighted — different from ordinary dry heat.
  3. It does not matter if there are no phenomena (the most important point): if you have sat a long time and seen nothing at all, as long as there is one expanse of clear breath in the mind, without thought or worry — the breath of the true One is still there; just go on cultivating it, and never let your mind scatter because of this.
  4. Safety note: “cutting off the outer breathing” means that in utmost stillness the mouth-and-nose breathing naturally becomes so fine it seems absent — not holding the breath; “moving the spirit to the upper field” belongs to a very advanced stage, and beginners should not attempt it. If ringing in the ears, a sound of wind in the head, or flashes before the eyes appear together with a swollen head, headache, palpitations or insomnia, these are not “signs” but deviations: stop at once and relax, and consult a doctor if necessary.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is three paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The first paragraph is divided into two short passages.
  2. “An eagle’s cry in the ears” (耳有鹫鸣): some elixir books read “an eagle’s cry behind the ears”, meaning a sound behind the ears as if an eagle were crying.
  3. “Moving the spirit to the upper field” (迁神于上田): the original book has 迁神; reproduced as it stands.
  4. The poem “On the Winding River the moon is pale and the water clear…” (曲江月淡水澄清……) is attributed by Qiu Ling’s note to Lü Dongbin; reproduced as it stands.

Chapter Eight · Gathering, Refining and Fire Phasing · 文火武火

Civil Fire and Martial Fire

Civil fire and martial fire — martial fire for battle in the field, civil fire for defending the city; fire phasing is only effort and naturalness; ordinary fire harms people, take the Prior Heaven spirit-breath as the master; forging the sword and casting the mirror

About This Chapter

This section is the most concentrated and most practical passage on fire phasing in the whole book.

  1. Four kinds of fire: civil fire, martial fire, the fire of ablution, and the fire of returning to the furnace and sealing it.
  2. Martial fire: at the very start of practice, when the spirit has not yet concentrated, the breath is not yet regulated, and spirit and breath have not yet joined, apply a little intention and tighten up slightly — counting the breaths, for example, is martial fire.
  3. Civil fire: when the spirit has concentrated a little and the breath is a little regulated, and spirit and breath have joined slightly but not yet become fully practiced, lighten the intention a little and no longer count the breaths rigidly — this is civil fire, “with intention and without intention”.
  4. Metaphors: martial fire for battle in the field (bandits rising on all sides, one has no choice but to use troops); civil fire for defending the city (weapons at rest, settling the people in their work, yet still on guard); when the people are at peace, goods abundant, rain and wind in season, neither civil nor martial fire is used — only the fire of warm nourishing and ablution.
  5. Fire phasing is only “effort” and “naturalness”: before the medicine is born, cook fiercely and refine urgently, rousing the spirit as if going into battle; after the medicine is born, run the River Chariot — if the spirit is not roused, it is hard to make the clear rise and the turbid sink; “concentrating the breath to reach softness” means single-minded devotion and utmost harmony, not telling people to go slack.
  6. Ordinary fire harms people: the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath is ordinary fire and must not be used in the slightest; take the Prior Heaven spirit-breath as the master. Even if the ordinary breath has not stopped, pay it no mind, and only merge with the Prior Heaven spirit-breath thought after thought — then body and mind, inside and out, are like a crystal pagoda, a glass vase, one body with heaven and earth.
  7. Forging the sword and casting the mirror: the sword is the Prior Heaven original breath (lift it constantly, do not let it weaken; the sword of wisdom can cut down the Three Corpses); the mirror is the Prior Heaven original spirit (keep awakening it, do not let it wander; the bright mirror hangs on high).
  8. The fault in how the disciples use fire: the injury is not the fault of effort, but of stirring the Posterior Heaven fire of the triple burner; and now they are close to being soft and feeble, so true yang sinks and drowns.
  9. The key to fire phasing: “Before the medicine leaves the ore, fierce fire is needed; once it has returned to the furnace, warm nourishing is fitting”; the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit is only a gently warm fire of the spirit, neither clinging nor apart; “concentrate the spirit, soften the intention”. When the medicine-breath rises to the Muddy Pellet, concentrate the spirit for a moment, and the yin essence transforms into sweet dew that descends into the Crimson Palace and returns to the elixir field to be warmly nourished — few people know this step of concentrating the spirit in the Muddy Pellet.

Qiu Ling’s note: at the very start, when spirit and breath have not yet joined, use martial fire; as spirit and breath gradually join, use civil fire; before the medicine leaves the furnace, use martial fire; once it has left the furnace, use civil fire; when it returns to the furnace, warmly nourish. The ancients said “fire phasing is only breathing” and “fire phasing is only the True Breath” — martial fire requires skill in using the outer breathing, civil fire requires skill in regulating the True Breath.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

修炼之术别无他妙，但火候而已。夫炼丹有文火、有武火、有沐浴之火、有归炉封固之火，此其大概也。夫武火何以用何时用哉？当其初下手时，神未凝息未调，神气二者未交，此当稍着意念，略打紧些，即数息以起刻漏者，是其武火也。迨至神稍凝，气稍调，神气二者略略相交但未至于纯熟，此当用文火以固济之，意念略略放轻，不似前此之死死执着数息，是即文火也。

The method of cultivation has no other secret — it is only fire phasing. In refining the elixir there is civil fire, martial fire, the fire of ablution, and the fire of returning to the furnace and sealing it — that is the general outline. Why is martial fire used, and when? At the very start of practice, when the spirit has not yet concentrated, the breath is not yet regulated, and spirit and breath have not yet joined, one should apply a little intention and tighten up slightly — for example, using the counting of breaths to keep time (setting up the water clock): this is martial fire. When the spirit has concentrated a little and the breath is a little regulated, and spirit and breath have joined slightly but are not yet fully practiced, one should use civil fire to make it firm: lighten the intention a little, no longer holding rigidly to counting the breaths as before — this is civil fire.

§2
Original (Chinese)

古云，野战用武火，守城用文火。野战者何？如兵戈扰攘之秋，贼氛四起，不可不用兵以战退魔寇，即是武火之谓。迨至干戈宁静，烽烟无警，又当安置人民各理职业，虽不用兵威，然亦不可不提防之耳，此为文火，有意无意者也。若民安物阜，雨顺风调，野无鸡犬之惊，人鲜雀鼠之讼，斯可以文武火不用，而专用温养浴沐之火。

The ancients said: “Martial fire for battle in the field, civil fire for defending the city.” What is battle in the field? It is like a time of war and chaos, with bandits rising on all sides — one cannot but use troops to drive back the demon-bandits: this is called martial fire. When weapons are at rest and no beacon smoke gives alarm, one should settle the people, each in their own work; although military might is not used, one cannot fail to stay on guard — this is civil fire, “with intention and without intention”. When it comes to the time when the people are at peace and goods abundant, wind and rain are in season, there is no alarm among the chickens and dogs in the countryside, and little quarreling of sparrows and rats (petty disputes) among people, one may use neither civil nor martial fire, but only the fire of warm nourishing and ablution.

§3
Original (Chinese)

火候之事别无机密，只是一个勉强、自然，分文分武而已。药未生时，必须猛烹急炼以煅真金，如打仗然，务要振顿精神，奋勇争先，切不可输与他。故丹经云降魔杵，斩妖剑，字字皆金鍼也。药既生后，当行河车功法，若精神不振亦难使清升而浊降。古云：“专气致柔”，亦不过言一心一德之专致，极其和顺，非教之放弱也。

The matter of fire phasing has no other secret: it is only “effort” (using intention) and “naturalness” (not using intention), divided into civil and martial. Before the medicine is born, one must cook fiercely and refine urgently to forge the true metal, as if going into battle — one must rouse one’s spirit and charge forward bravely, and never lose to it (stray thoughts, torpor). That is why the elixir classics speak of “the demon-subduing pestle” and “the monster-slaying sword” — every word is a golden needle (precious guidance). After the medicine is born, one should practice the method of the River Chariot; if the spirit is not roused, it is also hard to make the clear breath rise and the turbid breath sink. The ancients said “concentrate the breath to reach softness”, but that only means single-minded, whole-hearted focus, utmost harmony — it does not teach people to go slack and lax.

§4
Original (Chinese)

总要将后天凡息停止，不可丝毫运用。盖后天凡息凡火也，凡火伤人不可用，必须以先天神息无形无象者为主。纵有后天之息未止，我亦不理他，只心心念念融会先天神息，而后天凡息一听其上下往来，我不睬他张他，即得先天神息之用。于是身心内外自如水晶塔子、琉璃宝瓶，通天通地、亘古亘今，觉得天地人物无不与我同体，两相关切。迨至二元混合返乎太古之天，此时用火无火，几如大化流行，上下与天地一也。

In all cases the ordinary Posterior Heaven breathing must be stopped and not used in the slightest. For the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath is ordinary fire, and ordinary fire harms people and must not be used; one must take the formless, imageless Prior Heaven spirit-breath as the master. Even if the Posterior Heaven breath has not yet stopped, I pay it no mind, and merge with the Prior Heaven spirit-breath with every thought; let the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath come and go up and down — I ignore it and do not attend to it, and so I obtain the working of the Prior Heaven spirit-breath. Then body and mind, inside and out, naturally become like a crystal pagoda, a precious glass vase, reaching through heaven and earth, spanning ancient and present, and one feels that heaven, earth, people and things are all one body with oneself, each bound up with the other. When the two originals (spirit and breath) have mingled and returned to the heaven of high antiquity, one uses fire yet there is no fire, almost like the great transformation flowing on, above and below at one with heaven and earth.

§5
Original (Chinese)

学人第一要炼剑。剑即先天元气也。第二要铸镜。镜即先天元神也。神无杂妄，常常唤醒，不许走作，即明镜高悬，物来必照矣！气由积累，时时提掇，不放他弱，即慧剑排空，能斩三尸矣！

The student must first forge the sword — the sword is the Prior Heaven original breath; second, cast the mirror — the mirror is the Prior Heaven original spirit. When the spirit is without stray thoughts and delusions, keep awakening it and do not let it run off — that is “the bright mirror hanging on high”: whatever comes is sure to be reflected! The breath relies on accumulation; lift it at all times and do not let it weaken — that is “the sword of wisdom sweeping the void”, able to cut down the Three Corpses (three evil spirits in the body that lead people to fall, a metaphor for greed and the other desires)!

§6
Original (Chinese)

吾观诸子用火有伤，不是用力之过，是动后天三焦火之过。而今又近柔懦，故真阳陷溺，不经神火猛烹急炼，断不能飞腾而上泥丸，以补脑而还精。

I see that you have suffered some injury in using fire; this is not the fault of effort, but the fault of stirring the Posterior Heaven fire of the triple burner. And now you are again close to being soft and feeble, so true yang sinks and drowns; without being fiercely cooked and urgently refined by the fire of the spirit, it can certainly not soar up to the Muddy Pellet to replenish the brain and return the essence.

§7
Original (Chinese)

古云：“药物生玄窍，火候发阳炉。”斯时金已煅出，惟有略用一点真意采而受之足矣。若药未出时，不妨温温铅鼎。故曰：“药未出矿须猛火，药已归炉宜温养。”足见药生之火武火也。药还之火文火也。火候文武只有意无意之分焉耳。其余周天火候，只一个温温神火，不即不离，斯无危殆焉！故曰：“凝其神，柔其意。”盖神不凝则丹不聚；意不柔则火不纯。火不纯而丹亦难成也。故升降之际有浴沐抽添者此耳。

The ancients said: “The medicine is born in the mysterious opening; fire phasing sets out from the yang furnace.” At this point the metal has already been forged out; one need only use a little true intention to gather it and receive it, and that is enough. If the medicine has not yet come out, it does no harm to keep the lead cauldron (the lower elixir field) warm and warm. So it is said: “Before the medicine leaves the ore, fierce fire is needed; once it has returned to the furnace, warm nourishing is fitting.” Thus the fire when the medicine is born is martial fire, and the fire when the medicine returns is civil fire — the civil and martial of fire phasing are only the distinction between with intention and without intention. As for the rest of the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit, it is only a gently warm fire of the spirit, neither clinging nor apart, and then there is no danger! So it is said: “Concentrate the spirit, soften the intention.” For if the spirit is not concentrated, the elixir does not gather; if the intention is not soft, the fire is not pure; and if the fire is not pure, the elixir is hard to complete. That is why, in the rising and descending, there is ablution, withdrawing and adding.

§8
Original (Chinese)

到得药气已上泥丸，尤当一意不散，一尘不起，凝聚精神，团于一处温养片刻，然后脑中阴精化为甘露神水滴入绛宫。冶炼片时而后化为金液，归于丹田温养成珠。此处务要温温铅鼎，以行封固可也。然此封固，内想不出，外想不入，人多知之。若泥丸宫内凝聚一时烹炼成药，人少知也。夫以此个宫内极是清虚玄朗。落于后天致有渣滓之窒塞，所以其神不清，其心不灵，常不免于昏懵。若能凝聚半晌，则浊气自降，清气自升，常与天地轻清之气相通。苟能久久温养，则清气充而浊气去，不但身体康强，颜色光耀，而金液大还无非由此静养之功积成也。

When the medicine-breath has risen to the Muddy Pellet, one should all the more keep the intention from scattering and let not a speck of dust arise, concentrating the spirit and gathering it in one place to nourish warmly for a moment; then the yin essence in the brain transforms into sweet dew, the spirit water, and drips into the Crimson Palace; refined there for a while, it transforms into golden fluid, returns to the elixir field, and is warmly nourished into a pearl. Here one must keep the lead cauldron warm and warm, and carry out the sealing. This sealing — “thoughts within do not go out, thoughts without do not come in” — many people know; but concentrating for a time within the Muddy Pellet palace, cooking and refining it into medicine, few people know. This palace is by nature utterly clear, empty, mysterious and bright; after falling into Posterior Heaven, it has dregs blocking it, so the spirit is not clear and the mind not alert, and one often cannot avoid being dazed and muddled. If one can concentrate there for a good while, the turbid breath naturally descends and the clear breath naturally rises, constantly communicating with the light, clear breath between heaven and earth. If one can nourish warmly for a long time, the clear breath becomes full and the turbid breath is removed; not only is the body healthy and strong and the complexion glowing, but the Great Returned Elixir of golden fluid, too, is nothing but the accumulation of this work of quiet nourishing.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§8
Original note (Chinese)

此言文武火在采炼中之运用。初下手时神气未交用武火，神气渐交时则用文火。药未出炉时用武火，药已出炉用文火，药归炉则宜温养。

古人说，火候只是呼吸二字。又说，火候只是真息。则知武火须善用外呼吸；文火则须善调真息。古人用“温温铅鼎”形容武火，此句出自吕洞宾《沁园春》词：“温温铅鼎，光透帘帏。”意即火气温温于产铅之鼎。

用文火或沐浴温养中如调真息得当，元神有如明镜高悬，专意致柔，则可做到身心白化晶化，我与天地同体，人天合一的境界。

运行河车中药气上于泥丸，其火候最为重要，当用温养之候。只有温养片刻，脑中阴精才能化为甘露神水而实现还丹。

This section speaks of the use of civil and martial fire in gathering and refining: at the very start of practice, when spirit and breath have not yet joined, use martial fire; when spirit and breath are gradually joining, use civil fire. Before the medicine leaves the furnace, use martial fire; once the medicine has left the furnace, use civil fire; when the medicine returns to the furnace, warm nourishing is fitting.

The ancients said “fire phasing is only the two words ‘breathing’”, and also “fire phasing is only the True Breath”. From this we know: martial fire requires skill in using the outer breathing, civil fire requires skill in regulating the True Breath. The ancients used “the lead cauldron warm and warm” to describe martial fire; this line comes from Lü Dongbin’s lyric to the tune Spring in the Qin Garden: “The lead cauldron warm and warm, light coming through the curtains.” It means that the breath of the fire is gently warm in the cauldron that produces the lead.

When using civil fire or the warm nourishing of ablution, if the True Breath is properly regulated, the original spirit is like a bright mirror hanging on high, the intention concentrated to reach softness, and one can make body and mind “turn white and turn crystal” (pure white and crystal clear), oneself one body with heaven and earth, reaching the state of the unity of heaven and the human.

When running the River Chariot, as the medicine-breath rises to the Muddy Pellet, the fire phasing at this time is most important, and one should use the fire phasing of warm nourishing; only by nourishing warmly for a moment can the yin essence in the brain transform into sweet dew, the spirit water, and the returned elixir be achieved.

Key Terms

  • Fire phasing: civil fire, martial fire, the fire of ablution, the fire of returning to the furnace and sealing it.
  • Martial fire: applying a little intention at the very start, when spirit and breath have not yet joined, such as counting the breaths.
  • Civil fire: lightening the intention when spirit and breath have joined slightly, with intention and without intention.
  • Water clock: an ancient timekeeping device; here, keeping time by counting the breaths.
  • Battle in the field, defending the city: metaphors for martial fire and civil fire.
  • Effort and naturalness: using intention and not using intention.
  • Demon-subduing pestle, monster-slaying sword: metaphors in the elixir classics for diligent practice.
  • Golden needle: precious guidance.
  • The ordinary Posterior Heaven breath is ordinary fire: the fire of mouth-and-nose breathing, which harms people and must not be used.
  • Prior Heaven spirit-breath: the formless, imageless True Breath; see True Breath.
  • Forging the sword and casting the mirror: the sword is the Prior Heaven original breath, the mirror the Prior Heaven original spirit.
  • Three Corpses: in Daoism, three evil spirits in the body that lead people to do wrong; a metaphor for greed, anger and the other desires.
  • Fire of the triple burner: Posterior Heaven dry fire.
  • Before the medicine leaves the ore, fierce fire is needed; once it has returned to the furnace, warm nourishing is fitting: the overall key to fire phasing.
  • Concentrate the spirit, soften the intention: the key to the fire phasing of the Celestial Circuit.
  • Great Returned Elixir of golden fluid: a name for the completed elixir.

Putting It into Practice

  1. In one sentence: first martial, then civil — at first, when the spirit is not concentrated and the breath not regulated, you may count the breaths (martial fire); once spirit and breath settle a little, lighten up and stop counting rigidly (civil fire); once settled, use neither civil nor martial, only warm nourishing.
  2. Do not “refine” with Posterior Heaven breathing: simply let the breathing be natural, without controlling or pumping it; the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath is ordinary fire and harms people.
  3. Rouse yourself without forcing: martial fire means the spirit being roused, not the body exerting force. When Huang Yuanji says people are injured by using fire, it is from “stirring the Posterior Heaven fire of the triple burner” — that is, forcing, hurrying, dry heat.
  4. Safety note: “concentrating the spirit in the Muddy Pellet” applies only for a moment, once the medicine-breath has already risen to the Muddy Pellet; in ordinary practice do not place the intention in the head. If dry heat, a swollen head, palpitations or insomnia appear during practice, ordinary fire has been stirred: relax at once, return to natural breathing, and rest the intention lightly on the lower belly.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is four paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The first paragraph is divided into two short passages, the second into three, and the fourth into two.
  2. “Every word is a golden needle” (字字皆金鍼也): 鍼 is the same as 针, “needle”.
  3. “I pay him no mind” (我不睬他张他): zhang 张 here means to pay attention to, to take notice of.
  4. “Refined there for a while” (冶炼片时): the original book has 冶炼; reproduced as it stands.
  5. Qiu Ling’s note says “the lead cauldron warm and warm” describes martial fire, while Huang Yuanji’s own text — “if the medicine has not yet come out, it does no harm to keep the lead cauldron warm and warm”, and later “here one must keep the lead cauldron warm and warm, and carry out the sealing” — leans rather toward the sense of warm nourishing; readers may compare the two.

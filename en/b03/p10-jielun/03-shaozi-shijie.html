Chapter Ten · Conclusion · 邵子月窟天根诗解

Explaining Master Shao’s Poem on the Cave of the Moon and the Root of Heaven

Explaining Master Shao’s poem on the Cave of the Moon and the Root of Heaven — tread the Root of Heaven with intention, explore the Cave of the Moon with the mind; between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon, coming and going at leisure, all thirty-six palaces are spring

About This Chapter

The last section of the book. At his disciples’ request, Huang Yuanji explains the famous poem “The Cave of the Moon and the Root of Heaven” by Shao Yong (Shao Kangjie) of the Northern Song:

耳目聪明男子身，鸿钧赋予不为贫。须探月窟方知物，未蹑天根岂识人。乾遇巽时观月窟，地逢雷处见天根。天根月窟闲来往，三十六宫都是春。

  1. The Root of Heaven: the one yang within Kan — in heaven it is the Supreme Ultimate, in the human being it is life, the root by which all the parts of the body are nourished; it sprouts in the lower elixir field.
  2. The Cave of the Moon: the one yang within Kan stirs and rises through to the crown of the head like a full moon, gathering in the Muddy Pellet — “the moon reaching the heart of the sky”; full to the extreme, it wanes, hence it is called a “cave”.
  3. Treading and exploring: “treading the Root of Heaven” is done with intention — the intention single-mindedly poured into the Root of Heaven, like feet planted on solid ground; “exploring the Cave of the Moon” is done with the mind — the mind single-mindedly illuminating the Cave of the Moon, like a hand feeling the thing in a bag.
  4. Qian meets Xun (Gou, Heaven over Wind): the yang essence rises and fills the Muddy Pellet; at the extreme of yang, yin is born — the Cave of the Moon is verified in the upper field. Earth meets Thunder (Fu, Earth over Thunder): the yang essence accumulates and stirs in the elixir field; at the extreme of yin, yang is born — the Root of Heaven sprouts in the lower field.
  5. Coming and going at leisure: yang stirs below and rises to the Muddy Pellet; essence fills above and descends back to the elixir field, turning without cease — “at leisure” means stirring naturally, composed and unhurried, which is Mencius’s “neither forget nor help it grow”.
  6. All thirty-six palaces are spring: the organs and channels in the belly number twelve, the joints of the back twenty-four, thirty-six palaces in all, all filled with the warm harmony of yang.
  7. Heaven and earth, years, months and hours are all like this: the summer solstice is the Cave of the Moon of heaven and earth, the winter solstice its Root of Heaven; the third and sixteenth day of each month, the wu hour and the zi hour of each day — all are the coming and going between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon. If a person wants to last, he must model himself on this mechanism of the waxing and waning of yin and yang.
  8. The results: shedding the hair (true yang attacking the yin evils beneath the hair), reversing the bones (true yang moving in reverse through the bones, also called changing the bones), washing the marrow, returning the elixir (returning the Golden Elixir that has been lost).
  9. Knowing life, one knows death: the reason people die is that they no longer have this one breath; so one must nurture this one breath. The First Emperor of Qin and Emperor Wu of Han sought the elixir beyond the seas; people in the world swallow the essence of the sun and the radiance of the moon, eat grains of elixir, dine on rosy clouds, abstain from grain — none of this has anything to do with nature and life; it is of no benefit and even harmful. Those who aspire to lengthen their years, and those who aspire to become true ones, should all take Master Shao’s teaching as their starting point.

Qiu Ling’s note explains the hexagram images clearly: the Root of Heaven is a person’s life, sprouting in the lower elixir field; the Cave of the Moon is the one yang rising through to the crown of the head, appearing in the Muddy Pellet; “treading the Root of Heaven” is the intention single-mindedly poured into the lower field, “exploring the Cave of the Moon” is the mind single-mindedly illuminating the upper field.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

“耳目聪明男子身，鸿钧赋予不为贫。须探月窟方知物，未蹑天根岂识人。乾遇巽时观月窟，地逢雷处见天根。天根月窟闲来往，三十六宫都是春。”

“A man’s body, with keen ears and clear eyes — what the Great Potter has bestowed is not poor. Only by exploring the Cave of the Moon does one know things; without treading the Root of Heaven, how could one know the human? When Qian meets Xun, look at the Cave of the Moon; where Earth meets Thunder, see the Root of Heaven. Between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon, coming and going at leisure, all thirty-six palaces are spring.”

(General meaning: born a man with keen ears and clear eyes, what creation (the Great Potter) has bestowed is already not meager. One must explore the Cave of the Moon to know the principles of the ten thousand things; without treading the Root of Heaven, how can one know what makes a human being human? When the Qian trigram meets the Xun trigram — one yin born within pure yang — look at the Cave of the Moon; where thunder sounds beneath the earth of Kun — one yang born within pure yin — see the Root of Heaven. Between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon there is a leisurely coming and going, and all thirty-six palaces of the body are full of spring.)

§2
Original (Chinese)

天根者，天一生水之根也。得之一数生于水，盖坎中之一阳也。此一阳乃天之气，于人为命，于天为太极，在天地为发生万物之根本，在人为百体资生之根本。其气在人，其原出于天，是以谓之根，而推本于天也。月者，金水之精，人身之用，指坎水也。坎有水而无金。何以名月？不知坎中之一阳得乾金之中爻，是以为中男。乾为金，此爻即金精也。金与水俱，是以谓之月。

“The Root of Heaven” is the root of “heaven’s one gives birth to water”. Obtaining this number “one”, born within water, it is the one yang in the middle of Kan (☵). This one yang is the breath of heaven: in the human body it is “life”, in heaven it is “the Supreme Ultimate”; in heaven and earth it is the root from which the ten thousand things arise, in the human being it is the root by which all the parts of the body grow. This breath is in the human body, yet its origin comes from heaven, so it is called a “root”, and traced back to “heaven”, it is called “the Root of Heaven”. “The moon” is the essence of metal and water, the function of the human body, and refers to Kan water. Kan has water but no metal — why call it “the moon”? Know that the one yang in the middle of Kan was obtained from the middle line of Qian metal, which is why Kan is the “middle son”; Qian belongs to metal, and this line is the essence of metal. Metal and water together, hence it is called “the moon” (the moon being the essence of metal and water).

§3
Original (Chinese)

言窟者何？月亏而有窟也。人身之月窟安在？在乎泥丸。盖坎中空阳发动，上贯头顶如满月，然头为乾为金，天水之精团聚于斯，所谓月到天心也。精象之成，活活泼泼，如风之来于水面，此月之盈也，盈极则亏而有窟矣！不言月满而言月窟者，言亏，以微其盈之极也。况盈则必亏，亏则又有所往，天机原无一息之停，此所以状月之盈而言窟也。

Why call it a “cave”? Because when the moon wanes there is a “cave” (a gap). Where is the Cave of the Moon in the human body? In the Muddy Pellet (the crown of the head). The empty yang within Kan stirs and rises through to the crown of the head, like a full moon; and the head belongs to Qian, to metal, and the essence of heavenly water gathers here — this is what is called “the moon reaching the heart of the sky”. When the scene of this essence takes shape, it is lively and alive, like wind passing over the water — this is the moon’s “fullness”; full to the extreme, it wanes, and so there is a “cave”! To say not “the full moon” but “the Cave of the Moon” is to speak of “waning”, to hint that it has already been full to the extreme. Moreover, what is full must wane, and what wanes moves on again; the heavenly dynamic never stops for a single breath — so in describing the fullness of the moon, one speaks of a “cave”.

§4
Original (Chinese)

天根何以蹑？以意蹑之也。一意注于天根，如足踏实地，卓然自立，是以谓之蹑。蹑乎此，乃识人之为人，其根在是。月窟何以探？以心探之也。一心照乎月窟，如手摩囊物，显然可指，是以谓之探。探乎此，方知物之有是妙，其窟最明，乾遇巽者，天风姤也。盖坎中之阳精升而满乎泥丸，阳极阴生，一阴伏五阳之下，是乾之遇巽也，是即月窟之验于上田也。地逢雷者，地雷复也。盖坎中之阳精积而动乎丹田，阴极阳生，一阳配五阴之下，是地之逢雷也，是即天根之萌于下田也。

How is the Root of Heaven “trodden”? It is trodden with intention. The intention single-mindedly poured into the Root of Heaven, like feet planted on solid ground, standing firm on one’s own — hence it is called “treading”. Having trodden here, one knows what makes a human being human: its root is right here. How is the Cave of the Moon “explored”? It is explored with the mind. The mind single-mindedly illuminating the Cave of the Moon, like a hand feeling the thing in a bag, plainly able to point it out — hence it is called “exploring”. Having explored here, one knows that the ten thousand things have such wonders; this “cave” is the plainest of all. “Qian meets Xun” is the hexagram Gou, Heaven over Wind (䷫): the yang essence within Kan rises and fills the Muddy Pellet; at the extreme of yang, yin is born, one yin lying beneath five yang — this is Qian meeting Xun — this is the verification of the Cave of the Moon in the upper elixir field. “Earth meets Thunder” is the hexagram Fu, Earth over Thunder (䷗): the yang essence within Kan accumulates and stirs in the elixir field; at the extreme of yin, yang is born, one yang beneath five yin — this is Earth meeting Thunder — this is the sprouting of the Root of Heaven in the lower elixir field.

§5
Original (Chinese)

往来者，阳动于下，升而上乎泥丸，是天根往乎月窟也。精满于上，降而下乎丹田，是月窟来于天根也。来而复往，往而复来，轮转不息，所谓上下与天地同流也，所谓直养无害则塞乎天地之间也。谓之间者，有自然发动之机，有从容不迫之意，所谓此日中流自在行，即孟子所谓“心勿忘助长”也。三十六宫者，腹之脏腑及包经络其数十有二，背之骨节其数二十四，合之共三十六宫。都是春者，皆为阳和之气，布濩充周，生意盎然也。邵子之诗意盖如此。

“Coming and going”: yang stirs below and rises to the Muddy Pellet — this is the Root of Heaven “going” to the Cave of the Moon; the essence fills above and descends back to the elixir field — this is the Cave of the Moon “coming” to the Root of Heaven. Having come it goes, having gone it comes, turning without cease — this is what is called “above and below flowing together with heaven and earth”, what is called “nourished with uprightness and unharmed, it fills the space between heaven and earth”. To call it “at leisure” is to say that it has the vitality of stirring naturally and the flavor of being composed and unhurried — what is called “this day it moves freely in midstream”, which is Mencius’s “let the mind not forget, nor help it grow”. “The thirty-six palaces”: the organs in the belly together with the pericardium channels number twelve; the joints of the back number twenty-four; together, thirty-six palaces. “All are spring”: they are all filled with the warm harmony of yang, spread throughout the body, brimming with life. Such is roughly the meaning of Master Shao’s poem.

§6
Original (Chinese)

所以然者，得天地阴阳之气以生，欲延生机，其运行当与天地等耳。天地之所以时行物生万古不敝者，亦以天根月窟妙于来往也。天地之月窟安在？上下皆乾，四月纯阳之卦。至五月则阳极阴生，一阴伏五阳之下，是乾之遇巽也，是夏至即天地之月窟也。上下皆坤，十月纯阴之卦。至冬月则阴极阳生，一阳配五阴之下，是地之逢雷也，是冬至即天地之天根也。

The reason for this is that people are born of the yin and yang breaths of heaven and earth; if they want their vitality to continue, its movement should be the same as that of heaven and earth. That heaven and earth move through the four seasons, the ten thousand things grow, and it does not decline through ten thousand ages, is also because the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon are wondrous in their coming and going. Where is the Cave of the Moon of heaven and earth? Qian above and below is the hexagram of pure yang of the fourth month; in the fifth month, at the extreme of yang, yin is born, one yin lying beneath five yang — this is Qian meeting Xun — the summer solstice is the Cave of the Moon of heaven and earth. Kun above and below is the hexagram of pure yin of the tenth month; in the winter month (the eleventh month), at the extreme of yin, yang is born, one yang beneath five yin — this is Earth meeting Thunder — the winter solstice is the Root of Heaven of heaven and earth.

§7
Original (Chinese)

自冬至一阳之复，而二阳临，三阳泰，四阳大壮，五阳夬，六阳乾。阳极而阴复生，是天地之天根，七日往乎月窟也。往何间也？自夏至一阴之姤，而二阴遯，三阴否，四阴观，五阴剥，六阴坤。阴极而阳复生，是天地之月窟，七日而来于天根。来何间也？此所谓七日来复，见天地心也。寒来暑往，暑往寒来，阴阳迭为消长，流而不息，而一岁三百有六旬，生机不已，亦犹人身之三十六宫，得月窟天根之来往，而生意不息也。

Beginning from Fu (䷗), with one yang born at the winter solstice, to two yang, Lin (䷒), three yang, Tai (䷊), four yang, Dazhuang (䷡), five yang, Guai (䷪), six yang, Qian (䷀); at the extreme of yang, yin is born again — this is the Root of Heaven of heaven and earth “going” through seven stages to the Cave of the Moon. How leisurely its going! Beginning from Gou (䷫), with one yin born at the summer solstice, to two yin, Dun (䷠), three yin, Pi (䷋), four yin, Guan (䷓), five yin, Bo (䷖), six yin, Kun (䷁); at the extreme of yin, yang is born again — this is the Cave of the Moon of heaven and earth “coming” through seven stages to the Root of Heaven. How leisurely its coming! This is what is called “returning on the seventh day, one sees the heart of heaven and earth”. Cold comes and heat goes, heat goes and cold comes, yin and yang alternately waxing and waning, flowing without cease, three hundred and sixty days a year, vitality without end — just as the thirty-six palaces of the human body, receiving the coming and going of the Cave of the Moon and the Root of Heaven, have life without cease.

§8
Original (Chinese)

且月窟天根，岂特岁月有然哉！惟月亦然，月之初三，一阳生于下，是地逢雷也，是月之天根也。月之十六，一阴生于下，是乾遇巽也，是月之月窟也。一来一往，而成一月之生机，岂特月有然哉！惟时亦然，巳时阳极，时之四月也。午时则一阴生矣，是午即时之月窟也。亥时阴极，时之十月也。子时则一阳生矣，是子即时之天根也。一来一往，而成昼夜之生机焉。是则积时而月，积月而岁，皆赖此月窟天根之来往，故运行而不息。人欲长存乎天地，以历岁月日时之久，不默法天地岁月日时阴阳消长之机，乌乎可？

Moreover, the Cave of the Moon and the Root of Heaven — it is not only so for a year! A month is also so: on the third day of each month, one yang is born below (the new moon first appears) — this is Earth meeting Thunder, the Root of Heaven of the month; on the sixteenth day of each month, one yin is born below (the full moon begins to wane) — this is Qian meeting Xun, the Cave of the Moon of the month. One coming and one going make the vitality of a month. And it is not only so for a month! The hours of a day are also so: at the si hour (nine to eleven in the morning) yang is at its extreme, corresponding to the fourth month of the year; at the wu hour (midday) one yin is born, and the wu hour is the Cave of the Moon of the day. At the hai hour (nine to eleven at night) yin is at its extreme, corresponding to the tenth month of the year; at the zi hour (midnight) one yang is born, and the zi hour is the Root of Heaven of the day. One coming and one going make the vitality of day and night. So hours accumulate into months, and months into years, all moving without cease by this coming and going between the Cave of the Moon and the Root of Heaven. If people want to last as long as heaven and earth, through years, months, days and hours, how could they do it without silently modeling themselves on the mechanism of the waxing and waning of yin and yang in the years, months, days and hours of heaven and earth?

§9
Original (Chinese)

于斯二者而往来之，是之谓伐毛，是之谓反骨，是之谓洗髓，是之谓还丹。伐毛者，真阳之气，攻伐毛下之阴邪。反骨者，真阳逆行于骨中，自顶至踵，如水泻地，无微不入，一气贯注，通体之骨节皆灵，阴气消除，通体之骨节皆健，故又谓之换骨。洗髓者，即空阳洗涤骨中之阴髓也。还者，还既失之金丹也。丹以药而得名，药以治病，坎中之一阳，乃先天之祖气，即人身之太极，此长生之大药也，故谓之丹。以得于乾金，故谓之金丹。

Coming and going between these two (the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon) is called “shedding the hair”, called “reversing the bones”, called “washing the marrow”, called “returning the elixir”. Shedding the hair: the breath of true yang attacks the yin evils beneath the pores. Reversing the bones: true yang moves in reverse through the bones, from the crown of the head to the heels, like water poured on the ground, getting in everywhere, one breath pouring through; all the joints of the body become supple, the yin breath is dispelled, and all the joints of the body become strong — hence it is also called “changing the bones”. Washing the marrow: the empty yang washing the yin marrow within the bones. Returning: returning the Golden Elixir that has been lost. The “elixir” takes its name from medicine; medicine is for curing illness. The one yang within Kan is the Prior Heaven ancestral breath, the Supreme Ultimate of the human body; it is the great medicine of long life, hence it is called the “elixir”; because it is obtained from Qian metal, it is called the “Golden Elixir”.

§10
Original (Chinese)

人得此气而成形以生，则此丹为与生俱来之物，自男女交而此金失其初矣。梏之反覆，而此气愈觉其微矣。至此气绝，而坎变为坤，则命气绝矣，天根拔而月窟空矣。后天呼吸之气，亦须臾而与之俱尽。知人之所以死，以无此气。即知人之所以生，不可不培此气。孔子曰：“未知生，焉知死？”是明言之其所以生，即知其所以死，是教以求死之理于生之理，斯知之矣。愚者不察，反疑圣人不明乎死生之理也，不大谬哉！

People take form and are born by obtaining this one breath, so this “elixir” is something they are born with; once there is the union of man and woman, this “metal” loses its original state. Harmed again and again, this one breath grows ever fainter. When this one breath is cut off, Kan turns into Kun (when the one yang in the middle of Kan ☵ is gone, it becomes the pure yin of Kun ☷), and the breath of life is cut off — the Root of Heaven is pulled up, the Cave of the Moon is empty, and the breath of Posterior Heaven breathing soon comes to an end along with them. Knowing that the reason people die is that they no longer have this one breath, one also knows that, for the sake of what makes people live, one cannot but nurture this one breath. Confucius said: “Not yet knowing life, how can one know death?” This says plainly: knowing why people live, one knows why they die — it teaches people to seek the principle of “death” within the principle of “life”, and so they will know. Foolish people, not looking closely, instead suspect that the sage did not understand the principle of life and death — is that not a great mistake!

§11
Original (Chinese)

人能以既失之丹，正心诚意以采之，养性立命以培之，使天根动而往乎月窟，月窟满而来于天根，一动一静，互为其根，则固有之元气，反之于身，如久客归家，如故物重逢，是以谓之还。邵子之诗，复参以愚说，天人一贯之理可以窥其底蕴，是丹道初功，已得其大半也，然不过以其人之道治其人之身耳。

If people can gather the elixir they have lost with a rectified mind and sincere intention, cultivate it by nourishing nature and establishing life, making the Root of Heaven stir and go to the Cave of the Moon, and the Cave of the Moon fill and come to the Root of Heaven, one moving and one still, each the root of the other — then the original breath one has always possessed returns to oneself, like a long-absent traveler coming home, like meeting an old possession again; that is why it is called “returning”. With Master Shao’s poem, and my explanation for reference, the principle of the unity of heaven and the human can be glimpsed to its depths; the first work of the elixir way is already more than half achieved — it is no more than “using a person’s own way to govern that person’s own body” (using the way inherent in the human body to cultivate the human body).

§12
Original (Chinese)

彼秦皇、汉武求丹于海外，是不能明乎圣贤之理，不能窥乎天地之机也。世之吞日精月华以求长生者，是欲速死于外感，其愚更可笑也！无论第吞其气，即使纳日月于腹中，试问能长生乎？有不顷刻立毙者乎？世之左道多矣，服粒、餐霞、辟谷诸说，俱无关于性命，不惟无益，而又害之矣！窃愿忠孝之人，有志延年，以邵子之说为确，即有志成真，亦必以邵子之说为始。

The First Emperor of Qin and Emperor Wu of Han sought the immortals’ elixir beyond the seas because they did not understand the way of the sages and could not glimpse the mechanism of heaven and earth. Those in the world who swallow the essence of the sun and the radiance of the moon in search of long life are courting a speedy death through outside influences — foolish to the point of absurdity! Never mind merely swallowing their breath — even if one stuffed the sun and moon into one’s belly, could one live long? It would be a wonder if one did not die on the spot! The side doors and crooked ways in the world are many — eating grains of elixir, dining on rosy clouds, abstaining from grain and so on — none of these has anything to do with nature and life; not only are they of no benefit, they are harmful as well! My private hope for loyal and filial people is this: those who aspire to lengthen their years should take Master Shao’s teaching as their standard; and even those who aspire to become true ones must take Master Shao’s teaching as their starting point.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§12
Original note (Chinese)

此节选自黄氏《乐育堂语录》第四卷，为黄氏应门人之请，解释邵康节《月窟天根》一诗之谈话记录。

天根者即人之命，乃坎中之一阳，萌于下丹田；月窟者即坎中一阳发动上贯头顶如满月，见于泥丸上丹田。“蹑天根”，即一意注于下田；“探月窟”，即一心照于上田（泥丸）。

“乾遇巽时”成为姤卦。乾卦为☰，象天，巽卦为☴，象风，乾上巽下为天风姤（䷫），是天上有风吹起的形象，意为相遇，又代表五月。此时坎中之阳升于泥丸，阳极阴生，一阴（爻）伏于五阳（爻）之下，观照泥丸最明，即月窟验于上田。

“地逢雷处”成为复卦。坤卦为☷，象地，震卦为☳，象雷，坤上震下为地雷复䷗，是雷在地下的形象，也是象征阳在下方兴起，有来复之意，又代表十一月冬至。此时坎中之阳动于下田，阴极阳生，一阳（爻）配五阴（爻）之下，内视下田感其阳动不已，即天根萌于下田。

于是阳动于下田，自然升而上泥丸，精满于上田，自然降落下田，来而复往，即“天根月窟闲来往”。脏腑、包络、脊骨因此都含阳和之春意，即“三十六宫都是春”。如此行之久久，遂收伐毛、反骨、洗髓、还丹之效，即真阳之气无微不入，驱尽一身阴气，结成金丹。

This section is taken from Volume Four of Huang’s Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture; it is the record of a talk in which Huang, at his disciples’ request, explained Shao Kangjie’s (Shao Yong’s) poem “The Cave of the Moon and the Root of Heaven”.

The Root of Heaven is a person’s life, the one yang within Kan, sprouting in the lower elixir field; the Cave of the Moon is the one yang within Kan stirring and rising through to the crown of the head, like a full moon, appearing in the Muddy Pellet, the upper elixir field. “Treading the Root of Heaven” is the intention single-mindedly poured into the lower elixir field; “exploring the Cave of the Moon” is the mind single-mindedly illuminating the upper elixir field (the Muddy Pellet).

“When Qian meets Xun” forms the Gou hexagram. The Qian trigram is ☰, imaging heaven; the Xun trigram is ☴, imaging wind; Qian above and Xun below is Gou, Heaven over Wind (䷫), the image of wind rising under heaven, meaning meeting, and also representing the fifth month. At this time the yang within Kan rises to the Muddy Pellet; at the extreme of yang, yin is born, one yin (line) lying beneath five yang (lines); illuminating the Muddy Pellet is plainest of all — this is the verification of the Cave of the Moon in the upper elixir field.

“Where Earth meets Thunder” forms the Fu hexagram. The Kun trigram is ☷, imaging earth; the Zhen trigram is ☳, imaging thunder; Kun above and Zhen below is Fu, Earth over Thunder (䷗), the image of thunder beneath the earth, also symbolizing yang rising from below, with the meaning of return, and representing the eleventh month, the winter solstice. At this time the yang within Kan stirs in the lower elixir field; at the extreme of yin, yang is born, one yang (line) beneath five yin (lines); looking within at the lower elixir field, one feels yang stirring without cease — this is the Root of Heaven sprouting in the lower elixir field.

So yang stirs in the lower elixir field and naturally rises to the Muddy Pellet; the essence fills the upper elixir field and naturally descends back to the lower elixir field; coming and going again — this is “between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon, coming and going at leisure”. The organs, the pericardium and the spine thereby all hold the springtime of the warm harmony of yang — this is “all thirty-six palaces are spring”. Doing this for a long time, one gains the results of shedding the hair, reversing the bones, washing the marrow and returning the elixir; that is, the breath of true yang gets in everywhere, drives out all the yin breath of the body, and forms the Golden Elixir.

Key Terms

  • Master Shao, Shao Kangjie: Shao Yong, Neo-Confucian thinker of the Northern Song.
  • Great Potter (鸿钧): creation, heaven.
  • Root of Heaven: the one yang within Kan, a person’s life, sprouting in the lower elixir field; see One Breath of Prior Heaven.
  • Cave of the Moon: the one yang within Kan rising through to the crown of the head like a full moon, appearing in the Muddy Pellet; see Muddy Pellet.
  • Treading: stepping; treading the Root of Heaven with intention is the intention single-mindedly poured into the lower field.
  • Exploring: feeling; exploring the Cave of the Moon with the mind is the mind single-mindedly illuminating the upper field.
  • Gou, Heaven over Wind ䷫; Fu, Earth over Thunder ䷗: the hexagrams of one yin first born and one yang first born.
  • The twelve waxing-and-waning hexagrams: Fu, Lin, Tai, Dazhuang, Guai, Qian, Gou, Dun, Pi, Guan, Bo, Kun, showing the waxing and waning of yin and yang through the year.
  • Thirty-six palaces: the twelve organs and channels in the belly, the twenty-four joints of the back.
  • Spread throughout (布濩): spread everywhere.
  • Shedding the hair, reversing the bones (changing the bones), washing the marrow, returning the elixir: the cleansing and refining by the breath of true yang, from outside to inside, from bone to marrow.
  • Golden Elixir: the one yang within Kan, obtained from Qian metal; see Golden Flower.
  • Eating grains of elixir, dining on rosy clouds, abstaining from grain: side-door arts of taking substances, swallowing clouds, and cutting off grain.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The division of labor between two words: “treading” is intention — the intention poured into the lower elixir field, like feet on solid ground; “exploring” is the mind — the mind illuminating the upper elixir field. In ordinary practice, take the lower elixir field as the main thing.
  2. The word “leisure” matters most: the coming and going between the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon stirs naturally, composed and unhurried — it is not pushing up and down with the intention.
  3. Model yourself on heaven and earth: heaven and earth have winter and summer solstices, the moon has new and full, the day has zi and wu — human vitality too rises and falls. Keeping regular hours and following day and night is in itself nourishing this one breath.
  4. Keep away from side doors: swallowing the essence of sun and moon, eating substances, dining on clouds, abstaining from grain and the like — Huang Yuanji says “not only are they of no benefit, they are harmful as well”.
  5. Safety note: “exploring the Cave of the Moon” involves illuminating the crown of the head. People with yin deficiency and yang excess, or with high blood pressure, should not shine much on the upper elixir field (see Qiu Ling’s notes to Chapter One, Section 1, and Chapter Nine, Section 8). The “natural rising and descending” of yang energy comes once the practice is mature; do not lead the breath up to the head with your intention; if a swollen head, headache, flushed face or palpitations appear, stop, return to the lower elixir field and relax naturally.

Translator's Notes

  1. This section first quotes Shao Yong’s poem, followed by Huang Yuanji’s explanation, taken from Volume Four of the Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture (see Qiu Ling’s note); the explanation is divided into eleven short passages.
  2. “To call it 间”, “how 间 its going”, “how 间 its coming” (谓之间者、往何间也、来何间也): in three places the original book prints 间, corresponding to 闲 (“at leisure”) in the poem’s “coming and going at leisure”; 间 here is 閒, the same as 闲, and the translation renders it as “at leisure” (composed).
  3. “The hours are also so; at the si hour yang is at its extreme” (惟时亦然，巳时阳极): the character 巳 (si) is printed in the original book in the form 已, and is read as 巳 according to the sense.
  4. “The organs in the belly and the 包 channels” (腹之脏腑及包经络): 包 refers to the pericardium.
  5. “What is called ‘this day it moves freely in midstream’” (所谓此日中流自在行): based on Zhu Xi’s poem “Thoughts on Reading”, “this day it moves freely in midstream”.
  6. “Never mind merely swallowing their breath” (无论第吞其气): 第 means “merely”.
  7. “It is no more than using a person’s own way to govern that person’s own body” (然不过以其人之道治其人之身耳): based on Zhu Xi’s “using a person’s own way to govern that person in turn”; here it means using the way inherent in the human body to cultivate the human body.

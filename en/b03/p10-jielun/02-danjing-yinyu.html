Chapter Ten · Conclusion · 丹经隐语破释

Decoding the Coded Language of the Elixir Classics

Decoding the coded language of the elixir classics — dragon and tiger, lead and mercury, wu and ji, other and self are all coded terms for refining the breath with the spirit; the six lines of Qian describe the waxing and waning of yang energy; helping it grow stunts its growth

About This Chapter

This section is taken from Volume Four of the Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture; it is a talk in which Huang Yuanji, at his disciples’ request, explained one by one the coded terms of the elixir classics that give people the most headaches. It falls into two main parts:

First part: refining the breath with the spirit, and what those coded terms mean

  1. Beginning: thoughts within not sprouting, impressions from without not received, the mind like a halted cloud and still water; then concentrate the spirit and pour it into the lower field, joining ears, eyes and mind there — like a cat catching a mouse, looking here, listening here, binding the thought here.
  2. Why: the one yang in the middle of Kan is the Supreme Ultimate of the human body (what Master Shao calls the Root of Heaven). A person grows by it; with the union of man and woman it is felled and spent day after day, so the Kan palace grows emptier by the day, the water cold and the metal chill — the body takes on the image of the “Pi” hexagram (heaven and earth not joining) and the “Weiji” hexagram (water and fire not joining), and all illnesses break out.
  3. The remedy: concentrate the Prior Heaven spirit and pour it into the Prior Heaven breath — the Way of Heaven descending to help, Qian shining on Kun, fire warming water: this is “the golden stove first lit”; nourishing the yang in the middle of Kan with the yin in the middle of Li is “Xiaoxu” (yin restraining yang); the yin in the middle of Li going down to seek the yang in the middle of Kan is “the true husband and wife meeting again in another life”. This is the secret mechanism by which the spirit can refine the breath.
  4. Decoding the coded terms:
    • Dragon: a spiritual creature, unpredictable in its changes, a metaphor for the spirit, the empty yin within Li (fire is born of wood, and wood is green, so it is called the green dragon; fire is red, so it is also called the red dragon).
    • Tiger: a fierce beast, a metaphor for the breath, the empty yang within Kan — pure yang moves easily, hard and fierce, difficult to master. Only when the dragon descends can the tiger be tamed.
    • Mercury: quicksilver, lively and nimble, a metaphor for the spirit. Lead: black tin, black like water, heavy like metal, a metaphor for the breath. Lead cannot transform without mercury, and breath cannot transform without spirit.
    • Knowing the white, guarding the black: white is metal (the yang within Kan), black is water; guard the white within the black — what is guarded is the breath, what guards it is the spirit.
    • Wu and ji: within Kan is wu earth, within Li is ji earth; joining ji to wu is lowering the spirit into the breath. Other and self: the other is the yang within Kan (the breath), the self the yin within Li (the spirit); the spirit is host, the breath guest.
    • Conclusion: these are all coded terms for “refining the breath with the spirit”, with no deeper meaning in themselves; the books use them to keep people out, the curious marvel at them as wonders, and some even twist them into heresy — hence the special effort to explain them plainly.

Second part: the growth of yang energy — told through the six lines of the Qian hexagram

  1. After long refining, the one yang sprouts (Earth meets Thunder, the Root of Heaven appears) → first nine, “hidden dragon, do not act”: yang energy is still faint, only to be nurtured → nine in the second place, “dragon appearing in the field”: yang energy gradually grows, clearly visible → nine in the third place, “all day creative and active, wary as if in danger”: halfway there, nurture it day and night, cautious, not to be neglected → nine in the fourth place, “now leaping in the deep”: shaking from below from time to time, a good omen → nine in the fifth place, “flying dragon in the heavens”: yang energy is strong and cannot be held back → at the extreme of yang, the moon is at the heart of the sky, and once full it is left to wane of itself (Qian meets Xun, the Cave of the Moon) — full and not waning is “arrogant dragon, there will be regret”; full and at once waning is “a flight of dragons without a head, good fortune”. Rising and descending without cease, heaven and earth joining, the ten thousand things flowing through — this is the “Tai” of the human body, “the elixir completed in nine turns” — the first work, the small attainment, of the elixir way.
  2. Everything must be natural: the stirring of yang is stirring of itself after long stillness, the turning of yang is turning of itself once the breath is strong, the stilling of yang is stilling of itself at the extreme of movement. Mencius says “neither forget nor help it grow”: forgetting loses the nurture, helping stunts the growth. What the world calls “moving the breath” is utterly mistaken — the breath can only be nourished, not over-nourished; wait for it to move of itself; forcing it along is like the fire-oxen charging into the Yan camp, or the man of Song pulling up his sprouts to help them grow.
  3. Mystery upon mystery: after long refining, Li turns into Qian, bright through inside and out; the spirit refines the breath out of no-mind, the breath transforms into spirit, at peace in no-intention. What lies further on would only frighten people if spoken; when the practice gets there, one knows of oneself.

Qiu Ling’s note explains all this with the hexagrams: Pi (heaven and earth not joining), Weiji (water and fire not joining); the returning way of practice begins with “the conjunction of Kan and Li, taking from Kan to fill Li”; turning from Pi to Tai, from Weiji to Jiji, are both inversions of the hexagram; and he explains how to read the six lines of Qian, “first nine, nine in the second place…”.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

静坐之功，必俟内念不萌，外感不接，此心如停云止水，然后凝神而注于下田，合耳目与心，皆交并于其间，如猫捕鼠，视于斯，听于斯，结念于斯，此道家顾諟天之明命也。其所以然者何哉？盖坎中之一阳为人身之太极，即邵子所谓天根也。人受此气以生，自孩提以至成立，皆赖一阳以滋长。自男女交，而此气遂损矣。旦旦伐之，而此气愈损矣。伐之不已，久之而其气渐微，久之而此水渐涸，坎宫日虚，水冷金寒，地道不能上行，天道不能下济，上乾下坤，此否之象也。天地不交，火日炎于上，而不能下。水日润于下，而不能上。水火不融，心肾不交，上离下坎，此未济之象也。人身有此二卦之象，生机日危，百病皆作矣。

The work of quiet sitting must wait until thoughts within do not sprout and impressions from without are not received, the mind like a halted cloud and still water; then concentrate the spirit and pour it into the lower elixir field, joining ears, eyes and mind there — like a cat catching a mouse: the eyes looking here, the ears listening here, the thought bound here. This is the Daoist “attending to the bright mandate of heaven” (watching attentively over the nature heaven has bestowed). Why do this? Because the one yang in the middle of Kan (☵) is the Supreme Ultimate of the human body, which is what Master Shao (Shao Yong) calls “the Root of Heaven”. A person receives this one breath and is born, and from childhood to adulthood relies on this one yang to nourish and grow. Once there is the union of man and woman, this one breath begins to be spent; felled day after day, it is spent more and more; felled without cease, in time this one breath gradually grows faint, in time this water gradually dries up, the Kan palace grows emptier day by day, the water cold and the metal chill; the way of earth (yin breath) cannot move upward, and the way of heaven (yang breath) cannot descend to help — Qian above and Kun below: this is the image of the “Pi” hexagram (䷋, Obstruction) (heaven and earth not joining, blocked and closed). Heaven and earth not joining, the fire burns above day after day and cannot come down; the water moistens below day after day and cannot go up; water and fire do not blend, heart and kidneys do not join — Li above and Kan below: this is the image of the “Weiji” hexagram (䷿, Before Completion) (the matter not yet completed). When the human body has these two images, its vitality grows more endangered day by day, and all illnesses break out.

§2
Original (Chinese)

道者，知其然也。以先天之神凝而注于先天之气，是天道下济也。孟子曰：“志，气之帅也”。将帅从天而下，卒徒必随而俱下，是以乾照坤矣，是以火温水矣，是即所谓金灶初开火也。灶因火而名。金者指坎中之一阳也，得于乾之金也。火初开者，初得乾阳离火之下照也。是以离之上下二阳，暖坎中之上下二阴，以离中之空阴，养坎中之空阳。以中女之畜中男也。

Those who cultivate the Way know this principle. Concentrating the Prior Heaven spirit and pouring it into the Prior Heaven breath — this is “the Way of Heaven descending to help” (the yang breath of heaven coming down to help the earth). Mencius said: “The will is the commander of the breath.” When the commander descends from heaven, the soldiers are bound to come down with him — this is using Qian to shine on Kun, using fire to warm water, which is what is called “the golden stove first lit”. The “stove” is so called because it has fire; “golden” refers to the one yang in the middle of Kan, the metal obtained from Qian; “the fire first lit” means that the yang of Qian, the fire of Li, has just begun to shine downward. This is using the upper and lower yang lines of Li (☲) to warm the upper and lower yin lines of Kan (☵); using the empty yin in the middle of Li to nourish the empty yang in the middle of Kan — that is, using the “middle daughter” (Li) to restrain and nourish the “middle son” (Kan).

§3
Original (Chinese)

其所以然者又何哉？盖阳性主动，动则易泄，惟阴可以畜之。故云：男性见女则悦，得女则留，此小畜，皆取以阴畜阳之义也。况前以乾坤一交，乾之中爻入于坤，而为坎。坤之中爻入于乾，而为离，是夫妇之情投意洽，阴阳互易也。今以离中坤入于乾之阴，下求坎中乾，入于坤之阳，是再世重逢之真夫妇也。两情交悦，可以畜空阳而不使之泄。孤阴不生，独阳不长，有此空阴以养此空阳，一动一静，互为其根，乃可以回既损之元气，使潜滋暗长于极阴之地，以冀七日之来复也，此神能炼气之秘机也。世传性命诸书，从未有如此透发。

Why do this? Because the nature of yang is to move, and moving it easily leaks; only yin can hold it in store. So it is said: a man’s nature is to delight on seeing a woman and to stay on gaining one — this is “Xiaoxu” (䷈, Small Taming), all taking the meaning of “yin restraining yang”. Moreover, long ago, when Qian and Kun first joined, the middle line of Qian entered Kun, and Kun became Kan; the middle line of Kun entered Qian, and Qian became Li — this is husband and wife in perfect accord, yin and yang exchanging with each other. Now, using the yin in the middle of Li, which came from Kun and entered Qian, to go down and seek the yang in the middle of Kan, which came from Qian and entered Kun — this is “the true husband and wife meeting again in another life”. The two delighting in each other, the empty yang can be held in store and not allowed to leak. Lone yin does not give birth, solitary yang does not grow; with this empty yin to nourish this empty yang, one moving and one still, each the root of the other, one can recover the original breath that has been spent, letting it grow quietly in the place of utmost yin, looking forward to “returning on the seventh day” (the one yang coming back again) — this is the secret mechanism by which the spirit can refine the breath. Of all the books on nature and life handed down in the world, none has ever explained it so thoroughly.

§4
Original (Chinese)

即以神炼气，亦多隐语，如龙虎汞铅诸说是也。龙者，灵物也，变化莫测，喻离中空阴之神，以火生于木，木青色，故或云：龙。火色赤，又或云赤龙。虎者，猛物也，坎中空阳之气，此气纯阳，阳则易动，有如虎之难防。此气最刚，刚则性烈，有如虎之难制。惟龙之下降，可以伏此虎也。汞者，水银也，活泼灵动，无微不入，喻空阴之神。铅者，黑锡也，其色黑，有似坎中之水，其体重，似坎中之金，以喻空阳之气。且铅非汞不能化，亦犹气非神不能化，而铅又可以乾汞，气又可以化神，故以为喻。

Even “refining the breath with the spirit” has many coded terms, such as dragon and tiger, mercury and lead. The “dragon” is a spiritual creature, unpredictable in its changes, a metaphor for the “spirit”, the empty yin within Li; because fire is born of wood and wood is green, it is sometimes called the “green dragon”; because fire is red, it is also sometimes called the “red dragon”. The “tiger” is a fierce beast, a metaphor for the “breath”, the empty yang within Kan: this breath is pure yang, and yang moves easily, as hard to guard against as a tiger; this breath is utterly hard, and hardness makes it fierce, as hard to master as a tiger. Only when the dragon descends can this tiger be tamed (only when the spirit shines downward can this breath be tamed). “Mercury” is quicksilver, lively and nimble, getting in everywhere, a metaphor for the “spirit” of empty yin. “Lead” is black tin, black in color like the water within Kan, heavy in substance like the metal within Kan, used as a metaphor for the “breath” of empty yang. Moreover, lead cannot transform without mercury, just as breath cannot transform without spirit; and lead can refine mercury until it is dry, as breath can transform into spirit — that is why they are used as metaphors.

§5
Original (Chinese)

老子所谓知白守黑，又所谓抱一者皆是也。白者，金之色。黑者，水之色。知坎有乾金之白，故守水之黑者，正以守黑中之白也，所守者气也，守之者神也。又云戊己者，云彼我者。戊己属土，以坎中有戊土，离中有己土。五行分配四时，分配脏腑，而惟土则旺于四时之季，统乎脏腑之全，故人之六脉，皆取有胃气则生，以万物发生于土也，故《河》、《洛》之数一与六共宗，二与七同道，三与八为朋，四与九为友，皆以中隔五数，阴阳乃相生，而又以五十居中。盖天地之数皆不离乎土，惟人亦然。所以坎有阳土之戊，离有阴土之己也。以己合戊，亦指降神于气也。彼者，指坎中之阳也。我者，谓离中之阴也。气无知，神有知，以有知之神，求无知之气，以神为主，以气为宾。主者，我也。宾者，彼也。凡此皆以神炼气之隐语也，本无关于精义，而诸书皆以此拒人，好异者惊为奇谈，甚至谬解而入于邪语，特破之以释其疑。

Laozi’s “know the white, guard the black”, and his “embrace the One”, all mean this. White is the color of metal; black is the color of water. Knowing that within Kan there is the white of Qian metal, one therefore guards the black of the water, precisely in order to guard the white within the black — what is guarded is the breath, and what guards it is the spirit. Some also speak of “wu and ji”, or of “other and self”. Wu and ji belong to earth: within Kan there is wu earth, within Li there is ji earth. When the five phases are assigned to the four seasons and to the organs, only earth flourishes at the end of every season and governs all the organs; so all six pulses of a person live by having stomach breath, because the ten thousand things all spring from earth. Thus in the numbers of the River Chart and the Luo Writing: one and six share an ancestry, two and seven share a way, three and eight are companions, four and nine are friends — all separated by the five in the middle, so that yin and yang can give rise to each other; and five and ten sit in the center. None of the numbers of heaven and earth can do without earth, and so it is with people. So Kan has wu, the yang earth, and Li has ji, the yin earth. Joining ji to wu also refers to lowering the spirit into the breath. “Other” refers to the yang within Kan (the breath); “self” refers to the yin within Li (the spirit). The breath has no knowing, the spirit has knowing; using the knowing spirit to seek the unknowing breath, the spirit is host and the breath guest — the host is the self, the guest the other. These are all coded terms for “refining the breath with the spirit”, with no deeper meaning in themselves; yet all the books use them to keep people out, the curious marvel at them as wonders, and some even twist them and fall into heresy — so I have made a point of explaining them plainly, to clear up everyone’s doubts.

乾卦由三个阳爻（☰），坤卦由三个阴爻（☷）构成，乾象天，坤象地，天地不交，上乾下坤构成否卦（䷋），卦象为闭塞，为黑暗。

从事练功，走返还之道，补回既损之元气，当从坎离交媾、取坎填离始。

离火下照，以离之上下二阳暖坎中之上下二阴；以离中之空阴养坎中之空阳。坎卦又象中男，离卦又象中女，此以中女畜中男，成小畜卦，巽上乾下（䷈），意味着小的蓄积。

以离之中爻下求换取坎之中爻，即取坎以填离，恢复乾坤卦象，所谓再世重逢之真夫妇，进而乾坤交媾，炼成乾健之体。

以上用卦爻补述，详见本书第六章坎离交媾和乾坤交媾。

This passage is taken from Volume Four of Huang Yuanji’s Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture; it is the record of a talk in which Huang, while teaching his disciples, decoded the coded language of the elixir classics at their request.

Once the human body, through the union of man and woman, has become a “leaking body” (a body whose essence and breath can leak away), it has the two images of the Pi and Weiji hexagrams; its vitality grows more endangered day by day, and all illnesses break out.

The Qian trigram is made of three yang lines (☰), the Kun trigram of three yin lines (☷); Qian symbolizes heaven, Kun earth. Heaven and earth not joining, Qian above and Kun below form the Pi hexagram (䷋), whose image is blockage and darkness.

The middle line of Qian entering Kun makes Kan (☵), that is, one yang sunk between two yin; the middle line of Kun entering Qian makes Li (☲), that is, one yin clinging between two yang. Kan images water, Li images fire; water and fire not blending, Li above and Kan below form the Weiji hexagram (䷿), all six of whose lines are out of their proper places, meaning “not yet completed”.

In taking up practice, walking the returning way and restoring the original breath that has been spent, one should begin with the conjunction of Kan and Li, taking from Kan to fill Li.

The fire of Li shines downward: the upper and lower yang lines of Li warm the upper and lower yin lines of Kan; the empty yin in the middle of Li nourishes the empty yang in the middle of Kan. The Kan trigram also symbolizes the middle son, and the Li trigram the middle daughter; this is the middle daughter restraining and nourishing the middle son, forming the Xiaoxu hexagram — Xun above and Qian below (䷈), meaning a small storing-up.

Using the middle line of Li to go down and exchange with the middle line of Kan is “taking from Kan to fill Li”, restoring the images of Qian and Kun — what is called “the true husband and wife meeting again in another life”; going further, Qian and Kun unite, refining a body of Qian’s strength.

The above uses the trigrams and lines to explain further; for details see Chapter Six of this book, “The Conjunction of Kan and Li and of Qian and Kun”.

Original (Chinese)

总之因天气不交而否，欲由否而转泰，不得不恭敬以礼下。因水火相隔而未济，欲由未而求济，不得不降心以相从，此以神炼气之由来。炼之久，而水渐生，地渐复，积而至于一阳萌动，所谓地逢雷也。此即天根之发现也。然阳气尚微，动而乃伏，正宜培养而不可恃，此《易》所谓“初九潜龙，勿用”也。积而至于阳气渐长，已有反骨之势，显然可见，即《易》所谓“见龙在田”也。积而至于阳气愈长，送信骨中，计程已得其半，然不安于下，又不能即上，更宜日夜培养，兢兢而不可忽，即《易》所谓“君子终日乾乾，惕若厉”也。

In short: because the breaths of heaven and earth do not join, there is “Pi”; to turn Pi into “Tai” (䷊, Peace — heaven and earth joining in peace), one cannot but humbly and courteously descend (let the spirit above come down); because water and fire are kept apart, there is “Weiji”; to seek “Jiji” (䷾, After Completion) from Weiji, one cannot but lower the mind and comply. This is the origin of “refining the breath with the spirit”. After long refining, water gradually comes forth and the earth gradually recovers, accumulating until the one yang sprouts — what is called “Earth meets Thunder” (spring thunder sounding underground) — this is the Root of Heaven appearing. But the yang energy is still very faint; it stirs and then lies low again, and should be nurtured, not relied upon — this is what the Book of Changes calls “first nine: hidden dragon, do not act” (the dragon hidden at the bottom of the water cannot be used for now). Accumulating until the yang energy gradually grows and already has the force of “reversing the bones” (moving in reverse through the bones), clearly visible — this is what the Book of Changes calls “dragon appearing in the field”. Accumulating until the yang energy grows still more, sending its messages through the bones, and reckoning the road it is already halfway, yet it cannot stay below nor go up at once — all the more must it be nurtured day and night, with caution and care, not to be neglected — this is what the Book of Changes calls “the noble one is creative and active all day, and in the evening wary as if in danger”.

§7
Original (Chinese)

积而至于阳气弥长，进而愈上，且其下不时震动，此佳兆也，即《易》所谓“或跃在渊，无咎”也。积而至于阳气已战不可遏抑，即《易》所谓“飞龙在天”也，即《庄子》所谓“搏扶摇羊角而上”者是也。积而至于阳气已极，月在天心，三五而盈，盈则听其自亏，所谓乾遇巽也，即邵子所谓月窟也。倘盈极而不亏，即《易》所谓“亢龙有悔”也。盈而即亏，即《易》所谓“见群龙无首，吉”也。至降而复升，升而复降，流行不息，天地交，万物通，此人之泰也。天根月窟自此可以闲来往矣，此亦可谓九转丹成也。九者阳也，转者阳气逆而轮转也，指坎中之一阳，上蟠下际，生息无穷，长生之大药，亦可谓之小成也。此丹道之初功也。下学上达，入妙通神，皆从此始。

Accumulating until the yang energy grows still more, going ever higher, with shaking from below from time to time — this is a good omen, what the Book of Changes calls “now leaping, now in the deep, no blame”. Accumulating until the yang energy is already strong and cannot be held back — this is what the Book of Changes calls “flying dragon in the heavens”, and what the Zhuangzi calls “spiraling up on the whirlwind like a ram’s horn” (the great Peng bird circling upward on a whirlwind). Accumulating until the yang energy has reached its extreme, like the moon at the heart of the sky, full on the fifteenth; once full, it is left to wane of itself — this is what is called “Qian meets Xun” (one yin born within pure yang), which is what Master Shao calls “the Cave of the Moon”. If, full to the extreme, it will not wane, that is what the Book of Changes calls “arrogant dragon, there will be regret” (the dragon flying too high is bound to regret it); full and at once waning is what the Book of Changes calls “a flight of dragons without a head, good fortune” (the dragons not vying to lead, auspicious). When it descends and rises again, rises and descends again, flowing without cease, heaven and earth joining and the ten thousand things flowing through — this is the “Tai” of the human body. From then on the Root of Heaven and the Cave of the Moon can come and go at leisure, and this too may be called “the elixir completed in nine turns”. “Nine” is yang, and “turns” is the yang energy circling in reverse; it refers to the one yang within Kan, spiraling upward and reaching downward, breathing and growing without end — this is the great medicine of long life, and may also be called “the small attainment”. This is the first work of the elixir way. Learning the lowly and reaching the lofty, entering the wondrous and communing with the spirit — all begin from here.

§8
Original (Chinese)

然行之有自然之机，而不可一毫勉强。老子曰：“道生一，一生二，二生三，三生自然。”言此数之生，由一而二，二而三，此阴阳自然之机也。《河洛》之数，天一生水，地六成之，天阳也，地阴也。六数阴极，而阳则自然而生也。地二生火，天七成之，七数阳极，而阴则自然而生也。天三生木，地八成之，八数阴衰，而阳之三自然而长也。阳生阴成，阴阳生长之机，何一而非自然者？其阳之动也，静之久而自动也。阳之转也，气之战而自转也，阳之静也，动之极而自静也。行乎其所不得不行，而不可或止。止乎其所不得不止，而不可或行。即孟子所谓勿忘勿助长也。

Yet all of this has its natural pattern, and not the slightest forcing is allowed. Laozi said: “The Way gives birth to one, one gives birth to two, two gives birth to three, three gives birth to the natural.” That is, the birth of these numbers, from one to two and from two to three, is the natural pattern of yin and yang. In the numbers of the River Chart and the Luo Writing: heaven’s one gives birth to water and earth’s six completes it (heaven is yang, earth is yin); six is the extreme of the yin numbers, and yang is then naturally born; earth’s two gives birth to fire and heaven’s seven completes it; seven is the extreme of the yang numbers, and yin is then naturally born; heaven’s three gives birth to wood and earth’s eight completes it; at the number eight yin declines, and the yang of three naturally grows. Yang gives birth and yin completes; in the pattern of yin and yang growing, what is not natural? The stirring of yang is its stirring of itself after long stillness; the turning of yang is its turning of itself once the breath is strong; the stilling of yang is its stilling of itself at the extreme of movement. When it must go, it cannot but go and must not be stopped; when it must stop, it cannot but stop and must not go on — this is what Mencius calls “neither forget nor help it grow”.

§9
Original (Chinese)

忘则失养之道，助则挫长之机矣，世言运气则谬甚。气可养也，而不可过养，当俟其自动。如气自坎生，所谓源头活水来，过而迫之使行，则气从离出，无殊火牛入燕垒矣，是与揠苗之宋人何以异？知长不可助，而动静亦听其自然，则不至养人者害人矣！

If you forget it, you lose the way of nourishing; if you help it, you stunt the pattern of growth. What the world calls “moving the breath” is utterly mistaken. The breath can be nourished, but must not be over-nourished; one should wait for it to move of itself. For example, the breath born from Kan (the lower elixir field) is what is called “the living water coming from the source”; if one forces it along too hard, the breath comes out of Li (the heart) instead — no different from the fire-oxen charging into the Yan army’s ramparts (rushing about wildly), and how is it any different from the man of Song who pulled up his sprouts to help them grow? Knowing that growth cannot be “helped”, and leaving movement and stillness to nature, one will not end up with “what should nourish people harming them instead”!

§10
Original (Chinese)

老子曰：“玄之又玄，众妙之门”。妙难悉数，姑以益人之妙言之。其始也以神炼气，至气之逆而轮转，则坎中之一阳时过而化离中之一阴。化之久，空阴得空阳之照，如月之得日光而明，则离变为乾，内外通明，所谓至诚之道可以前知也。离中之二变为一，则诚矣。诚则心愈清，神愈明，所谓诚精故明者此也，所谓以神化气也。但神炼气出于无心，气化神安于无意，炼必凝乎其神，如火之炼夫顽金也。化惟听之于气，如物之化于时雨也。至全体一气相通，翻天倒地，反骨洗髓，阴阳团为一气，五行并为一途也。鸢飞鱼跃之机，常静观而自得。雷动风行之象，非外人所及知。行云流水，别有天地，时见道之上下察也，此玄之妙也。过此以往，日久功深，更有妙之又妙。此无关于人事，言之徒骇听闻，功至自知，不可预言。

Laozi said: “Mystery upon mystery, the gate of all wonders.” The wonders are hard to list one by one; let me just speak of the wonders that benefit people. At first one refines the breath with the spirit; when the breath circles in reverse, the one yang within Kan constantly passes through to transform the one yin within Li; after long transforming, the empty yin, receiving the radiance of the empty yang, is like the moon brightened by the sunlight, and so Li turns into Qian (☲ becomes ☰), bright through inside and out — this is what is called “the way of utmost sincerity can know beforehand”. When the “two” in the middle of Li (the broken yin line) becomes “one” (the unbroken yang line), that is “sincerity”. With sincerity, the mind grows ever clearer and the spirit ever brighter — what is called “sincerity refined, hence brightness” is this, which is also what is called “transforming the breath with the spirit”. But the spirit refines the breath out of no-mind, and the breath transforms into spirit at peace in no-intention: refining requires concentrating the spirit, like fire refining stubborn metal; transforming can only be left to the breath, like the ten thousand things nurtured by timely rain. When the whole body is pervaded by one breath, heaven and earth overturned, bones reversed and marrow washed, yin and yang rolled into one breath, the five phases joined in one course — the lively vitality of “the hawk flying, the fish leaping” (the hawk flying in the sky, the fish leaping in the deep) is often grasped naturally in quiet contemplation; scenes like “thunder moving and wind going” are not something outsiders can know. Like floating clouds and flowing water, there is another world, and at every moment one sees the “Way” shining clearly in heaven above and earth below — this is the wonder of the “mysterious”. Beyond this step, as the days lengthen and the practice deepens, there are wonders upon wonders. But these have nothing to do with human affairs, and speaking of them would only frighten people; when the practice gets there, one knows of oneself, and it cannot be told beforehand.

Qiu Ling's noteon §6–§10
Original note (Chinese)

由否卦（䷋）而转泰卦（䷊），乾天下降为下卦，坤地上升为上卦，即泰卦为否卦的倒转，这是天地相交，阴阳沟通，卦象安泰亨通。

由未济卦（䷿）而求既济卦（䷾），也是卦的倒转，由未济的离上坎下变为既济的坎上离下，卦象亦由意味不完成到完成。

《易经》之卦由阳爻和阴爻构成。凡阳爻称“九”而阴爻称“六”。一卦的阳爻自下而上为初九、九二、九三、九四、九五及上九。一卦的阴爻自下而上为初六、六二、六三、六四、六五及上六。另外，乾坤两卦还有“用九”与“用六”，以示阳爻与阴爻的变化。

上文用乾卦六爻由下而上来表示阳气的盈亏变化。如初九（即乾卦第一爻）“潜龙，勿用”，象征阳气尚微，动而仍伏。九二（即乾卦第二爻）“见龙在田”，象征阳气渐长，显然可见，等等。

From the Pi hexagram (䷋) to the Tai hexagram (䷊): Qian, heaven, descends to become the lower trigram, and Kun, earth, rises to become the upper trigram — Tai is the inversion of Pi. This is heaven and earth joining, yin and yang communicating, and the image is peaceful and prosperous.

Seeking the Jiji hexagram (䷾) from the Weiji hexagram (䷿): this too is an inversion of the hexagram, from Weiji’s Li above and Kan below to Jiji’s Kan above and Li below, and the image too changes from “not yet completed” to “completed”.

The hexagrams of the Book of Changes are made of yang lines and yin lines. A yang line is called “nine”, a yin line “six”. The yang lines of a hexagram, from the bottom up, are called first nine, nine in the second place, nine in the third place, nine in the fourth place, nine in the fifth place, top nine; the yin lines from the bottom up are called first six, six in the second place, six in the third place, six in the fourth place, six in the fifth place, top six. In addition, the Qian and Kun hexagrams also have “use of the nines” and “use of the sixes”, indicating the changes of yang lines and yin lines.

The text above uses the six lines of Qian, from the bottom up, to show the waxing and waning of yang energy. For example, first nine (the first line of Qian), “hidden dragon, do not act”, symbolizes that yang energy is still very faint, stirring and then lying low; nine in the second place (the second line of Qian), “dragon appearing in the field”, symbolizes yang energy gradually growing, clearly visible; and so on.

Key Terms

  • Attending to the bright mandate of heaven: quoted from the Book of Documents in the Great Learning; watching attentively over the nature heaven has bestowed.
  • Root of Heaven: the one yang within Kan, the Supreme Ultimate of the human body; see Section 3.
  • Leaking body: the body after adulthood, whose essence and breath can leak away.
  • Pi ䷋, Tai ䷊: heaven and earth not joining, and heaven and earth joining.
  • Weiji ䷿, Jiji ䷾: water and fire not joining, and water and fire joining.
  • Xiaoxu ䷈: yin restraining yang.
  • The golden stove first lit: the yang of Qian, the fire of Li, first shining down on the true yang within Kan.
  • The true husband and wife meeting again in another life: taking from Kan to fill Li; see the conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Returning on the seventh day: the one yang coming back; based on the Book of Changes, the Fu hexagram.
  • Dragon and tiger: spirit and breath; mercury and lead: spirit and breath; see Lead and Mercury.
  • Know the white, guard the black: Daode jing, chapter 28.
  • Wu and ji: wu earth within Kan, ji earth within Li; joining ji to wu is lowering the spirit into the breath; see True Intention.
  • Other and self, guest and host: the other is the breath, the self the spirit; the spirit is host, the breath guest.
  • The six lines of Qian: hidden dragon, do not act; dragon appearing in the field; creative and active all day; now leaping in the deep; flying dragon in the heavens; arrogant dragon, there will be regret; a flight of dragons without a head.
  • Qian meets Xun: one yin born within pure yang; the Cave of the Moon; see Section 3.
  • The elixir completed in nine turns: yang energy circling in reverse; the first work and small attainment of the elixir way.
  • Neither forget nor help it grow: Mencius, “Gongsun Chou I”.
  • The fire-oxen charging into the Yan ramparts: Tian Dan of the Warring States period charged the Yan army with a formation of fire-oxen; a metaphor for rushing about wildly.
  • Pulling up the sprouts to help them grow: the story in the Mencius of the man of Song who pulled up his sprouts.
  • Sincerity refined, hence brightness: found in the Cantong qi and elsewhere; utmost sincerity brings brightness.
  • The hawk flying, the fish leaping: words from the Book of Odes, quoted in the Doctrine of the Mean as a metaphor for the liveliness of the Way.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The coded terms are not mysterious: dragon, mercury, Li, self, ji — all “spirit”; tiger, lead, Kan, other, wu — all “breath”. In reading elixir books, simply hold on to the four words “refining the breath with the spirit”.
  2. The essentials of quiet sitting (first short passage): no thoughts arising within, no impressions received from without, the mind like a halted cloud and still water; then gently pour the spirit into the lower elixir field, joining eyes, ears and mind there.
  3. The growth of yang energy has its own rhythm: when faint, only nurture it, do not use it; as it grows, be all the more careful; when it reaches its extreme, let it come down of itself — do not be greedy.
  4. The most important sentence: “What the world calls moving the breath is utterly mistaken” — the breath can only be nourished, not forced. Do not use your intention to push or transport the breath. If dry heat, a swollen head, palpitations or insomnia appear, you have been “helping it grow”; relax at once and return to naturalness.
  5. There is no need to imagine the higher states in advance: Huang Yuanji says, “When the practice gets there, one knows of oneself; it cannot be told beforehand.”

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is two paragraphs, taken from Volume Four of the Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture (see Qiu Ling’s note); the first paragraph is divided into five short passages, and the second into five.
  2. “Some also speak of wu and ji … within Li there is ji earth … joining ji to wu” (又云戊己者……离中有己土……以己合戊): the character 己 (ji) is printed in several places in the original book in the form 已, and is read as 己 according to the sense.
  3. “Yet all the books use them to keep people out” (而诸书皆以此拒人): so in the original book; 拒 means keeping people out (making it hard for them to find the way in).
  4. “Three gives birth to the natural” (三生自然): the received text of the Daode jing, chapter 42, reads “three gives birth to the ten thousand things” (三生万物); reproduced as in the original book.
  5. “The noble one is creative and active all day, wary as if in danger” (君子终日乾乾，惕若厉): the received text of the line statement for nine in the third place of the Qian hexagram reads “…and in the evening wary as if in danger” (夕惕若厉); the original book lacks the word 夕 (“evening”); reproduced as it stands.
  6. “Striking the whirlwind like a ram’s horn and rising” (搏扶摇羊角而上): the Zhuangzi, “Free and Easy Wandering”, reads 抟 (“spiraling”); the original book has 搏 (“striking”); reproduced as it stands.
  7. “Yang energy is already at war and cannot be held back” and “the breath at war turns of itself” (阳气已战不可遏抑、气之战而自转): 战 (“war”) is probably an error for 壮 (“strong”); the translation follows 壮.
  8. “At the number eight yin declines” (八数阴衰): so in the original book.

Chapter Ten · Conclusion · 全功论述

An Account of the Whole Practice

An account of the whole practice — the great Way is no more than spirit and breath; in guarding the center, use martial fire to urge on the ordinary breath; the River Chariot must borrow the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath; fire too slow and it is raw, fire too fast and it is scorched

About This Chapter

The last chapter of the book is the “Conclusion”. The first section is taken from the last paragraph of the commentary on the final chapter (chapter 81) of Huang Yuanji’s Commentary on the Daode jing; it runs briefly through the whole practice, focusing on the two methods of guarding the center and the River Chariot.

  1. The great Way is only the two words spirit and breath, divided into Prior and Posterior Heaven. The Prior Heaven original spirit is the substance, the Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit the function: without the original spirit, the great Way has no master; without the cognitive spirit, the great Way has no use. One must first, in muddled unknowing, nurture the original spirit as master, and then, at a moment of awareness, stir it forth as the cognitive spirit. This “cognitive spirit” is not the selfish consciousness of ordinary wild thinking, but the original spirit of right awakening which, having stirred, has perception — the only fear is that, once it arises, it becomes a confused tumult.
  2. Illuminating with one mind: “One mind only on the fishing line, not seeing the reed flowers red on the far bank” — like an angler watching only his line. With one mind like this, although it is called the cognitive spirit, it is in fact the original spirit.
  3. Beginning: with one mind, subdue the deluded consciousness of eyes, mouth, ears, body and intention; gather the spirit at the elixir gate (the elixir field) and regulate the breath in the elixir field; the ordinary breath ceases and the original breath returns; when the original breath mingles richly and lively, turning like a living dragon, one may run the River Chariot.
  4. The danger of the breath-dynamic stirring greatly without running the River Chariot: yin essence not yet transformed by fire can hardly stay for long; as soon as ordinary fire arises, it is bound to stir the root of lust and pour out. Forcibly guarding it rigidly, it will leak all the same.
  5. The River Chariot must borrow the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath: the Prior Heaven original breath cannot rise and fall, refine and transform by itself; it must borrow the Posterior Heaven breathing as yang fire and yin tally to urge it on.
  6. Fire phasing: like cooking rice — fire too slow and it is raw, so alertness must always be present; fire too fast and it is scorched, so it must be continuous and unbroken. When thunder rumbles underground and fire drives the metal onward, martial fire must be used, but “let the blazing flames be ten thousand feet high, I use only one drop of cool water, sprinkled over the ten directions” — the breath strong and the mind at peace.
  7. Yet guarding the center must first use martial fire: at the stage of guarding the center, when the breath-dynamic is not yet free-flowing and the mind not yet at peace, if one relies purely on naturalness, spirit and breath cannot fuse into one piece. When the Mysterious Pass first appears, the kidney breath rises and the heart fluid descends, one must use the martial fire of counting the breaths, not letting a single thought wander, urging tightly and fanning slowly; only then does the ordinary breath stop and the True Breath appear. But once one is running the River Chariot, the dragon roaring and the tiger howling, the husband singing and the wife following, to use martial fire again will drive the true breath-energy apart — what Mencius calls “chasing a runaway pig”: the pig is already in the pen and one still chases it — a great mistake.

Qiu Ling’s note: Huang often said, “The work begins with guarding the center and is completed through embryonic breathing” — this is exactly the key to practice.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

夫大道之要，不过神气二者而已。但有先后天之别，修士不可不知。古经云：「先天元神体也，后天识神用也」，无先天元神，大道无主；无后天识神，大道无用。尔等用工修炼，必要于混混沌沌、无知无觉时，养得先天元神以为主宰；然后一惊而醒，一觉而动，发为后天识神。此个识神，非朋从尔思，憧憧往来之私识，乃是正等正觉之元神，因其发动而有知觉，故曰识神。只怕此识一起，即纷纷扰扰，恶妄杂念，纷至沓来而不已者，就堕于私流于欲，而不可以炼丹也。

The essentials of the great Way are no more than the two things spirit and breath. But spirit and breath both have their Prior Heaven and Posterior Heaven aspects, which those who cultivate the Way must not fail to know. An ancient scripture says: “The Prior Heaven original spirit is the substance; the Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit is the function.” Without the Prior Heaven original spirit, the great Way has no master; without the Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit, the great Way has no use. In your practice and refinement, you must, at the time of muddled undividedness, without knowing or perceiving, nurture the Prior Heaven original spirit as the master; then, startled awake, stirring at a moment of awareness, what comes forth is the Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit. This cognitive spirit is not the selfish, stray consciousness of which the Book of Changes says “your companions follow your thoughts, coming and going restlessly” (thoughts in crowds, coming and going); rather it is the original spirit of right and perfect awakening which, because it has stirred and has perception, is called the cognitive spirit. The only fear is that once this consciousness arises, it becomes a confused tumult, evil thoughts, deluded thoughts and stray thoughts crowding in one after another without end, and then it falls into selfishness and slides into desire, and cannot be used to refine the elixir.

§2
Original (Chinese)

惟有一心了照，矢志靡他，如此用志不纷，乃凝于神，神凝而息可调，息调即丹可结。故曰：“一心只在丝纶上，不见芦花对岸红。”如此一心，虽曰识神，其实即元神也。所以古云：“天心为主，元神为用”，巧使盗机返还造化，何患不立跻圣神。总要于天心发动之后，常常稳蓄，不许一念游移，一息杂妄，庶几天心常在，道心常凝，虽有识亦此无识也。

Only by illuminating clearly with one mind, setting one’s will and not shifting it elsewhere — thus the mind and will are single and undisturbed, and it is concentrated in the spirit; when the spirit is concentrated, the breath can be regulated; when the breath is regulated, the elixir can form. So it is said: “One mind only on the fishing line, not seeing the reed flowers red on the far bank.” (The angler’s mind is only on his line; however red the reed flowers on the far bank, he does not see them.) With one mind like this, although it is called the cognitive spirit, it is in fact the original spirit. That is why the ancients said: “The heavenly mind is the master, the original spirit the function.” Skillfully using the method of stealing the dynamic of heaven and earth to return to the source of creation — why worry that you cannot at once ascend to the realm of the sage and the spirit! In all cases, after the heavenly mind has stirred, one must constantly store it steadily, not allowing a single thought to waver or a single breath to be stray and deluded; thus the heavenly mind is always present and the mind of the Way always concentrated — though there is consciousness, it is as if there were none.

§3
Original (Chinese)

学者修真下手之际，贵乎一心制服两眼，并口、耳、身、意之妄识，于是集神于丹扃，调息于丹田，务使凡息断灭，然后元气始来归命。既得元气来归，氤氲活泼，宛转悠扬，如活龙动转，十分爽健，此元气之充壮，可以运行河车矣。苟气机大动，不行河车化精为气，化气为神之功，仍然凝聚丹鼎，奈未经火化，阴精难固，不能长留于后天鼎中，一霎时凡火一起，必动淫根生淫事而倾矣。即或强制死守，不使他动，奈后天精气皆属纯阴，未经煅炼，不强制他必泄，即强制他亦必泄也。夫以此诀一行，即可以夺天地鬼神之权，参造化阴阳之法，而自主自夺，我命由我不由天矣。

When the student begins cultivating the true, what matters is to subdue with one mind the deluded consciousness of the two eyes, and of the mouth, ears, body and intention, then gather the spirit at the elixir gate (the gateway of the elixir field) and regulate the breath in the elixir field, making sure the ordinary breath ceases (the mouth-and-nose breathing so fine it seems absent); then the original breath comes to return to the root and restore life. Once the original breath has returned, it mingles richly and lively, turning and floating, like a living dragon turning, very fresh and strong — this is the original breath grown full, and one may run the River Chariot. If the breath-dynamic has already stirred greatly, yet one does not do the River Chariot’s work of “transforming essence into breath and breath into spirit”, but still only concentrates it in the elixir tripod (the lower elixir field), then, alas, it has not been refined by fire, the yin essence can hardly be secured, and it cannot stay long in the Posterior Heaven tripod; as soon as ordinary fire arises, it is bound to stir the root of lust, give rise to lustful affairs and pour out. Even if one forcibly guards it rigidly, not letting it move, alas, the essence and breath of Posterior Heaven are all pure yin and have not been forged — not forced, it is bound to leak; forced, it is bound to leak too. Once this key is carried out, one can seize the power of heaven, earth, ghosts and spirits, take part in the method of creation and of yin and yang, and be one’s own master — “my life is in my hands, not in heaven’s.”

§4
Original (Chinese)

既有元气于丹田，而行河车之法，尤须假后天凡气，为阳火阴符逼迫而催促之，使之上升下降，往来无穷，鼓舞而煅炼之，使之化凡成真，变化莫测。苟徒有元气之发生，活子时之现象，而无后天凡气，则先天元气岂能自上自下，自煅自化，此金丹虽先天之元气为本，然亦必需后天凡气为之功用也。

When the elixir field already has the original breath and one goes on to carry out the method of the River Chariot, above all one must borrow the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath (the breathing) as yang fire and yin tally to press and urge it on, making it rise and descend, come and go without end, rousing it and forging it, so that it transforms from ordinary into true, changing unpredictably. If there were only the original breath arising and the Living Zi Hour appearing, without the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath, how could the Prior Heaven original breath rise by itself, descend by itself, refine itself and transform itself? Therefore, although the Golden Elixir takes the Prior Heaven original breath as its root, it must rely on the ordinary Posterior Heaven breath to do its work.

§5
Original (Chinese)

至于金丹始终全仗火候，古人临炉十分慎重，惟恐一息偶乖，有干阴阳造化。故曰进火行符，犹之煮饭，火缓则生，故贵惺惺常存；火急则焦，故贵绵绵不绝。生于此二语，可知用火之微矣。到得地下雷鸣，火逼金行，此时若非武火，金气安能上升，然必善于用武，任他烈焰万丈，光芒四射，我则以一滴清凉水，遍洒十方足矣。此即气壮而心亨之道也，亦即清净恬淡为本之妙术也。故曰：“龙虎相逢上战场，霎时顷刻定兴亡，劝君逢恶须行善，若要争强必损伤。”以势可畏，其机甚危，而其心不可不临炉审慎也。生既明得此旨，永无倾泄之患焉。

As for the Golden Elixir, from beginning to end it depends entirely on fire phasing. The ancients were extremely careful when they came to the furnace to refine the elixir, fearing only that a single breath might go wrong by chance and disturb the workings of yin and yang. So it is said: advancing the fire and moving the tally is like cooking rice — if the fire is too slow, the rice is raw, so what matters is that alertness is always present (always clearly illuminating); if the fire is too fast, the rice is scorched, so what matters is that it is continuous and unbroken (neither fierce nor interrupted). From these two sentences you can know the subtlety of using fire. When it comes to “thunder rumbling underground and fire driving the metal onward” (true yang stirring in the lower elixir field, driven upward by fire), if one did not use martial fire then, how could the metal breath (the breath of true yang) rise? Yet one must be skilled in using the martial: let the blazing flames be ten thousand feet high and the rays shine in all directions — I use only one drop of cool water, sprinkled over the ten directions, and that is enough — this is the way of “the breath strong and the mind at ease” (though the breath is strong and flourishing, the mind is at peace), the wondrous art rooted in purity, stillness and calm. So it is said: “When dragon and tiger meet, they go into battle; in an instant rise or fall is decided. I urge you: when you meet evil, do good; if you strive to be strong, you will surely be harmed.” (When dragon and tiger meet it is like going into battle, and success or failure is decided in an instant; I urge you to meet danger with goodness, for if you try to be strong you will surely be hurt.) Because the force is fearsome and the juncture very dangerous, one cannot but be careful at the furnace. Now that you understand this principle, you will never have to worry about pouring out.

§6
Original (Chinese)

虽然，此行河车之法，当如是耳，若一概施之于守中，气机未畅心神未宁，一以纯任自然之法行之，则神气安能打成一片，有何药物可采哉？此必于玄关初现之时，肾气上升，心液下降，用起数息之武火，不许一念走作，一息纷驰，如此紧催慢鼓，鼓动橐籥机关，然后凡息方停，真息始见，人心乃死，道心乃生。否则慢说自然，必无自然也。故曰虽有生知之圣人，亦必下困知勉行工夫始得。古云：“西山白虎正猖狂，东海青龙不可当，两手捉来令死斗，化成一块紫金霜”。又曰：“降龙须要志如天，伏虎心雄气似烟，痴蠢愚人能会得，管教立地作神仙”。

Even so, this is the method for running the River Chariot, and that is how it should be. If one applied it wholesale to the stage of guarding the center — when the breath-dynamic is not yet free-flowing and the mind not yet at peace — doing it purely by relying on naturalness, how could spirit and breath fuse into one piece? And what medicine would there be to gather? At this time, one must, when the Mysterious Pass first appears, the kidney breath rises and the heart fluid descends, take up the martial fire of counting the breaths, not allowing a single thought to run off or a single breath to scatter, urging tightly and fanning slowly, working the mechanism of the bellows (the breathing); only then does the ordinary breath stop and the True Breath appear, the human mind die and the mind of the Way come to life. Otherwise, never mind naturalness — you will certainly not attain naturalness. That is why it is said: even a sage who knows from birth must do the work of “knowing through difficulty and practicing with effort” to attain it. The ancients said: “On the western mountain the white tiger rages wild; in the eastern sea the green dragon cannot be withstood; seize them with both hands and make them fight to the death, and they transform into a block of purple-gold frost.” (The white tiger of the western mountain is raging, the green dragon of the eastern sea is irresistible; seize them with both hands and make them fight to the death, and they become a block of purple-gold frost — a metaphor for spirit and breath uniting to form the elixir.) And: “To subdue the dragon, your will must be like heaven; to tame the tiger, your heart must be bold and your breath like smoke; even a dull and foolish person who can grasp this is sure to become an immortal on the spot.” (To subdue the dragon, your will must be as vast as heaven; to tame the tiger, your heart must be bold and your breath full; even a dull person who grasps this will become an immortal on the spot.)

§7
Original (Chinese)

此种武火，施之于龙虎不交水火不济之时则可，若行河车则已龙吟虎啸，夫唱妇随，再用此法，则又恐迫逐真气散乱。孟子云：“如追放豚”。既入其笠，又从而招之，此大错矣。吾将全功毕露，生等须努力修持，以慰为师之望焉。切勿妄泄，自干罪咎。

This martial fire may be used when dragon and tiger have not joined and water and fire do not help each other; but when it comes to running the River Chariot, when the dragon is already roaring and the tiger howling, the husband singing and the wife following (spirit and breath already united), to use this method again — I fear it would drive the true breath-energy and scatter it. Mencius said: “Like chasing a runaway pig.” The pig is already in the pen, and one still chases it — this is a great mistake. I have told you the whole of the practice; you must practice diligently, to comfort your teacher’s hopes. Never divulge it carelessly and bring blame upon yourselves.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§7
Original note (Chinese)

此选自黄元吉《道德经注释》第八十一章注文最后一段。黄氏于此扼要论述其气功养生学守中和河车二法，对于学者理解功法程序及其精华是有帮助的。黄氏尝云：“其功始于守中，其成由于胎息”，即练功之关键所在。本节文较简明，本书前各章亦均有论及，不再详加解释。

This passage is taken from the last paragraph of the commentary on chapter 81 of Huang Yuanji’s Commentary on the Daode jing. Here Huang briefly sets out the two methods of guarding the center and the River Chariot in his qigong of nourishing life; it is helpful for students in understanding the sequence of the practice and its essence. Huang once said: “The work begins with guarding the center and is completed through embryonic breathing.” This is exactly the key to practice. The text of this section is fairly simple and clear, and has been discussed in the earlier chapters of this book, so it will not be explained in further detail.

Key Terms

  • Original spirit, cognitive spirit: the Prior Heaven spirit as substance, the Posterior Heaven spirit as function; see Original Spirit, cognitive spirit.
  • Your companions follow your thoughts, coming and going restlessly: from the Book of Changes, the Xian hexagram; thoughts in crowds, coming and going without stop.
  • One mind only on the fishing line: the angler’s mind only on his line, a metaphor for single-mindedness.
  • The heavenly mind is the master, the original spirit the function: see Zhang Sanfeng’s words quoted in Qiu Ling’s note to Chapter Five, Section 4.
  • Elixir gate: the gateway of the elixir field.
  • Elixir tripod: the lower elixir field.
  • Yang fire and yin tally: the advancing and withdrawing of fire phasing; see fire phasing.
  • Thunder rumbling underground, fire driving the metal onward: true yang stirring in the lower elixir field, driven upward by fire.
  • One drop of cool water, sprinkled over the ten directions: however fierce the breath-dynamic, the mind always stays cool and calm.
  • The breath strong and the mind at ease: the breath strong and flourishing, the mind at peace.
  • Dragon and tiger: spirit and breath; see Lead and Mercury.
  • The martial fire of counting the breaths: counting the breathing to gather in the mind; a martial fire with action.
  • Bellows (橐籥): a bellows, a metaphor for breathing.
  • Knowing through difficulty and practicing with effort: from the Doctrine of the Mean — studying hard and practicing with effort.
  • Like chasing a runaway pig: from the Mencius, “Jin Xin II”.

Putting It into Practice

  1. One mind: like an angler watching his line — this single-minded focus is the original spirit acting as master.
  2. Early on, you may “use effort” a little: when the mind is unsettled and the breath-dynamic not free-flowing, you may count the breaths to help gather the mind; do not “leave everything to it” from the very start.
  3. Once the breath-dynamic moves of itself, let go: when the breath is already moving by itself, do not keep counting or pushing to urge it on — that is “chasing a pig that is already in the pen”.
  4. The fire-phasing formula: neither too slack (torpor — “raw”) nor too tight (agitation — “scorched”).
  5. Safety note: what this section says about “running the River Chariot” and “borrowing the ordinary breath to press and urge it on” belongs to the time after the original breath has grown full and turns like a living dragon; Huang Yuanji also warns that using martial fire at that time will drive the true breath-energy apart. Beginners should not follow the descriptions mechanically and push the breath around the Celestial Circuit with breathing and intention.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is one paragraph, taken from Huang Yuanji’s Commentary on the Daode jing, chapter 81 (see Qiu Ling’s note); it is divided into seven short passages.
  2. “An ancient scripture says: ‘The Prior Heaven original spirit is the substance; the Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit is the function’” (古经云：「先天元神体也，后天识神用也」): the original book uses 「」 quotation marks here; reproduced as it stands.
  3. “Though there is consciousness, it is this without consciousness” (虽有识亦此无识也): so in the original book; 此 is probably an error for 如 (“as if”), and the translation renders it as “as if there were none”.
  4. “You (生) from these two sentences” (生于此二语): 生 refers to the disciples.
  5. The three poems “When dragon and tiger meet, they go into battle”, “On the western mountain the white tiger rages wild” and “To subdue the dragon, your will must be like heaven” are introduced in the original only with “the ancients said”, “so it is said” and “and it is said”, without giving their source.

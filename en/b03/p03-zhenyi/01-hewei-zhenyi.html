Chapter Three · The True Intention · 何谓真意

What Is the True Intention

What is the true intention — the Way gives birth to one, one to two, two to three; between yin and yang all depends on the true intention presiding; after one awakening, one mind and no second thought

About This Chapter

This section is taken from Huang Yuanji’s Commentary on the Daode jing, chapter 42, the chapter “The Way gives birth to one, one gives birth to two, two gives birth to three, three gives birth to the ten thousand things”. Huang Yuanji uses these words of Laozi to make clear the place of the “true intention” in cultivation:

  1. The Way gives birth to one, one gives birth to two: the Way is originally pure emptiness; within the emptiness a breath stirs; its movement is yang, its stillness yin, and so yin and yang are divided.
  2. Two gives birth to three: besides yin and yang there must be a “third” that presides over them — this is the Original Spirit, the true intention. In the human body, what is still and gathered is essence (yin), what moves and flows is breath (yang); without the true intention presiding, yin and yang scatter and can neither give birth to a person nor accomplish the Way.
  3. The blending breath makes harmony: if yin and yang have no blending, harmonizing breath, there is no master in the middle, and the spirit is not at peace.
  4. Where the true intention comes from: at the start of sitting, cut off ties of feeling and sweep away stray thoughts, until you are as still as a simpleton, as if asleep yet not asleep, as if awake yet not awake; suddenly one awakening and it stirs, and you wake from the deep and obscure — at this moment all images are empty, only one point of numinous clarity moves alone, there is only one mind and no second thought. This is the true intention, also called right mindfulness, or the right and perfect awakening of the Tathāgata.

Qiu Ling’s note is very practical: - The true intention is the Original Spirit, also called “right awakening within emptiness”. When beginning practice, one must train the true intention and use it to preside over movement and stillness. - Two ways of finding the true intention: one is when, amid a tangle of stray thoughts, there suddenly emerges a “thought” that can stop the stray thoughts (the moment when the previous thought has gone and the next has not followed); the other is to keep the mind on the elixir field, and when this reaches a certain point, the true intention also suddenly appears. - The reason the stillness of qigong is a “third state”, different from sleep and from waking, is the presence of the true intention — neither awake nor asleep, and yet not torpor.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

老子曰：“道生一”。道何有哉？虚而已矣。然至虚之中一气萌动，天地生焉。故曰“有物混成先天地生”也。无极之先，混混沌沌，祇是一虚，及动化为阳，静化为阴，即易有太极是生两仪，是所谓道生一，一生二也。其在人身，即微茫之中一觉而动，乾坤阖辟，气机往来，静而凝聚者为阴、为精，动而流行者为阳、为气，若无真意主之，则阴阳散乱，无由生人而成道。可见阴阳二气之间，甚赖元神、真意主持其际，所谓二生三也。由是一阴一阳，一动一静，气化流行主宰，故而万物生生不穷矣，所谓三生万物也。

Laozi says: “The Way gives birth to one.” What, after all, is there in the Way? Nothing but pure emptiness. Yet within this utmost emptiness a point of breath stirs, and Heaven and Earth are born from it. So Laozi says: “There was something formed in chaos, born before Heaven and Earth.” (There is something undivided and whole that existed before Heaven and Earth.) Before the Limitless (when the universe had not yet any distinctions), all was chaotic and undifferentiated, nothing but emptiness; when it moves, it transforms into yang; when it is still, it transforms into yin — this is what the Changes calls “the Changes has the Supreme Ultimate, which gives birth to the two modes” (the Supreme Ultimate gives birth to the two modes of yin and yang), and what Laozi calls “the Way gives birth to one, one gives birth to two”. Applied to the human body, this principle is one awakening and it stirs in the faint and indistinct (dimly, seeming present and absent), whereupon Qian and Kun open and close and the breath’s dynamism comes and goes: what is still and gathered belongs to yin, and is essence; what moves and flows belongs to yang, and is breath. If there is no True Intention to preside, yin and yang scatter, and can neither give birth to a person nor accomplish the Way. So between the two breaths of yin and yang, everything depends on the Original Spirit and the true intention presiding in the middle — this is “two gives birth to three” (besides the two, yin and yang, there is a third that presides over them). From this one yin and one yang, one movement and one stillness, the transforming flow of breath has a master, and so all things are born without end — this is “three gives birth to the ten thousand things”.

§2
Original (Chinese)

万物负阴而抱阳，冲气以为和。阴阳若无冲气，则中无主而神不宁。物之生也犹且不能，况修道乎？

Laozi also says: “The ten thousand things carry yin on their backs and embrace yang, and the blending breath makes harmony.” (All things carry yin and embrace yang, and rely on the blending, harmonizing breath to bring yin and yang into accord.) If yin and yang lack this blending breath, there is no master in the middle, and the spirit cannot be at peace. If even the generation of the ten thousand things cannot be accomplished, how much less cultivating the Way?

§3
Original (Chinese)

《悟真篇》云：“道自虚无生一气，便从一气产阴阳，阴阳自是成三体，三体重生万物昌”。此即一生二，二生三，三生万物之谓。

The Awakening to Reality says: “The Way, from emptiness, gives birth to one breath; from the one breath yin and yang are produced; yin and yang naturally form three bodies; the three bodies give birth again, and the ten thousand things flourish.” (The Way gives birth to one breath out of emptiness, and from the one breath gives birth to yin and yang; yin and yang together with what harmonizes them form three bodies; the three bodies give birth again, and all things flourish.) This is exactly “one gives birth to two, two gives birth to three, three gives birth to the ten thousand things”.

§4
Original (Chinese)

修行人打坐之初，必先寂灭情缘，扫除杂妄，至虚至静，不异痴愚，似睡非睡，似醒非醒，此鸿蒙未判之气象，所谓道也，忽焉一觉而动，杳冥冲醒，我于此一动之后，只觉万象咸空，一灵独运，抱元守一，或云真意，或云正念，或云如来正等正觉。此时只一心无两念焉。

When the practitioner begins to sit, he must first extinguish ties of feeling (cut off attachment to human affairs) and sweep away stray thoughts and delusions entirely, until he reaches utmost emptiness and utmost stillness, no different from a simpleton or a fool, as if asleep yet not asleep, as if awake yet not awake — this is the atmosphere of “the primal chaos not yet divided” (Heaven and Earth not yet separated, chaos not yet opened), which is what is called “the Way”. Suddenly one awakening and it stirs, and one wakes at once from the deep and obscure; after this stirring, I feel only that all images have become empty, and only one point of numinous clarity moves alone, embracing the origin and guarding the One — this some call “the true intention”, some call “right mindfulness”, some call “the right and perfect awakening of the Tathāgata”. At this moment there is only one mind, and no second thought.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

此为黄元吉氏《道德经注释》第四十二章部分注文。此章老子原文有“道生一，一生二，二生三，三生万物。万物负阴而抱阳，冲气以为和”之句。

“有物混成先天地生”句，见老子《道德经》第二十五章。

“万象咸空，一灵独运”，这是黄氏描述练功中运用真意的状态。“一灵”就是真意，就是眼前性光与真意融合为一之景。

古人说，真意就是元神，又称为“虚无中的正觉”。入手练功就要练出真意，用它来主宰气功中动静的状态。练功者垂帘闭目，于杂念纷纭中忽然显露出一个制止杂念的“念头”，此时即杂念已去，后念未续之际，这忽的一觉，就是真意。这一觉有如电光石火，亦如奔马驰过眼前，当前即是，转眼即非，所差只是毫厘间事；这一觉来得自自然然，不由感附。练功者要立刻不失时机地捕捉住它，保之育之。如此神与气合，气依神行，无中生有，气机由微而著，就是阳生活子时，就是玄关窍开。另一种寻找真意的办法是守一，如意守丹田，当守到一定程度，神气交合，真意也会忽然呈现，同样猛然一觉而动。

真意乃冲气，即太和一气所化，用于调和阴阳，实际上无论采取，烹炼、交媾等，无不赖真意主持之。

气功入静状态所以成为区别于人体睡眠态和清醒态的第三态，就因为有真意存在，使这种入静成为非醒非睡的状态。古人称之为“无知有知，有觉无觉”。此种状态又非昏聩，若是昏聩则成槁木死灰。所以黄元吉说：“当灭动心，莫灭照心”。“动心”即杂念，“照心”即真意观照。

This is part of Huang Yuanji’s commentary on chapter 42 of his Commentary on the Daode jing. Laozi’s original text for this chapter includes the lines: “The Way gives birth to one, one gives birth to two, two gives birth to three, three gives birth to the ten thousand things. The ten thousand things carry yin on their backs and embrace yang, and the blending breath makes harmony.”

The line “There was something formed in chaos, born before Heaven and Earth” is found in chapter 25 of Laozi’s Daode jing.

“All images are empty, and one numinous point moves alone” is Huang’s description of the state when the true intention is used in practice. “One numinous point” is the true intention, that is, the scene in which the light of nature before the eyes and the true intention merge into one.

The ancients said that the true intention is the Original Spirit, also called “right awakening within emptiness”. When beginning practice, one must train the true intention and use it to preside over the states of movement and stillness in qigong. The practitioner lowers the curtains and closes the eyes, and amid a tangle of stray thoughts there suddenly appears a “thought” that can stop the stray thoughts — this is just the moment when the stray thoughts have gone and the next thought has not yet followed; this sudden awakening is the true intention. This awakening is like a flash of lightning, like a spark from flint, like a galloping horse flashing past the eyes: right now it is, in the blink of an eye it is not; the difference is only a hair’s breadth; it comes quite naturally, not brought about by the touch of outer things. The practitioner must seize the moment at once to catch it, protect it and nourish it. In this way spirit and breath unite, the breath moves by relying on the spirit, being arises out of non-being, and the breath’s dynamism grows from faint to manifest — this is the Living Zi Hour when yang is born, this is the opening of the Mysterious Pass opening. Another way of finding the true intention is “guarding the One”, for example keeping the mind on the elixir field: when this reaches a certain point, spirit and breath unite, and the true intention also suddenly appears — likewise a sudden awakening and stirring.

The true intention is transformed from the blending breath, that is, the breath of supreme harmony (the most blending, harmonious breath between Heaven and Earth), and serves to harmonize yin and yang; in fact, gathering, cooking and refining, union and so on — not one of them does not depend on the true intention to preside.

The reason the stillness of qigong can be a “third state”, distinct from sleep and from waking, is that the true intention is present, making this stillness a state neither awake nor asleep. The ancients called it “without knowing yet knowing, aware yet without awareness”. Nor is this state dull torpor; if it were torpor, it would become withered wood and dead ashes. That is why Huang Yuanji says: “Extinguish the moving mind; do not extinguish the illuminating mind.” The “moving mind” is stray thoughts; the “illuminating mind” is the looking-on of the true intention.

Key Terms

  • The Way gives birth to one, one to two, two to three, three to the ten thousand things: chapter 42 of the Daode jing; Huang Yuanji explains “three” as the Original Spirit, the true intention that presides over yin and yang.
  • There was something formed in chaos, born before Heaven and Earth: chapter 25 of the Daode jing, referring to the undivided substance prior to Heaven and Earth.
  • The Limitless, the Supreme Ultimate, the two modes: before division is the Limitless; about to divide is the Supreme Ultimate; yin and yang divided out are the two modes.
  • Faint and indistinct (weimang): dim, seeming present and absent.
  • Opening and closing (hepi): closing and opening.
  • Blending breath: the blending, harmonizing breath that harmonizes yin and yang; Qiu Ling’s note: the true intention is transformed from the blending breath (the breath of supreme harmony).
  • The primal chaos not yet divided: the state before Heaven and Earth separated, before chaos opened.
  • One numinous point moving alone: amid the emptiness of all images, one point of numinous clarity moving alone — that is, the true intention.
  • Embracing the origin and guarding the One: guarding the One of the origin; see guarding the center and embracing the One.
  • True Intention: also called right mindfulness, right awakening, “right awakening within emptiness”; it is that point of presiding mind when the Original Spirit comes into use.
  • Guarding the One: keeping the mind on one place (such as the elixir field); when this reaches a certain point, the true intention appears naturally.
  • Third state: the stillness of qigong, neither awake nor asleep, and not torpor either.
  • Moving mind, illuminating mind: the moving mind is stray thoughts; the illuminating mind is the looking-on of the true intention.

Putting It into Practice

  1. What the true intention is like: when you have sat until you are as if asleep yet not asleep, as if awake yet not awake, and suddenly wake with a start, it is the mind in that moment with “only one mind and no second thought”, clear and awake, able to preside. It is not forceful thinking, nor calculation.
  2. Two ways of finding it:
    • when stray thoughts are tangled, a thought that stops the stray thoughts suddenly emerges — that instant when the previous thought has gone and the next has not followed;
    • keep the mind on the elixir field, and when this reaches a certain point, the true intention appears naturally.
  3. When it appears, protect it: recognize it at once, keep it and nourish it — not by adding force, but by not letting it get lost.
  4. The standard for stillness: neither awake nor asleep, and not torpor either. Extinguish stray thoughts (the moving mind), but do not extinguish the looking-on (the illuminating mind).

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in three paragraphs, from chapter 42 of Huang Yuanji’s Commentary on the Daode jing (see Qiu Ling’s note); the third paragraph is divided into two short passages.
  2. “Nothing but pure emptiness” (祇是一虚): 祇 is the same as 只, “only”.
  3. “The Changes has the Supreme Ultimate, which gives birth to the two modes”: from the “Xici” (Appended Statements), part one, of the Book of Changes.
  4. “Wakes at once from the deep and obscure” (杳冥冲醒): so in the original book; it means waking suddenly out of the deep and obscure.

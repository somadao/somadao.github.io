Chapter Three · The True Intention · 真意与真觉

True Intention and True Awareness

True intention and true awareness — sudden awareness without intention is true awareness, guarding within with a single mind is the true intention; true awareness is the root, the true intention its function

About This Chapter

In Huang Yuanji’s book, “true intention” and “true awareness” often appear together and are easily confused. This section is devoted to telling them apart:

  1. Awareness and intention are two yet one: when, without mind or intention, awareness happens to arise, it is called “true awareness”; when, after this awakening, one deliberately uses the mind within it to regulate and store, it becomes the “true intention”.
  2. The standard of the true intention: the intention has arisen, yet the mind is still empty, holding nothing — only this is the true intention, not far from that point of Prior Heaven true awareness.
  3. Neither can be lacking: without this awakening there is no root; without this intention there is no function.
  4. What the true intention is like: after the awakening, only one mind and no second thought, like a traveler following the main road without turning off onto the side paths — wandering thoughts and stray notions are not the true intention. And not only now: in the future, when the elixir is complete and the Way accomplished, all will still depend on this true intention at work.

Qiu Ling’s note makes it clear in one sentence: true awareness is the root, the true intention its function. Without the true intention, true awareness can hardly appear; once true awareness has appeared, it must be protected and upheld by the true intention.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

须知觉与意皆二而一者也，不过以无心无意，偶尔有知，谓之真觉。迨一觉之后，我必加意用心调停蕴蓄于其间，则为真意。然意发而心仍无有物，始为真意，与我先天一点真觉不甚相远。所以，无心忽觉为真觉，一心内守为真意，其实皆一觉而已，一意而已。

You must know that “awareness” and “intention” are both two yet one — spoken of as two, they are in fact one. It is only that when, without mind or intention, awareness happens to arise, it is called “true awareness”; after this awakening, I must deliberately use the mind to regulate and store within it, and this becomes the “True Intention”. But the intention has arisen, yet the mind is still empty, holding nothing — only this is the true intention, and it is not far from my point of Prior Heaven true awareness. So it is said: sudden awareness in the midst of no-mind is true awareness; guarding within with a single mind is the true intention. In fact it is all just one “awareness” and one “intention”.

§2
Original (Chinese)

学人欲采药炼丹，除此一觉则无本，除此一意则无用。无本无用，而欲成功则难矣！故古云，游思杂念非真意也。从一觉之后，只一心无两念，如走路人从一条大路而行，并不旁趋别径，即真意也。莫说此时离不得真意，即后来丹成道备，要皆此真意为之作用也。

If the student wants to gather the medicine and refine the elixir, without this awakening he has no root; without this intention he has no function. Without root and without function, success will be hard! So the ancients said: wandering thoughts and stray notions are not the true intention. From the awakening onward there is only one mind and no second thought, like a traveler following the main road without turning off onto the side paths — this is the true intention. Not only can one not do without the true intention now; even in the future, when the elixir is complete and the Way accomplished, all will depend on this true intention at work.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§2
Original note (Chinese)

黄氏书中谈及真意与真觉之处甚多，两者极易混淆。真意与真觉原相去不远，故有二而一之说。若区别之，则真觉为本，真意为用。若无真意则真觉难出；既出真觉，又须真意护持。

There are many places in Huang’s books that speak of the true intention and true awareness, and the two are very easily confused. The true intention and true awareness are originally not far apart, hence the saying “two yet one”. If one is to distinguish them, it is this: true awareness is the root, the true intention is the function. Without the true intention, true awareness can hardly appear; once true awareness has appeared, it must be protected and upheld by the true intention.

Key Terms

  • True awareness: that point of awareness that happens to arise without mind or intention, the “awakening” of one awakening and it stirs.
  • True Intention: that point of presiding mind which, after the awakening, guards within with a single mind, regulating and storing.
  • Two yet one: spoken of as two, but in fact one.
  • Regulating and storing: adjusting and accumulating.
  • Wandering thoughts and stray notions: scattered thoughts and jumbled ideas, which are not the true intention.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Tell the two steps apart: the sudden awakening in stillness is true awareness (it cannot be manufactured; one can only wait for it to come); guarding within with a single mind after the awakening is the true intention (this step you must take yourself).
  2. The test of the true intention: the intention is there, yet the mind is still empty, without content — no pictures, no calculation, no tension of “I am using intention”.
  3. One main road: after the awakening there is only one mind, with no other thought branching off. If it branches off, bringing it back is it (see Chapter One, section four, “gathering back is it”).

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is one paragraph; the original book does not give the source. It is divided into two short passages.
  2. “Wandering thoughts and stray notions are not the true intention”: the original only says “the ancients said”, without a source.

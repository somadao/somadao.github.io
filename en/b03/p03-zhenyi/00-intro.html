Introduction to Chapter Three

What this chapter covers

Chapter One spoke of the Mysterious Pass (one awakening and it stirs), Chapter Two of the gate of the Mysterious Female (the true breath, embryonic breathing). This chapter speaks of the master that runs through the whole process — the true intention.

Qiu Ling compares the true intention to a switch: if the practitioner can turn it on, he can reach utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness and the opening of the Mysterious Pass; whenever in the day he can turn it on, that is the Living Zi Hour. The true intention presides over both the still state and the moving state; in fact it presides over the whole process of practice; without the true intention, one falls into dull emptiness.

The five sections of this chapter form a sequence from shallow to deep:

What the true intention is (what is the true intention) → the intention in stillness and the intention in movement (wu and ji form the gui) → how exactly to use it (how to use the true intention) → how it differs from “true awareness” (true intention and true awareness) → its relation to the true breath and the true breath-energy (the true intention, the true breath and true breath-energy)

What this chapter is about (in one sentence)

When you have sat until you are as if asleep yet not asleep, and suddenly wake with a start, that point of clear, presiding mind with “only one mind and no second thought” is the true intention; in stillness use it to guard, in movement use it to gather, and beyond this add no intention and mix in no view — it is the master of the whole process of practice.

Reading tips

  1. The true intention is neither forceful fixing of the mind nor thinking. Huang Yuanji’s standard is “the intention arises, yet the mind still holds nothing” — the intention is there, yet the mind is still empty. In his note to Chapter One, section four, Qiu Ling also says that everything must follow its natural course, “without mixing in awareness or intentional thought”; the true intention is only that point of presiding.
  2. Wu-ji, true earth, the Yellow Dame and the matchmaker are all code names for the true intention. Alchemists match the five phases with the directions; wu and ji belong to earth and occupy the center, and are used as a metaphor for the true intention that harmonizes spirit and breath from the center; the two “earth” characters 土 of wu and ji stacked together make gui 圭, hence the expressions “wu and ji form the gui” and “knife-point” (daogui 刀圭).
  3. The “Living Zi Hour” is the practical focus of this chapter. There is no need to cling to the zi hour at midnight; whenever you can become still and turn on the true intention, you can do the work; once practiced, you can train while walking, standing, sitting or lying down.
  4. “Subduing the breath” and “bringing the ordinary breath under control” never mean holding the breath. The method is still the one given in Chapter Two: let the outer breathing draw in naturally, listen to the breath, and do not force it down.

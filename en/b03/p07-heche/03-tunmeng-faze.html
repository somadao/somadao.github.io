Chapter Seven · The River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit) · 《易经》屯蒙法则

The Rule of Zhun and Meng from the Book of Changes

The rule of Zhun and Meng from the Book of Changes — advance the fire with Zhun in the morning, withdraw by the tally with Meng in the evening; once the forward path is blocked, the breath’s dynamism rises by itself without being guided; softness within firmness, strong yet harmonious and yielding

About This Chapter

This section uses the hexagrams Zhun and Meng of the Book of Changes to explain the rule of advancing the fire and withdrawing by the tally in the River Chariot, called “Zhun in the morning, Meng in the evening”.

  1. Zhun in the morning — advancing the fire: the hexagram Zhun (䷂) has Kan above — the one yang in Kan is the medicine — and Zhen below — the one yang at the bottom of Zhen is the fire advanced. When yang is born, whatever the coming and going of the breath’s dynamism, let only a faint trace of true intention pour slightly down to the Caudal Funnel — the true original breath used to flow in the forward direction (dispersing outward and downward); now it is not allowed to flow forward; once the forward path is blocked, it has nowhere to go and naturally rises upward, from the back up to the Muddy Pellet — this is a natural principle; it rises without waiting to be guided.
  2. Meng in the evening — withdrawing by the tally: the hexagram Meng (䷃) has the water of Kan below with one yang in the middle — the medicine below — and the mountain of Gen above with one yang on top — the tally withdrawn: the yang breath rises to the Muddy Pellet, is nourished warmly for a while and transforms into sweet dew, the spirit water. At this time one should follow the forward path, not be firm and strong as in advancing the fire.
  3. The sign and the method of withdrawing by the tally: when the medicine has risen to the Muddy Pellet, the breath full and the medicine numinous, there is a scene of coolness and tranquility — this is the time to withdraw by the yin tally. At this time there is no need to lead it down; simply let the spirit preside over the Muddy Pellet with the intention fixed on the high heavens, and it naturally descends step by step down the Storied Tower into the Crimson Palace; nourish it warmly for a moment, lead it into the elixir field, and let it merge with the breath into one piece. Then neither advance nor withdraw, quietly wait for the news, and run the Celestial Circuit again.
  4. The changes of the breath: when yang is first born the breath’s dynamism is faint and tender, like Mencius’s “breath of the early morning”; then, drawing out lead and adding mercury, it binds ever more firmly, like Mencius’s flood-like breath, “utmost in greatness, utmost in firmness”.
  5. The zither and the sword: the breath of utmost yang contains utmost yin, and clinging to yang firmness one cannot form the elixir. The sword stands for firmness; adding the word “zither” brings in softness within firmness, strength with harmony and yielding. At the start one must know firmness and softness; once the work is mature, one no longer knows firmness or softness, but simply follows what is natural.

Qiu Ling’s note: the hexagrams Zhun and Meng come after Qian and Kun — Heaven and Earth unite and all things begin to be born (Zhun, the beginning of life, full of difficulty); after they grow, they are in a state of childish ignorance (Meng, needing step-by-step instruction). Zhun symbolizes advancing the fire, Meng withdrawing by the tally; firmness and softness proceed separately and also assist each other, and only so is the Golden Elixir formed.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

吾见生等阳生之时，进火之际，尚未明《易》道朝屯暮蒙真正法则。盖《易》之屯卦坎在上为药，以坎中一阳生也；震在下为火，以震下一阳即所进之火也。尔等逢阳生时，不管他气机往来如何，略以微微真意下注尾闾，那真元一气从前之顺行者，至此不许他顺，且意思向上，而顺行之常道遂阻。顺道既阻，无处可去，自然气机往上而升，自后而上，势必至于泥丸，此自然之理。有不待导之而后升，引之而后上者。

I see that when yang is born and you advance the fire, you still do not understand the true rule of “Zhun in the morning, Meng in the evening” in the way of the Changes. The hexagram Zhun (䷂) of the Book of Changes: Kan is above and is the medicine, because one yang is born in Kan; Zhen is below and is the fire, because the one yang at the bottom of Zhen is the fire that is advanced. When you meet the birth of yang, whatever the coming and going of its dynamism, let only a faint trace of true intention pour slightly down to the Caudal Funnel: the true original breath used to flow in the forward direction; now it is not allowed to flow forward, and the intention points upward, so the ordinary forward path is blocked. Once the forward path is blocked, it has nowhere to go, and naturally the breath’s dynamism rises upward, going up from the back, and is bound to reach the Muddy Pellet — this is a natural principle. It rises without waiting to be guided, goes up without waiting to be pulled.

§2
Original (Chinese)

暮取蒙之义何如？蒙卦坎水在下，中有阳，即药在下也。艮山在上，上有一阳，阳即所退之符，符即阳气升于泥丸，温养片时，化为甘露神水，实皆阳之所化，非真属阴也。以其行功至此，精化为气，气化为丹，宜行顺道，不宜如前进火时刚健之气，故曰：退符。

What is the meaning of taking Meng in the evening? In the hexagram Meng (䷃), the water of Kan is below with one yang in the middle — that is, the medicine is below; the mountain of Gen is above with one yang on top, and this yang is the tally that is withdrawn — the tally is the yang breath that has risen to the Muddy Pellet, been nourished warmly for a while and transformed into sweet dew, the spirit water; in fact it is all transformed from yang, and does not truly belong to yin. Because when the work reaches this point, essence has transformed into breath and breath into elixir, one should follow the forward path, and not use the firm, strong breath of the earlier advancing of the fire — hence it is called “withdrawing by the tally”.

§3
Original (Chinese)

总之，药朝上阙泥丸，气满药灵，有一片清凉恬淡之象，即阳气上升于头目，宜退阴符之时也。此时不须引之降下，但以神主宰泥丸，意注于高上之天，自然循循降下重楼，入于绛宫，温养片晌，导入丹田与气打成一片，和合一团。斯时不进不退，无出无入，静候个中消息再行周天。

In short, when the medicine has risen to the Muddy Pellet (the upper gate), the breath full and the medicine numinous, there is a scene of coolness and tranquility — this is when the yang breath has risen to head and eyes, and it is time to withdraw by the yin tally. At this time there is no need to lead it down; simply let the spirit preside over the Muddy Pellet with the intention fixed on the high heavens above, and it will naturally descend step by step down the Storied Tower, enter the Crimson Palace, be nourished warmly for a moment, and then be led into the elixir field, merging with the breath into one piece, harmonized into one mass. At this time neither advance nor withdraw, neither go out nor come in; quietly wait for the news within, and then run the next Celestial Circuit.

§4
Original (Chinese)

学者勿视为怪诞也，论阳生之始气机微嫩，若孟子所云：“平旦之气为最切。”继而抽铅添汞，渐采渐炼，愈结愈坚，又若孟子所云，其为气也至大至刚，以直养而无害，则塞乎天地之间为至论。古人又云，吾有一物，上柱天下柱地。非孟子所谓浩然之气充塞两间者乎？

Students must not regard this as something strange. When yang is first born, the breath’s dynamism is faint and tender; Mencius’s “breath of the early morning” (the clear breath of early morning before contact with things) is the most fitting comparison. Then, drawing out lead and adding mercury, gathering and refining step by step, it binds ever more firmly, and is again like what Mencius says: “As breath, it is utmost in greatness and utmost in firmness; nourish it with uprightness and do it no harm, and it fills the space between Heaven and Earth” — this is the best way of putting it. The ancients also said: “I have a thing that props up Heaven above and props up Earth below.” Is this not the flood-like breath filling the space between Heaven and Earth of which Mencius speaks?

§5
Original (Chinese)

又曰，琴剑者何？盖以至阳之气中含至阴，学者执着一个阳刚之气则不能成丹。剑之取义刚是也，而又加一琴字，取其刚中有柔、健而和顺之义。然在下手之初，不得不知刚柔健顺，方无差错。若到水火调和，金木合并，则刚者不刚，柔者不柔，且至纯熟之候，更不知有刚柔，惟顺其气机之流行自然天然而已矣！

And what is meant by “the zither and the sword”? The breath of utmost yang contains utmost yin; if the student clings only to a firm yang breath, he cannot form the elixir. The sword stands for firmness; adding the word “zither” brings in the sense of softness within firmness, strength with harmony and yielding. However, at the very beginning one cannot fail to know firmness and softness, strength and yielding, so as not to go wrong; when water and fire are harmonized and metal and wood combined, the firm is no longer firm and the soft no longer soft; when one is fully practiced, one no longer knows any firmness or softness, but simply follows the flow of the breath’s dynamism, natural and spontaneous, and that is all!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

此以《周易》中屯、蒙两卦发明运转河车进火退符之妙用。屯、蒙两卦在乾、坤两卦之后，乾是天，坤是地，天地交合则万物开始生长，接着是屯，象征着生的开始，充满生的艰难。再接着是蒙，象征万物生长之后处于幼稚蒙昧时期，须循序渐进的教化。屯为䷂。此卦坎在上，中有一阳生，故为药；震在下，下有一阳故为火。故屯卦象征运行河车的进火之候。蒙为䷃。此卦坎在下，中有一阳，即示药在下；艮在上，上有一阳，即所退之符，即阳气在于顶。故蒙卦象征运行河车退符之候。运用屯蒙法则，进火、退符分刚柔进行，又刚柔相济，才能结成金丹。

Here the two hexagrams Zhun and Meng of the Zhouyi are used to explain the wondrous use of advancing the fire and withdrawing by the tally in turning the River Chariot. The hexagrams Zhun and Meng come after the hexagrams Qian and Kun: Qian is Heaven and Kun is Earth; Heaven and Earth unite and all things begin to grow; next comes Zhun, symbolizing the beginning of life, full of the difficulty of birth; next comes Meng, symbolizing all things, after they grow, in a period of childish ignorance, needing step-by-step instruction. Zhun is ䷂: Kan above, with one yang born in the middle, hence the medicine; Zhen below, with one yang at the bottom, hence the fire — so Zhun symbolizes the time of advancing the fire in turning the River Chariot. Meng is ䷃: Kan below, with one yang in the middle, showing the medicine below; Gen above, with one yang on top, which is the tally withdrawn, that is, the yang breath at the crown — so Meng symbolizes the time of withdrawing by the tally in turning the River Chariot. Applying the rule of Zhun and Meng, advancing the fire and withdrawing by the tally proceed with firmness and softness respectively, and firmness and softness also assist each other, and only so can the Golden Elixir be formed.

Key Terms

  • Zhun in the morning, Meng in the evening: advancing the fire takes the image of Zhun, withdrawing by the tally the image of Meng.
  • Zhun ䷂: Kan above and Zhen below; the one yang in Kan is the medicine, the one yang at the bottom of Zhen is the fire.
  • Meng ䷃: Gen above and Kan below; the one yang in Kan is the medicine below, the one yang on top of Gen is the tally withdrawn.
  • The ordinary forward path: the usual road on which the true breath-energy disperses outward and downward; see the method of reversal.
  • Rising without waiting to be guided: once the forward path is blocked, the breath’s dynamism rises by itself.
  • Upper gate: the upper part, that is, the Muddy Pellet.
  • Fixing the intention on the high heavens: letting the intention go far, not clinging to the crown.
  • Breath of the early morning: Mencius, “Gaozi I”, the clear breath of early morning.
  • Utmost in greatness, utmost in firmness, nourish it with uprightness and do it no harm: the flood-like breath of Mencius, “Gongsun Chou I”.
  • The zither and the sword: the sword stands for firmness, the zither for softness — softness within firmness, strength with harmony and yielding.
  • Metal and wood combined: lead (metal) and mercury (wood) united; see Lead and Mercury.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Advancing the fire is only “letting a faint trace of true intention pour slightly down to the Caudal Funnel” — not pushing with force, but gently blocking the road of outward dispersal, so that the breath’s dynamism rises by itself.
  2. Withdrawing by the tally needs no leading: when the head feels cool and tranquil, do not pull it down; let the intention go far, and it descends step by step of itself.
  3. Firmness and softness in measure, forgotten once mature: everything follows what is natural.
  4. Safety note: “pouring down to the Caudal Funnel” is only a faint trace of intention, not forcefully contracting the anus or holding the breath; if tightness in the lower back, a swollen head, headache or palpitations appear, the intention is too heavy — relax at once and return to what is natural.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is one paragraph; the original book does not give the source. It is divided into five short passages.
  2. The hexagram signs are printed as hexagram diagrams in the original book; standard symbols (䷂ Zhun, ䷃ Meng) are used here instead.
  3. “And what is meant by ‘the zither and the sword’” (又曰，琴剑者何): so in the original book; alchemical books often name “the zither and the sword” together as a metaphor for firmness and softness assisting each other.
  4. “I have a thing that props up Heaven above and props up Earth below”: the original only says “the ancients also said”, without a source.

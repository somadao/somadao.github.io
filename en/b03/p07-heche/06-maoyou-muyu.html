Chapter Seven · The River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit) · 卯酉沐浴

Ablution at Mao and You

Ablution at mao and you — ablution at mao guards against yang becoming too hard, ablution at you guards against yin becoming too soft; never cling to images or hold fast to words, idly turning it into rigid sitting and dead guarding; returning to the furnace and sealing it, using fire without fire, gathering the medicine without medicine

About This Chapter

The last section of this chapter speaks of “ablution” in the River Chariot — stopping the fire and warmly nourishing.

  1. Two kinds of ablution:
    • Ablution at mao: when the fire has been advanced to its limit, for fear that it rise and rise again and do no small harm, the tally is stopped and not used; a little warm nourishing is enough. Although the work stops at this time, the breath-dynamic still moves upward, reaching the Muddy Pellet to forge the yin breath there. Yang energy is rising and the life-breath is at its fullest, so mao is called “the gate of life”.
    • Ablution at you: when the tally has been withdrawn to its limit, for fear that by fixing the mind on withdrawing one would instead draw yin breath into the central palace and keep the yang elixir from forming, the work is likewise stopped and not used; concentrate the breath to reach softness, warmly nourish, and wait for what is natural. The people of old called it “the gate of death”, which is gathering in the spirit-light and letting it settle in the Crimson Palace.
  2. Do not cling: all this only describes an image — never cling to the image or hold fast to the words, turning it into the work of rigid sitting and dead guarding. When returning to the furnace and sealing it, “using fire without fire, gathering the medicine without medicine”, it comes wholly from no-mind and no-intention — yet in truth mind and intention are everywhere. This is the gate of the Mysterious Female showing its true form.
  3. Why ablution is needed: yang should not be too hard — too hard and it breaks, so it is aided with softness; yin should not be too soft — too soft and it is feeble, so it is governed with hardness. When yang energy is too hard, ordinary fire surges upward and causes trouble in the upper burner (head and chest); when yin energy is too soft, true yang retreats, selfish desires come and go without cease, and the body is feeble and listless. The remedy: illuminate it and lift it with the spirit, not letting yin breath grow in secret; naturally yang grows and yin wanes, and the demon of sleep can be refined away as well.

Qiu Ling’s note: ablution at mao and you comes from “ablution at the four cardinal points” — the four double-hours zi, wu, mao and you. In terms of the body, the zi position is the perineum, the mao position the Spine-Straddling Pass, the wu position the Muddy Pellet, and the you position the Crimson Palace. Ablution at mao (the Spine-Straddling Pass) comes when the fire has been advanced to its limit and true yang rises too hard: stop the work and do not advance, gather the spirit into the Muddy Pellet and illuminate single-mindedly. Ablution at you comes when the Conception and Governing vessels join and the force of the fire gathers in the Crimson Palace: with a mild and harmonious intention gather the spirit in the Crimson Palace, and after a moment return the medicine-breath to the lower field. Zhang Ziyang says “mao and you are empty comparisons” — ablution too should be applied flexibly.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

沐浴有二。卯沐浴是进火进之至极，恐其升而再升为害不小，因而停符不用，稍为温养足矣！此时虽然停工，而气机之上行者犹然如故。上至泥丸，煅炼泥丸之阴气，此其时也。况阳气上升正生气至盛，故卯为生之门也。酉沐浴是退符退之至极，恐其着意于退，反将阴气收于中宫，使阳丹不就。学人至此又当停工不用，专气致柔，温之养之，以候天然自然。此即为酉沐浴也，昔人谓之死之门。是即吾所谓收敛神光落于绛宫，不似卯门之敛神泥丸也。

There are two kinds of ablution. Ablution at mao is when the fire has been advanced to its limit and, fearing that it rise and rise again and do no small harm, one stops the fire and the tally and does not use them; a little warm nourishing is enough! Although the work has stopped at this time, the upward movement of the breath-dynamic continues as before, rising to the Muddy Pellet and forging the yin breath within the Muddy Pellet — this is exactly the time. Moreover, yang energy is rising, just when the life-breath is at its fullest, so mao is called “the gate of life”. Ablution at you is when the tally has been withdrawn to its limit and, fearing that by fixing the mind on withdrawing one would instead draw yin breath into the central palace, the yang elixir could not form. When the student reaches this point, he should again stop the work and not use it, concentrate the breath to reach softness, warm it and nourish it, and wait for what is natural of itself — this is ablution at you, which earlier people called “the gate of death”. This is what I mean by “gathering in the spirit-light and letting it settle in the Crimson Palace”, unlike the mao gate, where the spirit is gathered into the Muddy Pellet.

§2
Original (Chinese)

然此不过言其象耳，学者切勿泥象执文，徒为兀坐死守之功夫焉。至归炉封固，此时用火无火，采药无药，全然出于无心无意，其实心意无不在也。此即玄牝之门现其真象。

But this only describes its image; students must never cling to the image or hold fast to the words, idly turning it into the work of rigid sitting and dead guarding. When the time comes to return to the furnace and seal it, one uses fire yet there is no fire, one gathers the medicine yet there is no medicine — it comes wholly from no-mind and no-intention, yet in truth mind and intention are everywhere. This is the gate of the Mysterious Female showing its true form.

§3
Original (Chinese)

卯酉二时沐浴以存真阳者也。要知阳不宜太刚，太刚则折，当以柔道济之。阴不宜太柔，太柔则懦，须以刚德主之。卯门沐浴者所以防阳之过刚也。酉门沐浴者所以防阴之过柔也。若阳气过刚，必将凡火引而至上以为患上焦。阴气过柔，必将真阳退却而阴气反来作主，私欲憧憧往来无息，身亦因之懦弱不振，此又将何以处之哉？法在以神了照之提摄之，不使阴气潜滋暗长于其中，自然阳长而阴消，可以炼睡魔矣！

Ablution at the two times mao and you is used to preserve true yang. Know this: yang should not be too hard — too hard and it breaks, and should be aided with the way of softness; yin should not be too soft — too soft and it is feeble, and must be governed by the virtue of hardness. Ablution at the mao gate guards against yang becoming too hard; ablution at the you gate guards against yin becoming too soft. If yang energy is too hard, it is bound to draw ordinary fire upward and become a calamity in the upper burner (head and chest); if yin energy is too soft, it is bound to make true yang retreat and yin breath take over as master instead, selfish desires coming and going in confusion without cease, and the body becoming feeble and listless as a result — and how is this to be dealt with? The method lies in using the spirit to illuminate it and lift it, not letting yin breath quietly grow and spread within; naturally yang grows and yin wanes, and the demon of sleep (torpor) can be refined away as well!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

真阳盛时正阴符起手之时，所谓阳极生阴。然亦讲究法度，故有卯沐浴与酉沐浴之别。卯酉沐浴出于四正沐浴，四正一词指子午卯酉四个时辰，也指春夏秋冬四个季节。子午是南北位置，在先天卦象是乾坤，卯酉是东西位置，在先天卦象是坎离，都属正位，为沐浴的四个正时。因阳生于此四正位，故于此行沐浴之法。就人体位言，子位即会阴，卯位即夹脊，午位即泥丸，酉位即绛宫。小周天行卯酉沐浴为停符不进。卯夹脊沐浴行于进火至极，真阳上升太刚之时，即古人所说若无救苦观音大药必然迸裂之际，以救苦观音喻卯沐浴，法当停工不进，再敛神于泥丸，一心了照。酉沐浴行于任脉与督脉相合，炎炎火势集于绛宫之时。法当以冲和之意敛神于绛宫，保之守之养之。片刻之后，将药气归入下田，觉照不息。张紫阳《金丹四百字》云：“火候不用时，冬至不在子，及其沐浴法，卯酉成虚比。”则知卯酉沐浴亦应灵活应用。

When true yang is at its height is precisely when the yin tally begins — what is called “yang at its extreme gives birth to yin”. But there are rules for this too, hence the distinction between ablution at mao and ablution at you. Ablution at mao and you comes from “ablution at the four cardinal points”: the term “four cardinal points” refers to the four double-hours zi, wu, mao and you, and also to the four seasons, spring, summer, autumn and winter. Zi and wu are the north–south positions, which in the Prior Heaven trigrams are Qian and Kun; mao and you are the east–west positions, which in the Prior Heaven trigrams are Li and Kan — all are cardinal positions, the four proper times for ablution. Because yang is born at these four cardinal positions, the method of ablution is practiced there. In terms of the body: the zi position is the perineum, the mao position the Spine-Straddling Pass, the wu position the Muddy Pellet, and the you position the Crimson Palace. In the Lesser Celestial Circuit, ablution at mao and you means stopping the tally and not advancing. Ablution at mao (the Spine-Straddling Pass) is practiced when the fire has been advanced to its limit and true yang rises too hard — the very moment the ancients spoke of when they said “without the great medicine of Guanyin who saves from suffering, it would surely burst apart”, so “Guanyin who saves from suffering” is used as a metaphor for ablution at mao — the method is to stop the work and not advance, then gather the spirit into the Muddy Pellet and illuminate single-mindedly. Ablution at you is practiced when the Conception vessel and the Governing vessel join and the blazing force of the fire gathers in the Crimson Palace; the method is to gather the spirit in the Crimson Palace with a mild and harmonious intention, preserving it, guarding it, nourishing it; after a moment, return the medicine-breath to the lower elixir field, with awareness unceasing. Zhang Ziyang’s Four Hundred Characters on the Golden Elixir says: “Fire phasing does not use the hours; the winter solstice is not at zi; and as for the method of ablution, mao and you are empty comparisons.” (Fire phasing is not bound to the double-hours, the winter solstice need not be at the zi hour; and as for the method of ablution, mao and you too are only figurative comparisons.) From this we know that ablution at mao and you should also be applied flexibly.

Key Terms

  • Ablution: stopping the fire and warmly nourishing.
  • Ablution at mao: stopping the tally when the fire has been advanced to its limit; the gate of life; gathering the spirit into the Muddy Pellet.
  • Ablution at you: stopping the work when the tally has been withdrawn to its limit; the gate of death; gathering the spirit into the Crimson Palace.
  • Clinging to images and holding fast to words: being bound by the image and attached to the letter.
  • Rigid sitting and dead guarding: sitting dully and guarding lifelessly.
  • Using fire without fire, gathering the medicine without medicine: no-mind and no-intention when returning to the furnace and sealing it.
  • Upper burner: the region of the head and chest.
  • Demon of sleep: torpor and drowsiness; see torpor and distraction.
  • The four cardinal points: zi, wu, mao and you, or spring, summer, autumn and winter.
  • Zi at the perineum, mao at the Spine-Straddling Pass, wu at the Muddy Pellet, you at the Crimson Palace: Qiu Ling’s correspondence with parts of the body.
  • Guanyin who saves from suffering: a metaphor for ablution at mao, guarding against true yang being too hard and bursting.
  • Mao and you are empty comparisons: mao and you are only figurative comparisons; a phrase from the Four Hundred Characters on the Golden Elixir.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Ablution simply means “stopping”: stop when the rising is too strong, and stop too when the descending goes too far; simply warmly nourish and look after it.
  2. Signs of too much hardness: ordinary fire surging upward, heat and swelling in the head and chest — at such times stop the work and soften.
  3. Signs of too much softness: torpor, sluggishness, selfish desires arising in confusion — at such times illuminate with the spirit and lift your spirits.
  4. Do not hold rigidly to the format: mao and you are only metaphors; what is used is the principle, not hard rules of clock hours and body locations.
  5. Safety note: “When yang energy is too hard, ordinary fire surges upward and causes trouble in the upper burner” — this is a deviation Huang Yuanji names in his own words. If a swollen head, headache, flushed face, red eyes, palpitations or insomnia appear, stop moving the breath at once, relax the whole body, and gently bring your attention back to the lower belly or the soles of the feet; consult a doctor if necessary.

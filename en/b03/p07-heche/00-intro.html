Chapter Seven · The River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit) · 篇首导读

Introduction to Chapter Seven

What this chapter covers

The “River Chariot” is the Celestial Circuit: the true breath-energy starts from the perineum, rises along the Governing vessel at the back through the Caudal Funnel, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Jade Pillow to the Muddy Pellet, then descends at the front through the Storied Tower into the Crimson Palace and returns to the lower elixir field.

What is most noteworthy in this chapter is Huang Yuanji’s caution about the River Chariot:

  • Timing: only when the true breath-energy surges up to the heart and the seven openings and the pores all open (“the openings unfold”) may it be turned; otherwise “running fire without water will surely scorch the whole body”. Qiu Ling: without the openings unfolding, even if a little breath surges upward, one must not turn the River Chariot.
  • Attitude: with softness, silence and stillness. Advancing the fire is only “letting a faint trace of true intention pour slightly down to the Caudal Funnel”, and the breath’s dynamism rises by itself; withdrawing by the tally needs no leading — with the intention fixed on the high heavens, it naturally descends.
  • Warning: “The method of the River Chariot is said in all the alchemical scriptures to be greatly dangerous; practice that does not follow the way of Heaven will surely bring more misfortune than good.” If true yang does not stir greatly, nourish it in stillness for a long time.

The six sections of this chapter follow this order:

When it may be turned (when to turn the River Chariot) → which road it takes and how the fire is phased (the road of the River Chariot) → the rule of advancing the fire and withdrawing by the tally (the rule of Zhun and Meng from the Book of Changes) → the concrete technique (the method of kindling the fire: inhale, lick, contract, close) → the fundamental attitude (with softness, silence and stillness) → stopping the fire and warm nourishing (ablution at mao and you)

What this chapter is about (in one sentence)

The River Chariot is the natural flowing up and down of the true breath-energy once it is ample: turn it only when the openings have unfolded, advance the fire with only a faint trace of true intention, let the withdrawal by the tally descend of itself, and when it becomes too firm or too soft, stop for ablution — with softness, silence and stillness; if true yang does not stir, nourish it in stillness for a long time.

Reading tips (on safety — please read first)

  1. This chapter speaks of work at a fairly advanced stage; please read it mainly for understanding. Huang Yuanji himself and Qiu Ling both stress again and again: before the true breath-energy is naturally full and “the openings unfold”, do not use the mind to make the breath go round along the spine and over the crown. This kind of “imagined Celestial Circuit” is exactly what the book criticizes as “beginning without obtaining the true medicine” and “running fire without water”.
  2. Common deviations: a swollen feeling in the head, headache, a red face and eyes, ringing in the ears, palpitations, insomnia, or persistent heat and tightness in the back or at the crown. Huang Yuanji calls this “ordinary fire drawn upward, troubling the upper burner”. If these appear, stop moving the breath at once, relax the whole body and gently return your attention to the lower abdomen or the soles of the feet; if the symptoms do not go away, consult a doctor.
  3. Who should not practice: people with high blood pressure, heart disease, glaucoma, epilepsy, a history of mental illness, or who are pregnant should not practice the River Chariot, breath-holding, anal contraction or similar methods.
  4. The safest course is given in section five: “If true yang does not stir greatly, you may as well nourish it in stillness for a long time, without interruption throughout the twelve double-hours, and naturally the breath will be full and the medicine born.”

Chapter Two · The Gate of the Mysterious Female · 调息与听息

Regulating the Breath and Listening to the Breath

Regulating the breath and listening to the breath — in regulating the breath, regulate the breath of the true breath; unify your will and listen with the breath; the work of entering concentration lies in cessation and contemplation

About This Chapter

This is the longest section of the chapter, explaining concretely how to regulate the breathing. It has two parts.

First part: regulating the breath is regulating the fire phasing.

  1. It is enough to make the outer breathing even, neither too much nor too little, letting it go out by the mysterious and in by the female; whether it becomes coarse or fine, add only a little intention of drawing in and harmonizing. Never force coarse breath down to fineness or press floating breath into restraint — that would let the ordinary fire with form scorch the essence and blood of the whole body.
  2. Recognize the true breath and the true spirit: the true breath is the one in the elixir field that drifts and turns without cease; the true spirit is the awareness that suddenly arises in the midst of no thought and no worry. “In regulating the breath, regulate the breath of the true breath; in refining the spirit, refine the spirit that is not spirit.”
  3. The original breathing is like a banked fire: neither cold nor warm, no flame seen outside, no fire known within, only a warm, melting feeling — this is the formless fire of the spirit; fire with form blazes fiercely, flaring up and dying out suddenly, and harms the body.
  4. The outer breathing is like a blacksmith’s bellows: it blows from outside, protecting the breath of the spirit in the middle. The ordinary fire of breathing and the spirit fire of the elixir field are not entirely two different things either — like the flame of a lit candle and the fire latent in the wax.

Qiu Ling’s note: regulating the breath is regulating the fire phasing. The drawing-in of the outer breathing until it is gone must come naturally, and must not be forced down; forcing it instead chokes the breath and disturbs the breath’s dynamism. The true breath comes through utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, mind and breath relying on each other, as naturally as a channel forms where water flows.

Second part: listening to the breath.

  1. The procedure for beginning: put down all ties, lower the curtains and close the mouth, and turn the light back on the single opening of the Mysterious Pass. If the spirit is not concentrated and the breath is coarse, you may count the breaths for a while; once the spirit is concentrated and the breath settled, let the mind and spirit open out, do not contemplate rigidly, and simply keep the mind on listening to the breath.
  2. Listen to what? Zhuangzi: “Do not listen with the ear, but listen with the mind; do not listen with the mind, but listen with the breath.” This “breath” is not the breath of mouth and nose, nor the moving breath between the kidneys, nor the numinous breath in the heart, but the empty original breath in space. Keeping the mind on it is the union of spirit and breath.
  3. Why “listening”: when beginning, the openings of mouth, nose and eyes can all be closed; only the opening of the ears is hard to close; listen with a single mind, and the ears stay closed and all the openings fall silent. Huang Yuanji calls this “the finest method of cultivation”.
  4. Cessation and contemplation: let the mind rest below the navel in the elixir field, and contemplate the images that appear in emptiness.
  5. Tell true from false: merely settling the breathing roughly and calling it the spontaneous movement of embryonic breathing is far off the mark. When embryonic breathing truly stirs, the whole body is soft as cotton, like a warm spring day, like just waking from a deep sleep.

Qiu Ling’s note: commentators through the ages have mostly explained Zhuangzi‘s “listen with the breath” as some state of the mind; only Huang Yuanji holds that this “breath” is the empty original breath in space — this is his new insight.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

修炼第一要调得外呼吸均匀，无过不及，一任出玄入牝，如如自如，可开则开，可闭则闭。为粗为细，略加收敛调协之意足矣，切勿气粗而按之至细，气浮而按之使敛，致令有形凡火烧灼一身精血可也。生须认真此火，或文或武，或沐浴，或温养。虽火有不同，断无不是先天神火，而由后天凡息一出一入，往来迭运，而可以成丹也，故曰：“调息须调真息息，炼神须炼不神神。”无息之息，方是真息。不神之神，斯为主神。

What matters first in cultivation is to make the outer breathing even, neither too much nor too little, letting it go out by the mysterious and in by the female of itself, at ease and natural, opening when it should open and closing when it should close. Whether the breathing becomes coarse or fine, it is enough to add just a little intention of drawing in and harmonizing; never, when the breath is coarse, force it down to extreme fineness, or, when the breath is floating, press it hard into restraint, so that the ordinary fire with form (the dry heat produced by Posterior Heaven effort) scorches the essence and blood of the whole body. You must recognize this fire: the fire phasing has “civil” (slow, light), “martial” (quick, heavy), “ablution” (stopping the fire to rest) and “warm nourishing” (keeping it with a low fire). Though the fire phasing has all these differences, every one of them is the Prior Heaven fire of the spirit; only it must rely on the turning of the Posterior Heaven ordinary breath, going out and coming in, alternating back and forth, before the elixir can be formed. So it is said: “In regulating the breath, regulate the breath of the true breath; in refining the spirit, refine the spirit that is not spirit.” (What is regulated in regulating the breath is the breath of the true breath; what is refined in refining the spirit is “the spirit that is not spirit”.) The breathing that has no sense of breathing is the True Breath; the spirit that does not deliberately use spirit is the true spirit that presides.

§2
Original (Chinese)

学者调息凝神之际，务要寻得真息，认得真神，斯可浑合为一。否则，有形之息，皆凡火也。真火生神，凡火伤身。真神可作主张，凡神骚扰不宁。何谓真息？即丹田中悠悠扬扬，旋转不已者是。何谓真神？即无思无虑之中，忽焉而有知觉，此为真神。修炼家欲采元气以化凡精，欲升真铅以制阴汞，使之返还乾性，仍成不思不虑之元神，非采先天元息不能。夫元息在丹田，若有若无，不寒不暖，如火种者然，外不见有焰，内不知有火，只觉暖气融融，熏蒸在抱。斯无形之神火，自然变化无穷，神妙莫测。否则有形之火，气势炎炎，未有不忽焉而起，忽焉而灭，其为身心性命之害，不可胜言。

When the student regulates the breath and concentrates the spirit, he must find the true breath and recognize the true spirit; only then can spirit and breath be merged into one whole. Otherwise, breathing that has form and image is all ordinary fire. True fire can give birth to and nourish the Original Spirit, while ordinary fire harms the body; the true spirit can take charge, while the ordinary spirit (deluded thoughts) only harasses and disturbs. What is the true breath? It is the one in the elixir field that drifts and turns without cease. What is the true spirit? It is an awareness that suddenly arises in the midst of no thought and no worry — this is the true spirit. Alchemists who want to gather the original breath to transform the ordinary essence, to raise true lead to subdue yin mercury (true lead is the Prior Heaven true yang breath, yin mercury the restless, wavering mind-spirit), making it return to its nature of pure Qian (pure yang) and become once more the Original Spirit that neither thinks nor worries, cannot do it without gathering the Prior Heaven original breathing. The original breathing in the elixir field seems present and absent, neither cold nor hot, like a banked fire buried in the ashes of a stove: no flame is seen outside, and no fire is perceived within; one only feels a warm, melting warmth, as if steamed and embraced by warm breath. This formless fire of the spirit naturally transforms without end, wondrous and unfathomable. Conversely, fire that has form blazes fiercely; none of it fails to flare up suddenly and die out suddenly, and the harm it does to body, mind, nature and life is beyond telling.

§3
Original (Chinese)

修行人以无形之真火为用，而外面呼吸有形之火，非谓全法不用，不过如铁匠之风扇吹嘘在外，周围包裹，以卫中间神息而已。吾恐诸子未明用火之道，故将呼吸有形之凡火，与先天无形之真火，相提并论，庶以免妄采妄炼。然外边呼吸凡火，与丹田中悠扬活泼神火，未必划然二物，犹烛照之火，乃后天之火，丹田外之呼吸是也。烛未燃时，油中亦自有火，此即先天之神火，未经燃点者。采此神火，可以千万年不朽。若采凡火，顷刻而即消灭，此可观其微矣！愿诸子闲时打坐，用此有形之火，祛逐一身风寒暑湿；复用此无形之火，煅炼此身之渣滓阴霾，而金丹可成矣！

The practitioner takes the formless true fire as the main thing to use; the fire with form of the outer breathing is not entirely unused, only it is like a blacksmith’s bellows, blowing from outside and wrapping around, to protect the breath of the spirit in the middle. I fear you do not understand the principle of using fire, so I speak of the ordinary fire with form of breathing together with the formless true fire of Prior Heaven, hoping you will not gather and refine at random. Still, the ordinary fire of the outer breathing and the free, lively fire of the spirit in the elixir field are not necessarily two entirely separate things: the flame of a candle lit for light is Posterior Heaven fire, corresponding to the breathing outside the elixir field; before the candle is lit, the wax already has fire in it (the nature to burn) — this is the Prior Heaven fire of the spirit, fire not yet kindled. Gather this fire of the spirit, and it can last ten million years without decay; gather the ordinary fire, and it goes out in a moment — from this one can see the subtlety! I hope that when you sit in your free time, you will first use this fire with form (well-regulated breathing) to drive out the wind, cold, summer heat and damp of the whole body, and then use this formless fire (the fire of the spirit in the elixir field) to refine away the dregs and the yin gloom in the body, and the Golden Elixir can be refined!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

调息实即调节火候。调息之义在于寻得真息。学者行功时外呼吸之逐渐收敛而至于无，乃出于自然而然，不应强加按压。若强行按压，反窒其气息，乱其气机，欲求真息而不可得。真息之来，决定于虚极静笃，心息相依，有如水到渠成。

Regulating the breath is in fact regulating the fire phasing. The point of regulating the breath lies in finding the true breath. When the student practices, the outer breathing is gradually drawn in until it is gone; this comes about naturally, and must not be forced down. If it is forced down, it will instead choke the breath and disturb the breath’s dynamism, and the true breath one seeks will not be found. The coming of the true breath depends on utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, mind and breath relying on each other, as naturally as a channel forms where water flows.

§4
Original (Chinese)

近时修养一事，坐下存神入听，务将万缘放下，然后垂帘塞兑，回光返照于玄关一窍之中。始而神或不凝，息或有粗，不妨以数息之武火，微微的一其志，定其神，如是片晌，神凝息定，然后将心神放开，不死死观照虚无一窍，惟存心于听息。此个听字，大有法机。《庄子》云：“一其志，无听之以耳而听之以心，无听之以心，而听之以气。”要知此气，不是口鼻之气，不是肾间动气，更不是心中灵气。此气乃空中虚无元气，生天生地，生人生物者此也。惟能存心于虚无一气，此心此神，即与太和元气相往还，所谓神气合一，烹炼而成丹也。

On the matter of cultivation and nourishment that we have recently discussed: sit down, gather the spirit and listen quietly; you must first put down all ties, then lower the curtains of the eyes, close the mouth (“block the opening”), and revert the light to shine within the single opening of the Mysterious Pass. At first the spirit may not concentrate and the breath may still be coarse; you may first use the “martial fire” of counting the breaths to gently unify the will and settle the spirit; after a little while like this, once the spirit is concentrated and the breath settled, then let the mind and spirit open out, no longer contemplating that single opening of emptiness rigidly, but simply keeping the mind on listening to the breath. This word “listening” holds a great method and a great mystery. Zhuangzi says: “Unify your will: do not listen with the ear, but listen with the mind; do not listen with the mind, but listen with the breath.” You must know that this “breath” is not the breath of breathing through mouth and nose, not the moving breath between the two kidneys, still less the numinous breath in the heart. This breath is the empty original breath that fills space; it is what gives birth to Heaven and Earth, to people and things. If only one can keep the mind on this one breath of emptiness, this mind and this spirit communicate back and forth with the harmonious original breath between Heaven and Earth (the original breath of supreme harmony) — this is what is called spirit and breath uniting into one, cooked and refined into the elixir.

§5
Original (Chinese)

若著凡息，还不是神与凡息相交，又何以成丹哉？经云：“不神之神，真神也。无息之息，真息也。”我须于混沌中落出先天一点意，以之翕聚元气，是元神与元气相交，而大道可成。苟有粗息，我即轻轻微微将此凡息收敛至静，到凡息已停，不问他元气动否，而元气自在个中矣！我当凝神以正，抱意以听，此亦阴阳交媾之一端也。况乎下手之时，口鼻眼目之窍皆能固闭，独有这个耳窍尚未尽闭，我一心以听，即耳窍常闭，而众窍无音矣！此个听法，乃第一修炼良法。如此久听，自然真阳自生，而玄牝现象矣！

If one clings to the ordinary breath (breathing through mouth and nose), is that not just spirit meeting ordinary breath? How could that form the elixir? A scripture says: “The spirit that is not spirit is the true spirit; the breath that is no breath is the true breath.” (The spirit that does not deliberately use spirit is the true spirit; the breathing without any sense of breathing is the true breath.) I must, in the midst of chaos, bring forth a point of Prior Heaven true intention and use it to gather and assemble the original breath — this is the Original Spirit meeting the original breath, and the great Way can be accomplished. If there is coarse, heavy breathing, I gently, lightly draw this ordinary breath in to stillness; once the ordinary breath has stopped, never mind whether the original breath has stirred or not — the original breath is naturally there within! I need only keep the spirit concentrated and upright, hold the intention and listen; this too is one aspect of the union of yin and yang. Moreover, when beginning, the mouth, nose and eyes can all be closed; only the opening of the ears cannot be closed; if I listen with a single mind, the ears instead stay closed (no longer chasing outer sounds), and all the other openings fall silent too! This way of listening is the finest method of cultivation. Listening like this for a long time, true yang will naturally arise of itself, and the gate of the Mysterious Female will appear!

§6
Original (Chinese)

学人欲归根复命，惟将此心放下，轻轻微微以听气息之往来。若气太粗浮，则神亦耗散，而不得返还本窍，为我身之主宰。若听其气息似有似无，则凡息将停，胎息将现，而本心亦可得见矣。古人谓心易走作，以气纯之是矣！苟不知听息以收心习气，则神难凝，息难调，而心息亦终难相依。此听息一法，正凝神调息之妙诀也。

If the student wants to return to the root and restore life (return to the root of life and recover one’s original nature and life), he need only put this mind down and listen gently, lightly to the breath coming and going. If the breathing is too coarse and too floating, the spirit is dissipated along with it, and cannot return to its original opening to be the master of this body of mine. If one hears the breath seeming present and absent, then the ordinary breath is about to stop, embryonic breathing is about to appear, and the original mind (the originally pure mind) can be seen. The ancients said: the mind easily runs off, and must be made one by means of the breath — how true! If one does not know how to use listening to the breath to gather the mind and train the breath, the spirit will be hard to concentrate, the breath hard to regulate, and mind and breath will in the end find it hard to rely on and keep to each other. This one method of listening to the breath is precisely the wondrous formula for concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath.

§7
Original (Chinese)

果能以神入气，炼息归神，则清气自升，浊气自降，而一身天地自然清宁。得到天清地宁之候，暂见清空一气，日回环于一身上下内外之间，而非第胎息发现已也。尤要知此个胎息，非等寻常，是父母未生前一点元气，父母既生后一段真灵，性得之而有体，心得之而有用，在天为枢，在地为轴，在人为归根复命之原。人欲希贤希圣希天，舍此胎息，无以为造作之地也。

If one can truly send the spirit into the breath and refine the breathing back into the spirit, then the clear breath naturally rises and the turbid breath naturally sinks, and the “Heaven and Earth” within the body (Heaven above, Earth below) are naturally clear and at peace. When Heaven is clear and Earth at peace, one will for a time see a clear, empty breath circulating through the whole body, above and below, inside and out — and this is not merely the appearance of embryonic breathing. Above all, you must know that this embryonic breathing is no ordinary thing: it is the point of original breath from before my parents gave me birth, the stretch of true numinosity hidden in the body after my parents gave me birth; the nature has its substance by obtaining it, the mind has its function by obtaining it; in Heaven it is the pivot of revolution, on Earth the axis of turning, and in the human being the source of returning to the root and restoring life. If people want to aspire to worthiness, to sagehood, to Heaven (to emulate the worthies and the sages, even to become one with Heaven), apart from this embryonic breathing there is no foundation on which to put in effort and achieve anything.

§8
Original (Chinese)

诸子近来用功，惟将心神了照不内不外之际，虚心以听气息之往来，庶几神依息而立，气得神而融，未生前一团胎息，可得而识矣！由是言之，此个胎息，诚修炼之要务也，夫岂易得者哉？古云：“入定功夫在止观。”何以止？止于脐下丹田。何以观？观于虚无法象。如此则心神自定，慧光日生，以之常常了照于不睹不闻、无声无臭之地，而胎息常在个中矣！

In your recent work, simply let the mind and spirit shine on the place neither inside nor outside, and listen with an empty mind to the breath coming and going; perhaps you will then reach the point where the spirit rests by relying on the breath, and the breath melts by obtaining the spirit, and that mass of embryonic breath from before birth can be recognized! From this it follows that this embryonic breathing is truly the most important matter in cultivation — how could it be obtained easily? The ancients said: “The work of entering concentration lies in cessation and contemplation.” How does one “cease”? Let the mind rest below the navel in the elixir field. How does one “contemplate”? Contemplate the images that appear in emptiness. In this way the mind and spirit become naturally settled, and the light of wisdom grows day by day; use this light of wisdom constantly to look on that place unseen and unheard, without sound or scent, and embryonic breathing will always be there within it!

§9
Original (Chinese)

若但粗定其息，未入大定，此谓胎息自动，则失之远矣！人到胎息真动，一身苏软如绵，美快无比，真息冲融，流行于一身上下，油然而上腾，勃然而下降，其气息熏蒸，有如春暖天气，熟睡方醒，其四肢之快畅真有难以名言者。到此地位，清气上升于泥丸宫内，恍觉一股清灵之气直冲玄窍，耳目口鼻亦觉大放光明，回不同于凡时也。他如凡息初停，胎息亦不无动机，总不若此大定大静之为自得耳。吾昨教栖神泥丸，只须以一点神光默朝上宫，不可太为着意，着意则动后天浊气，犹天本清明，忽然阴云四塞，则清者不清矣！

If one has merely settled the breathing roughly, without entering great concentration, and calls this the spontaneous movement of embryonic breathing, one is far, far off the mark! When a person truly reaches the moment when embryonic breathing stirs, the whole body is soft as cotton, delightful beyond compare; the true breath is harmonious and melting, flowing through the whole body above and below, rising leisurely upward and descending abundantly downward; its steaming warmth is like a warm spring day, just waking from a deep sleep, the four limbs comfortable and at ease in a way hard to put into words. At this stage, the clear breath rises into the palace of the Muddy Pellet (in the brain at the crown), and one dimly feels a clear, numinous breath rushing straight into the mysterious opening, and the ears, eyes, mouth and nose also seem to shine with great light, quite unlike ordinary times. As for when the ordinary breath has just stopped, it is not that embryonic breathing has no movement at all, but it is never as natural and genuine as after such great concentration and great stillness. Yesterday I taught you to lodge the spirit in the Muddy Pellet (letting the spirit rest at the crown); you need only let a point of spirit-light face the upper palace silently, and must not use too much intention; as soon as intention is used, it disturbs the turbid Posterior Heaven breath, just as a sky that was clear is suddenly covered with dark clouds on every side, and what was clear is clear no longer!

Qiu Ling's noteon §4–§9
Original note (Chinese)

《庄子》第四篇《人世间》载有著名的庄子听息法，其文为：“颜回曰：‘敢问心斋。‘仲尼曰：‘若一志，无听之以耳而听之以心，无听之以心而听之以气！耳止于听，心止于符。气也者，虚而待物者也。唯道集虚。虚者，心斋也。'”黄元吉氏所述听息法，即据《庄子》此文。

又止观法门，为佛家功夫。

历代注家对《庄子》所载听息法“无听之以心而听之以气”的“气”字，多解释为心的某种状态，如空明灵觉境界的心等，惟黄元吉氏则认为此“气”乃空中虚无元气，“听之以气”即存心于虚无元气以听之，则能神气合一，促进阴阳交媾。

至于止观法门的运用，黄氏亦有新意。

The fourth chapter of Zhuangzi, “In the World of Men”, records Zhuangzi’s famous method of listening to the breath. The original text is: “Yan Hui said: ‘May I ask what the fasting of the mind is?' Confucius said: ‘Unify your will: do not listen with the ear, but listen with the mind; do not listen with the mind, but listen with the breath! The ear stops at hearing; the mind stops at matching things. The breath is empty and waits upon all things. The Way gathers only in emptiness. This emptiness is the fasting of the mind.'” The method of listening to the breath Huang Yuanji teaches is based on this passage of Zhuangzi.

Also, the method of cessation and contemplation is a Buddhist practice.

Commentators through the ages have mostly explained the word “breath” in Zhuangzi‘s method of listening to the breath — “do not listen with the mind, but listen with the breath” — as some state of the mind, such as a mind in a state of empty, clear, numinous awareness; only Huang Yuanji holds that this “breath” is the empty original breath in space, so that “listening with the breath” means keeping the mind on the empty original breath and listening to it; in this way spirit and breath can unite, promoting the union of yin and yang.

In applying the method of cessation and contemplation, too, Huang has new ideas.

Key Terms

  • Regulating the breath: regulating the outer breathing; Qiu Ling’s note: regulating the breath is in fact regulating the fire phasing.
  • Civil fire, martial fire: the slow and quick in fire phasing; see fire phasing. Counting the breaths belongs to martial fire.
  • The breath of the true breath, the spirit that is not spirit: “In regulating the breath, regulate the breath of the true breath; in refining the spirit, refine the spirit that is not spirit” — that is, the breath that is no breath, the spirit that is not spirit.
  • The presiding spirit: the spirit that takes charge, that is, the true spirit.
  • Ordinary fire, true fire: the fire of breathing with form, and the formless fire of the spirit in the elixir field; see Samādhi True Fire.
  • Banked fire: the original breathing in the elixir field, no flame seen outside, no fire known within, only a warm, melting feeling.
  • Fengshan: a bellows. The outer breathing is like a bellows, protecting the breath of the spirit in the middle from outside.
  • Lowering the curtains and blocking the opening: the eyes with lowered lids, the mouth closed. Dui refers to the mouth, from the Daode jing‘s “block the opening”.
  • Counting the breaths: counting the breaths to gather the mind and spirit; Huang Yuanji uses it only as “martial fire” for a while at the start.
  • Listening to the breath: keeping the mind on the empty original breath and listening to the breath coming and going; see regulating the breath.
  • The moving breath between the kidneys: the moving breath between the two kidneys; Huang Yuanji says the “breath” of listening to the breath is not this.
  • The original breath of supreme harmony: the harmonious original breath between Heaven and Earth.
  • Holding the intention and listening: guarding it with the intention, listening with the mind.
  • Running off (zouzuo): the mind running off, scattering.
  • Cessation and contemplation: a Buddhist practice; Huang Yuanji’s use is to let the mind rest below the navel in the elixir field and contemplate the images in emptiness.
  • Light of wisdom: the light of wisdom born in concentration; see light of Nature / light of cognition.
  • Lodging the spirit in the Muddy Pellet: letting a point of spirit-light silently face the upper palace, without deliberate intention.
  • Fasting of the mind: from Zhuangzi, “In the World of Men”: listening with the breath, being empty and waiting upon things.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Regulating the outer breathing: making it even is enough. Whether coarse or fine, add only a slight intention of drawing in; never force it down. Qiu Ling says: forcing it chokes the breath and disturbs its dynamism, and the true breath is then not found.
  2. The procedure for beginning: put down all ties → lower the curtains, close the mouth, turn the light back within → if the spirit is not concentrated and the breath is coarse, count the breaths for a while → once the spirit is concentrated and the breath settled, open out the mind and spirit, do not guard one opening rigidly, and switch to listening to the breath.
  3. What to listen to: not catching the sound of breathing with the ears, but resting the mind lightly on the one breath of emptiness, listening gently, lightly to the breath coming and going. When it seems present and absent, the ordinary breath is about to stop and embryonic breathing about to appear.
  4. Do not chase the original breath: once the ordinary breath has stopped, “never mind whether the original breath has stirred” — the original breath is there within.
  5. Where cessation and contemplation land: rest below the navel in the elixir field, contemplate the images in emptiness — not staring rigidly at one point.
  6. Tell true from false: breathing that has merely settled roughly is not embryonic breathing; when embryonic breathing truly stirs, the whole body is soft as cotton, like a warm spring day, like just waking from a deep sleep.
  7. Safety note: “lodging the spirit in the Muddy Pellet” is only letting a point of spirit-light silently face the upper palace, and Huang Yuanji himself says not to use too much intention. In line with Qiu Ling’s note in Chapter One, people with yin deficiency and yang excess or high blood pressure should not fix the mind on the head; if a swollen feeling in the head, headache, a flushed face or palpitations appear during quiet sitting, stop and simply relax naturally instead; consult a doctor if necessary.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in two paragraphs; the original book does not give the source, and each paragraph is followed by a note by Qiu Ling. The first paragraph is divided into three short passages; the second, which has two natural paragraphs in the original book, we have divided into six.
  2. “So that the ordinary fire with form scorches the essence and blood of the whole body — that is acceptable” (致令有形凡火烧灼一身精血可也): so in the original book; the final 可也 (“that is acceptable”) contradicts the sense, and a 不 seems to be missing (不可也, “that is not acceptable”); the translation follows the sense.
  3. “This is the presiding spirit” (斯为主神): it pairs with “this is the true breath” in the preceding sentence; the “presiding spirit” is the spirit that takes charge, the “true spirit” mentioned below.
  4. Fengshan 风扇: a bellows, used in former times by blacksmiths to blow on the fire.
  5. “I must, in the midst of chaos, bring forth a point of Prior Heaven intention” (我须于混沌中落出先天一点意): so in the original book; it means a point of Prior Heaven true intention.
  6. “Hold the intention and listen” (抱意以听): so in the original book, recorded as is.
  7. “One will for a time see a clear, empty breath, daily circulating…” (暂见清空一气，日回环于一身上下内外之间): 日 (“daily”) is probably a mistake for 自 (“of itself”); the translation does not render 日.
  8. “And this is not merely the appearance of embryonic breathing” (而非第胎息发现已也): di 第 means “merely”; yi 已 is the same as eryi 而已, “and that is all”.
  9. “Quite unlike ordinary times” (回不同于凡时也): 回 stands for 迥, “utterly”.
  10. Huang Yuanji quotes Zhuangzi as “unify your will” (一其志), while Zhuangzi reads 若一志; Qiu Ling’s note quotes the original. Qiu Ling’s note gives the chapter title as Ren shijian 人世间, while the title in Zhuangzi is Renjian shi 人间世, “In the World of Men”; the translation uses the correct title.
  11. “The work of entering concentration lies in cessation and contemplation”: the original only says “the ancients said”, without a source.

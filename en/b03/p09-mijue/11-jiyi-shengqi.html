Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 集义生气，虚无生阳

Accumulating Righteousness Gives Rise to Breath, Emptiness Gives Rise to Yang

Accumulating righteousness gives rise to breath, emptiness gives rise to yang — the lead of the ground of nature is born of accumulating righteousness; daily conduct, reading and friendship, music, chess, calligraphy and painting all have moments of yang arising; as soon as it stirs, be aware; as soon as you are aware, gather it in

About This Chapter

The yang arising spoken of earlier was mostly a matter of quiet sitting. This section says: yang arises not only in quiet sitting, but everywhere in daily life.

  1. Two ways of accumulating lead: practicing in stillness, the yang of life arises — that is one way of accumulating lead (accumulating Prior Heaven true yang); the other is “the lead of the ground of nature”, that is, the vast, flowing breath born of accumulating righteousness of which Mencius spoke.
  2. There are many occasions for accumulating righteousness: gaining something from reading the Odes and discussing the rites, being moved by talk of past and present, coming to an insight in meeting friends, seeing something while roaming mountains and rivers, and all kinds of righteous deeds — showing humaneness, practicing courtesy, relieving the poor, helping those in danger — a chance stirring, and suddenly one awakens into the Great Vehicle. At that moment, see it clearly at a glance, hold it firmly in hand, illuminate it in close-knit stillness day and night — that is true awakening, a lasting gain.
  3. Mencius’s “four sprouts”: when the heart of compassion or the heart of shame arises, let it flow out following the original heart, and keep it constantly brewing, not letting it arise and vanish in an instant — that is extending it, the real practice of accumulating righteousness. Careful day and night, doing nothing to be ashamed of, the heart is naturally glad and at ease — this is “utmost in greatness, utmost in strength, filling heaven and earth”: accumulating lead to its utmost.
  4. Moments of yang arising: chaste women and steadfast wives, loyal ministers and heroic men who will not bend through a hundred setbacks — that is real yang arising; in daily life, fulfilling filial and brotherly duty, relieving the poor and the widowed, doing it just right and feeling glad and uplifted; while reading, selfish desires suddenly gone and one spiritual brightness alone remaining; talking with a friend in perfect accord, yang breath suddenly soaring; finding contentment at leisure in music, chess, calligraphy and painting, in fishing, woodcutting, farming and reading — all are signs of yang arising.
  5. Two faults: suddenly seeing it, and then being blocked by one’s habits; or, once knowing it, strutting about with pleasure written all over one’s face, not knowing to hold back, and so it scatters. Always: as soon as it stirs, be aware; as soon as you are aware, gather it in.

Qiu Ling’s note: a stern sense of justice, noble conduct, refined feeling, single-minded faith, when called forth by events, can also give rise to yang and to breath; gathering it in and nurturing it at the moment this mechanism happens to show itself, not letting it arise and vanish in an instant, is also a method of nourishing life.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

命阳发生，静里修持之事，此积铅之一法也。若性地之铅，即孟子所谓浩然之气由集义而生者是。夫义之所在不止一端，或于敦诗说礼而有得，或于谈今论史而有感，或于朋友相会而有所悟，或于观山玩水而有所见，更有型仁讲让，济困扶危种种义举，偶然感附忽地悟入大乘。此等积义犹为真真踏实行持，人能于机关偶露之际，实实认得为我家本来故物，一眼觑定，一手握定，日夜用绵密寂照之功。如此之悟是为真悟，如此所得是为永得，此为集义妙法。

The arising of the yang of life is a matter of practicing in stillness; this is one method of accumulating lead (accumulating Prior Heaven true yang). As for “the lead of the ground of nature” (true yang accumulated in the mind and nature), it is the “vast, flowing breath” of which Mencius spoke, born of “accumulating righteousness” (accumulating conduct in keeping with the Way and righteousness). Righteousness is found in more than one place: one may gain something in studying the Odes and the Documents and discussing rites and laws; be moved in talking of the present and discussing history; come to an insight in meeting friends; see something in roaming mountains and rivers; and there are all kinds of righteous deeds — setting an example of humaneness, practicing courtesy, relieving the poor, helping those in danger — a chance stirring, and suddenly one awakens into the Great Vehicle. Accumulating righteousness in this way is all the more a truly real, feet-on-the-ground practice. If, at the moment this mechanism happens to show itself, one can truly recognize it as the old possession of one’s own house, see it clearly at a glance, hold it firmly in hand, and apply the close-knit work of still illumination day and night — such awakening is true awakening, and such gain is lasting gain. This is the wondrous method of accumulating righteousness.

§2
Original (Chinese)

孟子云恻隐之心仁之端，羞恶之心义之端等语，我能如心而出，平情而施，且随时随处将所发情景，常常酝酿，不使随来随去，旋灭旋生，即是扩充，集义之真实行持也。自是日夜谨慎，不稍使此心有不仁不义之处，以负惭于幽独，抱憾于神明，则我心无不快畅，我志自然圆满，即孟子所谓直养无害，至大至刚塞乎天地之间是，是即积铅积到极处也。

Mencius said: “The heart of compassion is the sprout of humaneness; the heart of shame is the sprout of righteousness.” If I can let it flow out following my original heart, carry it out calmly, and at every time and place keep brewing the feeling that has come forth, not letting it come and go, vanish and arise again — this is “extending it”, the real practice of accumulating righteousness. From then on, careful day and night, not letting this heart have the slightest place of inhumanity or unrighteousness, so as to feel ashamed when alone or harbor regret before the spirits — then my heart is glad and at ease everywhere, and my will is naturally whole: this is what Mencius called “nourished with uprightness and unharmed, utmost in greatness, utmost in strength, filling the space between heaven and earth”. This is accumulating lead to its utmost.

§3
Original (Chinese)

诸子谈及阳生之道已非一端，总不外无思无虑而来。即如贞女烈妇矢志靡他，一旦偶遇不良，宁舍生而取义；又如忠臣烈士惟义是从，设有祸起非常，愿捐躯以殉难，此真正阳生也。不然何以百折不回若是耶！由是推之，举凡日用常行，或尽伦常孝友，或矜寡困穷一切善事义举做到恰好至常，不无欢欣鼓午之情，此皆阳生之候。只怕自家忽焉见得，忽焉又为气阻也。又怕自家知道，因而趾高气扬，喜发于言，形动于色，洋洋诩诩不知自收自敛，视有如无，因被气习牵引而散矣！

You have spoken of more than one way of yang arising, but none goes beyond coming from no-thought and no-worry. For example, chaste women and steadfast wives whose resolve does not waver will, once faced with something improper, rather give up life to keep righteousness; or loyal ministers and heroic men who obey only righteousness will, once a great calamity comes, willingly lay down their lives — this is real yang arising; otherwise how could they not bend through a hundred setbacks! Extending from this: in all daily conduct, whether fulfilling the ways of filial piety and brotherly love, pitying orphans and widows, or relieving the poor and distressed, when every good and righteous deed is done just right, there cannot but be a feeling of gladness and uplift in the heart — these are all moments of yang arising. The only fear is that one suddenly sees it and is suddenly blocked again by one’s habits; or that, once knowing it, one struts about, pleasure spilling out in one’s words and showing on one’s face, pleased with oneself, not knowing to hold back, unable to “regard having as not having”, and so it is drawn off by one’s habits and scatters!

§4
Original (Chinese)

又或读书诵诗，忽焉私欲尽去，一灵独存，此亦阳生之一端也。又或朋友聚谈，相契天怀，忽然阳气飞腾，真机勃发，此亦阳生之一道也。更有琴棋书画，渔樵耕读，果能顺其自然，本乎天性无所求亦无所欲，未有不优游自得消遣忘情者，此皆阳生之象也。总要一动即觉，一觉即收，庶几神无外慕，气有余妍，而丹药不难于生长，胎婴何愁不壮旺。果能持守不失，神常返于穴中，气时归于炉内，久久真阳自发生矣。

Or, while reading books and reciting poems, selfish desires suddenly melt away entirely and only a point of spiritual brightness remains alone — this too is a kind of yang arising. Or, when friends gather and talk, hearts in accord, yang breath suddenly soars and the true dynamic bursts forth — this too is a kind of yang arising. And then there are music, chess, calligraphy and painting, fishing, woodcutting, farming and reading: if one can truly follow their nature, rooted in one’s heavenly nature, seeking nothing and wanting nothing, there is no one who does not find contentment at leisure, absorbed and forgetting oneself — all these are signs of yang arising. Always: as soon as it stirs, be aware; as soon as you are aware, gather it back; then the spirit will not long for outer things, the breath will have abundance and goodness, the elixir-medicine is not hard to grow, and why worry that the embryo-infant will not be strong? If one can truly hold to it without losing it, the spirit constantly returning to its cavity and the breath always returning to the furnace, in time true yang naturally arises.

§5
Original (Chinese)

诸子从此悟得，方知阳即道，道即虚无自然。子思子谓道也者不可须臾离也，其即此收敛阳光不许一毫渗漏之说欤？诸子卓有见地，吾故以铺天迎地亘古历今之真正之阳无时无处而不有者示之。

Awakening from here, you will know: yang is the Way, and the Way is emptiness and naturalness. Master Zisi said, “The Way cannot be departed from for an instant” — is this not speaking of the principle of gathering in the yang light and not allowing the slightest leak? You have real insight, so I point out to you this true yang that covers heaven and earth, spans ancient and present, and is present at every time and in every place.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

虽言虚无能生阳生气，但人若有凛然之正义感、高尚之品行、雅致之情怀、专一之信仰，遇事而发，亦能生阳生气。阳即真阳，气即真气。我当于此机偶露之际，收之敛之，知所保养，使此气此阳不致旋生旋灭，向外渗漏，亦是学者养生之法。

Although it is said that emptiness can give rise to yang and to breath, if a person has a stern sense of justice, noble conduct, refined feeling and single-minded faith, these, when called forth by events, can also give rise to yang and to breath. The yang is true yang, and the breath is true breath-energy. I should, at the moment this mechanism happens to show itself, gather it in and know how to nurture it, so that this breath and this yang do not arise and vanish in an instant and leak outward — this too is a method of nourishing life for students.

Key Terms

  • Accumulating lead: accumulating the breath of Prior Heaven true yang; see Lead and Mercury.
  • Lead of the ground of nature: true yang accumulated in the mind and nature.
  • Vast, flowing breath; accumulating righteousness: Mencius, “Gongsun Chou I” — the vast, flowing breath is born of accumulating conduct in keeping with the Way and righteousness.
  • Setting an example of humaneness, practicing courtesy (型仁讲让): showing humaneness and valuing courtesy.
  • The four sprouts: the hearts of compassion, shame, deference, and right and wrong; Mencius, “Gongsun Chou I”.
  • Feeling ashamed when alone (负惭于幽独): feeling guilty at heart when alone.
  • Gladness and uplift (欢欣鼓舞): the original book writes 鼓午, that is, 鼓舞.
  • Regarding having as not having: having it yet as if not having it, not making a show of it.
  • Embryo-infant: the Holy Embryo; see Holy Embryo.
  • Master Zisi: Confucius’s grandson, traditionally the author of the Doctrine of the Mean.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “Yang arising” is everywhere in life: the warmth in your heart after helping someone, a sudden clarity of mind while reading, a lift of spirits in a good conversation with a friend, forgetting the time while doing something you love — all of these are it.
  2. As soon as it stirs, be aware; as soon as you are aware, gather it in: when that warmth or that clarity comes, notice it and gently gather it back into the heart and the elixir field, without making a show of it.
  3. Do not be pleased with yourself: if you talk about every good deed and good feeling and wear it on your face, the breath scatters.
  4. A clear conscience: do nothing to be ashamed of, and the heart is naturally open, glad and at ease — this in itself is nourishing the breath.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is three paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The first paragraph is divided into two short passages, and the second into two.
  2. “There cannot but be a feeling of gladness and uplift” (不无欢欣鼓午之情): 鼓午 should be 鼓舞 (“uplift”); the translation follows 鼓舞.
  3. “Pitying orphans and widows, relieving the poor and distressed” (矜寡困穷): 矜 is interchangeable with 鳏 (“widower”); 鳏寡 refers to the lonely and helpless; here it is used as a verb, meaning to pity and relieve them.

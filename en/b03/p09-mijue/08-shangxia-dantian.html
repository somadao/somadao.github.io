Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 上下丹田配合

Combining the Upper and Lower Elixir Fields

Combining the upper and lower elixir fields — one must not attend only to the lower field; when necessary, look back to the upper field and sweep away the yin breath of the Muddy Pellet; gather it back into the palace, without fixing rigidly on a single point

About This Chapter

This section speaks of how the upper elixir field (the Muddy Pellet) and the lower elixir field work together:

  1. One must not attend only to the lower field. When the breath of the lower field is strong, it naturally rises to the Muddy Pellet; but if spirit and breath are still weak and have not reached round brightness, one may use the true mind to give rise to true intention and look back for a moment to the upper field; the yin breath of the Muddy Pellet, once illuminated by yang breath, naturally dissolves, and the head and eyes are no longer dizzy.
  2. Why: guarding only the lower field, the spirit-light finds it hard to rouse itself for a while, and one easily becomes torpid and distracted — because true yang does not rise and true yin does not descend.
  3. A metaphor: yang suddenly arising in the middle of the night is like a point of yang breath arising deep underground in the cold of winter; if it is not lifted, in the blink of an eye one falls back asleep. In ordinary guarding of the center too, if spirit and breath sink to the bottom of the sea and the head and eyes are dizzy, it does no harm to lift them up.
  4. But it is not a matter of letting spirit and breath rise and scatter outward: spirit and breath should circulate through the whole body, making the body of flesh and blood lively; but one must not cling to the melting, soft delight of the whole body and drift away without returning — one must still gather it back into the palace; and in gathering it back, one must not fix rigidly on a single point as the Yellow Court — gathered to the utmost, it is one body with the great void.

Qiu Ling’s note is important: - The combination of the upper and lower elixir fields bears on the dual cultivation of nature and life and on the balance of yin and yang; using only one of them is one-sided learning. - People with yin deficiency and yang excess, or with high blood pressure, should not illuminate the upper elixir field (the Muddy Pellet or the space between the eyes) too much, or their blood pressure will rise; people with yang deficiency and yin excess, or with low blood pressure, should illuminate the upper elixir field more. - Most methods popular today attend only to the lower field, which can also strengthen the lower origin and is especially suited to the elderly; if one can, when necessary, look back to the upper field and avoid torpor, the benefit is greater.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

诸子近时用功，不可专顾下田。虽下田气壮自能升至泥丸，消铄上田渣滓，若神气犹懦未至圆明，须久久顾諟，不妨以真心发真意，回顾上田，则泥丸阴气被阳气一照，自然悉化而头目不至昏晕也。故古人谓顶上圆光者此也。又观绘画之工塑一泥木神像必画一圆光于上者，就是此神光也。所谓毫光照彻世界照开地狱者，就是此元神之光。

In your recent practice, you must not attend only to the lower elixir field. Although once the breath of the lower elixir field is strong it can naturally rise to the Muddy Pellet and dissolve the dregs of the upper elixir field, if spirit and breath are still weak and have not reached round fullness and brightness, you must watch over it attentively for a long time (gushi) — it does no harm to use the true mind to give rise to true intention and turn back to look after the upper elixir field for a moment; then the yin breath in the Muddy Pellet, once illuminated by yang breath, naturally dissolves completely, and the head and eyes will not be dizzy. The “round light above the crown” of which the ancients spoke is this. Look too at how painters and makers of clay and wooden images of the gods always paint a circle of round light around the head — that is this spirit-light. What is called “the fine light shining through the world and opening up the hells” is this light of the original spirit.

§2
Original (Chinese)

若单守于下田，则神光一时不能自整，未免多昏沉散乱。其昏沉散乱者即真阳不升，真阴不下降之故。今欲升降得宜，不可过急亦不可太缓，比如半夜忽然阳生，此是一派寒冬忽有阳气生于地下深深之处，若不提摄阳气，转眼之间又昏睡不知矣！尔等此时起立，即依吾前法修持，尤要知稍用意思将神气摄之至上，庶几天清地朗，霎时间即三阳开泰乐不可及矣！不但此也，平日守中若神气沉于海底，头目昏晕，亦不妨提摄而上。

If one guards only the lower elixir field, the spirit-light cannot rouse itself for a while, and there will inevitably be much torpor and distraction. Torpor and distraction come about because true yang does not rise and true yin does not descend. Now, to make the rising and descending proper, one must be neither too hasty nor too slow. For example, when yang suddenly arises in the middle of the night (the Living Zi Hour), it is like a point of yang breath suddenly arising deep underground in the midst of the cold of winter; if one does not lift the yang breath up, in the blink of an eye one falls back asleep and knows nothing at all! At this time you must “get up” (rouse your spirits) and practice according to the method I taught you earlier; above all, know this: use a little intention to draw spirit and breath upward; only then are heaven clear and earth bright, and in an instant it is like “three yang bring prosperity” (spring returning to the earth), joyful beyond compare! Not only that, but in ordinary times when guarding the center, if spirit and breath sink to the bottom of the sea (deep in the lower elixir field) and the head and eyes are dizzy, it does no harm to lift them up.

§3
Original (Chinese)

夫玄功别无妙法，只在升降上下往来运变而已，亦非教诸子专用神气升散于外而不收敛也。夫以神气不运于周身，则周身阴气不化，无非死肉一团，终是无用，且日积一日，不免疾病纠缠，故吾教修命，是教人以水火周身运动，使血肉之躯化为活活泼泼，随心所用无有阻碍。到得一身毛窍晶莹肌肤细腻得矣！又不可贪神气之周于一身，苏软快乐流荡忘返，还要收之回宫不准外泄。

The mysterious work has no other wondrous method; it lies only in the turning and transformation of rising and descending, up and down, coming and going; but it is not teaching you to let spirit and breath only rise and scatter outward without gathering them in. If spirit and breath do not move through the whole body, the yin breath of the whole body cannot be dissolved; it is nothing but a mass of dead flesh, of no use in the end, and as days and months accumulate, illness will inevitably cling to it. So when I teach people to cultivate life, I teach them to use water and fire (spirit and breath) moving through the whole body, making the body of flesh and blood lively and alive, usable at will, without obstruction; when all the pores of the body are crystal clear and the skin is fine and smooth, you have it! But you must not cling to the melting, soft delight of spirit and breath circulating through the whole body and drift away without returning; you must still gather it back into the palace, not letting it leak out.

§4
Original (Chinese)

却不要死死执着一个穴道认为黄庭，须知收之至于极处，无非与太虚同体，浑不知其所在。时而动也亦与电光同用，一动即觉，一觉即灭，前无所来，后无所去，仍一杳冥光景，还于无极焉耳。功夫至此，身外有身。若未到此，不过有相之灵神。

But neither must you fix rigidly on a single point and decide that it is the Yellow Court. Know that gathered to the utmost, it is nothing other than one body with the great void, so seamless that you do not know where it is. Sometimes it stirs, and that too is like lightning: as soon as it stirs one is aware, as soon as one is aware it vanishes, with no place it came from before and no place it goes to after — still one expanse of obscure darkness, simply returning to the Limitless. When the practice reaches this point, there is “a body beyond the body”. If it has not reached this step, it is no more than a spiritual spirit that still has form.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

运行真气升降上下往来，须顾到上、下丹田的互相配合，此关系到是否性命双修和平衡人体阴阳。若专用下田而忽略上田，或专顾上田而不用下田，均是一偏之学，断难有所成就。古云：“性者天也，常潜于顶，故顶者性之根也；命者海也，常潜于脐，故脐者命之蒂也”。因此，性命双修者必因机而配合使用上、下丹田。人身上半属阳，下半属阴，为求阴阳平衡亦必须顾到上、下丹田的配合和调剂。

对患病者来说，更须注意上、下丹田的运用。阴虚阳亢者或高血压病患者则不宜过份观照上丹田（泥丸或两眼之间的玄关窍），否则，必招致阳更亢而阴更虚或血压上升。阳虚阴盛者或低血压患者则宜多观照上丹田，使真阳能上升而真阴下降。

今人流行功法多专顾下田，偏重于修炼命功，亦可强壮下元，尤为老年人或破体所宜。但若能如黄氏上文所言，必要时回顾上田，以扫除泥丸阴气，免致昏沉散乱，当得益更大。

In moving the true breath-energy, rising and descending, up and down, coming and going, one must take care that the upper and lower elixir fields work together; this bears on whether nature and life are cultivated together, and on whether yin and yang in the body are in balance. If one uses only the lower elixir field and neglects the upper, or attends only to the upper and does not use the lower, it is one-sided learning, and it is extremely hard to achieve anything. The ancients said: “Nature is heaven, always hidden at the crown, so the crown is the root of nature; life is the sea, always hidden at the navel, so the navel is the stem of life.” (Nature is like heaven, always hidden at the crown of the head, so the crown is the root of nature; life is like the sea, always hidden below the navel, so the navel is the stem of life.) Therefore those who cultivate nature and life together must combine the use of the upper and lower elixir fields according to the moment. The upper half of the body belongs to yang and the lower half to yin; to achieve a balance of yin and yang, one must also take care to combine and adjust the upper and lower elixir fields.

For people who are ill, the use of the upper and lower elixir fields calls for even more care. People with yin deficiency and yang excess, or with high blood pressure, should not illuminate the upper elixir field (the Muddy Pellet, or the opening of the Mysterious Pass between the eyes) too much, otherwise yang will inevitably become more excessive and yin more deficient, or blood pressure will rise. People with yang deficiency and yin excess, or with low blood pressure, should illuminate the upper elixir field more, so that true yang can rise and true yin can descend.

Most methods popular today attend only to the lower elixir field and lean toward cultivating life; they too can strengthen the lower origin, and are especially suited to the elderly or to those with a “broken body” (adults whose essence and breath are already depleted). But if, as Huang says above, one can look back to the upper elixir field when necessary, sweeping away the yin breath of the Muddy Pellet so as to avoid torpor and distraction, the benefit will be greater.

Key Terms

  • Upper field, lower field: the upper elixir field (the Muddy Pellet) and the lower elixir field.
  • Gushi (顾諟): watching over attentively.
  • Round light above the crown: the halo above the heads of images of the gods; a metaphor for the light of the original spirit.
  • Three yang bring prosperity: in the first month yang breath gradually flourishes; a metaphor for yang breath rising, clear and expansive.
  • Bottom of the sea: deep in the lower elixir field.
  • Spiritual spirit that still has form: a spiritual spirit still with form, not yet at the state of a body beyond the body.
  • Nature hidden at the crown, life hidden at the navel: the upper elixir field is the root of nature, the lower elixir field the stem of life; see inner nature / life.
  • Broken body: an adult whose essence and breath are already depleted.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Take the lower elixir field as the main thing. In ordinary practice, let the spirit-light shine down on the elixir field.
  2. When torpid, lift a little: if during sitting you feel drowsy and your head and eyes are heavy, you may lift your attention slightly to the crown of the head and shine there lightly once; when you are awake again, bring it back to the elixir field. This is only “using a little intention”, not forcing the breath upward.
  3. Please first consider your own constitution: people with yin deficiency and yang excess, high blood pressure, a tendency to “internal heat” or a swollen head should not shine much on the upper elixir field; people with a cold constitution, low blood pressure or a tendency to drowsiness may shine on it somewhat more. When in doubt, take the lower elixir field as the main thing.
  4. Gather it back, but do not guard rigidly: after the breath-dynamic has circulated through the whole body, gather it back to the elixir field, but do not stare at a single point.
  5. Safety note: if, while lifting, a swollen head, headache, flushed face, swollen eyes or palpitations appear, stop at once and return to letting the spirit-light shine down on the elixir field with natural breathing; people with high blood pressure should first follow their doctor’s advice.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is one paragraph; the original book does not give the source. It is divided into four short passages.
  2. The third and fourth short passages of this section, “If spirit and breath do not move through the whole body … a body beyond the body”, are largely the same as the second paragraph of Chapter Five, Section 5; the original book includes both, and both are reproduced.
  3. “Three yang bring prosperity” (三阳开泰): the first month of the lunar calendar corresponds to the Tai hexagram (its lower three lines all yang), a metaphor for yang breath rising.

Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 觉照之心

The Mind of Illuminating Awareness

The mind of illuminating awareness — the Buddhists call it the ever-burning lamp, the Daoists the opening of the Mysterious Pass, the Confucians the palace of empty spirituality; the original nature is the head of the elixir, the mind of illuminating awareness the commander

About This Chapter

This section speaks of “the mind of illuminating awareness” — that one point of mind in quiet sitting that is always clear and able to be master:

  1. Three traditions, one thing under different names: the Buddhists call it the ever-burning lamp, the Daoists the opening of the Mysterious Pass, the Confucians the palace of empty spirituality — all are that one “spirit that clearly illuminates” amid no-thought and no-worry, neither good nor evil.
  2. Neither of two can be lacking: if one does not find the original nature within emptiness, there is no basis for the elixir; if one does not nurture within emptiness a spirit of spiritual brightness and wondrous awareness that sees through inside and out, there is no master — and without a master, even diligent practice day and night is only dazed and muddled.
  3. The original nature is the head of the elixir; the mind of illuminating awareness is its commander (the master). Let go of all ties, slowly dissolve the delusions of perception and the selfishness of desire for things, look within and find nothing clinging, empty and open — the original nature is seen; once it is seen, there must also be an aware mind, “illuminating without illuminating, not illuminating yet illuminating” — the master is always present.
  4. Do not set the bar too high: wanting from the start for the original nature to be seamless and boundless, vast without limit, is impossible; it is enough to be at ease with nothing on one’s mind, cool and clear at heart, with a little zest. At the beginning the mind cannot necessarily be subdued; as long as it does not run off or fall into a daze and is not carried away by outer things, this mind is always present.
  5. Do not be too tense: in removing thoughts and holding the breath still, do not use too much force; tension instead stirs Posterior Heaven yin breath, and one cannot sit long. When there is nothing to do, as long as one thought is still and one pays no attention to the second, nature is there; gather it back and let it go, let it go and gather it back.

Qiu Ling’s note: the qigong state differs from sleep and from waking precisely because of this mind of illuminating awareness; without it, one either becomes drowsy and falls into sleep, or becomes alert and is simply in the waking state. This mind of illuminating awareness is the original spirit.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

夫人之所以前知后晓、灵明不昧者，无非此一个觉照之心而已。佛曰：长明灯。道曰：玄关窍。儒曰：虚灵府。要皆无思无虑、无善无恶中一个了照之神焉。下手时不寻出虚无无际物事出来，则无性。无性则无丹本。不从虚无中养出个灵明妙觉洞彻内外之神出来，则无主宰。无主宰虽日夜勤行终是昏昏罔罔，到头而无用也。

What enables a person to know what came before and understand what comes after, spiritually bright and unclouded, is nothing other than this one “mind of illuminating awareness”. The Buddhists call it the “ever-burning lamp”, the Daoists the “opening of the Mysterious Pass”, the Confucians the “palace of empty spirituality” — in short, it is that one spirit that clearly illuminates amid no-thought and no-worry, neither good nor evil. When beginning, if one does not find that boundless thing of emptiness, there is no nature; without nature, there is no basis for the elixir. If one does not nurture within emptiness a spirit of spiritual brightness and wondrous awareness that sees through inside and out, there is no master; without a master, even diligent practice day and night is in the end dazed and muddled, and comes to nothing.

§2
Original (Chinese)

诸子务先把万缘放下，直将知觉之妄，物欲之私，慢慢的起风运火，煅化于无何有之乡，自家内照果然一无染着，一无束缚，空空荡荡，了不知其起止，此为本性见矣！本性一见，又要有个觉心，照而不照，不照而照，此即主宰常存，昔人谓之主人翁是也。有此主宰炼丹可成，无之犹一家无主，焉能兴得起家来？此个主人翁实为炼丹之主帅。至于本性，是炼丹之丹头。

You must first let go of all ties, and slowly fan the wind and work the fire on the delusions of perception and the selfishness of desire for things, forging them away in “the land of nothing whatever”; look within yourself, and indeed find nothing clinging, nothing binding, empty and open, with no idea at all where it begins or where it ends — this is the original nature appearing! Once the original nature appears, there must also be an “aware mind”, illuminating without illuminating, not illuminating yet illuminating (illuminating without deliberately trying to, not deliberately illuminating yet always illuminating) — this is the master always present, whom earlier people called “the master of the house”. With this master, the elixir can be completed; without it, it is like a household without a master — how could the family prosper? This master is truly the commander of refining the elixir; as for the original nature, it is the head (the basis) of the elixir.

§3
Original (Chinese)

但起初即欲本性发见，浑沦无际，浩森无垠，万不能有。只要一个泰然无事，心地清凉，有点趣味就是。若欲清清朗朗，浩浩渊渊，大无外小无内，则必火候到时方有此鸿鸿蒙蒙无可端倪之一候。惟于尘缘稍不沾滞，推得开放得下，即是性见，炼丹有本矣！下手之初此心未必即能降伏，洞照如神，只要此心不走作不昏迷，能为我家主宰，不为外物所夺而去，这即是此心常在，为我炼丹之主矣！

But to want from the start for the original nature to appear seamless and boundless, vast without limit, is utterly impossible. It is enough to be at ease with nothing on one’s mind, cool and clear at heart, with a little zest. If one wants to be clear and bright, vast and deep, so great it has no outside and so small it has no inside, that requires the fire phasing to have arrived; only then is there such a time of vast, primordial haze without any discernible boundary. As long as one is a little less stuck to worldly ties, able to push them away and let them go, that is seeing nature, and refining the elixir has its basis! At the start, this mind cannot necessarily be subdued and illuminate like a spirit; as long as this mind does not run off or fall into a daze, can be master of my house and is not carried away by outer things, this mind is always present, and it is the master of my refining the elixir!

§4
Original (Chinese)

诸子此时尚在阴阳之交，还须立起志气，扶持真阳抑制群阴，久之阳欲进而不能遽进，阴欲退而不肯遽退，所以有如痴如醉之状。盖以阳虽生而阴犹未卸驾也。吾故教诸子，不要除思虑屏气息太为着紧，紧则又动后天阴气，必不能耐久焉。总之，神妙无方，变化莫测，还不是此一点虚寂之性、灵应之神为之作主耳。

You are still at the time when yin and yang are alternating, and you must still set up your will, support true yang and suppress the mass of yin. In time, yang wants to advance but cannot advance all at once, and yin wants to withdraw but will not withdraw all at once, so you seem as if dazed or drunk — this is because, although yang has arisen, yin has not yet unharnessed its carriage (left the stage). So I teach you: in removing thoughts and holding the breath still, do not be too tense; once tense, you stir Posterior Heaven yin breath again, and you certainly cannot keep it up. In short, wondrous beyond method and unpredictable in its changes — is it not simply this one point of empty, still nature and spiritually responsive spirit that is master?

§5
Original (Chinese)

诸子于无事之时不要求浑沦磅礴，只此一念阳静，莫管二念，即是性在。古人收回又放下，放下又收回，即性之见者多矣！久久用功自然本性常圆，无在而无不在焉。只要此心常常了照，稍有闲思杂虑，我能随时觉照，即惺惺常存矣！

When you have nothing to do, do not seek a great state of seamless, surging vastness; as long as this one thought is still and you pay no attention to the second thought, nature is there. The ancients gathered it back and let it go, let it go and gathered it back, and many saw their nature! With long practice, naturally the original nature is always whole, nowhere and yet everywhere. As long as this mind always clearly illuminates, and whenever there is a slight idle thought or stray worry I can notice it at once, alertness is always present!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

气功态所以区别于睡眠状态和醒觉状态，就因为它有一个觉照之心。气功态中无论虚极静笃，混混沌沌，或恍惚杳冥，这个觉照之心总是起着主宰的作用，所以称之为长明灯、主人翁。一旦没有了这个觉照之心，就会昏昏沉沉成了睡眠状态；或者清清醒醒成了醒觉状态。所以古人说：“当灭动心，莫灭照心”。这个照觉之心就是元神，它一直主宰着气功动态的一动一静及如何动静。它作为发见本性的主宰。何谓本来之性体？《仙佛合宗》说：“万象空空，一念不起，六根大定，一尘不染，此即本来之性体完全也”。

The qigong state is distinguished from the state of sleep and the waking state precisely because it has a mind of illuminating awareness. In the qigong state, whether in utmost emptiness and deep stillness, muddled and undivided, or dim and obscure, this mind of illuminating awareness always plays the role of master; that is why it is called the ever-burning lamp, the master of the house. Once this mind of illuminating awareness is lost, one becomes drowsy and falls into the state of sleep; or one becomes alert and is in the ordinary waking state. That is why the ancients said: “Extinguish the moving mind; do not extinguish the illuminating mind.” This mind of illuminating awareness is the Original Spirit; it always presides over every movement and stillness in qigong, and over how to move and how to be still; it is the master that discovers the original nature. What is the substance of the original nature? The Common Origin of Immortals and Buddhas says: “The ten thousand phenomena empty, not a single thought arising, the six roots in great stability, untouched by a speck of dust — this is the substance of the original nature fully revealed.”

Key Terms

  • Mind of illuminating awareness: that one point of mind in stillness that is clear and able to be master; that is, the original spirit.
  • Ever-burning lamp, opening of the Mysterious Pass, palace of empty spirituality: the names given to it by the Buddhist, Daoist and Confucian traditions.
  • Basis of the elixir, head of the elixir: the root of refining the elixir; here, the original nature.
  • Master of the house, commander: the mind of illuminating awareness.
  • Illuminating without illuminating, not illuminating yet illuminating: illuminating without deliberation, without deliberation yet always illuminating.
  • Running off (走作): the mind wandering.
  • Unharnessing the carriage (卸驾): a metaphor for yin leaving the stage.
  • Alertness (惺惺): awake and unclouded.
  • Extinguish the moving mind; do not extinguish the illuminating mind: extinguish stray thoughts, not illuminating awareness; see Chapter One, Section 2.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Lower the bar: do not expect boundless vastness as soon as you sit; if your heart is at ease, cool and clear, with a little zest, that is seeing nature.
  2. If the mind does not run off or fall into a daze, the master is at home.
  3. Do not use too much force: forcibly suppressing thoughts and forcibly holding the breath only keep you from sitting long.
  4. One thought still, ignore the second: when a second thought comes, pay it no mind; if the mind runs off, gather it back, and having gathered it back, let go again.
  5. A test of entering stillness: are you asleep? Are you thinking about things? If neither, and there is a point of clarity present — that is right.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is one paragraph; the original book does not give the source. It is divided into five short passages.
  2. “Only this one thought yang-still” (只此一念阳静): so in the original book; 阳 (“yang”) is probably an error for 宁 (“tranquil”), and the translation renders it as “this one thought is still”.
  3. “Vast without limit” (浩森无垠): 森 is so in the original book, with the same meaning as 渺 (“vast, boundless”).

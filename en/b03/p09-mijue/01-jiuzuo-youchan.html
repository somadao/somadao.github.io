Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 久坐必有禅

Long Sitting Will Surely Bring Chan

Long sitting will surely bring Chan — once seated, one must gather the true spirit and original breath until they are completely full; the two reasons for not sitting to the end

About This Chapter

Chapter Nine is Qiu Ling’s collection of practical key points that Huang Yuanji scattered here and there and that do not fit easily into the first eight chapters — fifteen sections, each on one point. The first section deals with a very practical problem: being unable to sit still.

  1. Sit long enough: once seated, gather the true spirit and original breath until they are completely full. If you get up as soon as the true dynamic arrives, the true breath-energy is not yet full and the true spirit not yet strong — you have sat in vain. The saying goes “long sitting will surely bring Chan”; Zhang Sanfeng says that in sitting one should “unhorse the enemy with one thrust”, so as not to have to fight the battle again another day.
  2. The first reason one cannot sit still: before taking one’s seat, one has not thought through the matters to be dealt with that day and the instructions to be given to others, and as soon as one sits down one is pulled away by these concerns. The remedy: before taking your seat, think through each thing that needs doing and settle it properly, then sit.
  3. The second reason: knowing only how to rise and not how to descend, only how to advance and not how to withdraw — raising the true breath-energy up to the Muddy Pellet, the spirit leaks out along with it, and one does not know to lower one’s head and gather the spirit-light back to the elixir field, so the longer one sits the more hurried and confused one becomes; or, seeing the true breath-energy surge and the original spirit leap, one clings tightly to this news, stirs up the ordinary breath, and cannot go on sitting.
  4. The tricks of the cognitive spirit: while sitting, things feel as urgent as putting out a fire; once off the seat, not a single thing is urgent — this is the cognitive spirit acting as master, and the original spirit unable to hold its seat.
  5. The Way values gathering in, not pouring out. Whatever leaps and wants to burst out is the breath-dynamic leaking outward; gather it back to the central palace.

Qiu Ling’s note: not sitting to the end is a common failing among students today; the causes include a lack of faith, worldly feelings and miscellaneous affairs, and also improper concentration of the spirit and regulation of the breath, leaving spirit and breath blocked or leaking. The ordinary breath stopping, the True Breath appearing, the Mysterious Pass opening, the gate of the Mysterious Female being established — all of these arise only through long sitting.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

诸子近日静养，无非从色身上寻出真性出来，要做一次见一次功效，长一番精神，法身涵养久久始足昭高明广大之天。若真机初到遽行下榻，则真气未充，真神未壮，安能荡开云雾独见青天？从今后不坐则已，一坐必要将真神元气收得十分完足，自然真机在抱，不须守而自守，不费力而自在。俗云：“久坐必有禅”，洵不诬也。又张三丰云，大凡打坐，去欲存理，务须一枪下马，免得另来打仗。此等语非过来人不能知也。

In your quiet nourishing these days, you are simply trying to find the true nature from within this body of flesh; each time you practice you must see some result and gain fresh vigor; the Dharma-body must be nurtured for a long time before it can see the high, bright, vast heaven. If, as soon as the true dynamic arrives, you hurry off your seat, then the true breath-energy is not yet full and the true spirit not yet strong — how could you sweep away the clouds and fog and see the blue sky alone? From now on, either do not sit at all, or, once seated, be sure to gather the true spirit and original breath until they are completely full; then naturally the true dynamic is in your embrace — you need not guard it, it guards itself; you need not exert effort, it is there of itself. The saying goes: “Long sitting will surely bring Chan.” (Sit long enough and you will surely enter absorption.) This is indeed true. Zhang Sanfeng also said: whenever you sit, you must put away desire and preserve principle, and be sure to “unhorse the enemy with one thrust” (knock the enemy off his horse with a single thrust, settling it once and for all), so as not to have to fight the battle again another day. Such words could only be spoken by someone who has been through it.

§2
Original (Chinese)

吾教诸子静坐，始虽有思有为，终归大静大定。如此打坐，可以三五日不散。否则，忽焉而得，忽焉又失。如此行持一任千百次，坐有何益哉？望诸子耐心久坐，不起一烦恼心，庶几深造以道，此为近日切要，不似入门时但教之寻真机焉。

When I teach you quiet sitting, although at the beginning there is thinking and doing (still using a little thought and doing a little work), in the end it must return to great stillness and great stability. Sitting like this, it can hold for three to five days without scattering (the power of stability can be kept for several days). Otherwise, suddenly you have it, and suddenly you lose it again; in that case, even if you sat a thousand times, what good would it do? I hope you will sit patiently for a long time, without raising the slightest vexation; only then is there hope of going deep into the great Way. This is what matters most these days, unlike when you first entered the gate, when I only taught you to look for the true dynamic.

§3
Original (Chinese)

顾人不肯耐烦就榻者其故有二：一则由于未坐之时未曾将日间所应当应酬之事如何区处，如何分咐别人一一想透，故上榻时此心即为尘情牵挂，坐不终局也。非惟不能终局，且一段真机反为思虑识神牵引而去者多矣！诸子打坐之初，务将当行之事一一想过，安顿妥贴然后就坐，庶一心一德不致于中搅扰焉。

But there are two reasons why people are unwilling to keep sitting patiently: first, before sitting, they have not thought through, one by one, how to handle the matters they must attend to during the day and what instructions to give others, so as soon as they take their seat, the mind is tugged by worldly affairs and cannot sit to the end. Not only can they not sit to the end, but often a stretch of the true dynamic is even pulled away by thoughts and the cognitive spirit! At the start of your sitting, be sure to think through each matter to be done and settle it properly, and only then sit; only thus can you be single-minded, undisturbed within.

§4
Original (Chinese)

一则由于知升而不知降，知进而不知退，知存而不知亡，知得而不知失，是以摄提坎宫真气上冲泥丸，神因之而外泄，不知低头下盼，收敛神光于丹鼎。是以忙了又忙，慌了又慌，未到如如自如，了了自了，而即欲下榻也。且道本无物，修原无为，忽见真气冲冲，元神跃跃，不知此气机自然运动，于本来物事无相关涉，却死死执着这个消息，常存不放，因而惹动后天凡息不能平静，扰乱先天元神无以主持，又以坐未十分如意，而遽行下榻也。究之未下榻时，觉得吾身世忙有如救火追亡，一刻难缓；即至下榻又却无一急切之事，皆由识神为主，而元神不能坐镇故耳。

Second, they know only how to rise and not how to descend, only how to advance and not how to withdraw, only how to preserve and not how to let go, only how to gain and not how to lose. So they raise the true breath-energy of the Kan palace (the lower elixir field) and send it surging up to the Muddy Pellet (the crown of the head), and the spirit too thereby leaks and scatters outward; they do not know to lower their head and look downward, gathering the spirit-light back to the elixir tripod (the lower elixir field). So they are busy and busier, flustered and more flustered, and before they reach the point of being at ease and clear, they already want to get off their seat. Moreover, the Way is originally without things, and cultivation is originally non-action; suddenly seeing the true breath-energy surging and the original spirit leaping, they do not know that this is only the natural movement of the breath-dynamic, which has nothing at all to do with what is original, and they cling tightly to this news, holding on and never letting go, and so they stir up the Posterior Heaven ordinary breath, which cannot be calm, and disturb the Prior Heaven original spirit, which cannot preside; and because the sitting is not quite as they would wish, they hurry off their seat. In the end, while not yet off the seat, they feel that their affairs are as pressing as putting out a fire or chasing a fugitive, and not a moment can be lost; yet once off the seat, there is not a single urgent matter — all because the cognitive spirit is acting as master and the original spirit cannot hold its seat.

§5
Original (Chinese)

诸子安心就坐向水府求玄，升提阳气，将眼、耳、目、口，鼻一切神光会萃中宫，不令一丝外入外出，蕴蓄久久自焕发焉。尤要知道道本无物，至此跃跃欲出皆是气机发泄于外，吾道贵收敛，不贵发泄，此处尤须防闲，毋许后天识神扰动，庶可安坐榻上，切记切记！

You must settle down and sit, going to the water palace (the lower elixir field) to seek the “mysterious” (Prior Heaven true yang), raising the yang energy; gather all the spirit-light of the eyes, ears, mouth and nose into the central palace (the lower elixir field), not letting a thread go out nor a thread come in from outside; stored up long enough, it naturally shines forth. Above all, know this: the Way is originally without things, and whatever at this time leaps and wants to burst out is the breath-dynamic pouring outward. Our Way values gathering in, not pouring out; here especially one must be on guard, not allowing the Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit to stir things up; only then can one sit securely on one’s seat. Remember this, remember it well!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

练功坐不终局，遽行下榻，亦为今之学者通病。考其原因，多为对练功缺乏信心决心，或由于尘情牵挂、杂务缠身难以入静，不静则烦，故不耐久坐。至因凝神调息不当，致使神气窒塞或走泄，感到浑身不舒畅而下榻者亦有之。须知练功久坐必有禅，气机由微而著，凡息停，真息现，玄关窍开及玄牝之门立等等诸种真机及境界，均须久坐才生。坐不终局，必致练功半途而废。

Not sitting to the end in practice and hurrying off the seat is also a common failing among students today. Looking into the causes, it is mostly a lack of faith and resolve in the practice; or being tugged by worldly feelings and entangled in miscellaneous affairs, so that it is hard to enter stillness, and without stillness there is vexation, so one cannot bear to sit long; there are also those who, from improper concentration of the spirit and regulation of the breath, leave spirit and breath blocked or leaking, feel uncomfortable all over and get off their seat. Know that in practice “long sitting will surely bring Chan”: the breath-dynamic going from faint to evident, the ordinary breath stopping and the True Breath appearing, the opening of the Mysterious Pass opening and the gate of the Mysterious Female being established — all such true dynamics and states arise only through long sitting. Not sitting to the end inevitably leads to abandoning the practice halfway.

Key Terms

  • Getting off the seat (下榻): leaving one’s seat, ending the sitting.
  • Long sitting will surely bring Chan: sit long enough and you will surely enter absorption.
  • Unhorsing the enemy with one thrust: settling it once and for all, leaving no trouble behind.
  • Thinking and doing: at the start of sitting, still using a little thought and effort.
  • Cognitive spirit: the Posterior Heaven spirit of thinking and deliberating; see cognitive spirit.
  • Kan palace, water palace, elixir tripod, central palace: in this book, all refer to the lower elixir field.
  • Seeking the mysterious: seeking Prior Heaven true yang.
  • Lowering the head and looking down: gathering the upward spirit-light back to shine down on the elixir field.
  • Being on guard (防闲): guarding against.

Putting It into Practice

  1. “Clear the accounts” before taking your seat: think through what you need to do and what you need to tell others today, arrange it, then sit.
  2. Once seated, sit fully: do not get up as soon as the true dynamic arrives; sit patiently until the mind is at peace and the breath settled.
  3. When the breath surges upward, gather it downward: if the head feels swollen, the breath pushes upward and the mind is flustered — lower your head and look down, gather the spirit-light back to the elixir field, and do not chase the breath upward.
  4. See through the “urgency”: if, while sitting, you suddenly feel there is something urgent, it is mostly the cognitive spirit playing tricks; think back after getting up, and often there was nothing urgent at all.
  5. Improper concentration and breath regulation can also keep you from sitting: if you feel stifled or uncomfortable all over, relax and return to natural breathing; do not force yourself to hold on.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is two paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The first paragraph is divided into four short passages.
  2. “All the spirit-light of the eyes, ears, eyes, mouth and nose” (将眼、耳、目、口，鼻一切神光): so in the original book, with “eyes” (眼 and 目) appearing twice; reproduced as it stands.
  3. “Being as one is, at ease; being clear, clearly” (如如自如，了了自了): being at ease and clear.

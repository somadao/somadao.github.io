Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 守中之旨

The Meaning of Guarding the Center

The meaning of guarding the center — guarding the formed center, guarding the formless center; when the mind is level the spirit concentrates, when the breath is harmonious the breathing is regulated; the key lies only in the two words “a level mind”

About This Chapter

“Guarding the center” is something the elixir way often speaks of. This section explains the two layers of meaning of the word “center”:

  1. Guarding the formed center: reverting the light to shine back, attending to the center of the compass, neither clinging to nor apart from the spot one cun three fen below the navel.
  2. Guarding the formless center: the Doctrine of the Mean‘s “before joy, anger, sorrow and pleasure have arisen is called the center” — in that time before they arise, not hearing, not seeing, watchful and wary, naturally nature is settled and spirit clear, and one sees one’s original face.
  3. From effort to naturalness: at first one must guard it with effort; in the end it is pure naturalness. The three teachings use different names, but all take the word “center” as their guiding principle.
  4. The key lies only in the two words “a level mind”: concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath, look back to the original opening, the mind unaware of being a mind and the breath unaware of being breath — only then are the mind level and the breath harmonious; when the mind is level the spirit concentrates, when the breath is harmonious the breathing is regulated. The mind raising no waves is called level; being able to hold to the center is called level — this is the one opening of the Mysterious Pass; when spirit and breath rely on each other, the substance of the Mysterious Pass is established.
  5. Everything else: advancing the fire and withdrawing the tally, turning the River Chariot and other work of action all value humility, softness, ease and unhurriedness, neither helping nor forgetting; being returns to non-being, non-being gives rise to being again, and finally neither being nor non-being is established — this is “obtain the One, and the ten thousand affairs are complete”.

Qiu Ling’s note: the center is the substance of nature, empty as space; guarding the center is the work of returning to emptiness. For beginners, guarding the formless center seems to give nothing to start from; they should enter the realm of emptiness by way of guarding the formed center — reverting the light to shine on the elixir field.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

此个中字所包甚广，其在人身，一在守有形之中。朱子云：“守中制外”。夫守中者回光返照，注意规中，于脐下一寸三分处不即不离是。一在守无形之中。《中庸》云：“喜怒哀乐之未发谓之中。”此未发时不闻不睹，戒慎恐惧，自然性定神清，方见本来面目，然后人欲易净，天理复明，自古圣贤均以此为第一步功夫。但始须守乎勉强然之中，终则纯乎自然之中。

This word “center” has a very broad meaning. In the human body: first, there is guarding the formed center. Master Zhu said: “Guard the center to control the outer.” What is called guarding the center is reverting the light to shine back, attending to the center of the compass (the central place), neither clinging to nor apart from the spot one cun three fen below the navel. Second, there is guarding the formless center. The Doctrine of the Mean says: “Before joy, anger, sorrow and pleasure have arisen is called the center.” In this time before they arise, not hearing, not seeing, watchful and wary (guarding it with care and caution), naturally nature is settled and spirit clear, and only then can one see one’s original face; after that, human desires are easily cleared away and heavenly principle shines anew — sages and worthies since ancient times have all taken this as the first step of the work. But at first one must guard a center of effort (still using the mind to guard it); only in the end is it a center of pure naturalness.

§2
Original (Chinese)

三教圣人名目各有不同，总不外此中字为之宗，为之君，即如吾教以凝神调息为主，然后回顾本窍，心无其心，气无其气，乃得心平气和。心平则神始凝，气和则息始调，其要只在心平二字。心不起波之谓平，能执其中之谓平。平即在此中也，心在此中。即丹经之玄关一窍，到得神气相依，玄关之体已立，此为大道根源，金丹本始。

The sages of the three teachings — Confucian, Buddhist and Daoist — each use different names, but none goes beyond taking this word “center” as their guiding principle and master. In our Daoist teaching, for example, the main thing is concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath, and then looking back to the original opening; the mind unaware of being a mind, the breath unaware of being breath — only then are the mind level and the breath harmonious. When the mind is level, the spirit begins to concentrate; when the breath is harmonious, the breathing begins to be even — the key lies only in the two words “a level mind”. The mind raising no waves is called level; being able to hold to the center is called level. Levelness is within this “center”, the mind is within this “center” — this is what the elixir classics call the single opening of the Mysterious Pass. When spirit and breath come to rely on each other, the substance of the Mysterious Pass is established; this is the source of the great Way and the beginning of the Golden Elixir.

§3
Original (Chinese)

他如进火退符、搬运河车、有为有作，总贵谦和柔顺，以整以暇，勿助勿忘，有要归无，无又生有，至有无不立方合天然道体，此即得一而万事毕，吾道一以贯之之旨也。

As for the other work of action and doing, such as advancing the fire and withdrawing the tally and turning the River Chariot, all of it values humility, softness and compliance, being composed and unhurried (orderly and at leisure), neither helping nor forgetting; being must return to non-being, non-being gives rise to being again, and only when neither being nor non-being is established does it accord with the natural substance of the Way — this is “obtain the One, and the ten thousand affairs are complete”, the guiding principle of our Way, “one thread running through it all”.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

中即虚空之性体，守中即还虚之功。初学者守无形之中似无凭藉，当须通过守有形之中，回光返照丹田而进入虚无之境。

“The center” is the substance of nature, empty as space; “guarding the center” is the work of returning to emptiness. For beginners, guarding the formless center seems to give nothing to rely on; they should enter the realm of emptiness by way of guarding the formed center — reverting the light to shine back on the elixir field.

Key Terms

  • Guarding the center: guarding the formed center (the lower elixir field) and guarding the formless center (the mind before arising).
  • Center of the compass: the central place; here, the lower elixir field.
  • Guard the center to control the outer: words of Zhu Xi — holding to the inner in order to control the outer.
  • Before joy, anger, sorrow and pleasure have arisen is called the center: from the Doctrine of the Mean.
  • Watchful and wary: the Doctrine of the Mean, “watchful over what one does not see, wary of what one does not hear”.
  • The mind unaware of being a mind, the breath unaware of being breath: not feeling that there is a mind, not feeling that there is breath.
  • Orderly and at leisure (以整以暇): composed and unhurried.
  • One thread running through it all: from the Analects, “Li Ren”.
  • Returning to emptiness: refining the spirit to return to emptiness; see the three transmutations.

Putting It into Practice

  1. First guard the formed center: let the spirit-light shine lightly on the lower belly, neither clinging nor apart.
  2. Then guard the formless center: the calm before emotions arise — guarding it is guarding the center.
  3. The key is only “a level mind”: when the mind raises no waves, the spirit concentrates and the breathing is regulated of themselves.
  4. All work must be soft: not hurried, not forceful, neither helping nor forgetting.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is one paragraph; the original book does not give the source. It is divided into three short passages.
  2. “Guard the center to control the outer” (守中制外): words of Zhu Xi, found in the Classified Conversations of Master Zhu and elsewhere; the original meaning is holding to the inner mind in order to control outer things.

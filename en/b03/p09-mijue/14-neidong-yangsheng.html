Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 内动阳生

Inner Stirring and Yang Arising

Inner stirring and yang arising — the inner fire of the spirit and the outer fire of the tally, two kinds of warm nourishing; within non-action there is a true master; sit until heavenly flowers rain down, with neither being nor non-being established

About This Chapter

This section speaks of warm nourishing after yang arises, and Qiu Ling says plainly: this is the method of “nurturing the embryo for ten months” from the stage of refining breath into spirit, and beginners will find it hard to practice. Please read it for understanding.

  1. Inner stirring and yang arising: sometimes it arises naturally after long stillness and stability, sometimes it arises on the spot upon chancing into absorption — both are signs of the spirit entering the breath and merging into one.
  2. Two kinds of fire: within, store up the natural fire of the spirit, letting it be still and then move, move and then be still, circling in the elixir tripod; without, use the fire of the tally (the circuit of breathing), letting it go up and down, back and forth. Once the inner fire is strong, the outer fire naturally flows around the whole body, vast and bottomless — this is “the breath surrounding the spirit”. At this time, simply keep the master seated, concentrated and settled in the central palace, thoughts within not going out and thoughts without not coming in.
  3. The disciples’ fault: when withdrawing the tally, being wholly without thought or worry seems good, but without noticing they drift off again — they are not yet practiced, and it does no harm to count the breaths of the circuit again to call the mind back.
  4. The transformed state: no need to transport or push it along; the whole body is vigorous, above the steaming breath, below the essence of Kan water, flowing around without cease; the breathing regulates itself without being regulated, the essence refines itself without being refined — “always blowing a flute without holes, now and then strumming a lute without strings”.
  5. Lü Dongbin: “Warm nourishing is of two kinds — the inner fire of the spirit and the outer fire of the tally; preserve it for ten months, leaving action and going to non-action.” But within non-action there must be a true master presiding, so that it does not fall into emptiness; one must also revert the light to shine back, “counting the breaths as if not counting”.
  6. Finally: still upon still, patient upon patient, sit until heavenly flowers rain down and the blood and breath of the whole body leap; the body like the great void, as if without form, yet also like a spring silkworm wrapped in its cocoon by the breath-dynamic — only one spiritual brightness shining alone in the center, inside and outside both forgotten, neither being nor non-being established: this is the true meaning.

Qiu Ling’s note: the inner fire of the spirit is the fire of the Li palace, which at this stage of practice can be set going with the slightest concentration of the spirit; the outer fire of the tally is the circuit. Only those who have reached the point where the breath surrounds the spirit, the physical body seems to disappear, and the whole body seems wrapped in the breath-dynamic, one with the great void, may practice this method.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

夫内动阳生实由静定久久自然而生者，有由偶尔入定当下即生者，此神入气中融洽为一之象也。我于此再为蕴蓄内中天然神火，任其静而动、动而静，盘旋于丹鼎中，再用外之符火听其上下往来，行住起止，所谓周旋十二节，节尽更须亲。是到得内火一旺，外火自迴环于一身之中，鸿鸿蒙蒙无有底止，此即气周神外之候。我于斯时惟有坐镇主人，凝定中宫，务使内想不出，外想不入而已。

Inner stirring and yang arising sometimes truly come about naturally after long stillness and stability, and sometimes arise on the spot upon chancing into absorption — both are scenes of the spirit entering the breath and merging into one. At this time I further store up the natural fire of the spirit within, letting it be still and then move, move and then be still, circling in the elixir tripod (the lower elixir field); and I use the fire of the tally without (the fire phasing of breathing), letting it go up and down, come and go, move and rest, start and stop — this is what is called “circling through the twelve stages; when the stages are done, draw all the closer”. When the inner fire becomes strong, the outer fire naturally circles through the whole body, vast and hazy, without end — this is the time when “the breath surrounds the spirit” (the breath wraps around the outside of the spirit). At this time I simply sit as master, concentrated and settled in the central palace, making sure only that thoughts within do not go out and thoughts without do not come in.

§2
Original (Chinese)

诸子近时已做到此处，吾看来还未十分如法，当退符时一味无思无虑，似乎还佳，不觉又他去焉。盖因未曾老练，不妨再数周天之息以召回之。久之至于化境，不须搬运推迁，而吾身蓬蓬勃勃，上为熏蒸之气，下为坎水之精，周流一身，上下往来，无有穷期者。此息不期调而自调，精不期炼而自炼，所谓真橐籥，又谓“长吹无孔笛，时鼓没弦琴”者是。

You have lately reached this point, but I see that it is not yet quite in accord with the method: when withdrawing the tally you are wholly without thought or worry, which seems good, yet without noticing you drift off again. This is because you are not yet practiced; it does no harm to count the breaths of the circuit again to call the mind back. In time, when you reach the transformed state, there is no need to transport or push it along, and my body is vigorous and surging: above is the steaming breath, below the essence of Kan water, flowing around the whole body, going up and down, coming and going without end. At this time the breathing regulates itself without being regulated, and the essence refines itself without being refined — this is what is called “the true bellows”, also called “always blowing a flute without holes, now and then strumming a lute without strings” (always blowing a flute that has no holes, now and then playing a lute that has no strings — a metaphor for natural working that leaves no trace).

§3
Original (Chinese)

此非吾独撰也。吕洞宾云：“温养两般，内神火而外符火。保存十月，去有为而就无为”。是此时虽云无为，亦要知无为之中有个真正主人为我主宰，才不落空，又还要回光返照，数息而若无数者，方能保固真阳，生长圣胎。柳真人云：“一息去一息来，息息相依莫徘徊”。由此观之，内之神火须要安闲自得，调停中立；外之符火是为温养之火，惟加一番谨慎，著十分了照，听其息息归根，息息入定，化为自然之神符，毫不假一分人力得矣！

This is not something I have made up by myself. Lü Dongbin said: “Warm nourishing is of two kinds — the inner fire of the spirit and the outer fire of the tally; preserve it for ten months, leaving action and going to non-action.” So at this time, although it is called non-action, one must also know that within non-action there is a true master presiding for me, so that it does not fall into emptiness; one must also revert the light to shine back, “counting the breaths as if not counting”, and only then can one secure true yang and grow the Holy Embryo. The Perfected Liu said: “One breath goes, one breath comes; breath relying on breath, do not waver.” Seen in this light, the inner fire of the spirit must be at ease and content, kept balanced in the middle; the outer fire of the tally is the fire of warm nourishing — one need only add a measure of care, illuminate it with full clarity, and let breath after breath return to the root and enter absorption, transforming into the natural tally of the spirit without borrowing the slightest human effort, and you have it!

§4
Original (Chinese)

此中功用，须要静之又静，耐之又耐，坐到天花乱坠，周身血气自然踊跃，我身浑如太虚，直若无有身形者，然又若此身在气机包裹中，如春蚕作茧一般。我于此惟有一灵炯炯独照当中，内外浑忘，有无不立，才是真诠。

The work here requires being still upon still and patient upon patient, sitting until “heavenly flowers rain down” (all kinds of phenomena appear in profusion), and the blood and breath of the whole body naturally leap; my body is seamless like the great void, almost as if it had no form; and yet it is as if this body were wrapped by the breath-dynamic, like a spring silkworm making its cocoon. At this time there is only one point of spiritual brightness, shining clearly and alone in the center, inside and outside both seamlessly forgotten, neither being nor non-being established — this is the true meaning.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

此为炼气化神阶段的十月养胎功法。初学者难以练此。

内神火即离宫之火，功夫到此，略凝神即能发动。外符火即周天运行。如此内外结合，即能真气蓬勃周流一身。最妙者功夫臻于化境，不须搬运推移，只须元神主宰，便能使真气絪絪缊缊，上下往来，无有穷期。

学者行功能达气包神外，似若色身消失，周身似有气机包裹浑如与太空同体者，可练此功。

This is the method of “nurturing the embryo for ten months” from the stage of refining breath into spirit; beginners will find it hard to practice.

The inner fire of the spirit is the fire of the Li palace (the heart); when the practice reaches this step, it can be set going with the slightest concentration of the spirit. The outer fire of the tally is the circulation of the Celestial Circuit. Combining inner and outer in this way makes the true breath-energy vigorous and flowing through the whole body. Most wondrous of all, when the practice reaches the transformed state, there is no need to transport or push it along; with only the original spirit presiding, the true breath-energy can mingle richly, going up and down, coming and going, without end.

Only students whose practice has reached the point where “the breath surrounds the spirit”, where the physical body seems to disappear, and where the whole body seems wrapped by the breath-dynamic, seamlessly one with the great void, may practice this method.

Key Terms

  • Inner stirring and yang arising: yang breath stirring naturally within amid stillness and stability.
  • Inner fire of the spirit: the fire of the spirit of the Li palace (the heart); see Samādhi True Fire.
  • Outer fire of the tally: the fire phasing of the breathing circuit; see fire phasing, Celestial Circuit.
  • The breath surrounding the spirit: the breath wrapping around the outside of the spirit.
  • Circling through the twelve stages; when the stages are done, draw all the closer: turning through the twelve stages, and when they are done, guarding all the more closely.
  • Bellows (橐籥): a bellows.
  • Always blowing a flute without holes, now and then strumming a lute without strings: a metaphor for natural working that leaves no trace.
  • Nurturing the embryo for ten months: the stage of refining breath into spirit; see the three transmutations, Holy Embryo.
  • Counting the breaths as if not counting: counting and yet not counting.
  • Heavenly flowers raining down: all kinds of phenomena appearing in profusion.

Putting It into Practice

  1. This is an advanced method; read it for understanding only. Qiu Ling says it plainly: only those who have reached the point where “the breath surrounds the spirit and the physical body seems to disappear” may practice it.
  2. One point ordinary people can use: when, in sitting, you simply let everything go blank, you often drift off without noticing — at such times you may count a few breaths to call the mind back, then let go again.
  3. Non-action is not blankness: even at the highest stage, that point of “master” is there.
  4. Safety note: do not imitate the book’s descriptions of “the circulation of the Celestial Circuit” and “the outer fire flowing around” by moving the breath with your intention; when phenomena appear (heavenly flowers raining down and so on), pay no mind to any of them (see Section 4 of this chapter).

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is two paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The first paragraph is divided into three short passages.
  2. Lü Dongbin’s line “warm nourishing is of two kinds” and the Perfected Liu’s (Liu Huayang’s) line “one breath goes, one breath comes” are quoted in the original without the titles of their sources.
  3. “The outer fire naturally circles through the whole body” (外火自迴环于一身之中): 迴 is the same as 回, “to circle back”.

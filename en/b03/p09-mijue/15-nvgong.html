Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 女功一则

A Note on Women’s Practice

A note on women’s practice — an empty mind as the foundation, clarifying the mind and seeing nature; reverting the light to shine back on the breasts, concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath at the breasts

About This Chapter

This is the only section in the book devoted specifically to women’s practice:

  1. The root is the same: studying the Way is nothing other than washing the mind and cleansing the thoughts, with an empty mind as the foundation. Empty, it is spiritual; spiritual, the true mind appears and the original nature is born — clarifying the mind and seeing nature.
  2. Clarifying the mind and seeing nature: with one thought return to the elixir field and illuminate it with intention; that which first stirs as a thought is “the mind”, and “clarifying” means clarifying this; before any thought stirs, one expanse of empty brightness without a single thing — that is “nature”, and “seeing” means seeing this.
  3. The location for women: when, amid the congealing and closing of all the yin, the one yang suddenly first stirs and the light is reverted in a flash — that is the living zi hour; at this time revert the light to shine back on the breasts; this is called “the source of the water is utterly clear”, and the highest medicine can be refined, first dispelling illness and extending life, and finally becoming a sage and a true one.
  4. A simple method: sit settled with the body upright, the mind at ease and calmly composed; day and night illuminate within the breasts, letting the breath go up and down naturally — breathing out without surging up to the head and eyes, breathing in without surging down to the water palace; slow and unhurried, a hundred breaths or a thousand, let it go as it will. Simply concentrate the spirit at the breasts and regulate the breath at the breasts, following each going out and coming in. In time yang breath naturally arises and the whole body is strong; even if only a faint yang stirs by chance, gather it back into the furnace and do not get off your seat.

Qiu Ling’s note: apart from reverting the light to shine back on the breasts, women’s practice is mostly the same as men’s. The breasts are an important location in women’s practice, connecting above with the fluids of the heart and lungs and reaching below to the true sap of the Sea of Blood; some instead revert the light to shine on the “Breast Stream” between the two breasts (in which lie important points such as Danzhong and the Crimson Palace). Qiu Ling also mentions that the breasts and the Breast Stream may be massaged.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

要之学道无他，只是一个洗心涤虑，虚其心以为基。虚则灵，灵则真心见矣，元性生焉，此即明心见性之一端也。人能一念返还丹田之中，用意了照，始初动念即心矣，明则明此，别无明也。未动念之前一片空明，虚虚浑浑，了无物事，此即性也，见者见此，别无见也。

In short, studying the Way is nothing else: it is only washing the mind and cleansing the thoughts, with an empty mind as the foundation. Empty, it is spiritual; spiritual, the true mind appears and the original nature is born — this is one aspect of clarifying the mind and seeing nature. If one can, with one thought, return into the elixir field and illuminate it clearly with intention: that which first stirs as a thought is “the mind”; what is called “clarifying the mind” is clarifying this, there is nothing else to clarify; before any thought has stirred, one expanse of empty brightness, empty and undivided, with nothing at all — this is “nature”; what is called “seeing nature” is seeing this, there is nothing else to see.

§2
Original (Chinese)

果能明心见性如此，此即于群阴凝闭之时忽然一阳初动，瞥地回光，即古人谓冬至阳生夜半活子时至之一候也。我于是回光返照于乳房，是为水源至清，可以炼成上药，始之以却病延年，终之以成圣作真，要无非此一候为基也。

If one can truly clarify the mind and see nature in this way, this is when, amid the congealing and closing of all the yin, the one yang suddenly first stirs and the light is reverted in a flash — that is, the moment the ancients spoke of as “at the winter solstice yang arises; at midnight the Living Zi Hour arrives”. At this very moment I revert the light to shine back on the breasts; this is called “the source of the water is utterly clear”, and the highest medicine can be refined: at first it can dispel illness and extend life, and in the end one can become a sage and a true one — all of it founded on this moment.

§3
Original (Chinese)

然吾说此法极高，犹恐妇女难会，再示浅浅之学。下手之时身要正正当当坐定，心要安安闲闲静镇。日夜惟有观照乳房之中，出入之息一上一下任其天然自在。其呼而出也，上不至冲动头目；其吸而入也，下不至冲于水府。一听缓缓而行，悠扬自得，或百或千任其所之。惟是凝神于乳房，调息于乳房，顺其一出一入之常得矣！久久从事于此，自然阳气发生，一身健旺非常，此虽微阳偶动，仍收归炉内，不可下榻。

But the method I have described is very lofty, and I fear women will find it hard to grasp, so let me teach a simpler method. When beginning, sit settled with the body properly upright, and let the mind become calm, at ease and at leisure. Day and night simply illuminate within the breasts, letting the breath going out and coming in rise and fall naturally as it will: breathing out, it should not surge upward to disturb the head and eyes; breathing in, it should not surge downward to the water palace (the lower belly). Let it go slowly, unhurried and content, a hundred breaths or a thousand, as it will. Simply concentrate the spirit at the breasts and regulate the breath at the breasts, following its ordinary going out and coming in, and you have it! Doing this for a long time, yang breath naturally arises and the whole body becomes very strong. Even though this is only a faint yang stirring by chance, you must still gather it back into the furnace and not get off your seat.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

此女功一则除回光返照乳房外，余多与男功同。

乳房为女子功法之重要窍位，上通心肺之津液，下彻血海之真汁。古云：乳之左房通肝，右房通肺。

女功中除凝神观照乳房外，亦有回光返照两乳之间的乳溪者。盖乳溪通心通肾又通脾，中有膻中、绛宫等要穴。对乳房、乳溪还可加以按摩。

In this note on women’s practice, apart from reverting the light to shine back on the breasts, the rest is mostly the same as men’s practice.

The breasts are an important location in women’s practice, connecting above with the fluids of the heart and lungs and reaching below to the true sap of the Sea of Blood. The ancients said: the left breast connects with the liver, the right breast with the lungs.

In women’s practice, besides concentrating the spirit and illuminating the breasts, some instead revert the light to shine on the Breast Stream between the two breasts, because the Breast Stream connects with the heart, the kidneys and also the spleen, and within it lie important points such as Danzhong and the Crimson Palace. The breasts and the Breast Stream may also be massaged.

Key Terms

  • Washing the mind and cleansing the thoughts: washing clean the mind’s thoughts, cleansing away deliberation.
  • Clarifying the mind: understanding the mind that first stirs as a thought.
  • Seeing nature: seeing the nature that is one expanse of empty brightness before any thought stirs.
  • Amid the congealing and closing of all the yin: in an expanse of yin stillness.
  • The source of the water is utterly clear: the moment when the one yang first stirs and the light is reverted; see Chapter Five, Section 2.
  • Breast Stream: between the two breasts, containing Danzhong and the Crimson Palace.
  • Sea of Blood: the uterus in a woman’s lower belly.
  • The furnace: the elixir field.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The root is the same as men’s practice: first an empty mind, clarifying the mind and seeing nature.
  2. The location for women is in the chest: sit upright with the mind at peace, and gently illuminate the space between the breasts (the Breast Stream) or the breasts, breathing naturally.
  3. The measure of the breath: breathing out without surging to the head, breathing in without surging deep into the lower belly — steady and even, slow and unhurried.
  4. Even when the breath stirs, do not get off your seat: when there is a warm, easy feeling, gather it back to the center and continue nourishing quietly.
  5. A note: women’s elixir practice has long had its own specialized books (such as the Women’s Golden Elixir). This book has only this one note, and its content is brief; the massage mentioned in Qiu Ling’s note should be gentle. Ancient books have special provisions for practice during menstruation, pregnancy and breastfeeding; if in doubt, please consult an experienced teacher or a doctor.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is one paragraph; the original book does not give the source. It is divided into three short passages.
  2. “The light reverted in a flash” (瞥地回光): reverting the light in an instant; the original book writes 瞥地.
  3. “A hundred or a thousand, let it go as it will” (或百或千任其所之): referring to the number of breaths — a hundred breaths, a thousand breaths, leaving it to nature.

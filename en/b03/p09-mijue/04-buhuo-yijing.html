Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 不惑于异景

Not Being Misled by Strange Scenes

Not being misled by strange scenes — strange colors and rare fragrances, pay no mind to any of them; occasionally knowing good and ill fortune is also the cognitive spirit at work, and one must never feel the slightest delight

About This Chapter

This section deals with how to treat the various “strange scenes” that appear during practice — and Huang Yuanji’s answer is a single sentence: pay no mind to any of them.

  1. Strange colors and rare fragrances: strange colors seen by the eyes, rare fragrances smelled by the nose, strange sounds heard by the ears — pay no mind to any of them.
  2. The flower of the mind occasionally opening, being able to know past and future, good and ill fortune: here too, gather in the original spirit and keep it seated in the central court. Even if by chance one knows good and ill fortune without thinking, that too is the cognitive spirit at work, and one must never feel the slightest delight. Once delight arises, even if one does not fall into a demonic path, one will rely on one’s own cleverness and be led about by outside affairs, and cultivation stops there.
  3. All of these come from one’s own temperament and habits; they are not the original spirit or original breath, and are not to be believed.

Qiu Ling’s note gives a very concrete example: some people, having practiced to a certain degree, can occasionally “see” things in the next room, landscapes and figures of unknown origin, even images like those on a television screen; if they secretly rejoice at this and pursue it deliberately, sleepless all night, they will inevitably neglect the real inner practice and use up a great deal of original spirit and original breath, their complexion growing dull and haggard, even old illnesses recurring.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

近观生等功夫到此，将有异状显露。吾今道破，凡有异彩奇香，或见于目，或闻于鼻，或来于耳，总不要理他。抑或心花偶发，能知过去未来、一切吉凶祸福，总要收摄元神座镇中庭。虽偶而发露天然一念现前，不待思索而能知休咎，亦是识神用事，切不可生一喜心。喜心一生，即不入于魔道，亦恐自恃聪明反为外事纷驰，而修炼从此止步矣！

I see that your practice has lately reached this stage, and some strange scenes are going to appear. Today I will speak plainly: whenever there are strange colors or rare fragrances, whether seen by the eyes, smelled by the nose, or heard by the ears, pay no mind to any of them. Or if the flower of the mind occasionally opens and you can know past and future, all good and ill fortune, you must still gather in the original spirit and keep it seated in the central court (abiding securely at the center). Even if by chance a natural thought appears and you know good and ill fortune (xiujiu) without having to think, that too is the cognitive spirit at work, and you must never feel the slightest delight. Once delight arises, even if you do not fall into a demonic path, I fear you will rely on your own cleverness and instead be pulled this way and that by outside affairs, and your cultivation will stop there!

§2
Original (Chinese)

此为近时要紧之务。切不可羡慕景象，自堕魔道，妄论休咎。此皆自家气习所致，非元神元气，不可信也。

This is what matters most these days. You must never covet scenes, fall of your own accord into a demonic path, and talk recklessly of good and ill fortune. All of these come from your own temperament and habits; they are not the original spirit or original breath, and are not to be believed.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§2
Original note (Chinese)

此为古来练功者之戒律。功中所现各种异常景象无奇不有，五花八门，我均须不闻不问，保持虚无忘我境界，对异景无喜无爱，无动于衷，继续按练功程序进行练功，则功夫自能往深处发展。否则，必着于魔道，功夫亦从此止步。

试举例言之，学者练功到一定程度，就有可能出现不稳定的遥视功能，偶而能看到隔室事物；或不知来自何处的山水人物景象；住近城市或靠近边境的还能看到来自空中或别人电视机上的各种彩色图象，如果练功者为此种极不稳定、不知来自何处的图象所惑，私心窃喜自己有了一点遥视功能，遂彻夜不眠而刻意追求，胡思乱想，势必厌倦内功实质的修炼，并消耗大量的元神元气，从而变得脸色暗淡憔悴，甚至旧病复发，修炼亦从此止步，岂不可戒？！

This is a precept for practitioners since ancient times. The strange scenes that appear in practice are of every kind imaginable, a dazzling variety; I must neither listen nor ask, keep to the state of emptiness and self-forgetting, neither delighting in nor loving the strange scenes, unmoved, and go on practicing according to the sequence of the practice — then the practice can naturally develop in depth. Otherwise, one is bound to fall into a demonic path, and the practice stops there.

To give an example: when a student has practiced to a certain degree, an unstable sense of “remote viewing” may appear; occasionally it seems one can see things in the next room, or scenes of landscapes and figures from who knows where; those living near cities or close to a border may even “see” all kinds of color images as if coming from the air or from other people’s television sets. If the practitioner is bewitched by such highly unstable images of unknown origin, secretly rejoicing at having gained a little power of remote viewing, and so pursues it deliberately, sleepless all night, with wild imaginings, he is bound to grow weary of the substance of inner practice and use up a great deal of original spirit and original breath, so that his complexion grows dull and haggard, and even old illnesses recur, and his cultivation stops there — should this not be taken as a warning?!

Key Terms

  • Strange scenes, strange states: unusual phenomena appearing during practice.
  • The flower of the mind occasionally opening: a sudden insight or perception arising in the mind.
  • Xiujiu (休咎): good and ill fortune.
  • Central court: the center, where the original spirit is seated.
  • Cognitive spirit: the Posterior Heaven spirit of thinking and deliberating.
  • Demonic path: being bewitched by illusory states and straying from the right way; see realm of yin / realm of the aggregates.
  • Temperament and habits (气习): one’s disposition and habitual tendencies.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Pay no mind to any of it: whatever you see, smell or hear, take no notice, and continue with your original practice.
  2. Do not delight, fear or chase: if you like it, you will chase it; if you fear it, you will try to avoid it — either way you are being led by it. Let it pass plainly.
  3. Do not believe in “supernatural powers”: if occasionally you seem to know something, that too is the cognitive spirit; do not take it seriously, still less use it to tell people their fortunes.
  4. Safety note: if such phenomena keep recurring and will not go away, or begin to affect your sleep, mood or everyday judgment, pause your sitting practice, spend more time on outdoor activity and physical work, and consult a doctor if necessary.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is two paragraphs; the original book does not give the source.
  2. “Keep it seated in the central court” (坐镇中庭): the original book writes 座镇; 座 is interchangeable with 坐.
  3. The “remote viewing” and “television images” in Qiu Ling’s note are ways of speaking from the qigong boom around 1990; Qiu Ling cites them precisely to warn students not to be misled by them.

Chapter Nine · Other Secretly Transmitted True Formulas · 动处炼性，静处炼命

Refining Nature in Movement, Refining Life in Stillness

Refining nature in movement, refining life in stillness — cultivate nature when there are affairs to attend to; when there are none, let the spirit-light shine down on the elixir field to refine life; unless the true dynamic stirs of itself, never turn the River Chariot by force of intention

About This Chapter

This section is a way of joining cultivation with everyday life, and very practical:

  1. The two lines “refine nature in movement, refine life in stillness” already contain the essentials of the dual cultivation of nature and life.
  2. Refining nature in movement: “movement” does not mean moving without stop, but having affairs to attend to. Who has no relatives and friends coming and going, no clothing, food and household to see to? When there are affairs is just the time to cultivate nature: seeing, hearing, speaking and acting in accord with propriety; joy, anger, sorrow and pleasure in due measure; fulfilling one’s duty to others; clothing and food as is fitting; responding as things come and forgetting them once responded to — not thinking ahead before, not dwelling on it after, handling the present matter fairly, without the slightest worry or calculation. Without desire the mind is settled; with the mind settled, nature is settled.
  3. Why refining nature in movement progresses more easily: because facing circumstances there is a point for self-examination; as soon as a thought stirs or a desire arises, even if no one says anything, one feels uneasy oneself. Patriarch Qiu said: “I practiced in quiet places and gained little; only later, walking in the marketplace and attending to affairs, did I learn that refining in movement far surpasses refining in stillness!”
  4. Refining life in stillness: “stillness” does not mean not moving, but having no affairs to attend to. When there is nothing to do, whether walking, standing, sitting or lying, let the spirit-light shine down on the elixir field, let the spirit embrace the breath and the intention tie the breathing, spirit and breath never apart, floating lively back and forth in the elixir field; as days and months accumulate, true breath-energy fills the whole body, and the road of the River Chariot naturally opens.
  5. A warning: if it is not the true dynamic stirring of itself, and one casually uses intention to force the breath up onto the River Chariot, few are those who do not scorch the essence and blood of the whole body and bring on all kinds of strange disorders.
  6. When the River Chariot opens: spirit and breath relying on each other, essence and breath accumulating, true breath-energy surging and leaping through the body — add to this the kidney essence not leaking and the ears, eyes and mouth not running outward, and within a month or two the road of the River Chariot opens of itself.

Qiu Ling’s note: this is a fine method of closely joining qigong with life — “walking, standing, sitting and lying, never apart from this”. Refining nature in dealing with people and affairs is more effective than empty talk divorced from practice; when alone with nothing to do, not forgetting to illuminate the elixir field, the road of the Celestial Circuit is naturally not hard to open.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

昔人云：“动处炼性，静处炼命。”二语已包括性命双修之要，独惜人不知耳。何谓动处炼性？动非举动不停之谓，乃有事应酬之谓也。人生世间谁无亲戚朋友往来应酬，亦谁无衣服饮食身家意计，要知此有事之时，即是用功修性之时。于此不炼又从何处炼焉？

Earlier people said: “Refine nature in movement, refine life in stillness.” These two lines already contain the essentials of the dual cultivation of nature and life; it is only a pity that people do not know it. What is “refining nature in movement”? “Movement” does not mean moving without a moment’s stop, but having affairs, having things to attend to. Living in the world, who has no relatives and friends coming and going to be attended to? Who has no clothing and food, no household and livelihood to worry about? Know that the time when there are affairs is just the time to work at cultivating nature. If you do not refine here, where else would you refine?

§2
Original (Chinese)

我于此时视听言动必求中礼，喜怒哀乐必求中节，子臣弟友必求尽道，衣服饮食必求适宜，如此随来随应，随应随忘，以前不思，过后不忆，当前称物平施，毫无顾虑计较，所谓我无欲而心自定，心定而性自定。炼性之功莫此为最，否则，舍却现在，而于闲居独处之地自谓诚意正心，此皆空谈无着。

At such times I make sure that seeing, hearing, speaking and acting accord with propriety; that joy, anger, sorrow and pleasure are in due measure; that as a son, a subordinate, a younger brother and a friend I fulfill my duty; that clothing and food are as is fitting. In this way, when a matter comes I respond, and once I have responded I forget; before the matter I do not deliberate, after it I do not recall it; I handle the present fairly and reasonably (weighing things and dealing out evenly), without the slightest worry or calculation — this is what is called “I am without desire and my mind is settled of itself; my mind settled, my nature is settled of itself.” There is no better work for refining nature than this. Otherwise, setting aside the matter before one’s eyes and claiming to make one’s intentions sincere and rectify one’s mind while living at leisure alone — all of this is empty talk with nothing to stand on.

§3
Original (Chinese)

何如对境而有返勘之念，于时时应事即可时时养性。稍有念动欲起，人不指责于己，即己亦有不自安之处。此所以炼性于动处，其功夫为易进也。古人云：“炼己于尘俗”。邱祖云：“吾于静处修炼，不胜大益，及后游行于廛市，应酬于事为，始知动处之炼胜过静处之炼多矣！”

How much better, when facing circumstances, to have a thought of self-examination (fankan) — in responding to affairs at every moment, one can nourish nature at every moment. As soon as a thought stirs or a desire arises, even if no one else reproaches you, you feel uneasy yourself. That is why, refining nature in movement, the practice progresses easily. The ancients said: “Refine the self amid the dust of the world.” Patriarch Qiu (Qiu Chuji) said: “I practiced in quiet places and gained little benefit; only later, walking in the marketplace and attending to affairs, did I learn that refining in movement far surpasses refining in stillness!”

§4
Original (Chinese)

至于静处炼命又是何说？静亦非不动之谓，乃无事而未应酬之谓也。我能于无事之际，无论行住坐卧，总将一个神光下照于丹田之处，务使神抱住气，意系住息，神气恋恋两不相离，如此聚而不散，融会一团，悠扬活泼往来于丹田之中，如此日积月累，自然真气冲冲包固一身内外，而河车之路通矣！若非真机自动，漫将此气死死用意翻上河车，鲜有不烧灼一身精血，变生百怪诸症者。如此炼命，一日十二时中又有几时不得闻？只怕生等不自打紧耳。何患事累烦多而修性炼命无有空间之候耶！

Then what is meant by “refining life in stillness”? “Stillness” too does not mean not moving at all, but having no affairs, nothing to attend to. When I have nothing to do, whether walking, standing, sitting or lying, I always let a point of spirit-light shine down on the elixir field, making sure that the spirit embraces the breath and the intention ties the breathing, spirit and breath clinging lovingly to each other and never apart; thus gathered and not scattered, merged into one mass, floating lively back and forth in the elixir field; as days and months accumulate, naturally true breath-energy surges, enveloping and securing the whole body inside and out, and the road of the River Chariot opens! If it is not the true dynamic stirring of itself, and one casually uses intention to force the breath up onto the River Chariot, few are those who do not scorch the essence and blood of the whole body and bring on all kinds of strange illnesses. Refining life in this way, how many hours of the twelve double-hours of a day are there in which one has no leisure? I only fear that you do not seize them yourselves. Why worry that, with many affairs, there is no leisure for cultivating nature and refining life!

§5
Original (Chinese)

至若河车之路的于何时始通？如生等打坐时，始也神入气中，只觉神气相依，交会于黄庭之地，久久积精累气，则真气冲冲自踊跃于一身，觉得一身之中，真气已行包罗，我如在云烟之内，乘驭而上一般，如此再加积累功夫，肾精不泄，耳目口三宝亦无发通之处，不过一月两月之久，河车之路自通。惜人有此真气，又为尘垢所污、私欲所蒙耳。而五漏未除，精气又泄，所以将达其阶而又退下也。

As for when exactly the road of the River Chariot opens: when you sit, for example, at first the spirit enters the breath, and you only feel spirit and breath relying on each other, meeting in the Yellow Court (the lower elixir field); after long accumulating essence and breath, the true breath-energy surges and leaps of itself through the whole body, and you feel the whole body enveloped in true breath-energy, as if you were in clouds and mist, riding them upward. Adding to this the work of accumulation, the kidney essence not leaking, and the three treasures of ears, eyes and mouth not pouring outward, within no more than a month or two the road of the River Chariot opens of itself. It is a pity that people have this true breath-energy, yet it is stained by dust and dirt and clouded by selfish desires; the five leakages (the outward leaking of eyes, ears, nose, tongue and body) have not been removed, and essence and breath leak away again, so just as they are about to climb the steps, they slip back down.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

此为将气功与生活密切结合起来之良法，即“行住坐卧不离这个（气功）”，亦即《入药镜》所云：“一日内，十二时，意所到，皆可为”，也是古人所说的“道也者不可须臾离也”。

将性命分配于动和静的环境中去煅炼，这是合乎生活规律的。动处炼性，即待人接物中务求中礼、中节、合情合理。在待人接物中去炼性可以有所考验，当较脱离实践的空想空谈更有效益。静处炼命，即无事独处时不忘观照丹田，保持我身之精气神经常打成一片。学者如能做到此，则周天之路不难自通。

This is a good method of closely joining qigong with life — that is, “walking, standing, sitting and lying, never apart from this (qigong)”, which is also what the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine means by “within one day, in the twelve hours, wherever the intention goes, all can be done”, and what the ancients meant by “the Way cannot be departed from for an instant”.

Assigning nature and life to the settings of movement and stillness for refining accords with the pattern of life. Refining nature in movement means striving, in dealing with people and affairs, to accord with propriety, with due measure, with feeling and with reason; refining nature in dealing with people and affairs can be put to the test, and is more beneficial than empty imaginings and empty talk divorced from practice. Refining life in stillness means not forgetting to illuminate the elixir field when alone with nothing to do, so that the essence, breath and spirit of my body are constantly fused into one piece. If students can do this, the road of the Celestial Circuit is not hard to open of itself.

Key Terms

  • Refining nature in movement: cultivating the mind and nature when there are affairs to attend to.
  • Refining life in stillness: when there is nothing to do, letting the spirit-light shine down on the elixir field to nourish the original breath.
  • Accord with propriety, in due measure (中礼、中节): in keeping with propriety and with due measure.
  • Son, subordinate, younger brother, friend: one’s duties in each of these roles.
  • Weighing things and dealing out evenly (称物平施): assessing matters and handling them fairly.
  • Fankan (返勘): self-examination.
  • Refining the self amid the dust of the world: refining the self in worldly life; see refining the self.
  • Marketplace (廛市): the streets and markets.
  • River Chariot: true breath-energy moving along the Conception and Governing vessels; see Celestial Circuit.
  • The five leakages: the five roots (eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body) leaking outward.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Doing things is cultivating nature: at work, with people, handling household matters — respond when things come, let go once you have responded; do not anticipate before, do not savor afterward.
  2. Check yourself: at the end of the day, which matter has left a knot in your mind? That is where you have not yet “responded and forgotten”.
  3. Refine life in your spare moments: waiting for a bus, walking, lying down — bring your attention gently back to the lower belly, and let the breath and the mind rely on each other there.
  4. Safety note (important): Huang Yuanji says explicitly that if the true breath-energy has not stirred of itself and one forces the breath “up onto the River Chariot” by intention, it will “scorch the essence and blood of the whole body and bring on all kinds of strange disorders”. Do not use your intention to make the breath circle along the spine. If a swollen head, headache, dry heat, insomnia or palpitations appear, stop and return to natural relaxation.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is one paragraph; the original book does not give the source. It is divided into five short passages.
  2. “As for when exactly the road of the River Chariot opens” (至若河车之路的于何时始通): 的 here means “exactly, after all”.
  3. “How many hours are there in which one has no hearing” (又有几时不得闻): 闻 (“hearing”) should be 闲 (“leisure”); the translation follows 闲.
  4. “The Way cannot be departed from for an instant” (道也者，不可须臾离也): from the Doctrine of the Mean.

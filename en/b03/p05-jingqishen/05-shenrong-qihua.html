Chapter Five · Essence, Breath and Spirit · 神融气化

Spirit Melting, Breath Transforming

Spirit melting, breath transforming — the thousand words and ten thousand sayings of the alchemical way are nothing but the two words spirit and breath; leap out of nature and feeling and refine only a point of empty original breath; gathered to the utmost, it is nothing but one with the Great Void

About This Chapter

This section speaks of the end point of the cultivation of essence, breath and spirit: spirit and breath melting and transforming, returning to the Great Void.

  1. One sentence sums it up: the thousand words and ten thousand sayings of the alchemical way are nothing but the two words “spirit and breath”. At first spirit and breath are apart, so I regulate the breath with the spirit and concentrate the spirit with the breath; finally spirit and breath melt into emptiness and form a precious pearl the size of a grain of millet, suspended where neither the four elements nor the five phases can touch — a vast emptiness, which is “breaking through the Great Void, standing alone in the body of the Dharma King”.
  2. The work too is nothing but the two words “nature and feeling”: first harmonize feeling with nature, then return feeling to nature, until nature and feeling are one and the original Dharma-body appears.
  3. But this is not yet the highest: before a person is born, a point of numinous light is hidden in the Great Void — what nature, what feeling is there then? Even nature and feeling are still “the Prior Heaven within the Posterior Heaven”. The last step does not even establish the being or non-being of nature and feeling, but refines only a point of empty original breath — not even “emptiness” remains, and only then is it wondrous and unfathomable.
  4. The meaning of cultivating life (§3): if spirit and breath do not move through the whole body, the yin breath of the whole body is not transformed, and it is only a lump of dead flesh, beset by illness over time. So people are taught to move water and fire (spirit and breath) through the whole body, making the body of flesh and blood lively. But one must not cling to the softness and joy of the whole body; one must gather it back into the palace; nor must one cling rigidly to one point as the Yellow Court — gathered to the utmost, it is one with the Great Void, not knowing where it is. When the work reaches this point, there is a body beyond the body.

Qiu Ling’s note: the metaphors of the alchemical scriptures are of every kind — for nature they say mercury, dragon, fire, sun, Li, Qian, cinnabar, the lovely girl…; for life they say lead, tiger, water, moon, Kan, Kun, quicksilver, the infant… — but in truth it is only the two words “spirit and breath”. This section speaks of the advanced stage that runs from concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath and the meeting of spirit and breath to spirit melting, breath transforming and becoming one with the Great Void, which the ancients called “refining the body in true emptiness”.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

总之丹道千言万语，不过神气二字。始而神与气离，我即以神调气，以气凝神，终则神气融化于空，结成一团大如黍米之珠，悬于四大五行不着之处，一片虚空境象，是即打破太虚空独立法王身是也。而其功总不外性情二字，始而以性和情，继则以情归性，到性情合一，现出本来法身，即返本还原，复吾身受气之初。

In short, the thousand words and ten thousand sayings of the alchemical way are nothing but the two words “spirit” and “breath”. At the beginning spirit and breath are apart, so I use the spirit to regulate the breath and the breath to concentrate the spirit; finally spirit and breath melt into emptiness and form a precious pearl the size of a grain of millet, suspended where neither the four elements (earth, water, fire and wind, that is, the body of flesh) nor the five phases can touch, a scene of vast emptiness — this is what is called “breaking through the Great Void, standing alone in the body of the Dharma King” (breaking through the limits of emptiness, the Dharma-body standing alone). And this work is nothing but the two words “nature” and “feeling”: first use nature to harmonize feeling, then return feeling to nature, until nature and feeling are one and the original Dharma-body appears — this is returning to the root and restoring the origin, going back to when I first received breath.

§2
Original (Chinese)

然而还未到无上上之妙境也。夫人未生之初，一点灵光浑然藏于太虚，视之不见，听之不闻，搏之不得，此时有何性？又有何情？以此思之，连性情二字都是有形有质，只算得后天中之先天，以其犹有依傍也。到此绝顶一步，不著于有性，亦不著于无情，连性情之有无亦且不立，此即跳出性情，独炼一点虚无元气，所谓空空忘忘，其实空无所空，还于太虚，连天地都不为我作用。若只不离一个虚无，还是二乘，此连虚无亦无，所以神妙莫测也。要知此是金丹始终之功法也。

Yet this has not yet reached the wondrous realm beyond the highest. Before a person is born, a point of numinous light is undividedly hidden in the Great Void, unseen, unheard, untouched — what nature is there then? What feeling? Thinking of it this way, even the two words “nature and feeling” still have form and substance, and count only as “the Prior Heaven within the Posterior Heaven”, because they still have something to lean on. At this last step, one does not cling to having nature nor to having no feeling, not even establishing the being or non-being of nature and feeling — this is leaping out of nature and feeling and refining only a point of empty original breath, what is called “empty and forgotten”, in truth empty with nothing to empty, returning to the Great Void, so that even Heaven and Earth cannot make use of me (no longer bound by the limits of Heaven and Earth). If one still cannot leave behind an “emptiness”, that is still the two vehicles; here even emptiness is gone, and so it is wondrous and unfathomable. Know that this is the method of the Golden Elixir from beginning to end.

§3
Original (Chinese)

夫以神气不运于周身，则周身阴气不化，无非死肉一团，终是无用，且日积一日，不免疾病纠缠。故吾教修命，是教人以水火周身运动，使血肉之躯化为活活泼泼，随心所用，无所阻碍，到得一身毛窍晶莹肌肤细腻得矣！又不可贪神气之周于一身，苏软快乐，流荡忘返，还要收之回宫，不准外泄。却不要死死执着一个穴道，认为黄庭，须知收之至极处，无非与太虚同体，浑不知其所在。时而动也，亦与电光同用，一动即觉，一觉即灭，前无所来，后无所去，仍一杳冥光景，还于无极焉耳。工夫到此，身外有身。

If spirit and breath do not move through the whole body, the yin breath of the whole body cannot be transformed, and it is nothing but a lump of dead flesh, useless in the end, and accumulating day by day, one can hardly avoid being beset by illness. So when I teach people to cultivate life, I teach them to move water and fire (spirit and breath) through the whole body, making the body of flesh and blood lively, usable at will, without obstruction, until every pore of the body is crystalline and the skin fine — and it is accomplished! But one must not cling to the softness and joy when spirit and breath circulate through the whole body, drifting away and forgetting to return; one must gather it back into the palace (the lower elixir field), not letting it leak out. Yet neither cling rigidly to one point, thinking that is the Yellow Court — know that, gathered to the utmost, it is nothing but one with the Great Void, undividedly not knowing where it is. If at times it stirs, it is like a flash of lightning: as soon as it stirs it is noticed, as soon as it is noticed it vanishes, coming from nowhere before and going nowhere after, still a scene of the deep and obscure, returning to the Limitless and that is all. When the work reaches this point, there is “a body beyond the body”.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

丹经巧喻多端，如论顶上之性，喻之曰：汞、龙、火、根、日、魂、离、乾、己、天、君、虚、兔、无、主、浮、朱砂、扶桑、姹女、昆崙等。如论脐中之命，喻曰：铅、虎、水、蒂、月、魄、坎、坤、戊、地、臣、实、鸟、有、宾、沉、水银、华嶽、婴儿、曲江等。其实，只用神气二字可以概括之。古人说：“铅汞是水火，水火是龙虎，龙虎是神气，神气是性命，总来只是这两个字，两个字原是一个理”。

这里黄氏指出的气功态，乃是从凝神气穴、神气相交而至神融气化入于虚无而与太虚同体的高级阶段的气功态，所谓上上乘。这是从太极判两仪分，返还到无极的混沌。古人叫“真空炼形”，已达到内舍意念，外舍万缘，顿忘天地，粉碎形骸的境界，即“真空”的境界，亦即《清静经》所云：“内观其心，心无其心。外视其形，形无其形。”此时应是浑身俨如水晶塔，心身灿然，灵光显现，功中不见色身渐见法身，感到我与太虚同体。

The ingenious metaphors in the alchemical scriptures are of every kind. For instance, the “nature” at the crown of the head is likened to: mercury, dragon, fire, root, sun, hun soul, Li, Qian, ji, Heaven, ruler, emptiness, hare, non-being, host, floating, cinnabar, Fusang, the lovely girl, Kunlun and so on; the “life” in the navel is likened to: lead, tiger, water, stalk, moon, po soul, Kan, Kun, wu, Earth, minister, substance, crow, being, guest, sinking, quicksilver, Mount Hua, the infant, the winding river and so on. In truth, the two words “spirit and breath” alone can sum them all up. The ancients said: “Lead and mercury are water and fire, water and fire are dragon and tiger, dragon and tiger are spirit and breath, spirit and breath are nature and life — in all, it is only these two words, and the two words are originally one principle.”

The qigong state Huang points out here is the advanced stage that runs from concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath and the meeting of spirit and breath to spirit melting, breath transforming, entering emptiness and becoming one with the Great Void — what is called “the supreme vehicle”. This is the Supreme Ultimate, having divided into the two modes, returning to the chaos of the Limitless. The ancients called it “refining the body in true emptiness”: having reached the realm of letting go of intention within and all ties without, suddenly forgetting Heaven and Earth and shattering the bodily frame, which is the realm of “true emptiness” — what the Scripture of Clarity and Stillness says: “Look within at your mind, and the mind has no mind; look without at your form, and the form has no form.” At this time the whole body should be like a crystal pagoda, body and mind radiant, the numinous light appearing; in practice the physical body is not seen, gradually the Dharma-body is seen, and one feels oneself one with the Great Void.

Key Terms

  • Spirit and breath: the summary of all the metaphors of the alchemical way; see Lead and Mercury and conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • A pearl the size of a grain of millet: the elixir formed when spirit and breath melt into emptiness; see Golden Flower.
  • Where neither the four elements nor the five phases can touch: the emptiness that neither the body of flesh nor the five phases can reach.
  • Breaking through the Great Void, standing alone in the body of the Dharma King: going beyond the limits of emptiness, the Dharma-body standing alone; see Dharma-body.
  • Harmonizing feeling with nature, returning feeling to nature: nature is the original nature, feeling the stirring of nature.
  • The Prior Heaven within the Posterior Heaven: though nature and feeling belong to the Prior Heaven, they still have something to lean on and are not yet ultimate.
  • The two vehicles: a Buddhist term, the śrāvakas and pratyekabuddhas, here a metaphor for not yet reaching the ultimate.
  • Moving water and fire through the whole body: moving spirit and breath through the whole body, that is, cultivating life.
  • Returning to the palace: gathering back into the lower elixir field.
  • A body beyond the body: when the work reaches the utmost, the Dharma-body appears; see Dharma-body.
  • Refining the body in true emptiness: the realm of letting go of intention within and all ties without, suddenly forgetting Heaven and Earth.
  • Scripture of Clarity and Stillness: that is, The Most High Lord Lao’s Scripture of Constant Clarity and Stillness.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Hold on to the two words “spirit and breath”. All the coded terms of the alchemical books can in the end be brought back to spirit and breath. When reading alchemical books, do not let the terminology make your head spin.
  2. After circulating through the whole body, gather it back. When spirit and breath flow through the whole body and it feels soft and joyful, do not cling to it; gather it back into the elixir field.
  3. Gathering back is not guarding one place rigidly either. Gathered to the utmost, it is one with the Great Void — do not cling to “which point the Yellow Court is”.
  4. This section speaks of a very advanced stage; it is enough to understand it as a direction, without deliberately seeking scenes such as “the physical body is not seen”.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in two paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The first paragraph is divided into two short passages.
  2. “The wondrous realm beyond the highest” (无上上之妙境): so in the original book, meaning the wondrous realm above the highest.
  3. In the list of metaphors in Qiu Ling’s note, one item for life is printed 鸟 (“bird”) in the original book; it should be 乌 (“crow”, the golden crow). By the alchemical convention “the crow of the sun, the hare of the moon”, the crow (sun) should belong to nature and the hare (moon) to life; the original book lists “hare” under nature and “crow” under life, so the two seem to be transposed; we keep the original order and translate as “crow”.
  4. Ji 己 in the list in Qiu Ling’s note is printed in the original book in a form like 已, paired with wu 戊, and is read as 己.

Chapter Six · The Union of Kan and Li and of Qian and Kun · 篇首导读

Introduction to Chapter Six

What this chapter covers

“The union of Kan and Li” and “the union of Qian and Kun” are two of the most common expressions in alchemical books; this chapter is devoted to making clear what they are, how they are done and what scenes accompany them.

Huang Yuanji’s account can be summed up in two steps:

  • Kan and Li unite and the medicine is produced (below): the spirit-light shines down on the elixir field — “fire enters the land of water, spirit enters breath” — the spirit in the heart (Li) and the essence-breath in the body (Kan) meet, the elixir field grows warm and a moving breath arises; this is true lead (the medicine).
  • Qian and Kun unite and the elixir forms (above): this yang breath rises to the Muddy Pellet and unites with the yin essence there; the breath transforms into cool, fragrant, sweet dew (ghee poured on the crown), which then descends back to the elixir field to be warmly nourished and sealed — only this is forming the elixir.

Qiu Ling’s notes add: the methods of accumulating essence and breath come down to “guarding the center” (at the front of the body: warm nourishing in the elixir field, “the forward course is short”) and the “River Chariot” (at the back of the body: the turning of the Celestial Circuit, “the backward course is long”); obtaining the medicine by guarding the center is only half of the learning, and must be followed by the River Chariot.

The five sections of this chapter follow this order:

What Kan and Li are (the true meaning of Kan and Li) → Kan and Li unite and the medicine is produced → Qian and Kun unite and the elixir forms → ghee poured on the crown (the scene) → concentrating the spirit in the Muddy Pellet (the key step)

What this chapter is about (in one sentence)

The spirit-light shines down, fire enters the land of water, and true lead is born in the elixir field (Kan and Li unite and the medicine is produced); true lead rises naturally to the Muddy Pellet, the spirit is concentrated there for a moment, and it unites with the yin essence and transforms into sweet dew, which is swallowed and sent back to the elixir field for warm nourishing (Qian and Kun unite and the elixir forms) — the key formula is “move the spirit, not the breath”.

Reading tips

  1. Kan and Li are not the two organs of heart and kidneys. Huang Yuanji says plainly: “It is the breaths of water and fire that are Kan and Li, not the heart and kidneys” — Li refers to spirit (perception), Kan to essence and breath.
  2. The key formula of this chapter is “move the spirit, not the breath”. The rising of the breath happens naturally once true yang is ample; the spirit only presides and looks on.
  3. This chapter involves yang breath rising to the Muddy Pellet and concentrating the spirit in the Muddy Pellet, which belong to a fairly advanced stage; please pay close attention to safety. If you have not reached this stage, do not deliberately fix the mind on the head or lead the breath up to the head with the mind. People with yin deficiency and yang excess, high blood pressure, headaches or insomnia should especially avoid it. If a swollen feeling in the head, headache, a flushed face or ringing in the ears appear, stop at once and return to the spirit-light shining down on the elixir field.

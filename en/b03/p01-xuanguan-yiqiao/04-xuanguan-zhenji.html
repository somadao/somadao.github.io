Chapter One · The Single Opening of the Mysterious Pass · 玄关真际

The True Reality of the Mysterious Pass

The true reality of the Mysterious Pass — one awakening in chaos becomes the seed of immortality; awakening can be true or false; when a thought arises, awaken, and in awakening sweep it away

About This Chapter

The previous section described what the Mysterious Pass looks like in the body; this one explains what kind of “awakening” is true, and the most common faults:

  1. Where the awakening comes from: it is not sought on purpose, nor obtained by chance without intention; it comes “from being nourished long within the undifferentiated, stored up deeply”, until the numinous spring is touched and the sound of Heaven stirs of itself. Huang Yuanji gives a comparison: banking a fire — a mass of smoldering warmth lies within; no fire is seen, yet the fire is there; when it has been stored long enough and its strength is full, it suddenly blazes up to the sky.
  2. What to do after awakening: hold it at once; do not let it drift off with nowhere to return to; let only one point of numinous light remain “ever alert, ever bright and never darkened” — and yet without knowing that you are awake, illuminating, holding. This is called “when the time comes, the spirit knows”.
  3. True and false awakening: “People today, when they are dazed for a moment, take it for chaos; when knowledge suddenly arises, they take it for awakening — all this is taking a thief for one’s son.” The true awakening in chaos is “clear and bright, empty and alive”, with a state of deep stillness, perfect and luminous.
  4. The most common fault (second passage): before the Mysterious Pass opens, deliberately searching for it; after it opens, deliberately guarding it — like clouds that have dispersed, to which one adds smoke and dust oneself. “Putting a bed upon a bed, seeking movement within movement, seeking stillness within stillness.”
  5. The key formula: when thoughts run wild, gather them back — “gathering back is it”; when the spirit is dull and confused, set it in order — “setting in order is it”. Let thoughts be as tangled as they will; one awakening and it stirs, and they are swept away at once — “this is the Mysterious Pass”.

Qiu Ling’s notes put it in two sentences: the spring of the Mysterious Pass is “movement lodged within stillness”, and everything must follow its natural course without mixing in awareness or intentional thought; thinking about obtaining the Mysterious Pass is often exactly what keeps it from opening, and when it does open, thinking about how to guard it makes it close as soon as it opens, so that all previous effort is wasted.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

古云：“混沌一觉，即成仙种子。”洵非虚也。但要知此一觉，不是有心去寻，亦不是无心偶得。从混混沌沌中涵养既久，蕴蓄得深，灵机一触，天籁自动，所谓前后际断是。是即性光也，即正觉也，即无上正等正觉也，亦即本来人也。吾不先将神气二者交会于虚无窟内，积习既久，神融气畅，打成一片，两不分开，安有突然而醒之一觉哉？此殆无心有心，有心无心，有如种火者然。始而一团熏蒸之气凝聚于中，不见有火，而火自在此。犹混沌里内蕴知觉之神，追积之久久，火力蓄足，忽然阳光发现，烧天灼地，有不可遏之机。而对火初不自知，而亦不自禁，是即知觉中仍还混沌之象，此喻最切。你等须从混沌中有如此之蕴蓄，使神光凝而不散，然后一觉始圆明洞达，无碍无欠，才是我一点灵光本来真面目。尤要知一觉之前，只是一段细缊，一觉之后，只有一段灵光独运空中，并无半点念虑知觉夹入其中。莫道以外之事，就是我灵光一点，亦不自知也，惟适其天而已矣！

The ancients said: “One awakening in chaos becomes the seed of immortality.” (That single awakening within the undifferentiated is the seed of becoming an immortal.) This is truly no idle saying. But you must know that this awakening is not found by seeking it on purpose, nor obtained by chance without intention. Rather, when one has been nourished long within the undifferentiated and stored up deeply, the numinous spring is touched and the natural vital impulse stirs of itself — this is what is called “the gap between before and after” (the moment when the previous thought has passed and the next has not yet arisen). This awakening is the light of nature; it is right awakening; it is what the Buddhists call “unsurpassed, right and perfect awakening”; and it is also “the original person” (one’s original true self). If I did not first bring spirit and breath together in the Cave of Emptiness, and practice this long, until the spirit melts and the breath flows freely, the two fused into one and inseparable, how could there be this sudden awakening? This is probably “intention within no intention, no intention within intention”, just like banking a fire: at first a mass of warm, smoldering breath gathers within; no fire is seen, yet the fire is right there. Just so, within chaos lies hidden the spirit of awareness; when it has accumulated for a long, long time and the strength of the fire is full, the yang light suddenly appears, burning up to the sky with irresistible force. And the fire at first does not know itself, nor does it hold itself back — this is the image of “awareness still remaining within chaos”, and this comparison is the most fitting. You must have this kind of storing-up within chaos, letting the light of the spirit gather without scattering; then, when the awakening comes, it can be perfectly bright and penetrating, unobstructed and lacking nothing — only then is it the original true face of my one point of numinous light. Above all you must know: before the awakening, there is only a mass of diffuse, mingling breath; after the awakening, there is only one point of numinous light moving alone in emptiness, without the slightest admixture of thought or awareness. Not to speak of things outside the body — even my one point of numinous light does not know itself (does not observe itself); it simply follows its own nature!

§2
Original (Chinese)

至人穷究造化妙义，识得生死根源于此混沌忽然有觉，立地把持，不许他放荡无归，但只一晕灵光洞照当空，惺惺常存，炯炯不昧。初不知有所觉，并不知有所照，更不知有所把持，斯为时至神知。由此日运阳火，夜退阴符，包裹此太极无极之真谛，久久神充气盛。

The accomplished person investigates deeply the wondrous principles of the creative process of Heaven and Earth, and recognizes that the root of life and death lies in this moment of sudden awakening within chaos; so he holds it on the spot and does not let it drift away with nowhere to return to; he only lets a halo of numinous light shine through the void, alert and ever present, bright and never darkened. At first he does not know that he is awake, nor that he is illuminating, still less that he is holding — this is called “when the time comes, the spirit knows” (when the moment arrives, the spirit knows of itself). From then on, by day he moves the yang fire (advancing the fire), by night he withdraws by the yin tally (withdrawing the fire), wrapping up and nourishing this true meaning of the Supreme Ultimate and the Limitless; in time, the spirit becomes full and the breath abundant.

§3
Original (Chinese)

勿谓此一觉非我仙家根本，而别求妙术。盖此时一觉，但见我身心内空洞了灵，无尘无翳，不啻精金良玉。故一觉之后，其乐陶陶不可名状。是一念知觉，即一念之菩提；一刻晏息，即一刻之涅槃也。虽然混沌一觉，有真亦有伪，如今之人，昏迷一下，即以为混沌，知识忽起，即以为一觉，此皆认贼作子，断难有成。惟于无所有中，忽然天机发动，清清朗朗，虚虚活活，莫可名言，方算真混沌真觉，不然未有不以昏迷为混沌，以知识为一觉也。你等须知混沌非本，一觉非标，必从混沌中而有湛寂圆明、清虚玄朗之境，方得真际，切勿以恍惚二字混过可也。

Do not think that this awakening is not the root of us immortals, and go looking for some other wondrous art. For at the moment of this awakening one sees only that within body and mind all is empty and clear, without a speck of dust or a veil, just like pure gold or fine jade. So after the awakening, there is a joy beyond description. Truly one may say: one thought of awareness is one thought of bodhi (awakening); one moment of rest is one moment of nirvana (quiescent purity). Even so, the awakening in chaos can be true or false. People today, when they are dazed and muddled for a moment, take it for chaos; when thoughts and knowledge arise, they take it for awakening — all this is taking a thief for one’s son, and they can never succeed. Only when, in the midst of having nothing at all, the spring of Heaven suddenly stirs, clear and bright, empty and alive, beyond words, does it count as true chaos and true awakening; otherwise people invariably take torpor for chaos and thoughts for awakening. You must know: chaos itself is not the root, and awakening itself is not the goal; only when there arises from chaos a state of deep stillness, perfect brightness, clear emptiness and mysterious luminosity does one reach the true reality. Never let the word “dim and indistinct” serve to blur things over!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

玄关窍开之机实即寓动于静中。我神气初交，即有玄关窍开之萌芽在于其中，有如种火，一但神冥气合，遂火势燎原。但此属于人体之自然规律，一切按其自然行去，不得夹杂知觉意念，如此到一觉而动之时，才能圆明洞达，清清朗朗，活活泼泼，即黄氏所云：“方得真际”。

The spring of the opening of the Mysterious Pass is in fact “movement lodged within stillness”. When my spirit and breath first meet, the bud of the opening of the Mysterious Pass is already present within; it is like a banked fire: once spirit is merged and breath united, it spreads like a prairie fire. But this belongs to the natural laws of the human body; everything must be done following its natural course, without mixing in awareness or intentional thought. Then, when the moment of “one awakening and it stirs” arrives, it can be perfectly bright and penetrating, clear and bright, lively and alive — which is what Huang calls “only then does one reach the true reality”.

§4
Original (Chinese)

尘情杂虑纷纷扰扰时，从中一觉而出，即是玄关。所谓回头是岸。又曰：“彼岸非遥，回光返照即是。”但恐于玄关未开之前，先加一番意思去寻度；于玄关既开之后，又加一番意思去守护，此念虑纷纷，犹天本无云翳，云翳一散即现太空妙景，而却于云翳已散之后，又复加一番烟尘，转令清明广大之天，因之而窄逼难容，昏暗莫辨矣！

When worldly feelings and stray thoughts are in a tangle, to awaken out of them all at once — that is the Mysterious Pass. This is what is meant by “turn your head and there is the shore”. It is also said: “The other shore is not far; reverting the light to shine within is it.” The only fear is that, before the Mysterious Pass opens, one first adds a layer of intention to search for it and speculate about it, and after it opens, adds another layer of intention to guard it — with thoughts so tangled, it is as if the sky were originally without clouds, and when the clouds dispersed the wondrous scene of the vast void appeared, yet after the clouds had dispersed one added another layer of smoke and dust, turning the clear, vast sky narrow and cramped, so dark that nothing can be made out!

§5
Original (Chinese)

佛云：应如是住，如是降伏其心。此等玄机，总著不得一毫拟议，拟议即非。著不得半点思虑，思虑即错。惟于玄关未开时，我只顺其了照之意。于玄关既开后，我亦安其坐照之常。念若纷驰，我即收同，收回即是。神如昏罔，我即整顿，整顿即是。是如何之简而捷、便而易乎？特患人于床上安床，动中寻动，静里求静，就涉于穿凿。而玄关分明在前，却又因后天知虑遮蔽而不在矣！吾今示一要诀，任他思念纷纭莫可了却，我能一觉而动即便扫除，此即玄关。足见人之修炼，只此觉照之心，亦如天宫赤日，常须光明洞照，一毫昏暗不得，昏暗即落污暗地狱。苟能拨开云雾，青天白日明明在前，如生他想，即落凡夫窠臼。总之，无他玄妙，惟明心见性，乃修炼要诀。

The Buddha said: “Thus should one abide; thus should one subdue one’s mind.” (The mind should abide like this and be subdued like this.) In such mysterious matters not the slightest deliberation or comparison will do — to deliberate is already wrong; not half a thought of calculation will do — to calculate is already a mistake. Only this: before the Mysterious Pass opens, I simply follow that clear intention of looking on; after it opens, I simply rest in my ordinary sitting and looking on. If thoughts run wild, I gather them back — gathering them back is it; if the spirit grows dull and confused, I set it in order — setting it in order is it. How simple and direct, how convenient and easy this is! The only trouble is that people put a bed upon a bed (doing something superfluous), seek movement within movement, seek stillness within stillness, and so fall into forced interpretation. The Mysterious Pass is clearly right before them, yet because it is veiled by Posterior Heaven knowledge and thought, it is no longer there! Today I give you a key formula: let thoughts be as tangled as they will, impossible to bring to an end; if I can just awaken at once, they are swept away on the spot — this is the Mysterious Pass. So you see, a person’s cultivation is only this one mind of awakened illumination, like the red sun in the heavens, which must always shine brightly everywhere; not a trace of darkness is allowed — a moment of darkness and one falls into the hell of filth and darkness. If one can part the clouds and mist, the blue sky and bright sun are clearly there before one; if at that moment one gives rise to other thoughts, one falls back into the rut of ordinary people. In short, there is no other mystery: only clarifying the mind and seeing the nature (understanding one’s original mind and seeing one’s original nature) is the key to cultivation.

Qiu Ling's noteon §4–§5
Original note (Chinese)

行功时思虑着要得玄关，往往不得其开，且无一觉而动之境。盖因后天识神堵塞之故。一旦玄关窍开，一觉而动之后，我又思虑着如何守护，而非用一点灵光的真意主持，则玄关亦旋开旋闭，真精真气随之走泄，而前功尽废。

When, during practice, one thinks about obtaining the Mysterious Pass, it often fails to open, and there is no state of “one awakening and it stirs”. This is because the Posterior Heaven cognitive spirit blocks it. Once the Mysterious Pass has opened and one has awakened and stirred, if I then think about how to guard it, instead of letting the True Intention of the one point of numinous light preside, then the Mysterious Pass will close as soon as it opens, true essence and true breath will leak away with it, and all previous effort will be wasted.

Key Terms

  • One awakening in chaos becomes the seed of immortality: a saying of the ancients, which Huang Yuanji holds to be truly no idle claim.
  • The gap between before and after: the moment when the previous thought has gone and the next has not arisen; see True Intention.
  • Right awakening, unsurpassed right and perfect awakening, the original person: all refer to the original nature revealed in the awakening; see Original Spirit.
  • Banking a fire: fire hidden within smoldering warmth, unseen yet present, which after long accumulation spreads like a prairie fire — a metaphor for the spring of the Mysterious Pass as movement lodged within stillness.
  • When the time comes, the spirit knows: when the moment arrives, the spirit knows of itself, without deliberation.
  • Yang fire, yin tally: “by day he moves the yang fire, by night he withdraws by the yin tally”; see fire phasing.
  • Rest (yanxi): quiet repose.
  • Taking a thief for one’s son: taking dullness for chaos, and knowledge for awakening.
  • Deep stillness and perfect brightness, clear emptiness and mysterious luminosity: the state of the true awakening in chaos.
  • Searching and guarding: deliberately looking for the Mysterious Pass before it opens and deliberately guarding it after — both are faults.
  • Putting a bed upon a bed: doing something superfluous, adding another layer of intention on top of the original awakened illumination.
  • Gathering back is it, setting in order is it: when thoughts run wild, gather them back; when the spirit is dull, set it in order — that very moment is it.
  • Cognitive spirit: the Posterior Heaven spirit of thinking, which blocks the Mysterious Pass.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Do not “search for” the Mysterious Pass, and do not “guard” it. This is the most practical sentence in this section. Think “open, open quickly” and it will not open; once it opens, think “don’t run away, don’t run away” and it runs away.
  2. Tell the two faults apart: being dazed for a moment is not chaos, and a thought popping up is not awakening. True awakening is clear and bright, empty and alive.
  3. When thoughts wander, bring them back — “gathering back is it”. No need for regret, no need to fight the thoughts; the very moment you notice you have drifted is itself awakening, is itself the work.
  4. When the spirit grows dull, set it in order — “setting in order is it”. Sit up straight, rouse yourself a little, and go on sitting.
  5. The method in one sentence: let thoughts be as tangled as they will; I awaken, and they are swept away — that awakening is the Mysterious Pass.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in two paragraphs, both from the Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture. We have divided the first into three short passages and the second into two.
  2. “When it has accumulated for a long, long time”: the original book has a character resembling 追 (zhui, “to pursue”); from the sense it should be 迨 (dai, “until”).
  3. “And the fire at first does not know itself”: the character 对 (dui) in the original appears to be superfluous; we translate as if it were absent.
  4. “A mass of diffuse, mingling breath” (yiduan xiyun 一段细缊): so printed in the original; it is the same as 絪缊 or 氤氲, the diffuse, undifferentiated state of primordial breath.
  5. “I gather them back” (wo ji shou tong 我即收同): so printed in the original; 同 is a misprint for 回 (the next phrase indeed reads “gathering back is it”).
  6. “Thus should one abide; thus should one subdue one’s mind”: from the Diamond Sutra.
  7. “Once spirit is merged” (yidan 一但): so printed in the original; 一但 stands for 一旦, “once”.

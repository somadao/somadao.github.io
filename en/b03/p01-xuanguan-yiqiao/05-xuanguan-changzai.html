Chapter One · The Single Opening of the Mysterious Pass · 玄关常在

The Mysterious Pass Is Always Present

The Mysterious Pass is always present — do not seek it narrowly in great stillness; as when someone calls your name and you answer with a start, that is the Mysterious Pass

About This Chapter

The preceding sections may give the impression that the Mysterious Pass opens only after very deep concentration. This section is devoted to dispelling that misunderstanding — the Mysterious Pass is present at all times:

  1. There is no need to wait for great concentration and great stillness. “I desire humaneness, and humaneness arrives” — within a little concentration and a little stillness, one suddenly becomes still and settled, knowing nothing at all, and suddenly a mind of awareness arises, with nothing thought before and nothing recalled after, clean and clear: this is “the original face of the single breath of Qian the Origin”.
  2. The same holds in the midst of turmoil. When affairs are hurried and thoughts are countless, one only has to turn the light back for one thought — like a drunkard collapsed by the roadside who is woken by a bowl of cold water — and this waking is the Mysterious Pass.
  3. The finest comparison: someone calls your name. You will surely answer. Who is it that answers? Not the mouth, but the “true original substance of the mind” that governs the answer. The Mysterious Pass lies in that instant of “answering with a start”: after the answer, yin and yang separate, and it is no longer the Mysterious Pass.
  4. Movement is not it, and stillness is not it either; it lies between stillness reaching its extreme and turning to movement, and movement reaching its extreme and turning to stillness.
  5. The wondrous formula (second passage): the Mysterious Pass is present at every moment; “it is enough to let the spirit preside over it”. Let the waxing and waning of the breath’s dynamism follow its own rise and fall, but the presiding master must not wax and wane along with it.

Qiu Ling’s note: in the qigong state, whenever one can enter the obscure, the Mysterious Pass opens, and entering the obscure depends on the true intention. The true intention is born “when the previous thought has gone and the next has not yet followed”; at the moment of “one awakening and it stirs”, the true intention is most easily lost — and if it is lost, this awakening is not a true awakening — so it must rise at once and take charge.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

夫玄关一窍，是吾人炼道丹头，勿区区于大静中求。孔子曰：“我欲仁斯仁至矣！”若必待大定大静然后才有，孔子又不如是便宜指点。可见学人修养时，忽然静定，一无所知所觉，突起知觉之心，前无所思，后无所忆，干干净净，即乾元一气之本来面目也。从此一念修持，采取烹炼，封固温养，久久自成不测之仙。

The single opening of the Mysterious Pass is the elixir head (the starting point and root) of our refining of the Way; do not seek it narrowly only in great stillness. Confucius said: “I desire humaneness, and humaneness arrives!” (I want humaneness, and humaneness comes — there is no need to seek it far away.) If the Mysterious Pass came only after great concentration and great stillness, Confucius would not have pointed things out so conveniently. So we see that when a student, in self-cultivation, suddenly becomes still and settled, knowing nothing and aware of nothing, and suddenly a mind of awareness arises, with nothing thought before and nothing recalled after, clean and clear — this is the original face of the single breath of Qian the Origin (the primal mass of pure yang breath of Heaven and Earth). Begin to practice from this one thought — gathering, cooking and refining, sealing and warmly nourishing — and in time one naturally becomes an immortal of unfathomable wonder.

§2
Original (Chinese)

然而小定小静亦见天心之来复，若人事匆匆，思虑万端，事为烦扰，如葛之绿蔓，树之引藤，愈起愈纷，愈纷愈乱，无有止息，为之奈何？但能一念回光，一心了照，如酒醉之夫，迷睡路傍，忽地一碗凉水，从头而喷去，猛然一惊而醒，始知昏昏迷迷，一场空梦，此即玄关窍也。

Even so, within a little concentration and a little stillness one can also see the return of the Celestial Heart (the original mind reappearing, like the return of one yang). But if affairs are hurried and thoughts are countless, if one is harassed by all sorts of matters, like the green tendrils of the kudzu vine or the climbing creepers on a tree, growing ever more numerous and ever more tangled, without end — what is to be done? Simply revert the light in one thought (turn the thought around and gather back the light of the mind that was racing outward), and look on clearly with a single mind — like a drunkard asleep in a stupor by the roadside, when suddenly a bowl of cold water is poured over his head and he wakes with a start, only then realizing that all his muddled daze was an empty dream — this waking is the opening of the Mysterious Pass.

§3
Original (Chinese)

昔鹤臞子示，真元心体实自玄关一窍寻来，动静与俱，随时皆有，但非感动无以觉耳。试有人呼子之名，子必应之，有此一应是谁？虽曰是口，然主宰其应者，是真元心体也。是一应间，直将真元心体凭空提出与人看，此真善于指点者也！

Formerly Heququzi taught: the true original substance of the mind is in fact found in the single opening of the Mysterious Pass; it is there in movement and in stillness, present at every moment, only it cannot be perceived without being stirred. Suppose someone calls your name: you will surely answer. In this answering — who answers? Though we say it is the mouth, what governs the answer is the true original substance of the mind. In this one answer, the true original substance of the mind is lifted out of thin air and shown to people — he was truly good at pointing things out!

§4
Original (Chinese)

是知知觉不起时，万境皆灭，即呼即应，真元显露，方知此心不与境俱灭。知觉纷起时，万境皆生，一呼一应，真元剖露，方知此心不与境俱生。以此思之，知觉不起时，心自若也。知觉纷起时，心亦自若也，以其为虚而灵也，虚则有何生灭哉！总之，此窍只此息之顷，以前不是，以后不是，如人当阒寂之时，忽有人呼其名，猛然一应，即玄关矣！一应之后，阴阳判为两仪，又非玄关也。玄关者，太极将两分两仪之时也。动不是，静亦不是，其在静极而动，动极而静之间乎？！所谓动静无端，玄关亦无端，学者须善会之。

From this we know: when awareness does not arise, all outer scenes vanish, yet when called one answers at once, and the true origin is revealed — only then does one know that this mind does not vanish together with the scenes. When awareness arises in profusion, all scenes appear, yet with one call and one answer the true origin is laid bare — only then does one know that this mind does not arise together with the scenes. Thinking of it this way: when awareness does not arise, the mind is as it is; when awareness arises in profusion, the mind is still as it is — because it is empty and numinous; and since it is empty, how could it arise or vanish! In short, this opening lies only in the space of this one breath: before it, it is not; after it, it is not; as when a person in utter silence is suddenly called by name and answers with a start — that answer is the Mysterious Pass! After the answer, yin and yang divide into the two modes (the mind separates into subject and object, before and after), and it is no longer the Mysterious Pass. The Mysterious Pass is the instant when the Supreme Ultimate is about to divide into the two modes (yin and yang). Movement is not it, and stillness is not it either; perhaps it lies at the juncture where stillness reaches its extreme and is about to move, and movement reaches its extreme and is about to be still! As the saying goes, “movement and stillness have no beginning” (they cycle endlessly, with no starting point to be found); the Mysterious Pass too has no fixed sign, and students must understand this well.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

真元心体，清代鹤臞子著《唱道真言》云：“一心端坐，洞然玄朗，无渣滓，无知识，即先天性体也。”又曰：“吾教子静坐，一无知觉，忽有人呼子之名，子必跃然应之曰：在。这便是真元心体”。

玄关窍开，无论在大静小静中均可出现，但均有一觉而动之境。一呼一应者，只是借以说明玄关窍开就是真元显露而已。玄关窍开本是人体潜能，故曰随时皆有，但非感动无以显露。

“True original substance of the mind”: the True Words of Chanting the Way by Heququzi of the Qing dynasty says: “Sitting upright with a single mind, vast, open, mysterious and bright, without dregs, without knowledge — this is the Prior Heaven substance of the nature.” It also says: “I teach you to sit quietly, without any awareness; suddenly someone calls your name, and you will surely spring up and answer: ‘Here!' That is the true original substance of the mind.”

The opening of the Mysterious Pass can occur in great stillness or in little stillness, but in either case there is the state of “one awakening and it stirs”. “One call and one answer” is only borrowed to show that the opening of the Mysterious Pass is simply the revealing of the true origin. The opening of the Mysterious Pass is originally a latent potential of the human body, which is why it is said to be present at every moment; but without being stirred it has no way to reveal itself.

§5
Original (Chinese)

玄关一窍随时都在，只须一觉心了照之，主宰之，则玄关常在，而太极常凝矣！特患人不入于杳冥，无患玄关之不发见也。要知此个杳冥，不是空空可得，须从动极而静，真意一到为造化，才能入于杳冥。及静极而动，此时阴阳交媾，将判未判，未判欲判，恍恍惚惚中，忽觉真铅发生，此即玄关现象，全赖元神为之主持。吾见生迷于此个消息久矣，今将妙理一口吐出，俾生等知得玄关一窍无时不有，无在不然，但以神主之足矣！至于气机之消长，且听其盛衰，而主宰切不可因之有消长，此即是真正妙诀。

The single opening of the Mysterious Pass is present at every moment; one only needs to look on it clearly with the mind of that one awakening and preside over it, and the Mysterious Pass will always be present, and the Supreme Ultimate (that originally undivided point) will always stay gathered! The only fear is that people cannot enter the obscure (the deep, silent state beyond knowing and perceiving); there is no fear that the Mysterious Pass will fail to appear. You must know that this obscurity is not to be had by staring blankly into emptiness; only when movement reaches its extreme and returns to stillness, and the True Intention arrives and sets the work of creation going, can one enter the obscure. When stillness reaches its extreme and there is movement again, at this moment yin and yang unite, about to divide yet not divided, not divided yet about to divide; in the dim and indistinct one suddenly feels true lead (the Prior Heaven true yang breath) being produced — this is the phenomenon of the Mysterious Pass, and it depends entirely on the Original Spirit to preside over it. I have seen you students bewildered by this news for a long time; today I speak out this wondrous principle in one breath, so that you know: the single opening of the Mysterious Pass is present at every moment and in every place, and it is enough to let the spirit preside over it! As for the waxing and waning of the breath’s dynamism (now strong, now weak), let it rise and fall as it will; but the one point of spirit that presides must never wax and wane along with it — this is the truly wondrous formula.

Qiu Ling's noteon §5
Original note (Chinese)

气功态中凡能入于杳冥，便有玄关窍开，故云玄关常在。而要入于杳冥，则靠真意之作用。真意生于前念已去，后念未续之际，即“前后际断”，此时动极而静，无知无觉，一旦一觉而动，乃静极而动之初，因一动故，真意易失，失则一觉并非真觉，须得真意立即起而主宰之，方得玄关之妙。故其诀为不管气机之消长，我真意不受其影响而始终主宰之。

In the qigong state, whenever one can enter the obscure, the Mysterious Pass opens; that is why it is said that “the Mysterious Pass is always present”. And entering the obscure depends on the working of the true intention. The true intention is born when the previous thought has gone and the next has not yet followed, that is, “the gap between before and after”; at this moment movement has reached its extreme and become stillness, without knowing or perceiving. Once one awakens and it stirs, it is the beginning of stillness reaching its extreme and turning to movement — and because of this movement, the true intention is easily lost; if it is lost, this awakening is not a true awakening. The true intention must rise at once and take charge; only then does one obtain the wonder of the Mysterious Pass. So the formula is: however the breath’s dynamism waxes and wanes, my true intention is unaffected by it and presides from beginning to end.

Key Terms

  • Elixir head: the starting point and root of refining the elixir.
  • I desire humaneness, and humaneness arrives: from the Analects (“Shu er”); a metaphor for the Mysterious Pass arriving with a single thought, with no need to seek it far away.
  • The original face of the single breath of Qian the Origin: that clean, clear thought with nothing thought before and nothing recalled after.
  • Return of the Celestial Heart: even in a little concentration and stillness there is the return of one yang; see Mysterious Opening / Mysterious Pass.
  • A bowl of cold water poured over the head: the waking of turning the light back for one thought, a metaphor for the Mysterious Pass.
  • True original substance of the mind: the substance that governs the answer in one call and one answer; see Original Spirit.
  • The true origin revealed, the true origin laid bare: whether awareness does not arise or arises in profusion, with one call and one answer the substance of the mind appears.
  • Utter silence (qiji): silent and soundless.
  • The instant when the Supreme Ultimate is about to divide into the two modes: before the answer, when yin and yang are not yet divided.
  • Movement and stillness have no beginning, and the Mysterious Pass has no beginning either: the Mysterious Pass has no fixed time or place.
  • The obscure: see the dim and obscure.
  • True lead: the Prior Heaven true yang breath; see Lead and Mercury.
  • The presiding master must never wax and wane along with it: the breath’s dynamism may rise and fall, but the true intention remains the same throughout.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Do not wait for “great stillness”. At any moment in daily life, turning the light back for one thought, waking up at once, is an opportunity for the Mysterious Pass.
  2. The “someone calls your name” exercise: when during sitting there is suddenly a sound or someone calls, in that instant of “answering” the mind is awake, clean, without before or after — recognize this, and you recognize the true original substance of the mind.
  3. It works in turmoil too: when your thoughts grow more and more tangled like creeping vines, turn the light back for one thought, as if woken by cold water — that one moment is enough.
  4. Let the breath’s dynamism follow its course; the master does not follow it. When the sensation of breath during sitting is now strong, now weak, now present, now absent, do not chase it and do not suppress it; that one point of true intention stays the same throughout. This is the wondrous formula of this section.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in two paragraphs, both from the Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture. We have divided the first into four short passages.
  2. “Heququzi” (鹤臞子): a Qing-dynasty alchemist, the transmitter of the True Words of Chanting the Way. 臞 is read qú, “lean”.
  3. “The true origin is laid bare” (zhenyuan poulu 真元剖露): so printed in the original; it pairs with “the true origin is revealed” (真元显露) in the previous sentence and means the same.
  4. “I have seen you students (sheng) bewildered…”, “so that you students…”: sheng refers to the disciples attending the lectures.
  5. “The mind of that one awakening”: the original reads 只须一觉心了照之, i.e. to look on it clearly with the mind of one awakening.
  6. “Perhaps it lies at the juncture…!”: the original punctuation is “?!”, which we keep.

Chapter One · The Single Opening of the Mysterious Pass · 回光返照

Reverting the Light to Shine Within

Reverting the light to shine within — the capacity of great virtue is the opening of the Mysterious Pass; the spirit-light shines down on the Palace of Kan, Kan and Li unite and true essence is born

About This Chapter

This is the first section of the book, taken from Huang Yuanji’s Commentary on the Daode jing, chapter 21 (the chapter “The capacity of great virtue follows the Way alone”). Huang Yuanji takes Laozi’s “dim, indistinct” and “deep, obscure” and matches them line by line to the scenes that really appear in quiet sitting, explaining clearly how the opening of the Mysterious Pass opens:

  1. The Mysterious Pass is an “empty vessel”, but not dull emptiness. “The capacity of great virtue” is the opening of the Mysterious Pass — Heaven, Earth, people and things are all born out of this “emptiness”; but emptiness without reality is dull emptiness, with no medicine to gather. It must be “empty yet containing the utmost reality, nothing yet comprising the utmost being”.
  2. Beginning: put down all ties, nourish stillness for a moment, and contemplate this opening. In the dimness, an image arises out of no image — this is the floating of one’s own light of nature, called “the birth of faint yang”.
  3. The spirit-light shines down: then borrow this bit of spirit-light, join it to the light of the two eyes and shine down on the Palace of Kan, using the method of “gathering the muni in two stages” to bring the breath of the yinqiao to meet it in the Cavity of Breath — fire enters the bottom of the water, metal is born in the water, and the essence of Kan and Li is born.
  4. The “sign” when true essence is born: the body stands like a wall, the intention is like cold ashes, the whole body is soft and joyful, and the essence and breath of the four limbs and hundred members all return into the mysterious opening — “melting like ice thawing, vast like the rising tide”.
  5. The greatest danger comes at this moment: with one stray thought or a trace of lust, the true essence leaks away. So one must guard within with a single mind, “bear it and bear it again”, and in two or three months the outer yang will naturally draw back.

Qiu Ling’s note is long and is a master key to the whole book: it sets out the main cavity positions Huang Yuanji uses (the upper, middle and lower elixir fields, the yinqiao, the Root of the Mountain), explains that the central yellow, the central palace, the Yellow Court, the Palace of Kan and the Cavity of Breath in Huang’s usage all refer to the lower elixir field, and explains that Huang advocates joining the spirit-light of the two eyes to the light of the nature and shining down on the elixir field, rather than fixing the mind on the ancestral opening between the two eyes — because the latter is not suitable for people with yin deficiency and yang excess, or with high blood pressure.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

孔德之容，即玄关窍也。古云：一孔玄关窍，乾坤共合成，中藏神气穴，名为坎离精。又曰：“一孔玄关大道门，造铅结丹此中存”。《契》曰：“此两孔穴法，金气亦相胥”。故道曰：玄牝之门。儒曰：道义之门。佛曰：不二法门。总之，皆孔之德器能容。天地人物咸生自个中，无非是空是道，非空非道，即空即道，空与道两不相离，无空则无道，无道亦即无空。故曰：“惟道是从”。欲求道者，舍此空器，何所从哉！但空而无状，即属顽空，学者又从何处采药而炼丹乎？必须虚也而含至实，无也而赅至有，方不为一偏之学。

What Laozi calls “the capacity of great virtue” (the empty vessel contained by great virtue) is the opening of the Mysterious Pass. The ancients said: “The single opening of the Mysterious Pass is formed by Qian and Kun together; within it is hidden the cavity of spirit and breath, called the essence of Kan and Li.” They also said: “The single opening of the Mysterious Pass is the gate of the great Way; making lead and forming the elixir both lie within it.” The Cantong qi says: “In the method of these two openings, metal and breath depend on each other.” So the Daoists call it “the gate of the Mysterious Female”, the Confucians call it “the gate of the Way and righteousness”, and the Buddhists call it “the gate of non-duality” — the names differ, but all speak of what the empty vessel of “great virtue” contains. Heaven, Earth, people and things are all born from within it: it is emptiness and it is the Way; it is neither emptiness nor the Way; emptiness is the Way, and the Way is emptiness. Emptiness and the Way cannot be separated: without emptiness there is no Way, and without the Way there is no emptiness to speak of. That is why Laozi says: “It follows the Way alone.” If those who seek the Way abandon this empty vessel, what else could they follow? But if it is merely empty — so empty that there is no sign of anything — it belongs to dull emptiness (dead emptiness, empty and without life), and where then would the student gather the medicine and refine the elixir? It must be empty yet containing the utmost reality, nothing yet comprising all being; only then is it not a one-sided learning.

§2
Original (Chinese)

修行人但将万缘放下，静养片晌，观照此窍，惚兮似无，恍兮若有，虚极静笃之中，神机动焉，无象者有象。此离己之性光木火浮动之象，即微阳生时也。再以此神光偶动之机，合目光而下照，恍兮若有觉，惚兮若无知，其中之阳物动焉，此离光之初交于坎宫者。

Practitioners need only put down all ties, nourish stillness for a moment and contemplate this opening: dim and indistinct, as if there were nothing, and yet as if there were something. In the midst of emptiness brought to its utmost and stillness steadfastly guarded, the spirit’s mechanism stirs, and where there was no image an image appears (light appears before the eyes, for example). This is the image of one’s own light of nature in the palace of Li (the heart) — belonging to wood and fire among the five phases — floating up; it is the moment when “faint yang” (weak yang breath) is first born. Then take advantage of this chance stirring of the spirit-light, join it to the light of the two eyes and shine downward: dim and indistinct, as if aware and as if unaware; at this time the thing belonging to yang within begins to stir — this is the light of Li (the light in the heart) uniting for the first time with the Palace of Kan (the lower elixir field).

§3
Original (Chinese)

其时气机微弱，无可采取，惟有二候采牟尼法，调度阴跻之气，相会于气穴之中。调度采取为一候，归炉温养为一候。依法行持，不片晌间，火入水底，水中生金，杳杳冥冥，不知其极，此神气交而坎离之精生矣！然真精生时，身如壁立，意若寒灰，自然而然周身苏软快乐，四肢百体之精气，尽归于玄窍之内。其中大有信在，溶溶似冰泮，浩浩如潮生，非若前此之恍恍若有，惚惚似无，不可指名者也。

At this time the mechanism of the breath is still very weak, and there is nothing to gather; one can only use the method of “gathering the muni in two stages”: direct the breath of the yinqiao (the perineum) to meet the spirit-light in the Cavity of Breath (the lower elixir field). Directing and gathering is one stage; returning it to the furnace and nourishing it warmly is another. Practice according to this method, and in no time the fire (the spirit-light shining down) enters the bottom of the water (the lower elixir field), and metal is born in the water (the Prior Heaven true yang breath arises within it), deep and obscure, boundless — this is spirit and breath uniting, and the essence of Kan and Li being born! When true essence is born, the body stands upright like a wall and the intention is still like cold ashes; naturally the whole body becomes soft and joyful, and the essence and breath of the four limbs and the hundred members all return into the mysterious opening. Within this there is a sure sign (a “sign”): melting and flowing like ice thawing, vast and surging like the rising tide — no longer dim and indistinct, as if present and as if absent, beyond naming, as before.

§4
Original (Chinese)

此个真精，实为真一之精，非后天交感之精可比，亦即为天地人物发生之初，公共一点真精是矣，如冬至之阳，半夜之子。一岁一日之成功，虽不仅此，而气机要皆自此发端，俨若千层台之始于累土，万里行之始于足下，一般为天地人物生生之本。本原一差，末流何极？以故自古及今，举凡修道之士皆不离此真气之采，然后有生发之象。遍阅众物初生，无不同此一点真精成象而成形。我又何以知众物之生有同然哉？以此空窍之中，真气积累，久则玄关开而真精生焉。要之，恍是光之密，惚是几之微，离中真阴，是为恍惚中之物，坎中真阳，是杳冥中之精。学者必知之真，而后行之至也。

This true essence is truly “the essence of the true One” (the pure, unmixed essence of the Prior Heaven), not to be compared with the Posterior Heaven essence of sexual intercourse; it is the one point of true essence shared by Heaven, Earth, people and things when they first arise — like the one yang first born at the winter solstice in the year, or the one yang first stirring at the zi hour of midnight in the day. Although the harvest of a year and the achievement of a day do not depend on this alone, every mechanism of the breath starts from here; just as a tower of a thousand storeys begins from heaped earth and a journey of ten thousand li begins beneath one’s feet, it is likewise the root of the ceaseless generation of Heaven, Earth, people and things. If the root goes wrong, how far will the branches stray? So from ancient times until now, all who cultivate the Way have never departed from gathering this true breath, and only then is there the sign of growth. Look at all things when they are first born: every one takes image and form through this same point of true essence. And how do I know that the birth of all things is the same? Because in this empty opening the true breath accumulates, and in time the Mysterious Pass opens and true essence is born — one’s own body is the proof. In short: Laozi’s “dim” is the density of light; “indistinct” is the subtlety of the incipient movement; the true yin within Li (the heart) is the “thing” in “within the dim and indistinct there is a thing”; the true yang within Kan (the kidneys) is the “essence” in “within the deep and obscure there is essence”. The student must first know it truly; only then can he carry it through to the utmost.

§5
Original (Chinese)

此恍兮惚，是性光发越，故云有象。惚兮恍，是性光下照坎宫，而真阳发动，故云有物。窈冥之精，乃二五之精，故云甚真。欲得真精，须知真信。真信者，阴阳迭运不失其候之调侯。其信之初至，的当不易，即行擒伏之功得矣。凡人修炼之初，必要恍惚杳冥，而后人欲净尽，天理常存，凡息自停，真息乃见。此何以故？盖人心太明，知觉易生，若到杳冥，知觉不起，即元性元命打成一片。此个恍惚杳冥，大为修士之要。学人当静定之时，忽然偶生知觉，此时神气凝聚，胎田浑然粹然，自亦不知其所之，此性命返还于无极之天也。虽然外有是理，而丹田中必有融和气机，方为实据。由此一点融和，采之归炉，封固温养，自能发为真阳一气。

Laozi’s “dim, then indistinct” is the light of the nature spreading outward, so he says “there is an image”. “Indistinct, then dim” is the light of the nature shining down on the Palace of Kan and the true yang stirring, so he says “there is a thing”. “The essence of the deep and obscure” is “the essence of the two and the five” (the union of the two breaths of yin and yang with the quintessence of the five phases), so he says “its essence is most true”. To obtain the true essence, one must recognize the true “sign”. The true sign is the exact message of the right moment in the alternating movement of yin and yang, which never misses its time (like the tide coming in on time). When the sign first arrives, it is exact and unmistakable; at that moment carry out the work of “capturing and subduing” (taking hold of it and bringing it down into the furnace), and you have it. When ordinary people begin to cultivate, they must enter the dim and obscure (a state of stillness, dim and indistinct, deep and obscure); only then are human desires cleansed and the principle of Heaven (the original nature) constantly present, the ordinary breath (breathing through mouth and nose) stops of itself, and the True Breath appears. Why is this? Because the human mind is too clear and clever, and awareness (thoughts and discrimination) arises easily; if one reaches the deep and obscure, awareness does not arise, and the original nature and original life (mind-spirit and original breath) merge into one piece. This dim and obscure is the most important threshold for the practitioner. When the student is in still concentration and suddenly a flicker of awareness arises, at this time spirit and breath are concentrated, the embryo field (the lower elixir field) is whole and pure, and one does not even know where it has gone — this is nature and life returning to “the heaven of the Limitless” (the source before yin and yang were divided). But although there is this principle on the outside, there must really be a melting, harmonious mechanism of breath in the elixir field for it to count as real evidence. Gather this point of melting harmony back into the furnace, seal and secure it, nourish it warmly, and it will naturally develop into the one breath of true yang.

§6
Original (Chinese)

但行功到此，大有危机，惟有一心内守，了照当中，方能团聚为丹药，可以长生不老。若生一他念，此个元气即已杂后天而不纯矣！若动一淫一思，此个气机即驰于外，而真精从此泄漏矣！古人云：泄精一事，不必夫妇交媾，即此一念之动，真精也不守舍，如走丹。一般学人必心与气合，息与神交，常在此腔子里，久之自有无穷趣味生来。然而真难事也。设能识透玄机，亦无难事，起初不过用提掇之功，不许这点真气驰而在下，亦不许这个真气分散六根门头，总是一心皈命，五体投诚，久久自然精满不思色矣！愿学者保守元精，毫不渗漏，始因常行熟道，觉得不易，苟能一忍再忍，不许念头稍动，三两月间，外阳自收摄焉。外阳收摄，然后见身中元气充足，而长生不老从此得矣！

But when the work reaches this point, there is great danger: only by guarding within with a single mind and illuminating the center clearly can it gather into the elixir medicine, which can bring long life without aging. If one other thought arises, this original breath is already mixed with the Posterior Heaven and is no longer pure! If one stirs a trace of lust or a single thought, this mechanism of the breath rushes outward, and the true essence leaks away from then on! The ancients said: the leaking of essence does not require intercourse between husband and wife; the mere stirring of a single thought makes the true essence leave its dwelling, like the elixir running off. Students in general must join mind and breath and unite breathing and spirit, constantly keeping it within the cavity of the body (the empty hollow within the body); in time, endless delight will naturally arise. Yet this is truly hard. But if one can see through the mysterious mechanism, it is not hard either: at first one uses only the work of “lifting” (lifting gently upward with the intention), not allowing this point of true breath to rush downward, nor to scatter to the gates of the six faculties (the senses that face outward: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body and mind); always devoting oneself wholly to the Way with a single mind, surrendering with body and mind, and in time the essence will naturally be full and one will not think of lust! I hope students will guard the original essence, letting not a drop seep away. At first, because one is used to walking the familiar road (habitual desire), it will feel hard; but if one can bear it and bear it again, not allowing a single thought to stir, within two or three months the outer yang (the genitals) will naturally draw back. Once the outer yang draws back, one sees the original breath in the body become full, and long life without aging is obtained from here!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§6
Original note (Chinese)

此为黄元吉氏《道德经注释》书第二十一章注文。

老子原文为：“孔德之容，唯道是从。道之为物，惟恍惟惚。惚兮恍兮，其中有象；恍兮惚兮，其中有物。窈兮冥兮，其中有精；其精甚真，其中有信。自今及古，其名不去，以阅众甫。吾何以知众甫之状哉！以此。”历代气功家认为，《道德经》此章为论述玄关窍开现象的权威之作。黄氏注文则颇得原文本旨，并有所发现，有所泄露。

孔德之容，孔为大，德为道之显现与作用，容为样态或空器。黄氏认为孔德之容即玄关窍之喻。

《周易参同契》云：“此两孔穴法，金气亦相胥”。历代注家认为指坎与离、乾与坤、阴与阳、水与火或神与气等之互相作用。

阴跻，李时珍《奇经八脉考》附有张紫阳《八脉经》云：“阴跻脉在尾闾前，阴囊下，”则知即为会阴穴。张紫阳又曰：阴跻一脉“上通泥丸，下透涌泉，倘能知此，使真气聚散皆从此关窍，则天门常开，地户永闭，尻脉周流于一身，贯通上下，和气自然上朝，阳长阴消，水中火发，雪里开花，所谓’天根月窟常来往，三十六宫都是春’。得之者身体轻健，容衰返壮，昏昏默默，如醉如痴，此其验也。”

黄元吉氏将“孔德之容”（意为大道的空器）喻为玄关窍，即指在气功态中我身至玄至妙机关猛然开启，一阳初动，产出真精真气的一种现象。这种现象是真气积累到一定程度所引起的从无到有、由量到质的变化。这种现象在平时是看不到寻不着的，只有到窍开时才能感到，所谓“时至神知”，“感而遂通”。张紫阳在《金丹四百字》中谈到玄关窍时说：“此窍非凡窍，乾坤共合成，名为神气穴，内有坎离精。”黄氏引此作为玄关窍的定义。可知此窍由乾坤合成，乃神气相交之地，生产坎离之精、形成先天真气之所。亦即“产铅结丹此中存”，并非一般的凡窍可比。

欲得玄关窍开，必先实行回光返照。下手之初，但将万缘放下，静养片晌，观照下丹田。经一段时期锻炼，便能使性光发越，在恍惚中眼前便从无象而有象，从一片黑暗而出现性光（又名离光、慧光）及幽深广大之境。这就是老子说的“惚兮恍兮，其中有象。”盖将眼前性光或离光合两眼之神光返照下丹田，其光交于坎宫，于是调动阴跻（会阴穴）之气相会于下田，进而使神气交合，坎离交媾，凡息停而真息现，出现玄关现象，玄关窍突然打开，产生真精真气。这就是老子所说“恍兮惚兮，其中有物。窈兮冥兮，其中有精；其精甚真，其中有信。”信者为何？即黄氏所说如丹田浑然粹然，四肢百体精气溶溶，浩浩如潮生，周身苏软快乐等是。

黄氏气功养生学所应用的主要窍位，上丹田为泥丸（在头顶百会穴内），中丹田为绛宫（在两乳间膻中穴深入一寸三分处）下丹田为脐下一寸三分（实是脐内一寸三分）处，以及阴跻（会阴穴）、山根（即祖窍，在两眼之间鼻梁上）等等。黄氏所云中黄宫、中宫、中黄正位、黄庭、坎宫、水府和气穴等名称，均指下丹田。黄氏对下田的定位，虽说指明是脐下一寸三分，但古人将针灸铜人躺倒放置，脐下实是指脐内而言，而在气功态中执行也不严格，主张观照包括下田在内的心之下、肾之上的虚无窟子，还有内、外丹田之说，即观照脐丹田离皮肉一寸三分之间。他还说：丹田“有其名却无其实，然亦不可谓全无实也。以为虚也而万化生于此，以为实也究竟寻不着一个物事出来。”因此，他认为观照丹田不要死死执着，应“似在空中盘旋一般，然亦不可竟向空中驰逐也。”

黄氏一般只强调在下手兴功时，运用两眼神光汇合性光观照下丹田。但性光的发越，实际上与观照祖窍或存想山根有关，黄氏却不言明，也不强调。但他认为，人身有离宫、坎户和玄门、牝户之分，实际上离宫、玄门均可代表两眼之间的祖窍，坎户和牝户则可代表下田。他说：“自涌泉以至气海（指下田）皆属阳，则为坎；自泥丸以至玄关皆属阴，阴则为离。”黄氏在《乐育堂语录》一书中对山根给予高度的评价。他说：山根是人身“紧要之处”，“是通精气往来要道，人能存想山根，则真气自然复归黄庭旧处。”又说：“古云：‘山根是人初生命蒂’。吾人开督闭任，通气往来，即是此窍。苟能存神于兹，自可长生不老，却病延年。”由此可见，黄氏也是赞成存想山根、存神山根的。

世传丹经道书中不乏下手兴功时，存想山根的例子。《吕祖师先天虚无太一金华宗旨》一书功法下手时，就先着念于鼻端，然后存想山根。认为山根为人身之性户，上达泥丸，下通下丹田，故须凝聚光于此处，由此而下注。《尹真人东华正脉皇极阖辟证道仙经》一书功法下手之初，更主张“穷想山根”，以与元始祖气相通。清代史从龙撰《道乡集》一书认为，山根“乃下手收念之处，此窍乃后三关之门户，坐时二目先守此窍，少时念止，即可由门户而轻轻下沉海底，二目既至，神已随之，片时不起妄念，自然心定调息。”（以上均引自拙编《中国古代气功选注》一书）。

回光返照两眼之间的祖窍或存想山根，对阴虚阳亢或患有高血压症的学者不宜，因阳气会因此而上升，使病情加重。因有此弊端，黄氏著作中所言靠凝神用两眼神光汇合性光下照丹田，不失为一稳妥之法。盖此法亦可微微或适当地牵动祖窍，起一定程度的观照作用，且并非意守祖窍，则对有高血压及阴虚阳亢者无碍。

This is Huang Yuanji’s commentary on chapter 21 of his Commentary on the Daode jing.

Laozi’s original text is: “The capacity of great virtue follows the Way alone. The Way as a thing is dim and indistinct. Indistinct and dim — within it there is an image; dim and indistinct — within it there is a thing. Deep and obscure — within it there is essence; its essence is most true; within it there is a sign. From now back to antiquity its name has never departed, by which to survey the beginnings of all things. How do I know the state of the beginnings of all things? By this.” Qigong masters through the ages have regarded this chapter of the Daode jing as the authoritative account of the phenomenon of the opening of the Mysterious Pass. Huang’s commentary grasps the original meaning very well, and makes discoveries and reveals things besides.

“The capacity of great virtue”: kong means great, de is the manifestation and working of the Way, and rong is form or empty vessel. Huang regards “the capacity of great virtue” as a metaphor for the opening of the Mysterious Pass.

The Zhouyi cantong qi says: “In the method of these two openings, metal and breath depend on each other.” Commentators through the ages take this to refer to the mutual working of Kan and Li, Qian and Kun, yin and yang, water and fire, or spirit and breath.

The yinqiao: Zhang Ziyang’s Scripture of the Eight Vessels, appended to Li Shizhen’s Study of the Eight Extraordinary Vessels, says: “The yinqiao vessel is in front of the coccyx and below the scrotum.” From this we know that it is the perineum point. Zhang Ziyang also says of the yinqiao vessel: “Above it connects with the Muddy Pellet, below it reaches the Bubbling Spring; if one understands this, and lets the gathering and scattering of the true breath all pass through this pass and opening, then the gate of Heaven (the crown) is always open and the door of Earth (the lower openings) forever closed, so that essence and breath do not leak; the vessels from the sacrum circulate through the whole body, linking above and below; the harmonious breath naturally rises to pay homage above, yang grows and yin wanes, like fire arising in water and flowers blooming in snow — what is called ‘The Root of Heaven and the Moon Cave come and go constantly, and all thirty-six palaces are spring’ (the Root of Heaven is where yang is born, the Moon Cave where yin is born; yin and yang come and go between them without cease, and the whole body is full of spring). Those who obtain it become light and strong in body, their faded looks become vigorous again, dim and silent, as if drunk or entranced — this is its proof.”

Huang Yuanji compares “the capacity of great virtue” (meaning the empty vessel of the great Way) to the opening of the Mysterious Pass; he means a phenomenon in the qigong state in which the utterly mysterious and wondrous mechanism of my body suddenly opens, one yang first stirs, and true essence and true breath are produced. This phenomenon is a change from nothing to something, from quantity to quality, brought about when the true breath has accumulated to a certain degree. Normally it cannot be seen or found; only when the opening opens can one feel it — what is called “when the time comes, the spirit knows”, “it is stirred and then penetrates”. Speaking of the opening of the Mysterious Pass in the Four Hundred Characters on the Golden Elixir, Zhang Ziyang says: “This opening is no ordinary opening; it is formed by Qian and Kun together; it is called the cavity of spirit and breath; within it is the essence of Kan and Li.” Huang quotes these lines as the definition of the opening of the Mysterious Pass. We can see that this opening is formed by Qian and Kun, is the place where spirit and breath unite, and is where the essence of Kan and Li is produced and the Prior Heaven true breath is formed — that is, “producing lead and forming the elixir both lie within it”. It is not to be compared with an ordinary opening.

To make the opening of the Mysterious Pass open, one must first practice reverting the light to shine within. At the start, simply put down all ties, nourish stillness for a moment, and contemplate the lower Cinnabar Field. After a period of training, the light of the nature can be made to spread forth: in the dimness an image arises before the eyes out of no image, and out of total darkness there appears the light of the nature (also called the light of Li or the light of wisdom) and a deep, vast realm. This is what Laozi calls “indistinct and dim — within it there is an image”. Then join the light of the nature (the light of Li) before the eyes to the spirit-light of the two eyes and turn it back to shine on the lower elixir field; the light unites with the Palace of Kan, and so the breath of the yinqiao (the perineum point) is directed to meet it in the lower field; then spirit and breath unite, Kan and Li unite, the ordinary breath stops and the true breath appears, and the phenomenon of the Mysterious Pass occurs: the opening of the Mysterious Pass suddenly opens, and true essence and true breath are produced. This is what Laozi calls “dim and indistinct — within it there is a thing. Deep and obscure — within it there is essence; its essence is most true; within it there is a sign.” What is the “sign”? It is what Huang describes: the elixir field whole and pure, the essence and breath of the four limbs and hundred members melting, vast like the rising tide, the whole body soft and joyful, and so on.

The main cavity positions used in Huang’s qigong for nourishing life are these: the upper elixir field is the Muddy Pellet (inside the Baihui point at the crown); the middle elixir field is the Crimson Palace (one cun three fen inward from the Danzhong point between the breasts); the lower elixir field is one cun three fen below the navel (in fact one cun three fen inside the navel); and also the yinqiao (the perineum point), the Root of the Mountain (the ancestral opening, on the bridge of the nose between the two eyes), and so on. The central yellow palace, the central palace, the correct central yellow position, the Yellow Court, the Palace of Kan, the watery storehouse and the Cavity of Breath that Huang speaks of all refer to the lower elixir field. Although Huang explicitly places the lower elixir field one cun three fen below the navel, the ancients laid the bronze acupuncture figure on its back, so “below the navel” really means inside the navel; and in the qigong state he was not strict about it either — he advocates contemplating the Cave of Emptiness “below the heart and above the kidneys”, which includes the lower elixir field; and there is also the idea of an inner and outer elixir field, that is, contemplating the space one cun three fen in from the skin and flesh at the navel. He also says that the elixir field “has the name but not the substance — yet one cannot say it has no substance at all. Call it empty, and all transformations arise from it; call it substantial, and in the end one cannot find a single thing there.” So he holds that in contemplating the elixir field one should not cling rigidly, but should be “as if circling in empty space — yet one must not go chasing off into empty space either”.

Huang generally stresses only that when beginning the work one should use the spirit-light of the two eyes, joined to the light of the nature, to contemplate the lower elixir field. But the spreading forth of the light of the nature is in fact related to contemplating the ancestral opening or visualizing the Root of the Mountain, which Huang neither states nor stresses. He holds, however, that the human body has the distinction of the palace of Li and the door of Kan, the mysterious gate and the female door; in fact the palace of Li and the mysterious gate can both stand for the ancestral opening between the two eyes, and the door of Kan and the female door for the lower elixir field. He says: “From the Bubbling Spring up to the Sea of Breath (the lower field) all belongs to yang, and that is Kan; from the Muddy Pellet down to the Mysterious Pass all belongs to yin, and yin is Li.” In the Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture Huang thinks very highly of the Root of the Mountain. He says that the Root of the Mountain is “a vital place” of the body, “the main road by which essence and breath come and go; if one can visualize the Root of the Mountain, the true breath will naturally return to its old place in the Yellow Court”. He also says: “The ancients said: ‘The Root of the Mountain is the stalk of life at a person’s birth.' When we open the Governing vessel and close the Conception vessel, letting the breath come and go, it is this opening. If one can keep the spirit here, one can naturally live long without aging, ward off illness and lengthen one’s years.” From this we can see that Huang too approves of visualizing the Root of the Mountain and keeping the spirit there.

There is no lack of examples in the alchemical scriptures and Daoist books handed down of visualizing the Root of the Mountain when beginning the work. In the method of Patriarch Lü’s Purport of the Golden Flower of the Great One of Prior Heaven Emptiness, at the start one first fixes the thought on the tip of the nose and then visualizes the Root of the Mountain, regarding the Root of the Mountain as the door of the nature in the body, reaching up to the Muddy Pellet and down to the lower elixir field; so the light must be gathered here and poured down from here. The method of the Scripture of the Immortals Realizing the Way through the Opening and Closing of the Supreme Pole, in the Orthodox Eastern Lineage of the Perfected Yin goes further at the start, advocating “visualizing the Root of the Mountain to the utmost”, so as to connect with the Primordial Ancestral Breath. The Qing-dynasty Collection of Daoxiang by Shi Conglong holds that the Root of the Mountain “is the place for gathering the thoughts at the start; this opening is the gateway of the three rear passes. When sitting, the two eyes first guard this opening; after a while, when the thoughts have stopped, one can sink gently from this gateway down to the bottom of the sea; once the two eyes have arrived, the spirit has followed; for a while no deluded thought arises, and naturally the mind is settled and the breath regulated.” (All the above are quoted from my Selected Ancient Chinese Qigong Texts, Annotated.)

Turning the light back on the ancestral opening between the two eyes, or visualizing the Root of the Mountain, is not suitable for students with yin deficiency and yang excess or with high blood pressure, because the yang breath will rise as a result and make the condition worse. Precisely because of this drawback, the method in Huang’s works — concentrating the spirit and using the spirit-light of the two eyes, joined to the light of the nature, to shine down on the elixir field — is a safe method. For this method also stirs the ancestral opening slightly and appropriately, giving a certain degree of contemplation, without being a fixing of the mind on the ancestral opening, and so it does no harm to those with high blood pressure or yin deficiency and yang excess.

Key Terms

  • The capacity of great virtue: from chapter 21 of the Daode jing; Huang Yuanji takes it as a metaphor for the opening of the Mysterious Pass; kong is “great”, rong is “empty vessel”.
  • The single opening of the Mysterious Pass: another name for the opening of the Mysterious Pass; see Mysterious Opening / Mysterious Pass.
  • The gate of the Mysterious Female, the gate of the Way and righteousness, the gate of non-duality: the Daoist, Confucian and Buddhist names for the same opening.
  • Dull emptiness: emptiness without reality, dead and lifeless; see realm of yin / realm of the aggregates.
  • Empty yet containing the utmost reality, nothing yet comprising all being: the Mysterious Pass leans neither to emptiness nor to being.
  • Light of nature: the light that appears naturally before the eyes in stillness, also called the light of Li or light of wisdom; see light of Nature / light of cognition.
  • The birth of faint yang: when the light of the nature first stirs; it is still the yang within Li, the mechanism of the breath is weak and cannot yet be gathered.
  • Palace of Kan: the lower elixir field; see lower Cinnabar Field.
  • Gathering the muni in two stages: directing and gathering is one stage, returning to the furnace and nourishing warmly is another; see Golden Flower.
  • Yinqiao: the perineum point; see perineum.
  • Fire enters the bottom of the water, metal is born in the water: the spirit-light (fire) shines down on the elixir field (water), and true yang (metal) is born within it — that is, the conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • The body like a wall, the intention like cold ashes: the stillness of body and mind when true essence is born.
  • Sign: the sure proof of the arising of true essence; the movement of yin and yang never missing its time.
  • The essence of the true One, the essence of the two and the five: the Prior Heaven true essence, not the Posterior Heaven essence of intercourse; see Essence.
  • “Dim” is the density of light, “indistinct” the subtlety of the incipient movement: “dim” corresponds to the light of the nature, “indistinct” to the incipient movement.
  • The dim and obscure: the state in which awareness does not arise and original nature and original life merge into one piece.
  • Embryo field: the lower elixir field.
  • The work of lifting: lifting gently, so that the true breath does not rush downward or scatter outward.
  • The gates of the six faculties: eyes, ears, nose, tongue, body and mind — the gates through which the true breath scatters outward.
  • The outer yang drawing back: the outer kidney no longer rising at random in stillness, essence and breath drawing inward.
  • The central yellow palace, the central palace, the correct central yellow position, the Yellow Court, the watery storehouse: in this book (Huang Yuanji) they all refer to the lower elixir field, unlike their use in other books; see lower Cinnabar Field.
  • The palace of Li, the mysterious gate / the door of Kan, the female door: the first two can stand for the ancestral opening between the two eyes (Root of the Mountain), the last two for the lower elixir field.
  • Root of the Mountain: on the bridge of the nose between the two eyes, the ancestral opening; see the safety note in this section.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The beginning is a single sentence: put down all ties, nourish stillness for a moment, and contemplate the lower elixir field. Do not seek light or scenes; if light appears in the dimness, it is a natural thing.
  2. Do not cling rigidly in contemplating the elixir field. In Huang Yuanji’s own words: “as if circling in empty space — yet one must not go chasing off into empty space either” — do not stare at one point, and do not let the mind drift away.
  3. Join the spirit-light of the two eyes to the light of the nature and “shine downward”; do not keep the mind between the two eyes. This is Huang Yuanji’s way of beginning, and the safe method Qiu Ling recommends.
  4. Safety note (from Qiu Ling’s original note): people with yin deficiency and yang excess or with high blood pressure should not fix the mind on the ancestral opening between the two eyes or visualize the Root of the Mountain, because the yang breath will rise and make the condition worse; “the spirit-light shining down on the elixir field” does no harm. If a swelling feeling in the head, headache, a flushed face or palpitations appear during quiet sitting, stop and simply relax naturally instead; consult a doctor if necessary.
  5. The “sign” of true essence arising: the whole body soft and joyful, the essence and breath of the four limbs and hundred members all returning inward, melting and vast. What matters most at this time is guarding within with a single mind, raising no stray thoughts and no desire.
  6. “Bear it and bear it again”: at first it will feel hard, because desire is “the familiar road”; if you can bear it, there will be a change within two or three months.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section comes from Huang Yuanji’s commentary on chapter 21 of his Commentary on the Daode jing; it is in two paragraphs (the second beginning “Laozi’s ‘dim, then indistinct’”), which we have divided into six short passages.
  2. “Metal and breath depend on each other” (jin qi yi xiang xu 金气亦相胥): the current text of the Cantong qi reads 相须; 胥 and 须 are interchangeable, meaning “depend on each other”.
  3. “Yinqiao” (阴跻): the original book prints it everywhere as 阴跻, which is the 阴跷 (阴蹻) usually written, the perineal area. We keep the form of the original book.
  4. “The thing belonging to yang within begins to stir” (qi zhong zhi yangwu dong yan 其中之阳物动焉): the “thing belonging to yang” here is the thing of yang nature (the mechanism of true yang), not the outer kidney.
  5. “The exact message of the right moment in the alternating movement of yin and yang, which never misses its time” (阴阳迭运不失其候之调侯): so printed in the original book. 调侯 makes no sense and should be a mistake for 谓也 (“this is called”); the translation follows “this is called”.
  6. “Laozi’s ‘dim, then indistinct’ … ‘indistinct, then dim’ …”: here Huang Yuanji puts “dim” and “indistinct” in exactly the reverse order from Laozi’s text (“indistinct and dim — within it there is an image; dim and indistinct — within it there is a thing”), while Qiu Ling’s note follows Laozi’s text. We record it as it is; the reader need only take the general meaning: first comes the “image” of the light of the nature, then the “thing” of true yang.
  7. “The harvest of a year and the achievement of a day”: the year begins with the one yang of the winter solstice, the day with the zi hour; a metaphor for cultivation beginning with the return of one yang.
  8. “Like the elixir running off”: in alchemical books, the elixir already obtained (true essence and true breath) running off and being lost.
  9. Qiu Ling’s note quotes Laozi as “from now back to antiquity” (zi jin ji gu 自今及古); the current received text reads “from antiquity to now”, while the silk manuscripts read “from now back to antiquity”; we record it as it is.
  10. “All thirty-six palaces are spring”: a line from a poem by Shao Yong; chapter ten of this book has a section explaining it.
  11. “The vessels from the sacrum” (kaomai 尻脉): kao is the sacral area.

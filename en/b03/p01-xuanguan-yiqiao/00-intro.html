Chapter One · The Single Opening of the Mysterious Pass · 篇首导读

Introduction to Chapter One

What this book is

Huang Yuanji, also called Huang Shang, came from Fengcheng in Jiangxi. In the Daoguang and Xianfeng reigns of the Qing he went to Sichuan and for more than ten years taught in a “Hall of Joyful Nurture” around Fushun, in what is now Zigong, with several thousand disciples. He left only two books: his own Commentary on the Daode jing, and the Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture, recorded by his disciples and checked by him. Chen Yingning called these two books “required reading for Daoists”.

Qiu Ling took the Sayings as his main source and the Commentary as a supplement, and regrouped by subject the talks Huang Yuanji gave in class, arranging them in ten chapters: the single opening of the Mysterious Pass, the gate of the Mysterious Female, the true intention, clarifying the mind and seeing the nature, essence, breath and spirit, the union of Kan and Li and of Qian and Kun, the River Chariot, gathering, refining and fire phasing, other secret formulas, and a conclusion. Every section is “a text by Huang Yuanji + Qiu Ling’s note”, and all the chapter and section titles were added by Qiu Ling.

Compared with the previous two books, the great feature of this one is that its reasoning is clear and it uses few coded terms — in class Huang Yuanji often speaks straight to his students: “Now I point it out to you directly”, “Do not regard this as a difficult matter”.

Why this chapter comes first

Qiu Ling put “the single opening of the Mysterious Pass” at the very front of the book because Huang Yuanji himself says that this opening “is the first task of the practitioner” and “apart from this one opening of the Mysterious Pass, there is nowhere else to make progress”.

The six sections of this chapter form exactly one complete process:

How to begin (reverting the light to shine within) → what the Mysterious Pass is like (the appearance of the Mysterious Pass) → how it shows itself in the body (one awakening and it stirs) → how to tell the true from the false, and the most common mistakes (the true reality of the Mysterious Pass) → the Mysterious Pass is in fact always there (the Mysterious Pass is always present) → what to do once it has opened (after the Mysterious Pass opens)

What this chapter is about (in one sentence)

Put down all ties, and with the spirit-light of the two eyes joined to the light of the nature, shine down on the elixir field; in the moment of chaotic dimness, without knowing or awareness, suddenly there is an awakening and it stirs — this is the Mysterious Pass; at this time do not search, do not guard, do not think, but let the true intention be the master, concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath and nourish it warmly, and wait until true yang breaks forth fully before turning the River Chariot.

Reading tips

  1. The cavity terms in this book differ from those in the first two books. In his note to the first section Qiu Ling explains: the central yellow palace, the central palace, the correct central yellow position, the Yellow Court, the Palace of Kan, the watery storehouse and the Cavity of Breath that Huang Yuanji speaks of all refer to the lower elixir field. Whenever you meet these words, read them as the lower elixir field.
  2. Huang Yuanji’s way of beginning is “the spirit-light shining down on the elixir field”, not fixing the mind between the two eyes. Qiu Ling stresses that fixing the mind on the ancestral opening or visualizing the Root of the Mountain is not suitable for people with yin deficiency and yang excess, or with high blood pressure; joining the spirit-light of the two eyes to the light of the nature and shining down on the elixir field is a safe method.
  3. The most practical sentence of this chapter is in section four: before the Mysterious Pass opens, do not “add an extra thought to search for it”; after it has opened, do not “add an extra thought to guard it”. When thoughts are scattered, bring them back — “bringing it back is it”.
  4. The passage from Li Hanxu’s Secret Principles of the Three Carts quoted in Qiu Ling’s note to section six is translated in full in the second book, Essentials for Entering Alchemical Practice, and can be read alongside.

Chapter One · The Single Opening of the Mysterious Pass · 一觉而动

One Awakening and It Stirs

One awakening and it stirs — breathing heavily through the nose, one wakes with a start, and that is the Mysterious Pass; awake then lost, lost then awake, always going toward the side of awakening

About This Chapter

This is the section of which Huang Yuanji himself says: “The ancients only spoke of the Mysterious Pass; none has pointed it out in the human body as concretely as I do” — earlier masters only used “thunder” as a metaphor for the Mysterious Pass, while he says directly what the opening of the Mysterious Pass looks like in the body:

  1. What it looks like in the body: in concentration, spirit and breath unite; when one reaches true emptiness, one “falls asleep” without noticing, breathing heavily through the nose — and then wakes with a start. This waking is the root of Heaven and Earth and the ancestor of people and things, what the ancients called “a clap of thunder at midnight”.
  2. What to do: this awakening is one’s original true face; one must quickly guard it with the true intention, as if riding a thousand-li horse that flashes past — “before it, it is not; after it, it is not; it shows itself only in one breath”.
  3. “Quickly stand up” does not mean standing up: Qiu Ling explains specially that it means rousing the spirit, keeping the true intention as master, and continuing the work, not stopping the work.
  4. The second passage: awake then lost, lost then awake, “always going toward the side of awakening”; in time, “no awakening, and yet nothing not awake”. The work is only three words: awakening, stillness, constancy. There is no need to sit withered in the deep mountains; “as soon as my mind is still, it is naturally clearly aware”, and even in a camp of a thousand soldiers and ten thousand horses there is a pure, numinous mountain.

Qiu Ling’s note says that the so-called “falling asleep” is really half asleep and half not, still within the qigong state; one wakes with a start, and the body jolts along with it — this jolt is why the ancients used the “clap of thunder” as a metaphor for the Mysterious Pass. He also mentions that the Essentials of Great Accomplishment says the opening of the Mysterious Pass “may happen twenty or thirty times in a row before it stops”; he had doubts about this at first, and understood it only on reading Huang Yuanji’s “awake then lost, lost then awake”.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

夫玄关一窍，正阳生活子时。吕祖云：“万有无一臭，地下听雷声”。古仙云：“忽然夜半一声雷，万户千门次第开”。雷乎雷乎？神哉神哉！从此二说观之，难道玄窍之开，真阳之动，色身中岂无真实凭信，而漫以雷声喻之乎？张祖又云：“雷声隐隐震虚空，电光灼处寻真种。”古来仙师个个俱以雷鸣比之者何哉？吾今直为指出，即尔入定之时，忽然神与气交，直到真空地位，不觉睡着，鼻息齁齁，一觉而醒，此即是天地之根，人物之祖。

The single opening of the Mysterious Pass is precisely “the Living Zi Hour when yang is born” (the moment true yang is first born). Patriarch Lü says: “Among all things not a single scent — under the earth one hears the thunder.” (All things are silent, without sound or smell, and suddenly one hears thunder under the earth.) An immortal of old says: “Suddenly at midnight a clap of thunder; ten thousand doors and a thousand gates open one after another.” Thunder, thunder — what is it? Truly wondrous! Judging from these two sayings, could it be that when the mysterious opening opens and true yang stirs, there is no real evidence in the physical body, and thunder is used only as a loose metaphor? Patriarch Zhang also says: “The rumbling thunder shakes empty space; where the lightning flashes, seek the true seed.” Why do the immortal masters of old all compare it to thunder? Now I point it out to you directly: it is when you enter concentration and suddenly spirit and breath unite, and you go on until you reach true emptiness; without noticing you fall asleep, breathing heavily through the nose, and then you wake with a start — this waking is the root of Heaven and Earth and the ancestor of people and things.

§2
Original (Chinese)

吾身投胎夺舍，其来也即此倏忽杳冥忽焉惊醒之一念也。尔果于入定时，凭空一觉，即是我本来真面目，急忙以真意护持，切勿稍纵，如人乘千里骥，绝尘而奔，暂一经眼，便要认识，不可延迟，迟则无及矣。故曰以前不是，以后不是，露处只在一息，一息之后，不复见焉。尔等务要于静定时，偶有鼻息齁齁，急忙起立，趁此清空一气，收摄将来。如此坐一次，必有一次长益。果然不爽其时，不差其度，不待百日，基可得而筑矣！此等要诀，古人但说玄关，未有如吾实实在在向人身中指出者。是知丹诀，关乎功德心性，不易语也。

When this body of ours entered the womb and came into the world, its very origin was this one thought of sudden waking in a flash, out of the deep and obscure. If, when you enter concentration, there is this awakening out of nowhere, that is my original true face; quickly guard it with the True Intention and never let it loosen in the slightest — like riding a thousand-li horse galloping without raising dust, where the scenery flashes past the eyes: you must recognize it at a glance; do not hesitate, for if you hesitate it is too late. So it is said: before it, it is not; after it, it is not; it shows itself only in one breath, and after that breath it is seen no more. You must, in still concentration, whenever you happen to breathe heavily through the nose, “quickly stand up” (rouse the spirit — not really stand up; see Qiu Ling’s note), and taking advantage of this one clear, empty breath, gather it in. Sit like this once, and there will surely be one step of progress. If you truly do not miss its moment and do not lose its measure, the foundation can be laid in less than a hundred days! The ancients only spoke vaguely of “the Mysterious Pass”; none has pointed out this kind of formula in the human body as concretely as I do. From this we know that the formulas of the elixir concern a person’s merit, virtue, mind and nature, and are not easily spoken.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§2
Original note (Chinese)

黄氏于此直道出千古不传之秘，指出玄关窍开在人身中的表现。盖静定中神气交合，混混沌沌，恍恍惚惚，不觉“睡着”，实际上是似睡非睡，仍处于气功态中，或有齁声，或无齁声，猛然一觉而醒，身体亦随之一震。这个震动，乃静极而动，正是古人以雷震喻玄关窍开之故，乃是因醒觉而动，又因体内真阳发生而动。此一觉而动，只在一息之间，我必须不失时机，以真意主宰之，凝神气穴（或虚无窟子），待真阳大现，然后转运河车（周天），采药返炉，切不可在一觉之时妄想他事，以免精气神有一丝半点渗漏。要做到这点，要有黄氏所说的把得住，拿得定，所谓提雾拿云手段，即丹经所云：“时至神知”，“感而遂通”。

当鼻声齁齁时，急忙起立，并非停止行功，而是振作精神，保持真意主宰，继续行功。学者得此“一觉而动”之真诀，依法行持，倘常得玄关窍开之真景，功后必感头清目爽，浑身轻快，下元充实，若有宿疾亦可渐消，功乃可大进。

Here Huang states directly the secret not transmitted for a thousand ages, pointing out how the opening of the Mysterious Pass shows itself in the human body. In still concentration, spirit and breath unite, all is chaotic and undifferentiated, dim and indistinct, and one “falls asleep” without noticing — in fact half asleep and half not, still within the qigong state — with or without snoring, one wakes with a start, and the body jolts along with it. This jolt is stillness reaching its extreme and turning to movement; it is precisely why the ancients used a clap of thunder as a metaphor for the opening of the Mysterious Pass: it moves because of the waking, and also because true yang arises in the body. This awakening and stirring lies only in the space of one breath; I must not miss the moment, but let the true intention be its master, concentrate the spirit in the Cavity of Breath (or the Cave of Emptiness), wait until true yang appears fully, and then turn the River Chariot (the Celestial Circuit), gather the medicine and return it to the furnace; at the moment of awakening one must never think deludedly of other things, lest essence, breath and spirit leak away in the slightest. To do this one needs what Huang calls “holding it and grasping it firmly”, the skill called “lifting the mist and seizing the clouds” (being able to seize drifting clouds and mist in one grasp — a metaphor for seizing a fleeting moment), which is what the alchemical scriptures call “when the time comes, the spirit knows” and “it is stirred and then penetrates”.

“Quickly standing up” when the breath is heavy through the nose does not mean stopping the work, but rousing the spirit, keeping the true intention as master, and continuing the work. If students obtain this true formula of “one awakening and it stirs” and practice according to the method, often obtaining the true scene of the opening of the Mysterious Pass, then after practice they will surely feel the head clear and the eyes bright, the whole body light and easy, and the lower origin full; old ailments too may gradually disappear, and the work can advance greatly.

§3
Original (Chinese)

此一觉也，切不可轻视之。自此以后，觉而迷，迷而觉，总从觉一边去。久之自然无觉，而无不觉。如此者，非所谓不神之神乎？你等莫视为难事，只是用一个觉字、静字、常字，即可为正法眼矣！否则，静而不觉，觉而不常，神有间断，何时而后心定如止水，月印万川而无波哉！亦不必深山枯兀静坐也，只要我心一静，自然了觉，常常如是，无论千兵万马营中，皆是清净灵山也！总在各人自静、自觉、自常，即可证无上菩提矣！

This awakening must never be taken lightly. From then on, awake then lost, lost then awake, always going toward the side of “awakening”. In time one naturally reaches the state of “not deliberately awakening, and yet never not awake”. Is this not what is called “the spirit that is not spirit” (not deliberately using the spirit, and yet true spirit)? Do not regard it as a difficult matter; simply use the one word “awakening” (clear awareness), the one word “stillness” and the one word “constancy” (perseverance), and you have the eye of the true Dharma! Otherwise, still without awareness, aware without constancy, the spirit has gaps — when will the mind be settled like still water, like one moon reflected in ten thousand rivers without a ripple? Nor is there any need to sit withered and motionless in the deep mountains; as soon as my mind is still, it is naturally clearly aware; if it is always so, then even in a camp of a thousand soldiers and ten thousand horses there is the pure, numinous mountain (the holy place of the Way)! It all depends on each person being able to be still, aware and constant; then one can realize supreme bodhi (awakening)!

Qiu Ling's noteon §3
Original note (Chinese)

《大成捷要》一书有云：玄关窍开有一连二、三十次而后止。曾颇疑其说。现黄氏所云：“觉而迷，迷而觉，总从觉一边去”，正合此意。有迷才有觉，不觉则不迷，静极而动，动极而静，循环无已。若能行持久之，习练成熟，使成有序化，则达到我心一静自然了觉之境界，亦非难事。

The Essentials of Great Accomplishment says that the opening of the Mysterious Pass may happen twenty or thirty times in a row before it stops. I used to have considerable doubts about this. Now Huang’s words “awake then lost, lost then awake, always going toward the side of awakening” fit this meaning exactly. Only where there is being lost is there awakening; without awakening there is no being lost either; stillness reaches its extreme and turns to movement, movement reaches its extreme and turns to stillness, in an endless cycle. If one can practice for a long time until the practice becomes mature and orderly, then reaching the state of “as soon as my mind is still, it is naturally clearly aware” is not difficult.

Key Terms

  • The Living Zi Hour when yang is born: the moment the Mysterious Pass opens and true yang first stirs; see Living Zi Hour.
  • A clap of thunder at midnight: the ancients’ metaphor for the jolt when the Mysterious Pass opens.
  • True seed: the Prior Heaven true and single breath, the seed of the elixir; see Medicine.
  • Breathing heavily through the nose: the sound of the breath when half asleep and half not in concentration.
  • One awakening and it stirs: waking with a start, the body jolting with it — the opening of the Mysterious Pass.
  • Entering the womb and taking over the dwelling: the beginning of receiving a human life; Huang Yuanji holds that what comes into life is this one thought of awakening.
  • Original true face: the original nature, untainted by the Posterior Heaven; see Original Spirit.
  • Quickly stand up: rouse the spirit, keep the true intention as master and continue the work — not standing up, and not stopping the work.
  • Laying the foundation: “the foundation can be laid in less than a hundred days”.
  • The skill of lifting the mist and seizing the clouds: the work of holding it and grasping it firmly.
  • Awakening, stillness, constancy: Huang Yuanji’s three-word formula.
  • The spirit that is not spirit: from the Yinfu jing, “People know the spirit that is spirit, but not why what is not spirit is spirit”.
  • The eye of the true Dharma: a Buddhist term, the eye of wisdom that sees the true Dharma.
  • Becoming orderly: Qiu Ling’s term for the way, once the work is mature, being lost and awakening, movement and stillness, form a regular pattern of their own.

Putting It into Practice

  1. If during quiet sitting you “fall asleep” for a moment and then wake with a start, the body jolting, do not panic, and do not be annoyed that you “fell asleep again”. Huang Yuanji says this may be exactly the moment of the Mysterious Pass — the key is to raise the true intention at once the moment you wake, bring the spirit back to the elixir field, and go on sitting.
  2. “Quickly stand up” means rousing yourself, not getting up. Qiu Ling says it clearly: keep the true intention as master and continue the work.
  3. Telling them apart: after a true awakening of the Mysterious Pass the head is clear and the eyes bright, the whole body light and easy, the lower origin full; if you merely dozed off from tiredness and wake dull and weary, it is time to rest — there is no need to push on.
  4. The three words awakening, stillness, constancy. It does not matter if you get lost; wake up again; the work lies in “always going toward the side of awakening”.
  5. There is no need to wait for a large block of time or a quiet place. “As soon as my mind is still, it is naturally clearly aware” — turning the light back for one thought at any moment in daily life is also the work.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in two paragraphs, both from the Sayings from the Hall of Joyful Nurture (we have divided the first into two short passages).
  2. “Patriarch Lü”: Lü Dongbin. “Patriarch Zhang”: alchemists customarily call Zhang Sanfeng “Patriarch Zhang”; the original book does not give the source.
  3. “Among all things not a single scent” (wanyou wu yi xiu 万有无一臭): 臭 is read xiù, “smell”. Among all things, silence without sound or smell.
  4. “Do not miss its moment” (bu shuang qi shi 不爽其时): shuang means “to miss, to err”.
  5. “Nor is there any need to sit withered and motionless in the deep mountains” (亦不必深山枯兀静坐也): so printed in the original; kuwu means withered and motionless.
  6. “The Essentials of Great Accomplishment”: a Qing-dynasty alchemical book; Qiu Ling only quotes its view here, without giving the chapter.

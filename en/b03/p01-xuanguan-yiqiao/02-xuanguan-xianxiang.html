Chapter One · The Single Opening of the Mysterious Pass · 玄关现象

The Appearance of the Mysterious Pass

The appearance of the Mysterious Pass — extinguish the moving mind, do not extinguish the illuminating mind; in a moment without knowing or awareness, suddenly an awakening and it stirs

About This Chapter

This section is taken from Huang Yuanji’s Commentary on the Daode jing, chapter 1 (the chapter “The Way that can be spoken is not the constant Way”). It describes the whole order of beginning in Huang Yuanji’s inner practice for nourishing life, and how the Mysterious Pass appears in the body:

  1. Beginning: sit upright with a single mind, throw away all thoughts, lower the curtains and contemplate the Cave of Emptiness that seems to be there “below the heart and above the kidneys”; spirit and breath are concentrated in the lower elixir field, and in a moment they merge into one piece.
  2. Entering stillness: let it be chaotic and undifferentiated, without raising the “mind that wants to be clearly aware”; gradually it becomes dim, and one enters “the land of nothing whatever” — no others and no self, and yet not torpor.
  3. The key formula: “Extinguish the moving mind; do not extinguish the illuminating mind.” The moving mind (deluded thoughts) must be extinguished; the illuminating mind (that point of clear awareness) must not be — otherwise one becomes withered wood and dead ashes.
  4. The Mysterious Pass: in the moment without knowing or awareness, suddenly there is an awakening and it stirs — this is “the Supreme Ultimate laying its foundation”, the wondrous opening of the Mysterious Pass. It is like a flash of lightning or a spark struck from flint: “right now it is, in the blink of an eye it is not”.
  5. Laozi’s “observing the wonder” and “observing the opening”: still and still again, with nothing at all within, is “without desire, observing the wonder”; when the mechanism of the breath stirs, knowing it but not producing views about it, is “with desire, observing the opening”.

Qiu Ling’s note puts the key points very plainly: the Mysterious Pass has no fixed position in the body, nor is it the Root of the Mountain between the two eyes; it is the instant in the qigong state when the true breath changes from quantity to quality — in everyday words, “the breath has come, one has got the opening”. And Huang Yuanji sums it up in four characters: one awakening and it stirs.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

学人下手之初，别无他术，惟一心端坐，万念胥捐，垂帘观照。心之下，肾之上，仿佛有个虚无窟子。神神相照，息息常归，任其一往一来，但以神、气两者凝注中宫为主。不顷刻间，神、气打成一片矣！于是听其混混沌沌，不起一明觉心。久之恍恍惚惚，入于无何有之乡焉。

When the student first begins, there is no other technique: simply sit upright with a single mind, throw away all thoughts entirely, lower the curtains of the eyes and contemplate within. Below the heart and above the kidneys there seems to be an empty, hollow Cave of Emptiness. Let the spirit shine on the spirit, let every breath return here, let it come and go as it will, only taking as the main thing the concentration of both spirit and breath in the central palace (the lower elixir field). In a moment, spirit and breath merge into one piece! Then let it be chaotic and undifferentiated, without raising a single “I want to understand, I want to be aware” mind. In time, dim and indistinct, one enters “the land of nothing whatever” (a phrase from Zhuangzi: the realm where there is nothing at all).

§2
Original (Chinese)

斯时也，不知神之入气，气之归神，浑然一无人无我，何地何天景象，而又非昏聩也。若使昏聩，适成槁木死灰。修士于此，当灭动心，莫灭照心。惟是智而若愚，慧而不用，于无知无觉之际，忽然一觉而动，即太极开基。

At this time, one does not know whether the spirit has entered the breath or the breath has returned to the spirit; it is all one undivided whole, without others and without self, not knowing what place one is in or what heaven is above — and yet it is not stupor. If it were stupor, it would become exactly withered wood and dead ashes (lifeless). When the practitioner reaches this point, he should extinguish the moving mind, but not extinguish the illuminating mind. Simply be wise yet seem foolish, clever yet not use it; and in the moment without knowing or awareness, suddenly an awakening and it stirs — this is “the Supreme Ultimate laying its foundation” (the Supreme Ultimate beginning to divide yin and yang out of the Limitless, the first stirring of vitality).

§3
Original (Chinese)

须知此一觉中，自自然然，不由感附，才是我本来真觉，道家谓之玄关妙窍，只在一呼一吸之间。其吸而入也，则为阴为静为无；其呼而出也，则为阳为动为有。即此一息之微，亦有妙窍。人欲修成正觉，惟此一觉而动之时，有个实实在在、的的确确、无念虑、无渣滓、一个本来人在。故曰：天地有此一觉而生万物，人身有此一觉而结金丹。但此一觉，有如电光石火，当前则是，转眼即非，所争只毫厘间耳。学者务于平时审得清，临机方把得住。古来大觉如来，亦无非此一觉积累而成也。

Know that within this awakening, entirely natural, not brought about by outer sounds or things touching it — this alone is my original true awareness, which the Daoists call the wondrous opening of the Mysterious Pass; it lies only between one inhalation and one exhalation. When the breath is drawn in, it is yin, stillness, non-being; when it goes out, it is yang, movement, being. Even in the subtlety of a single breath, there is “wonder” and “opening”. If a person wishes to cultivate right awakening, it is only at this moment of awakening and stirring that there is a real, definite “original person” (the original true self), without thoughts or worries, without sediment. So it is said: Heaven and Earth have this awakening and give birth to all things; the human body has this awakening and forms the Golden Elixir. But this awakening is like a flash of lightning or a spark struck from flint: right now it is, in the blink of an eye it is not; the difference is only a hair’s breadth. The student must examine it clearly in ordinary times; only then can he hold it when the moment comes. The great awakened Tathāgatas of old were also nothing but this awakening accumulated time after time.

§4
Original (Chinese)

修士兴功不从有欲无欲，观妙观窍下手，又从何处以为本乎？虽然无与有、妙与窍，无非阴静阳动，一气判为二气，二气仍归一气而已矣！以其静久而动，无中生有，名曰阳生活子时；以其动极复静，有又还无，名曰复命归根。要皆一太极所判之阴阳也。两者虽有异名，而实同出一源。太上（即老子）谓之玄。玄者深远之谓也。学者欲得玄道，必静之又静，定而又定，其中浑无物事，是为无欲观妙，此一玄也。及气机一动，虽有知，却不生一知见，虽有动，却不存一动想，有一心无两念，是为有欲观窍，此又一玄也。至玄之又玄，实为归根之所，非众妙之门而何？所惜者凡人有此妙窍，不知直养，是以旋开旋闭，不至耗尽而不已。至人于玄窍开时，一眼觑定，一手拿定，操存涵养，不使须臾或失，所以直造无上根源。

If the practitioner does not begin his work from “with desire and without desire”, “observing the wonder and observing the opening”, where else could he take his root? Although we speak of non-being and being, wonder and opening, they are nothing but yin stillness and yang movement — the one breath dividing into two breaths, and the two breaths returning to the one breath! Because stillness lasts long and then moves, being arising out of non-being, it is called “the Living Zi Hour when yang is born”; because movement reaches its extreme and returns to stillness, being returning to non-being, it is called “restoring life and returning to the root”. In short, both are the yin and yang divided out of the one Supreme Ultimate. Although the two have different names, they truly come from the same source. The Most High (Laozi) calls this “mystery”. Mystery means deep and far. If the student wishes to obtain the mysterious Way, he must be still and still again, concentrated and concentrated again, with nothing at all in the mind — this is “without desire, observing the wonder”, and it is one mystery. When the mechanism of the breath stirs, though there is knowing, one does not produce a single view; though there is movement, one does not keep a single thought of moving; there is one mind and no second thought — this is “with desire, observing the opening”, and it is another mystery. As for “mystery upon mystery”, it is truly the place of returning to the root — what is it if not “the gate of all wonders”? The pity is that ordinary people too have this wondrous opening but do not know how to nourish it straightforwardly, so it opens only to close again, until it is used up. When the mysterious opening opens, the accomplished person fixes it with one look and grasps it with one hand, holding, preserving and nourishing it, never losing it for an instant, and so goes straight to the supreme source.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

此段选自黄元吉《道德经注释》第一章注文。虚无窟子，指入静中心之下，肾之上包括下丹田在内所形成的一种化境。

中宫即中黄宫，此处指下田，亦指虚无窟子。

观妙观窍、众妙之门，均引自《道德经》第一章。该章原文为：“道可道，非常道；名可名，非常名。无名天地之始；有名万物之母。故常无欲以观其妙；常有欲以观其徼。此两者，同出而异名，同谓之玄。玄之又玄，众妙之门。”丹书多把“徼”解为“窍”。

黄氏在此介绍了其养生内功的入手程序及概要，首先揭示了玄关现象的重要作用。他在其著作中几乎无处不谈玄关。他认为，玄关一窍“是修士第一要务”，“除此一个玄关窍，余无可进步也，”“玄关窍可以了结千经万典之义。”历代丹经道书亦多论及玄关窍开之重要性，但今人修习养生内功，能知玄关作用者甚稀，得其诀窍真传者更少，练功自难登堂入室。

玄关现象，此处所指在人身上并无定位，亦非指两眼之间的山根，实是指气功态中产生真气（能量流）由量变到质变的一刹那间，亦即静极而动从无到有的一刹那间。在这一刹那间，我体内真阳发生，古称阳生活子时，亦称太极开基，又称真种产生，即我体内之最玄最妙机关开启了，用俗话说，气、得气、得窍了！

玄关窍开，是回光返照下丹田，做到神气相交，坎离交媾，混混沌沌，恍惚杳冥，真息（胎息）绵绵，静极而动，无中生有的一种现象。

黄氏把玄关窍开现象在人体的表现归结为“一觉而动”四字，而且指出，掌握这“一觉而动”的时刻只在一息（一呼一吸）之间。一觉就是从无知无觉到猛然醒觉。动就是真阳之动而影响身体之动。古传止念之法可作为入窍途径。此法即排除种种杂念，只留正念，俄而此念顿息而后念未起之际，便是导致玄关窍开之时。

This passage is taken from Huang Yuanji’s commentary on chapter 1 of his Commentary on the Daode jing. The “Cave of Emptiness” refers to a transformed state formed in stillness below the heart and above the kidneys, including the lower elixir field.

The “central palace” is the central yellow palace; here it refers to the lower elixir field, and also to the Cave of Emptiness.

“Observing the wonder and observing the opening” and “the gate of all wonders” are both quoted from chapter 1 of the Daode jing. The original text of that chapter is: “The Way that can be spoken is not the constant Way; the name that can be named is not the constant name. Nameless, it is the beginning of Heaven and Earth; named, it is the mother of all things. So, ever without desire, one observes its wonder; ever with desire, one observes its boundary. These two come out together but have different names; together they are called mystery. Mystery upon mystery — the gate of all wonders.” Alchemical books mostly explain “boundary” (jiao 徼) as “opening” (qiao 窍).

Here Huang introduces the order and outline of beginning his inner practice for nourishing life, and first of all reveals the important role of the phenomenon of the Mysterious Pass. He speaks of the Mysterious Pass almost everywhere in his works. He holds that the single opening of the Mysterious Pass “is the first task of the practitioner”, “apart from this one opening of the Mysterious Pass, there is nowhere else to make progress”, and “the opening of the Mysterious Pass can settle the meaning of a thousand scriptures and ten thousand classics”. The alchemical scriptures and Daoist books of all ages also often discuss the importance of the opening of the Mysterious Pass; but among people today who practice inner work for nourishing life, very few know the role of the Mysterious Pass, and fewer still have received the true transmission of its knack, so it is naturally hard for their practice to enter the hall and the inner chamber.

The phenomenon of the Mysterious Pass spoken of here has no fixed position in the body, nor does it refer to the Root of the Mountain between the two eyes; it refers to the instant in the qigong state when the true breath produced (a flow of energy) changes from quantity to quality — the instant when stillness reaches its extreme and turns to movement, from nothing to something. In this instant true yang arises in my body — what the ancients called “the Living Zi Hour when yang is born”, also “the Supreme Ultimate laying its foundation”, or “the production of the true seed” — that is, the most mysterious and wondrous mechanism in my body opens. In everyday words: “the breath has come, one has got the breath, one has got the opening”!

The opening of the Mysterious Pass is a phenomenon in which, by turning the light back on the lower elixir field, spirit and breath unite, Kan and Li unite, all is chaotic and undifferentiated, dim and obscure, the True Breath (embryonic breathing) is continuous, stillness reaches its extreme and turns to movement, and being arises out of non-being.

Huang sums up how the opening of the Mysterious Pass shows itself in the body in four characters: “one awakening and it stirs”; and he points out that grasping this moment of “awakening and stirring” lies only in the space of one breath (one exhalation and one inhalation). “Awakening” is passing from no knowing and no awareness to sudden wakefulness; “stirring” is the stirring of true yang, which in turn affects the stirring of the body. The ancient “method of stopping thoughts” can serve as a way into the opening: remove all stray thoughts and keep only one right thought; soon this thought too stops of itself, and in the moment before the next thought has arisen comes the opening of the Mysterious Pass.

Key Terms

  • Throwing away all thoughts entirely (wannian xu juan 万念胥捐): xu means “all”, juan “to discard”. Putting down all thoughts.
  • Lowering the curtains: the eyes slightly closed, as if closed and yet not closed.
  • Cave of Emptiness: a transformed state formed in stillness below the heart and above the kidneys, including the lower elixir field.
  • Central palace: in this book, the lower elixir field (and also the Cave of Emptiness); see lower Cinnabar Field.
  • The mind that wants to be clearly aware: the mind that deliberately wants to “know clearly”.
  • The land of nothing whatever: a phrase from Zhuangzi, the empty, boundless realm.
  • Withered wood and dead ashes: deathly stillness without illuminating awareness; see realm of yin / realm of the aggregates.
  • Extinguish the moving mind, do not extinguish the illuminating mind: extinguish deluded thoughts, keep illuminating awareness.
  • One awakening and it stirs: suddenly waking in the moment without knowing or awareness, with true yang stirring along with it — the opening of the Mysterious Pass.
  • The Supreme Ultimate laying its foundation, the Living Zi Hour when yang is born: the moment when stillness reaches its extreme and turns to movement, being arising out of non-being; see Living Zi Hour.
  • Restoring life and returning to the root: movement reaching its extreme and returning to stillness, being returning to non-being; see concentrating the spirit in the Cavity of Breath.
  • Without desire, observing the wonder; with desire, observing the opening: stillness with nothing at all within is observing the wonder; when the mechanism of the breath stirs, knowing without producing views is observing the opening.
  • Fixing it with one look, grasping it with one hand: when the Mysterious Pass opens, holding it firmly with the true intention.
  • The method of stopping thoughts: removing all stray thoughts and keeping one right thought; when the right thought too stops and the next has not yet arisen, the Mysterious Pass opens.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The order of beginning: sit upright → put down all thoughts → lower the curtains → contemplate the empty space “that seems to be there” below the heart and above the kidneys → let spirit and breath concentrate in the lower elixir field. There is no need to look for a specific point.
  2. Chaos is not drowsiness. “Extinguish the moving mind; do not extinguish the illuminating mind”: stray thoughts have been put down, but that point of clear awareness remains. If after sitting a while your head is blank and you know nothing at all, that is torpor, not the undifferentiated state.
  3. One awakening and it stirs depends on “examining it clearly in ordinary times”. It is like lightning or a spark from flint; if you have not practiced beforehand, you cannot catch it. Ordinary quiet sitting is training in this “examining”.
  4. Aware, without producing views. When the mechanism of the breath stirs, know that it stirs, but do not think “is this the Mysterious Pass?” or “I have got the opening” — “one mind and no second thought”.
  5. The method of stopping thoughts can be a way in: stray thoughts → keep only one right thought → the right thought too comes to rest of itself → that moment when the previous thought has gone and the next has not yet arisen.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section comes from Huang Yuanji’s commentary on chapter 1 of his Commentary on the Daode jing; we have divided it into four short passages.
  2. “Not brought about by outer sounds or things touching it” (bu you gan fu 不由感附): not arising from contact with or dependence on outer things.
  3. “There is ‘wonder’ and ‘opening’” (yi you miao qiao 亦有妙窍): echoing “observe its wonder” and “observe its boundary (opening)” in chapter 1 of the Daode jing.
  4. “The Most High”: the Most High Lord Lao, that is, Laozi, as the original book itself notes.
  5. Qiu Ling’s note, “in everyday words, breath, got the breath, got the opening” (yong suhua shuo, qi, deqi, deqiao le 用俗话说，气、得气、得窍了): so printed in the original; a character seems to be missing before “breath” (such as 来, “come”), and the translation follows “the breath has come, one has got the breath, one has got the opening”.
  6. “Transformed state” (huajing 化境): Qiu Ling’s term for a state that forms naturally after entering stillness, not a physical body part.

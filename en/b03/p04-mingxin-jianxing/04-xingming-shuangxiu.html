Chapter Four · Clarifying the Mind and Seeing the Nature · 性命双修

The Dual Cultivation of Nature and Life

The dual cultivation of nature and life — first establish life through nature, then complete nature through life; cultivating nature without life, a yin soul for ten thousand kalpas can hardly enter sagehood; cultivating life without nature is like looking at one’s face without a precious mirror

About This Chapter

The last section of this chapter turns back from “nature” to “life”: cultivating nature alone is not enough; nature and life must be cultivated together.

  1. Three steps: first establish life through nature (lay the foundation with the work on nature), then complete nature through life (complete the work on nature with the work on life), and finally nature and life become one and return to emptiness.
  2. How nature is cultivated: put down all ties, and in utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, dim and obscure, a numinous light shines forth clearly — this is clarifying the mind and seeing the nature. The original nature is present at every moment; it does not exist only when one is still; stillness merely nourishes it.
  3. When the work is deep: as soon as the human mind is still, like ice and snow melting unawares, a thread of numinous light suddenly appears; all scenes are clear and transparent, not a single thought arises, and Heaven, Earth and all things are enfolded by me — this is the flood-like breath of which Mencius speaks.
  4. Why life must also be cultivated: when the mind has no arising and ceasing, the nature can last forever; when the breath has no going out and coming in, life can be preserved forever. The nature is the substance of the breath, the breath the function of the nature; without nature, life has no way to arise; without life, nature has no way to be established. Those who only preserve nature without establishing life — as soon as the breath stirs, the spirit follows it; they cannot cut off desire, nor escape birth and death. Cultivating nature is great, but refining life is still more urgent.
  5. Two kinds of one-sidedness:
    • Cultivating nature only: practicing concentration only in the palace of Li (the heart), without seeking true yang in the watery storehouse (the lower elixir field), one obtains only a dim, indistinct yin spirit — “cultivating nature without life, a yin soul for ten thousand kalpas can hardly enter sagehood”.
    • Cultivating life only: guarding only the lower field, preserving the original essence, seeking to subdue the breath, without knowing to refine breath into spirit upward; the breath has not returned to the spirit, and as soon as there are thoughts, lust and desire still stir — “cultivating life without nature is like looking at one’s face without a precious mirror”.

Qiu Ling’s note: those who cultivate nature without life do not know how to gather the true yang within Kan to replenish the true yin within Li; those who cultivate life without nature do not know the work of the spirit-fire shining down and of rising from below to the top of the head. When spirit and breath meet and Kan and Li unite, nature and life appear of themselves — the principle is simple, but unfortunately students mostly lean to one side.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

性命双修之学，非独吾道为然，即三教圣人亦莫能外。始以性立命，继以命了性，终则性命合一，以还虚无之体尽矣！夫性本虚无，浑无物事，然必至虚而含至实，至无而含至有，始不堕于虚空一流。学者下手兴功，万缘放下，纤尘不染，虚极静笃之时恍恍杳冥，而有灵光昭著，普照大千世界，此即灵台湛寂，佛所谓大觉如来，道所谓灵知真知是。但人自有身后，一点真灵面目久为尘垢所污，修行人所以必除思虑祛尘缘，而于静中养出端倪也。此即明心见性也。

The learning of the dual cultivation of nature and life is not ours alone as Daoists; even the sages of the three teachings — Confucian, Buddhist and Daoist — have never been able to depart from it. First “establish life through nature” (lay the foundation of the work on life with the work on nature), then “complete nature through life” (complete the work on nature with the work on life), and finally nature and life become one and return to the substance of emptiness — and it is complete! The nature is originally empty, undividedly holding not a single thing; but there must be utmost reality within utmost emptiness, utmost being within utmost non-being, and then it does not fall into the school of mere voidness. When the student begins the work, he puts down all ties, letting not a speck of dust cling, and in utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, dim and indistinct, deep and obscure, a point of numinous light appears clearly, shining over the whole great thousand worlds — this is “the numinous terrace deep and still” (the ground of the mind clear and limpid, still and unmoving), what the Buddhists call “the great awakened Tathāgata” and the Daoists “numinous knowing, true knowing”. But ever since a person has had a body, this point of true numinous face has long been stained by dust and dirt, so the practitioner must remove thoughts and worries, get rid of worldly ties, and nourish its beginnings in stillness. This is clarifying the mind and seeing the nature.

§2
Original (Chinese)

诸子探出这个消息，始知我生本性无时不在，非因静而后有，不过由静而养之耳。至人心一静，又如冰雪融化于不知不觉中，忽然现出一线灵光，非但人不及知，而己亦不自觉。斯时万境澄彻，片念不生，觉得天地万物无不自我包罗，古今万年无不自我贯注，此即孟子养浩然之气，至大至刚，以充塞乎两大之间者。是如此见性，方为真见。如此养性，始成真养，斯时也神游于穆之表，气贯太和之天，寂然湛然，浑然融然。

When you have found out this news, you will know that the nature I was born with is present at every moment; it does not exist only because I am still; it is simply nourished through stillness. As for when the human mind becomes still, it is again like ice and snow melting unawares: a thread of numinous light suddenly appears, which not only others have no time to know of, but even oneself does not notice. At this moment all scenes are clear and transparent, not a single thought arises, and one feels that Heaven, Earth and all things are without exception enfolded by me, the ten thousand years of past and present without exception penetrated by me — this is what Mencius calls nourishing the flood-like breath, “utmost in greatness, utmost in firmness, filling the space between the two great ones” (vast and firm beyond measure, filling all between Heaven and Earth). Seeing the nature like this is truly seeing; nourishing the nature like this is truly nourishing. At this moment the spirit roams beyond the deep and solemn, the breath penetrates the heaven of supreme harmony — still, limpid, undivided, melting.

§3
Original (Chinese)

诸子由此修持，始焉心无生灭，则性可长存矣！继焉息无出入，则命可长保矣！古云：“心在丹田身有主，气归元海寿无穷。”不诚然乎？无奈今之修士即有究心性之源，明造化之妙，又不知性为气体，气为性用，无性则命无由生，无命则性无由立。漫说尽性即可至命，须知立命乃可了性。彼徒存性不能立命，每见气动而神随，究不能断乎情欲，神游而气散，更不能逃夫生死。由此言之，修性大矣，而炼命尤急焉。

If you cultivate from here, at first, when the mind has no arising and ceasing, the nature can last forever! Next, when the breath has no going out and coming in, life can be preserved forever! The ancients said: “With the mind in the elixir field, the body has a master; with the breath returned to the original sea, life is without end.” (With the mind kept in the elixir field, the body has a master; with the breath returned to the original sea, one’s lifespan is endless.) Is it not just so? Unfortunately, today’s practitioners of the Way, even those who can investigate the source of mind and nature and understand the wonder of creation, do not know that the nature is the substance of the breath, and the breath the function of the nature: without nature, life has no way to arise; without life, nature has no way to be established. Do not say lightly that “exhausting the nature is enough to reach life”; know that “only by establishing life can one complete the nature”. Those who only know how to preserve the nature but cannot establish life often find that as soon as the breath stirs, the spirit goes along with it, and in the end they cannot cut off desire; as soon as the spirit wanders, the breath scatters, and still less can they escape birth and death. From this it follows that cultivating nature is indeed great, but refining life is still more urgent.

§4
Original (Chinese)

若但离宫修定，不向水府求玄，则离宫阴神犹是无而不有，虚而不实，纵静中寻静，深入杳冥之境，只得一个恍惚阴神样子，终不能聚则成形，散则成气，欲有则有，欲无则无，实实在在有个真迹也。故曰：“修性不修命，万劫阴灵难入圣。”

If one practices concentration only in the palace of Li (the heart), without going to the watery storehouse (the lower elixir field) to seek the “mysterious” (the Prior Heaven true yang), then the yin spirit of the palace of Li remains nonexistent without being, empty without reality; even if one seeks stillness within stillness and goes deep into the realm of the obscure, one obtains only the semblance of a dim, indistinct yin spirit, and in the end cannot gather into form when it gathers and disperse into breath when it disperses, being when one wants it to be and not being when one wants it not to be, with a real, solid trace. So it is said: “Cultivating nature without life, a yin soul for ten thousand kalpas can hardly enter sagehood.” (Cultivating only nature without life, even after ten thousand kalpas one is only a yin soul, hard put to become a sage.)

§5
Original (Chinese)

又有只知炼命者，但固守下田，保养元精，前此未闻尽性之功，后此但求伏气之术，惟炼离宫阴精使之化气，复守肾间动气使之不漏，不知移炉换鼎，向上做炼气化神功夫，虽胎田气满，然气未归神，神未伏气，有时念虑一起，神行气动，仍不免动淫生欲。故曰：“修命不修性，犹如鉴容无宝镜。”

There is also a kind of person who only knows how to refine life, merely guarding the lower elixir field and preserving the original essence; who has never heard of the work of exhausting the nature, and afterward seeks only arts of subduing the breath; who only refines the yin essence of the palace of Li to transform it into breath, and guards the moving breath between the kidneys so that it does not leak, yet does not know “moving the furnace and changing the tripod” (shifting the center of cultivation upward from the lower elixir field) to do the work of refining breath into spirit above; although the embryo field (the lower elixir field) is full of breath, the breath has not yet returned to the spirit, and the spirit has not yet subdued the breath; sometimes, when a thought arises and the spirit stirs, the breath stirs along with it, and one still cannot avoid lust and desire stirring. So it is said: “Cultivating life without nature is like looking at one’s face without a precious mirror.” (Cultivating only life without nature is like wanting to see one’s face without a precious mirror — one cannot see oneself clearly.)

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

修性不修命者虽知修定离宫，只守阴神，却不知采坎中真阳以补离中真阴，水府求玄，才能复还乾坤本来真面目。修命不修性者虽知固守下田，却不知神火下照及由下升至顶上之功，才能炼出至阳之气。各偏于一端，均不符性命双修之旨。

神气交，坎离合而性命见，其理亦简，惜古来学者不明，致陷于一偏之学，而难有大成。

Those who cultivate nature without life, though they know to practice concentration in the palace of Li, guard only the yin spirit, and do not know that they must gather the true yang within Kan to replenish the true yin within Li — this is “seeking the mysterious in the watery storehouse” — so as to restore the original true face of Qian and Kun. Those who cultivate life without nature, though they know to guard the lower elixir field, do not know that there is also the spirit-fire shining down, and the work of rising from below to the top of the head, so as to refine the breath of utmost yang. Each leans to one side, and neither accords with the purport of the dual cultivation of nature and life.

When spirit and breath meet and Kan and Li unite, nature and life appear — the principle is also very simple; unfortunately students since ancient times have not understood it, and so have fallen into one-sided learning and seldom achieved great things.

Key Terms

  • Dual cultivation of nature and life: nature is the mind-spirit, life the original breath in the body; the two cultivated together; see inner nature / life.
  • Establish life through nature, complete nature through life: first lay the foundation of the work on life with the work on nature, then complete the work on nature with the work on life.
  • The numinous terrace deep and still: the ground of the mind clear and limpid, still and unmoving.
  • The great awakened Tathāgata, numinous knowing and true knowing: Buddhist and Daoist names for the numinous clarity of the original nature.
  • Flood-like breath: from Mencius, “Gongsun Chou I”: “utmost in greatness, utmost in firmness; nourish it with uprightness and do it no harm, and it fills the space between Heaven and Earth”.
  • The two great ones: Heaven and Earth.
  • With the mind in the elixir field the body has a master, with the breath returned to the original sea life is without end: the original sea is the lower elixir field.
  • The nature is the substance of the breath, the breath the function of the nature.
  • Palace of Li, watery storehouse: the palace of Li is the heart, the watery storehouse the kidneys and lower elixir field; see conjunction of Kan and Li.
  • Seeking the mysterious in the watery storehouse: seeking the Prior Heaven true yang in the lower elixir field to replenish the true yin within Li.
  • Yin spirit: the dim, indistinct spirit obtained by cultivating nature without life, which cannot gather into form or disperse into breath.
  • Subduing the breath: hiding away the original breath; see Chapter Three, section five.
  • The moving breath between the kidneys: the moving breath between the two kidneys.
  • Moving the furnace and changing the tripod: shifting the center of cultivation upward from the lower elixir field to do the work of refining breath into spirit; see the three transmutations.
  • Embryo field: the lower elixir field.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Neither nature nor life may be neglected. Seeking only a still mind, seeing light, empty numinosity, without real evidence of true breath-energy in the body, is a “yin spirit”; guarding only the elixir field and cultivating breath sensations, with mind and nature impure, desire still stirs as soon as there is a thought.
  2. Where the cultivation of nature lands: the original nature is present at every moment; quiet sitting only nourishes it — there is no need to seek to “see” anything.
  3. Where the cultivation of life lands: the mind without arising and ceasing, the breath without going out and coming in — when the mind is still, breathing naturally becomes fine, and the original breath is preserved.
  4. Test: in stillness, is there only a dim, empty, numinous feeling, with no change of warmth and fullness in the body? Conversely, is there a strong feeling of breath in the elixir field, yet restlessness as soon as a thought arises? Both are one-sided.
  5. Safety note: the “work of rising from below to the top of the head” that Qiu Ling’s note mentions happens naturally after the true breath-energy in the lower elixir field is ample and the mind and nature pure; do not forcibly lead the breath up to the crown with the mind. If a swollen feeling in the head, headache, a flushed face or palpitations appear, stop and return to natural relaxation.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in three paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The first and third paragraphs are each divided into two short passages.
  2. “Only refines the yin essence of the palace of Li to transform it into breath” (惟炼离宫阴精使之化气): so in the original book. The preceding sentence says “merely guarding the lower field and preserving the original essence”, and “palace of Li” here does not seem to fit the sense very well; we record it as is, without change.
  3. “Do not say lightly that exhausting the nature is enough to reach life” (漫说尽性即可至命): manshuo, do not say lightly.
  4. The two lines “cultivating nature without life” and “cultivating life without nature” are often quoted by alchemists; the original only says “so it is said”.

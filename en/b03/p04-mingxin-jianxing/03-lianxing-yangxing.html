Chapter Four · Clarifying the Mind and Seeing the Nature · 炼性养性

Refining and Nourishing the Nature

Refining and nourishing the nature — seeing the nature takes a moment, refining the nature takes a lifetime; before things come, empty and open as before; when responding to them, numinously aware as always

About This Chapter

The previous section spoke of “seeing the nature”; this section speaks of what comes after: refining the nature and nourishing the nature.

  1. The process of seeing the nature: put down all ties, even setting the body aside; when one reaches the point where all is undivided, without others or self, that is the nature; an awakening at that moment is truly seeing the nature. What issues from the true nature as the Original Spirit is the true mind.
  2. The elixir is the Prior Heaven original nature, but it must be presided over by the true intention (the true mind) to be truly one’s own. With the true nature there is a root; with the true mind there is a function.
  3. After seeing the nature: one numinous point shines brightly, all images are empty, the body is full and surging, diffuse and mingling, and the Prior Heaven original breath moves through the whole body — this is the Original Spirit refining the great medicine. Cultivate earnestly, and in a year or so the foundation of the elixir can be made firm.
  4. Why so few accomplish the Way: because seeing the nature takes only a moment, while refining the nature takes a lifetime. The first seeing of the nature is only an awakening within chaos, not yet clear on every side; it must be nourished until the original breath in the body and the original breath of the Great Void have no gap between them, and then even the breaths going in and out all turn to light, and one feels only a point of numinous light — what the ancients called the precious pearl, the bright pearl, the muni pearl. This is the utmost of seeing the nature, when the original nature is about to become the Dharma-body.
  5. The work in movement: Huang Yuanji criticizes his disciples for being able to stay alert and still in quiet, yet growing tired and weary as soon as they deal with affairs and people — this is because their nourishing is not deep. The method: the mind like a bright mirror, empty and open as before when things have not come, numinously aware as always when responding to things that come; and distinguish the original nature from the ordinary nature, the Original Spirit from the cognitive spirit.
  6. The ancients gave their lives to fulfill humaneness and sacrificed life to uphold righteousness, all because they recognized this truly and nourished it firmly, and obtained the true joy within it.

Qiu Ling’s note: the opening of the Mysterious Pass, one awakening and it stirs, is seeing the nature; but if the refining and nourishing of the nature is not pure, one cannot hold on at the moment of awakening. Refining and nourishing the nature to the utmost is the Dharma-body; refining until the body is like the Great Void and the physical body is not seen is the first step of the Dharma-body. Ordinarily, refining and nourishing the nature comes down to nourishing it with emptiness and stillness.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

大凡打坐，必先将万缘放下，一丝不挂，即是此身亦置之于无何有之乡，我亦不觉其有象。如此一念操持，即一念归真，到得浑浑沦沦、无人无我、何地何天之候，即性也，性即仁也。我若有觉，即是真正见性也。由此真性发为元神，即真心也。明心见性又何难哉！盖炼而曰丹，丹即先天元性，然必以真意为之主宰，然后才为我有。夫曰真意，即真心也。有此真性，方为有本。得此真心，方为有用。

Whenever you sit, you must first put down all ties, without a thread of attachment, even sending this body off to “the land of nothing whatever”, so that I do not feel it has form. Holding to this one thought is returning to the true in one thought; when one reaches the point where all is undivided, without others or self, not knowing what place one is in or what heaven is above, this is “the nature”, and the nature is what the Confucians call “humaneness”. If I have an awakening at this moment, that is truly seeing the nature. What issues from this true nature is the Original Spirit, which is also the true mind. What difficulty is there, then, in clarifying the mind and seeing the nature! The “elixir” of refining the elixir is the Prior Heaven original nature; but it must be presided over by the True Intention before it can truly be my own. The true intention is the true mind. Having this true nature, one has a root; obtaining this true mind, one has a function.

§2
Original (Chinese)

到得见性之后，一灵炯炯，万象咸空，于是吾身蓬蓬勃勃，氤氤氲氲，先天至精元气运行于一身内外，上下往来，即是元神炼大药也。如此采取，如此烹炼，方不是后天神气，亦不致枉劳心力。大约真性一见，真气一动，认真修炼，不过一年半载之久，丹基可固。总要下手之初，认真性命二字，庶几采取先天，烹炼一过，自成一先天大道。若杂后天，犹种良苗而和乱草，乌有好结果哉！

After seeing the nature, one point of numinous clarity shines brightly, all images are empty, and then my body is full and surging, diffuse and mingling, the Prior Heaven utmost essence of original breath moving back and forth through the whole body, inside and out, above and below — this is the Original Spirit refining the great medicine. Gathering like this, cooking and refining like this, it is not the Posterior Heaven spirit and breath, and the effort is not wasted. Generally, once the true nature is seen and the true breath-energy stirs, if one cultivates earnestly, in no more than a year or so the foundation of the elixir can be made firm. One must always, at the very beginning, recognize the two words “nature and life” earnestly and clearly; only then can one gather the Prior Heaven thing, cook and refine it, and naturally accomplish a great Prior Heaven way. If Posterior Heaven things are mixed in, it is like weeds mixed in when planting good seedlings — how could there be a good result!

§3
Original (Chinese)

虽然，性之为物，如此易见，何以成道之人如此其少哉？亦以见性在一时，而炼性则在终身。惟能以先天元性为本，时刻操持，自然日积月累而有缉熙光明之候。如初见性时，不过混沌中一觉，不能八面玲珑，必养之久久，吾身元气与太虚元气无间，方有此境。

Even so, if the nature is so easy to see, why are there so few who accomplish the Way? It is because seeing the nature takes only a moment, while refining the nature takes a lifetime. Only by taking the Prior Heaven original nature as the root and holding to it at every moment will one naturally, day by day and month by month, reach a time of “continuing and growing bright” (becoming bright without cease, from the Book of Odes). For instance, when one first sees the nature, it is only an awakening within chaos, not yet clear and bright on every side; one must nourish it for a long, long time, until the original breath in my body and the original breath of the Great Void have no gap between them, before there is this state.

§4
Original (Chinese)

又曰：人身浑与天地一气，除却有我之私，皆是天也，天岂远乎哉！欲到此地位，须空心无物，性空似水，至于忘物、忘人、忘我，才有此太和一气。学者欲与太虚同体，必使内想不出，外想不入，即出入息一齐化为光明，浑觉自家只有一点灵光而已。所谓元始现一宝珠于空中，又谓一颗明珠永不离，又谓炼成一粒牟尼宝珠。其喻名不一，而要不过一灵显象，常应常静而已。此即见性见到极处也，先天元性亦将成为法身之时也。

It is also said: the human body is originally one undivided breath with Heaven and Earth; remove the selfishness of “having a self”, and all is Heaven — how is Heaven far away! To reach this point, the mind must be empty of things and the nature empty like water, until one forgets things, forgets others, forgets oneself; only then is there this one breath of supreme harmony. If the student wants to be one with the Great Void, he must reach “inner thoughts do not go out, outer thoughts do not come in”, until even the breaths going in and out all turn together into light, and one feels undividedly that one has only a point of numinous light. This is what is called “the Primordial reveals a precious pearl in the void” (the Celestial Worthy of Primordial Beginning reveals a precious pearl in space), also “one bright pearl never leaves”, also “refine into a single muni pearl” (the muni pearl is the wish-fulfilling jewel of the Buddhists). The metaphors differ in name, but all come down to this one point of numinous clarity showing its form, always responding to things and yet always still. This is seeing the nature to the utmost, and also the time when the Prior Heaven original nature is about to become the Dharma-body.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

此节指出元性本末始终形象，以明见性后炼性、养性之重要。所谓见性在一时，而炼性、养性则在终身。玄关窍开一觉而动，是为见性。《唱道真言》云：“心何以明？忽然而明，此玄关也。性何以见？忽然而见，此玄关也，玄关为明心见性之灵机，结胎炼丹之妙括。”然而，一觉而动之际，我炼性、养性不纯，便捉摸不着，坐失灵机。又我静中初见元性，必炼之养之，炼出太和一气，才能使我身与太虚同体，周身光明，一灵炯炯。炼性养性到了极点，就炼成法身。炼到我身浑如太虚，色身不见，是炼成法身的初步功夫。

This section points out the form of the original nature from beginning to end, to show the importance of refining and nourishing the nature after seeing it. As is said, seeing the nature takes only a moment, while refining and nourishing the nature takes a lifetime. The opening of the Mysterious Pass, one awakening and it stirs, is seeing the nature. The True Words of Chanting the Way says: “How does the mind become clear? Suddenly it is clear — this is the Mysterious Pass; how is the nature seen? Suddenly it is seen — this is the Mysterious Pass. The Mysterious Pass is the numinous spring of clarifying the mind and seeing the nature, the key knack of forming the embryo and refining the elixir.” Yet at the moment of one awakening and it stirs, if my work of refining and nourishing the nature is not pure, I cannot grasp it, and the numinous spring is missed in vain. Moreover, when I first see the original nature in stillness, I must refine it and nourish it, refining out the one breath of supreme harmony; only then can my body be one with the Great Void, the whole body full of light, one point of numinous clarity shining brightly. Refining and nourishing the nature to the utmost, one forms the Dharma-body. Refining until my body is undividedly like the Great Void and even the physical body is not seen — this is the first step of the work of forming the Dharma-body.

§5
Original (Chinese)

吾见生等处静，则惺惺寂寂，不昧心性之源。而其处动，因物为缘、随缘以应之机，尚未十分周到，十分恳切，犹不免有劳倦厌烦之态。何也？由养之未深，行之未至也。如今已明此心见此性矣，不妨随时随处都要空空洞洞，了了灵灵，使一尘不染，万缘咸空。如此则真心常存，而凡心自不能干矣！再示生一法，此心务如明镜，物未来时此心空洞如故，静以养其本体。物既应时，此心灵觉如常，亦虚以养其神明，更还要明得元性凡性、元神识神，而后不致于认贼作子也。学人果知得本来心性个中消息，其味自有无穷。

I see that when you are in quiet places, you can stay alert and still, not losing the source of mind and nature. But in places of movement, at the juncture where outer things give rise to circumstances and one responds to the circumstances, you have not yet been thorough and earnest enough, and still cannot avoid looking tired and weary. Why? Because your nourishing is not yet deep, and your doing not yet complete. Now that you have clarified this mind and seen this nature, you may as well be empty and open, clear and numinous (empty of everything in the mind, yet clear and bright, numinous and never darkened) at every time and place, letting not a speck of dust cling, all ties empty. In this way the true mind is always present, and the ordinary mind naturally cannot interfere! Let me teach you another method: this mind must be like a bright mirror — when outer things have not come, the mind is empty and open as before, and one nourishes its substance with stillness; when outer things come and one responds to them, the mind is still numinously aware as always, and one also nourishes its spiritual clarity with emptiness. Above all, understand the distinction between the original nature and the ordinary nature, between the Original Spirit and the cognitive spirit; only then will one not take a thief for one’s son. If the student truly knows this news of the original mind and nature, its flavor will naturally be endless.

§6
Original (Chinese)

古人杀身成仁，舍生取义，皆于此认得真，养得定，虽刀锯在前，鼎镬在后，宁丧身殒命，不肯昧心失性，不然彼独非人哉！何以不畏生命于此？殆由见道明，守道力，而得个中真乐也。

The ancients gave their lives to fulfill humaneness and sacrificed life to uphold righteousness, all because they recognized this truly and nourished it firmly; even with knives and saws before them and cauldrons (the great vessels for boiling people, an ancient cruel punishment) behind them, they would rather lose body and life than darken their conscience and lose their original nature — otherwise, were they alone not human? Why were they not afraid of losing their lives at such times? Probably because they saw the Way clearly, held to the Way with strength, and obtained the true joy within it.

Qiu Ling's noteon §5–§6
Original note (Chinese)

此教人练性养性于平时，不外虚静以养之而已。

This section teaches that refining and nourishing the nature in ordinary times comes down simply to nourishing it with emptiness and stillness.

Key Terms

  • Without a thread of attachment: not a single tie in the mind.
  • The land of nothing whatever: a phrase from Zhuangzi, the place where there is nothing at all.
  • The nature is humaneness: speaking of the original nature in terms of the Confucian “humaneness”.
  • True mind: the Original Spirit issuing from the true nature, that is, the true intention.
  • The Original Spirit refining the great medicine: after seeing the nature, the Prior Heaven original breath moves through the whole body.
  • Foundation of the elixir: the basis for refining the elixir.
  • Continuing and growing bright: becoming bright without cease, from the Book of Odes, “Daya”, “King Wen”: “how continuous and bright, reverent and still”.
  • Clear on every side: bright and penetrating in all directions.
  • Precious pearl, bright pearl, muni pearl: metaphors for the original nature, the one numinous point showing its form, always responding and always still; see Golden Flower.
  • Dharma-body: what is formed when the nature is refined and nourished to the utmost; see spontaneous Dharma Body.
  • Alert and still: awake and quiet.
  • The mind like a bright mirror: empty and open as before when things have not come, numinously aware as always when responding to them.
  • Original nature, ordinary nature: the Prior Heaven original nature and the Posterior Heaven acquired nature.
  • Original Spirit, cognitive spirit: the Prior Heaven spirit and the Posterior Heaven spirit of thinking.
  • Taking a thief for one’s son: taking the Posterior Heaven ordinary nature and cognitive spirit for the Prior Heaven original nature.
  • Cauldrons: ancient instruments of cruel punishment, used for boiling people.
  • True Words of Chanting the Way: an alchemical book transmitted by Heququzi of the Qing dynasty, already seen in Chapter One.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Seeing the nature takes a moment, refining the nature a lifetime. Seeing light once in quiet sitting does not mean you are finished; it must be nourished day by day and month by month.
  2. Training the mind in movement — the bright mirror method: before an affair comes, the mind is empty; when it comes, respond to it clearly; when it has passed, the mind is empty again. Leave no trace on things.
  3. Test yourself: if you are fine in quiet sitting but grow irritated, tired and weary as soon as you deal with affairs, your nourishing is not yet deep; carry the empty numinosity of stillness into your affairs.
  4. Tell true from false: the original nature and the ordinary nature, the Original Spirit and the cognitive spirit; do not take Posterior Heaven cleverness and emotions for your original nature.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in three paragraphs; the original book does not give the source, and Qiu Ling’s notes come in two places. The first paragraph is divided into four short passages; the second and third are one passage each.
  2. “The key knack of forming the embryo and refining the elixir” (结胎炼丹之妙括): so in the original book; miaokuo means the key knack, and the translation follows this.
  3. “Otherwise, were they alone not human?” (不然彼独非人哉): meaning “otherwise, were they alone not human (and so unafraid of death)?”
  4. The three metaphors beginning “the Primordial reveals a precious pearl in the void” are given in the original without sources.

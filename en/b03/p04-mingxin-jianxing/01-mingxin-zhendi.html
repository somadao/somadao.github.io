Chapter Four · Clarifying the Mind and Seeing the Nature · 明心见性真谛

The True Meaning of Clarifying the Mind and Seeing the Nature

The true meaning of clarifying the mind and seeing the nature — the nature is originally empty, and being pure and natural is seeing the nature; the mind is originally numinous, and being bright and aware is clarifying the mind; reality within emptiness — once the work on nature reaches this point, the work on life comes easily

About This Chapter

This chapter speaks of “the work on nature”: clarifying the mind and seeing the nature. The first section begins by explaining what clarifying the mind is, what seeing the nature is, and why one must begin from concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath:

  1. Two definitions: the nature is originally empty; as soon as it is pure, clear and natural, that is seeing the nature; the mind is originally numinous; as soon as it is bright, aware and unobstructed, that is clarifying the mind. Once seen, not a trace of deliberation or imagination may be added; one simply follows what is natural.
  2. Why one must concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath: after birth, a person is bound by temperament, veiled by desire, entangled in love and attachment, and the mind has long since ceased to be empty; if the spirit is not concentrated it scatters, if the breath is not regulated it runs loose. When the spirit is concentrated it is one, and being one it is empty — the emptiness of my mind is the nature endowed by Heaven; when the breath is regulated it is even, and being even it is harmonious — the harmony of my body is the life received from Heaven and Earth.
  3. Reality within emptiness: this emptiness is not manufactured but natural; as soon as my spirit is empty, the clear, harmonious breath of Heaven and Earth naturally comes to join it — this is reality within emptiness.
  4. How the mind becomes clear: being empty it is numinous, being numinous it is clear. Before it stirs it is vast and boundless, “inner thoughts do not go out, outer thoughts do not come in”; when it stirs, it responds to what touches it and penetrates, lively and unconstrained like a roaming dragon.
  5. Beginning: first let the mind be free and lively, entrusted to Heaven, roaming in the Great Void; then, like a cat catching a mouse or a hawk spotting a hare, guard and illuminate with a single mind that point of spirit and breath in the elixir field. Once the work on nature reaches this point, the work on life comes easily.

Qiu Ling’s note describes the scene of clarifying the mind and seeing the nature: the mind like still water, the intention like a cold pool, the body one with the Great Void, and also like a silkworm in spring wrapped in its cocoon by the breath’s dynamism, with only one point of numinous clarity shining alone in the middle. The knack lies in “reality within emptiness, being out of non-being”: using the emptiness of one’s body to draw in the original breath of space.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

性本虚也，一旦清净自如，即见性矣！心本灵也，一旦光明觉照，洞达无碍，即明心矣！于此心明性见之后，著不得一毫思议想象，惟有顺其天然自然而已矣！

The nature is originally empty; as soon as it is pure, clear and at ease, that is seeing the nature! The mind is originally numinous; as soon as it is bright, aware and unobstructed, that is clarifying the mind! After the mind is clear and the nature seen, there can be not the slightest deliberation, discussion or imagination — only following what is natural, and that is all!

§2
Original (Chinese)

吾示明心见性之真谛。夫先天之心即性，先天之性即虚无元气，要之一虚而已矣！人自有生后，气质之拘，情欲之蔽，恩爱之缠，此心之不虚者久矣！气为心使，精为神役，驰逐妄游，消耗殆尽，此学人下手兴功，所以贵凝神调息也。盖神不凝则散，散则游思妄想迭出，安能团聚一区以为炼丹主帅？惟能凝则一，一则虚，我心之虚，即本来天赋之性。外来太空之虚，即未生虚无之性。息不调则放，放则内而脏腑，外而肌肤，无非一团躁急之气运行，欲其凝聚一团而为我造命之本，盖亦难矣！

Let me tell you the true meaning of clarifying the mind and seeing the nature. The Prior Heaven mind is the nature, and the Prior Heaven nature is the empty original breath — in short, it is just the one word “empty”! Ever since a person was born, bound by temperament (the Posterior Heaven disposition), veiled by desire, entangled in love and attachment, this mind has long ceased to be empty! The breath is driven by the mind, the essence is put to work by the spirit, chasing after outer things and running about everywhere, until they are nearly used up — this is why, when the student begins the work, he must first value concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath. For if the spirit is not concentrated it scatters, and once it scatters, wandering thoughts and delusions arise one after another; how then could it gather in one place and serve as the commander for refining the elixir? Only by concentrating can one be one; only by being one can one be empty. This “emptiness” in my mind is the nature originally endowed by Heaven; the “emptiness” of space outside is the empty nature before birth (the two are originally one). If the breathing is not regulated it runs loose; once it runs loose, the five viscera and six bowels within, the muscles and skin without, are nothing but a mass of agitated breath moving about; to want to gather it into a mass as the root of my “creating life” (creating and continuing life) is then hard!

§3
Original (Chinese)

惟能调则平，平则和。我身之和，即我生以后受天地之命。太和一气，即未生以前悬于天地之命。此即真性真命，与天地人物合而不分之性命。生等欲复命归根，以臻神化之域，亦无他修，只是凝神令静，调息令匀，勿忘勿助，不疾不徐，使心神气息皆入于虚极静笃而已矣。但非造作之虚，乃自然之虚，故天地鬼神人物同一源也。然亦非虚而无实也。惟我之神既虚，则天地清和之气自然相投，人之所以参天地赞化育，变化无穷，神妙莫测者，即此神息之虚，得感清空之虚之气入来，此虚中所以有实也。

Only when it can be regulated is the breathing even; only when even is it harmonious. This “harmony” in my body is the life I received from Heaven and Earth after birth; the one breath of supreme harmony between Heaven and Earth is my life suspended between Heaven and Earth before birth. These are the true nature and the true life — the nature and life one and undivided with Heaven, Earth, people and things. If you want to restore life and return to the root, reaching the state of spiritual transformation, there is no other method of cultivation either: only concentrate the spirit to make it still, regulate the breath to make it even, neither forgetting nor forcing, neither fast nor slow, so that both mind-spirit and breath enter utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, and that is all. But this is not an emptiness manufactured by human effort; it is a natural emptiness — that is why Heaven and Earth, ghosts and spirits, people and things all come from one source. Yet neither is there only emptiness without reality. As soon as my spirit is empty, the clear, harmonious breath between Heaven and Earth naturally comes to join it; the reason people can take part with Heaven and Earth and assist in the nurturing of all things, transforming without end, wondrous and unfathomable, is that the “emptiness” of this spirit and breath draws in the empty breath of clear space — this is why there is reality within emptiness.

§4
Original (Chinese)

久久凝聚，自然身心内外，有刚健中正纯粹以精之景。如此见性，方是真性发见。心何以明？惟虚则灵，灵则明，明则众理俱备，万事兼赅，未动则浩浩荡荡，无知无识，所谓内想不出，外想不入，但觉光明洞达，一理中涵，万象咸包，斯得之矣！及触物而动，随感而通，遇圆则圆，随方则方，活泼不拘，似游龙之莫测。又云：“静则为元神，动则为真意”。神与意一也，不过动静之分焉耳。

When it has been gathered for a long, long time, there naturally appears in body and mind, inside and out, a scene “firm and strong, central and upright, pure and unmixed, refined to the utmost”. Seeing the nature like this is the appearing of the true nature. How does the mind become clear? Only by being empty is it numinous; only by being numinous is it clear; when it is clear, all principles are complete and all things are contained within it. Before it stirs, it is vast and boundless, without knowing or recognizing — what is called “inner thoughts do not go out, outer thoughts do not come in” (thoughts within do not go out, things outside do not come in); one feels only brightness and penetration, one principle contained within and all images enfolded — and so you have it! When it comes into contact with things and stirs, it responds to what touches it and penetrates, round with the round and square with the square, lively and unconstrained, wondrous and unfathomable like a roaming dragon. It is also said: “In stillness it is the Original Spirit, in movement the True Intention.” Spirit and intention are one thing, only distinguished by movement and stillness.

§5
Original (Chinese)

又闻古云：“心无性无主，性无心无依”。心所以载性，性所以统心，是知心之高明广大，神妙无穷者，即性之量也。明得这个真心，即明性矣！但此性未在人身，盘旋清空为元气，既落人身为元神，要皆虚而不有。学者下手之初，必要先将此心放得活活泼泼，托诸于穆之天，游于太虚之表，始能内伏一身之铅汞，外盗天地之元阳，久之神自凝而息自调，只觉丹田一点神息，浑浩流转，似有如无。我于此守之照之，有如猫之捕鼠，鹰之逢兔，一心顾諟，不许外游，自然外感内应，觉天地之元气流行于一身内外而无有休息也。性功到此，命功自易焉。

I have also heard the ancients say: “The mind without the nature has no master; the nature without the mind has nothing to rely on.” The mind carries the nature, the nature governs the mind; from this we know that the loftiness, brightness and breadth of the mind, its endless wonder, are the measure of the nature. Understand this true mind, and you understand the nature too! But this nature, before it falls into a human body, circling in clear space, is the original breath; after it falls into a human body, it is the Original Spirit — in short, it is all empty without having anything (empty and numinous, without form). When the student first begins, he must first let this mind be free and lively, entrusting it to that deep, pure and solemn Heaven, roaming beyond the Great Void; only then can he subdue within the Lead and Mercury (breath and spirit) of the whole body and steal without the original yang of Heaven and Earth; in time the spirit naturally concentrates and the breath naturally harmonizes, and one feels only a point of spirit and breath in the elixir field, turning vast and undivided, as if present and as if absent. I simply guard it here and illuminate it, like a cat catching a mouse or a hawk spotting a hare, watching it with single-minded attention, not allowing it to wander outward; naturally the outer stirs and the inner responds, and one feels the original breath of Heaven and Earth flowing through the whole body, inside and out, without a moment’s rest. Once the work on nature reaches this point, the work on life naturally becomes easy.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§5
Original note (Chinese)

虚极静笃中，心如止水，意若寒潭，我身浑然似与太虚同体，光明洞达，万象咸包，直若身形似无，然又若此身在气机包裹中，如春蚕作茧一般，我惟一灵炯炯，独照当中，内外浑忘，此即为明心见性之景。

此中诀窍在于虚中有实，无中生有。以我身之虚，吸取太空元气，外感内应，以收天人合一之机。

In utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, the mind is like still water, the intention like a cold, deep pool; my body seems undividedly one with the Great Void, bright and penetrating, all images contained within, almost as if the body had no form at all; and yet it is also as if this body were wrapped by the breath’s dynamism, like a silkworm in spring spinning its cocoon; I have only one point of numinous clarity, bright and shining, illuminating alone in the middle, inside and outside both undividedly forgotten — this is the scene of clarifying the mind and seeing the nature.

The knack within this lies in “reality within emptiness, being out of non-being”: using the emptiness of my body to draw in the original breath of space, the outer stirring and the inner responding, thus achieving the effect of the union of Heaven and the human.

Key Terms

  • Seeing the nature: the nature is originally empty; being pure, clear and natural is seeing the nature.
  • Clarifying the mind: the mind is originally numinous; being bright, aware, penetrating and unobstructed is clarifying the mind.
  • Bound by temperament: the constraint of the Posterior Heaven disposition.
  • Commander: the concentrated spirit is the commander for refining the elixir.
  • Creating life: creating and continuing life, that is, the work on life.
  • True nature and true life: the emptiness of my mind is the nature endowed by Heaven, the harmony of my body is the life received from Heaven and Earth; see inner nature / life.
  • Taking part with Heaven and Earth and assisting the nurturing of things: from the Doctrine of the Mean; people take part with Heaven and Earth and help nurture all things.
  • Firm and strong, central and upright, pure and unmixed, refined to the utmost: from the Book of Changes, “Qian”, “Commentary on the Words of the Text”.
  • Inner thoughts do not go out, outer thoughts do not come in: thoughts within do not race outward, things outside do not disturb within.
  • The mind without the nature has no master, the nature without the mind has nothing to rely on: the mind carries the nature, the nature governs the mind; the two cannot be separated.
  • That deep, pure and solemn Heaven: the profound, pure Heaven, from the Book of Odes, “The mandate of Heaven — how profound and unceasing”.
  • Lead and Mercury: here, the breath and spirit in the body.
  • Watching with single-minded attention (gushi): guarding attentively, from the Great Learning, “keep the bright mandate of Heaven always in view”.
  • Work on nature, work on life: the work of cultivating mind and nature, and the work of cultivating the original breath in the body; see inner nature / life.

Putting It into Practice

  1. First seek emptiness, then clarity. When the spirit is concentrated it is one, and being one it is empty; when the breath is regulated it is even, and being even it is harmonious — clarifying the mind and seeing the nature come from concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath, not from thinking.
  2. Let the mind be free: when beginning, first let the mind be free and lively, not tense; then guard and illuminate that point of spirit and breath in the elixir field.
  3. The measure of guarding and illuminating: like a cat waiting for a mouse — attentive, quiet, missing nothing, yet not pouncing and grabbing wildly.
  4. Once seen, add nothing: after the mind is clear and the nature seen, no more deliberating, comparing or imagining — simply follow what is natural.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in two paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The second paragraph is divided into four short passages.
  2. “Heaven and Earth, ghosts and spirits, people and things all come from one source” (天地鬼神人物同一源也): so in the original book; it means that Heaven and Earth, ghosts and spirits, people and things all come from the same source of emptiness.
  3. Huang Yuanji often quotes the Confucian classics (the Changes, the Doctrine of the Mean, the Great Learning, the Book of Odes) and Buddhist sayings to confirm the alchemical way; this occurs in many sections of this chapter, and the sources are noted in the translation as they come.

Chapter Four · Clarifying the Mind and Seeing the Nature · 本来面目

The Original Face

The original face — like a bright moon in the sky; when you first see it, be neither startled nor delighted; the dwelling place of the original person is in the elixir field

About This Chapter

This section speaks of the first thing in cultivating the elixir: recognizing one’s own original face.

  1. What the original face is like: like a bright moon in the sky, perfectly round and full, leaning to no side, shining on all the nine regions and ten thousand countries. The mind is the nature, the nature is the Buddha.
  2. What it is like when seen: in still concentration, one suddenly feels the light of the mind bright and clear, neither clinging nor detached, measureless and boundless, and yet in truth holding nothing at all — this is clarifying the mind and seeing the nature.
  3. The greatest fault: on first seeing this scene, one is startled and delighted, giving rise to a Posterior Heaven ordinary mind, and the Prior Heaven Original Spirit is scattered instead.
  4. The method: when there is a sense of being undividedly one with Heaven and Earth, become more concentrated and more still; when thoughts arise, ignore them all. Even if one suddenly sees the substance of the true nature, it is only a momentary turning of the light; one must still nourish it warmly for a long time, so that it is like “taking back an old thing from one’s own home” — nothing to be delighted about, nothing to be startled at.
  5. Where the original person dwells: after long nourishing, there is in the elixir field a mass of diffuse, harmonious, lively vitality — this is the original person taking form. Hide it deep, protect and nourish it, letting the breath grow fuller day by day. “Immortals and Daoists have no special powers; they accumulate essence and breath to become true.”

Qiu Ling’s note: seeing the nature, the original face, the original person, the Prior Heaven substance of the nature — the names differ, the meaning is the same, and where it dwells is in the elixir field. When first seen, there must be no feeling of surprise, delight or good fortune; if one goes to admire it, it is lost.

Original Text and Translation

§1
Original (Chinese)

修丹别无他妙，第一要认得自家本来面目。此个本来面目亦岂有他，犹如皓月当空，团团圞圞，不偏不倚，九州万国，无一不在照临之中。此即先天面目，即心即性，即性即佛，无二致也。学者于静定之时，忽然觉得我心光光明明，不沾不脱，无量无边，而实一无所有，此即明心见性，实实得先天面目也。但初见此景，不免自惊自喜，生一后天凡心，而先天浑沦之元神却又因此打散。

There is no other mystery in cultivating the elixir: the first thing is to recognize one’s own original face. And what else could this original face be? It is like a bright, clear moon hanging in the sky, perfectly round and full, leaning to no side, with not a place in the nine regions and ten thousand countries outside its light. This is the Prior Heaven face: the mind is the nature, the nature is the Buddha, and they are not two. When the student is in still concentration, he suddenly feels that the light of my mind is bright and clear, neither clinging to anything nor detached from anything, measureless and boundless, and yet in truth holding nothing at all — this is clarifying the mind and seeing the nature, truly obtaining the Prior Heaven face. But on first seeing this scene, one can hardly avoid being startled and delighted, giving rise to a Posterior Heaven ordinary mind, and the undivided Prior Heaven Original Spirit is scattered by it instead.

§2
Original (Chinese)

示生一法，大凡打坐习静，若有个浑然与天地同体之意在我怀抱，不妨再定再静。纵有念起，我总总一个不理他。那知觉心、惊讶心、喜幸心一概自无。再者，尔于静久时，勿入大乘，虽见真性本体，要不过瞥尔回光，还要多多调习，久久温养，使此心此性实实入我定中，还我家故物，无所喜，亦无所惊。如此久炼，始能返本还原，归根复命。

Let me teach you a method: whenever you sit and practice stillness, if there is in your embrace a sense of being undividedly one with Heaven and Earth, you may as well concentrate a little more and become a little more still. Even if thoughts arise, I always ignore them all. In this way the mind of awareness, the mind of surprise and the mind of delight naturally all disappear. Moreover, when you have been still a long time and suddenly enter the realm of the great vehicle, though you see the substance of the true nature, it is only a momentary reverting of the light (a glimpse of the returning light); you must still regulate and practice much, nourishing it warmly for a long time, so that this mind and this nature truly enter my concentration — like taking back an old thing from one’s own home: nothing to be delighted about, nothing to be startled at. Only by refining in this way for a long time can one return to the root and restore the origin, return to the root and restore life.

§3
Original (Chinese)

我今特示本来人所居之地。调养久久，丹田中觉有一团氤氲冲和活泼之机在内，即本来人现形也。我必修养于中，藏之深深，即《易》云洗心退藏于密是。若瞥地回光，忽觉丹田中上下往来，周流不息，有活泼不滞流行自如之机，我亦保之养之，务令此气日充月盛。故曰：“仙人道士非有神，积精累气以成真。”此即积累精气之细密工也。至于保身体，养心性，要不过由此而致之。

Today I point out specially the place where “the original person” dwells. After long nourishing, one will feel in the elixir field a mass of diffuse, harmonious, lively vitality — this is the original person taking form. I must cultivate and nourish it in the elixir field, hiding it deep; this is what the Changes calls “cleansing the mind and withdrawing it into secrecy” (washing the mind clean and withdrawing it into a hidden place). If, in a glimpse of the returning light, one suddenly feels in the elixir field a coming and going up and down, circulating without cease, a lively, unimpeded, freely flowing vitality, I must also protect it and nourish it, making sure that this breath grows fuller day by day and more abundant month by month. So it is said: “Immortals and Daoists have no special powers; they accumulate essence and breath to become true.” (Immortals and Daoists have no supernatural powers; they simply accumulate essence and breath and become true persons.) This is the fine, meticulous work of accumulating essence and breath. As for preserving the body and nourishing mind and nature, these too are accomplished from here and nowhere else.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§3
Original note (Chinese)

见性即见自家本来面目：本来人、先天性体。这些名目同一义也。其所居之地，其现形之处，原在丹田。

初见自家本来面目，容不得有惊喜之心和庆幸之情，否则，先天性体从此散失。我看见先天性体，乃瞥地回光所见，若去欣赏，则失之。学者宜警惕之！

Seeing the nature is seeing one’s own original face, also called “the original person” or “the Prior Heaven substance of the nature” — these names all mean the same thing. The place where it dwells, the place where it takes form, is originally in the elixir field.

When one first sees one’s own original face, there must be no mind of surprise or delight, no feeling of good fortune; otherwise the Prior Heaven substance of the nature is lost from then on. I see the Prior Heaven substance of the nature in a glimpse of the returning light; if I go to admire it, it is lost. Students should be on their guard!

Key Terms

  • Original face: the true self one has from Prior Heaven, also called the Prior Heaven face, the original person, the Prior Heaven substance of the nature.
  • Tuantuan luanluan: perfectly round and full. 圞 is read luán, round.
  • The mind is the nature, the nature is the Buddha: mind, nature and Buddha are originally one.
  • Neither clinging nor detached: not attached to anything, and not leaving the present either.
  • Great vehicle: a Buddhist term, here referring to the lofty state of suddenly seeing the substance of the true nature in stillness.
  • A glimpse of the returning light: a momentary turning of the light; see Reverting the Light.
  • Taking back an old thing from one’s own home: it was originally one’s own, so there is nothing to be startled or delighted about.
  • The original person taking form: a mass of diffuse, harmonious, lively vitality in the elixir field.
  • Cleansing the mind and withdrawing it into secrecy: from the “Xici” (Appended Statements), part one, of the Book of Changes.
  • Accumulating essence and breath: see Essence and Breath.

Putting It into Practice

  1. When you see it, be neither startled nor delighted. When in stillness everything suddenly becomes bright, open and boundless, that is when it is easiest to go “wow” — and that “wow” is the Posterior Heaven ordinary mind; the Original Spirit scatters at once. Treat it as an everyday matter.
  2. Ignore thoughts that arise: when there is a sense of being undividedly one with Heaven and Earth, become a little more still; when thoughts come, ignore them all.
  3. Seeing it once does not count: that is only a momentary turning of the light; you must still nourish it warmly for a long time, so that it enters your concentration steadily.
  4. Land it in the elixir field: the original person takes form in the elixir field — a mass of harmonious, lively vitality. Hide it deep, protect and nourish it, letting the breath grow fuller day by day and month by month; this is accumulating essence and breath.

Translator's Notes

  1. The original text of this section is in two paragraphs; the original book does not give the source. The first paragraph is divided into two short passages.
  2. “When you have been still a long time, do not enter the great vehicle” (尔于静久时，勿入大乘): the character 勿 (“do not”) does not fit the following “though you see the substance of the true nature”, and is probably a mistake for 忽 (“suddenly”) — suddenly entering the realm of the great vehicle; the translation follows 忽.
  3. “The fine, meticulous work of accumulating essence and breath” (积累精气之细密工也): 工 is gongfu, work.
  4. “Immortals and Daoists have no special powers; they accumulate essence and breath to become true”: the original only says “so it is said”, without a source.

Chapter Four · Clarifying the Mind and Seeing the Nature · 篇首导读

Introduction to Chapter Four

What this chapter covers

The first three chapters spoke of the Mysterious Pass, the gate of the Mysterious Female and the true intention, with an emphasis on method. This chapter speaks of the mind-and-nature side of cultivation — clarifying the mind and seeing the nature, that is, “the work on nature”; its last section then brings the work on nature and the work on life together, speaking of “the dual cultivation of nature and life”.

Huang Yuanji’s definition is very simple: the nature is originally empty, and being pure, clear and natural is seeing the nature; the mind is originally numinous, and being bright and aware is clarifying the mind. And the method for achieving this is still concentrating the spirit and regulating the breath: when the spirit is concentrated it is one, and being one it is empty; when the breath is regulated it is even, and being even it is harmonious.

The four sections of this chapter follow this order:

What clarifying the mind and seeing the nature is and where to begin (its true meaning) → what it is like when seen and what to watch out for (the original face) → how to refine and nourish it after seeing it (refining and nourishing the nature) → cultivating the nature alone is not enough; nature and life must be cultivated together (the dual cultivation of nature and life)

What this chapter is about (in one sentence)

Concentrate the spirit and regulate the breath until utmost emptiness and steadfast stillness, when the light of the mind is bright and clear, boundless and limitless, and yet holds nothing at all — this is seeing the original face; when you see it, be neither startled nor delighted; thereafter refine and nourish it all your life, empty and open in stillness, numinous and aware in movement; and you must also seek the mysterious in the watery storehouse and cultivate nature and life together, so as not to become a yin spirit.

Reading tips

  1. “Seeing the nature” is not seeing some marvelous scene. Huang Yuanji says it very clearly: the light of the mind is bright and clear, measureless and boundless, and yet “in truth holds nothing at all”. The greatest fault when seeing it is being startled or delighted — as soon as one is startled or delighted, the Original Spirit scatters.
  2. The practical focus of this chapter is the “bright mirror method” of section three: when affairs come, respond to them clearly; when they go, be empty and open as before. The empty numinosity of quiet sitting is to be carried into one’s daily dealings.
  3. The dual cultivation of nature and life is the guiding principle of the whole book. Cultivating only nature yields a dim, indistinct yin spirit; cultivating only life, the breath becomes full but the mind and nature are not pure, and desire still stirs. From Chapter Five onward the book turns to the work on life, such as “essence, breath and spirit”.
  4. “The work of rising from below to the top of the head” comes only after the true breath-energy is ample; do not forcibly lead the breath up to the head with the mind.

Appendix 4 · The Old Man of Qianfeng’s Method of Practice for Women (Niu Jinbao)

Appendix 4 · The Old Man of Qianfeng’s method of practice for women (Niu Jinbao)

About This Chapter

Mr. Qiu Ling says in his afterword that Zhao Bichen’s original book “is weighted toward the men’s practice and does not reach the women’s practice,” so before publication he reprinted this piece, The Old Man of Qianfeng’s Method of Practice for Women, written by Niu Jinbao, a disciple of Qianfeng, as Appendix 4.

  1. The author’s explanation: Niu Jinbao admits frankly that he knows little about the women’s method — when he became a disciple he was only eighteen or nineteen, steeped in the ideas of ritual propriety, and too embarrassed to ask; moreover, the women’s practice was traditionally passed by the teacher to the teacher’s wife, and by her to the women disciples. What he presents is only what he heard at his teacher’s side, but following it is enough to dispel illness and strengthen the body.
  2. How the men’s and women’s methods differ: he uses the eight trigrams to explain the difference of yin and yang between men and women; men set to work by “capturing the White Tiger,” women by “beheading the Red Dragon”; for men in old age there is “striking the bamboo and fighting the tortoise,” for women “striking the zither to lure the phoenix”; men have “closing the yang gate,” women “the method of returning the breath.” He does not go into these advanced methods.
  3. The formula song and its detailed explanation: what he presents is the basic part of the Dragon Gate school’s women’s practice of quiet nurturing, put into a song of sixteen lines and then explained line by line: relaxing and sitting down, closing the eyes and guarding the Crimson Palace, the hands pinching zi-wu, the tongue against the palate, regulating the breathing evenly, testing right and wrong by “the heart settled, thoughts stopped,” forgetting self and others and seeing the round light, and three ways of closing the practice: warming the eyes, stretching the arms and lengthening the waist, and rubbing the whole body dry.
  4. Ten original notes: at the end he explains terms such as “capturing the White Tiger,” “beheading the Red Dragon,” “the four gates,” “contemplating the opening” and “contemplating the wonder.”

Safety note (translator): The quiet sitting, breathing and self-massage in this text are all gentle health methods; let comfort be the measure. Advanced methods such as “beheading the Red Dragon” (stopping menstruation) are only named in the text; for questions involving menstruation or reproductive health, consult a doctor, and do not try on your own to change your physiological cycle. If you have a lump or pain in the breast, see a doctor promptly, and do not rely on massage alone. “Stretching the arms and lengthening the waist” requires lying back and bending forward; people with lumbar or cervical spine problems should make the movements small and slow.

Original Text and Translation

The Author's Explanation
Original (Chinese)

在介绍妇女练功法之前，首先向妇女读者说明，我对妇女的功法知道得不多。其原因是我向恩师千峰老人求学的时候，正在十八九岁青年时期，其封建礼教观念还很浓厚。道家的养生功法，讲的是性命双修，都属于生理方面的事情。我不愿问，也不想问。况且妇女的功法，是老师传给师母，再由师母传给妇女学者。故自古就主张夫妇双修。而我所知道的只是在老师跟前听到的，非是正式学到的。我介绍的功法虽然浮浅，但只要照功法去用，也足能祛病强身益寿延年。

Before presenting the method of practice for women, let me first explain to women readers: I know little about the women’s method. The reason is that when I studied under my gracious teacher, the Old Man of Qianfeng, I was a young man of eighteen or nineteen, and the ideas of feudal ritual propriety were still very strong in me. The Daoist health methods teach the dual cultivation of nature and life, and all of them touch on matters of the body’s physiology; I was unwilling to ask, and did not want to. Moreover, the women’s method was passed by the teacher to the teacher’s wife, and by her to the women students, so from ancient times it has been held that husband and wife should cultivate together (the husband and the wife each transmitting to students of their own sex). What I know is only what I heard at my teacher’s side, not something I learned formally. Although the method I present is simple, if you just follow it, it is still enough to dispel illness, strengthen the body and lengthen life.

How the Men's and Women's Methods Differ
Original (Chinese)

性命双修养生功法，不同于其他养生功法，其他养生功法不分男女，而性命双修养生功法不然。由于男女本身生理上的不同，故在功法上也有不同之处。以八卦来说，坤为老阴☷，巽为长女☴，离为中女☲，兑为少女☱；乾为老阳☰，震为长男☳，坎为中男☵，艮为少男☶。这是八卦中的男女阴阳。以养生功法而论，男子有男擒白虎①，女子有女斩赤龙②。男子衰老有敲竹斗龟③，女子衰老有敲琴引凤④。男有闭阳关⑤，女有回炁法⑥，因其生理不同，其功法也就各异。以上所说的功法，在整个功法中，是比较高深一些的功法，也是历代不轻易传人的功法。我现将龙门派性命双修静养功坤生（妇女）用的功法简介如下。

The health method of the dual cultivation of nature and life is not like other health methods: other health methods make no distinction between men and women, but the method of dual cultivation must. Because the physiology of men’s and women’s bodies differs, the methods also differ in some respects. Take the eight trigrams: the Kun trigram is old yin (the mother), Xun the eldest daughter, Li the middle daughter, Dui the youngest daughter; the Qian trigram is old yang (the father), Zhen the eldest son, Kan the middle son, Gen the youngest son — these are the male and female, yin and yang, of the eight trigrams. As for the health methods: men have “the man captures the White Tiger” (note 1), women have “the woman beheads the Red Dragon” (note 2); for men in old age there is “striking the bamboo and fighting the tortoise” (note 3), for women in old age “striking the zither to lure the phoenix” (note 4); men have “closing the yang gate” (note 5), women “the method of returning the breath” (note 6). Because the physiology differs, the methods differ too. These methods are among the more advanced in the whole system, and through the generations they have not been passed on lightly. Below I briefly present the method used by the “Kun students” (women) in the Dragon Gate school’s practice of quiet nurturing in the dual cultivation of nature and life.

The Formula Song
Original (Chinese)

盘膝坐，练静功，全身内外要放松。

闭目观心守本命⑦，回光返照意归中。

手掐子午除杂念，舌闭天池津自生。

深细长匀调呼吸，心定念止是正功。

坐到人我两忘时，光在眼前空不空。

下座拂面熨双睛，伸臂长腰气血通。

浑身上下搓一遍，揉完两乳揉绛宫。

持之以恒能驻颜，老人越活越年轻。

Sit down cross-legged and practice the quiet work; the whole body, inside and out, must relax. Close the eyes, contemplate the heart within, and guard the “root life” (note 7, that is, the Crimson Palace); turning the light around to shine back, the intention returns to the center. The two hands pinch the “zi-wu formula,” clearing away stray thoughts; the tongue closes the “Heavenly Pool” (pressing against the palate), and saliva is produced naturally. Regulate the breathing to make it deep, fine, long and even; only the heart settled and thoughts stopped is the right practice. When you sit until self and others are both forgotten, light appears before the eyes — this is “empty yet not empty.” When getting off the seat, stroke the face with the hands and warm the two eyes, stretch the arms and stretch the waist, and qi and blood flow freely. Rub the whole body from top to bottom once, rub the two breasts, then rub the Crimson Palace. Persist without slackening, and you can keep your looks; the old grow younger as they live.

Detailed Use: Relaxing and Sitting Down
Original (Chinese)

具体用法详说：

妇女坐功的要求与男子坐功的要求基本相同，在入坐前都要求从精神上要有一种愉快感，并将身上衣钮扣、衣带松开，使全身不受束缚，自在湛然而至虚静。即“盘膝坐，练静功，全身内外要放松”。

取坐势。在床上，椅凳，沙发上都可以，盘膝端坐，右腿在外，左腿在内，此为阴抱阳。所谓端坐，上身自然挺直，既不前俯也不后仰，会阴、百会垂直一线，但务必放松自然。应知松则炁顺，百脉流畅；僵则炁滞，有碍炁血流通。只有全身内外放松，才是入静的先决条件。

Below, the specific use is explained in detail line by line.

The requirements of women’s sitting practice are basically the same as men’s: before sitting down, one is asked to have a feeling of cheerfulness, and to loosen buttons and belts so that the whole body is unconstrained, at ease, clear (limpid) and reaches emptiness and stillness. This is what the song means by “Sit down cross-legged, practice the quiet work; the whole body, inside and out, must relax.”

The sitting posture: on a bed, a chair or stool, or a sofa are all fine; sit upright with legs crossed, the right leg outside and the left leg inside — this is called “yin embracing yang.” Sitting upright means the upper body is naturally straight, leaning neither forward nor back, with the perineum and Baihui (the exact center of the crown) on one vertical line, but it must be relaxed and natural. Understand: when you relax, the breath flows smoothly and all the vessels flow freely; when you are stiff, the breath stagnates and hinders the flow of breath and blood. Only relaxing the whole body inside and out is the precondition for entering stillness.

Close the Eyes, Contemplate the Heart, Guard the Root Life
Original (Chinese)

闭目观心守本命。男子垂帘，女子闭目。闭目是外闭而内睁，二目含光凝神，返观内视，意守绛宫。绛宫在心下一寸二分肝的根处，是女子真命所在处。男子性命相距八寸四分，女子性命相距一寸二分，这是道家传统说法。故女子见功比男子要快得多。另一种说法，男子全身属阴，只有中间一点属阳，练功是用这一点真阳去战胜群阴实属不易。相反，女子是全身属阳，只有中间一点属阴。女子练功是用大部群阳去战胜一点真阴，故耳不难。所难者是真传耳。这也是男女不同之处。

“Close the eyes, contemplate the heart, guard the root life”: men “lower the curtains” (the eyes slightly closed, leaving a thread of light), women “close the eyes.” Closing the eyes means closed outside and open inside: the two eyes hold the light and gather the spirit, looking back and seeing within, the intention guarding the Crimson Palace. The Crimson Palace is one cun two fen below the heart, at the root of the liver, the place where a woman’s “true life” resides. In men, inner nature and life (the two foundations) are eight cun four fen apart; in women, inner nature and life are one cun two fen apart — this is the traditional Daoist saying, and so women see results from practice much faster than men. There is another saying: a man’s whole body belongs to yin, with only one point in the middle belonging to yang, and practice uses this one point of true yang to overcome the host of yin in the whole body, which is truly not easy; women are the opposite: the whole body belongs to yang, with only one point in the middle belonging to yin, and practice uses the great host of yang to overcome one point of true yin, so it is not difficult (the original has “故耳不难,” see the Translator’s Notes). The only difficulty is obtaining the true transmission. This too is a way in which men and women differ.

The Hands Pinch Zi-Wu
Original (Chinese)

手掐子午。女子与男子相反，右手大拇指掐住本手中指尖，左手大拇指进入右手内去掐右手无名指的根部，两手扣抱放在小腹部。左腿在内，右腿在外为阴抱阳。右手在内，左手在外为阳抱阴。此为手脚和合扣连环，四门紧闭守正中⑧。手掐子午是为了排除杂念易于入静。因子午两道脉通寸、关、尺，而寸、关、尺三步脉通心，心通大脑，掐住子午是为了减轻动脉撞心的力量，有助于入静，而不是掐住子午即可入静。

“The hands pinch zi-wu”: the women’s hand position is the reverse of the men’s — the right thumb pinches the tip of the middle finger of the same (right) hand, the left thumb goes into the right hand and pinches the base of the right ring finger, and the two hands are clasped together and placed in front of the lower belly. The left leg inside and the right leg outside is “yin embracing yang”; the right hand inside and the left hand outside is “yang embracing yin.” This is “hands and feet joined, clasped and linked like rings; the four gates tightly closed, guard the exact center” (note 8). Pinching the zi-wu formula is for clearing away stray thoughts and entering stillness easily. Because the zi and wu vessels run to the cun, guan and chi of the wrist (the three positions where Chinese medicine takes the pulse), and the pulse at these three positions connects to the heart, and the heart connects to the brain, pinching zi-wu is meant to lessen the force of the arteries pounding against the heart and help one enter stillness — it is not that one enters stillness as soon as one pinches zi-wu.

The Tongue Closes the Heavenly Pool, Saliva Comes by Itself
Original (Chinese)

舌闭天池津自生　闭天池开玄膺男女一样。天池穴又名上腭，在上牙内寸许，口念而字舌尖正抵此处。此穴是说话漏炁之所，故有日出千言不损自伤之说。舌顶天池有三种益处，一闭住此穴不漏真炁，二闭天池开玄膺。玄膺穴在巧舌后，开玄膺好使真息往来畅通无阻。三闭天池有利生津，生津两穴在舌下根处。左为津井　右为石泉，是生津两穴。舌顶天池，自然津生舌底，这也说明心火下降，肾水上升，消化力强，食欲旺盛，身体自然无病。

“The tongue closes the Heavenly Pool, saliva comes by itself”: closing the Heavenly Pool and opening the Mysterious Breast is the same for men and women. The Heavenly Pool point is also called the palate, about one cun behind the upper teeth; say the sound “er” and the tip of the tongue rests exactly here. This point is where breath leaks when you speak, hence the saying “speak a thousand sentences a day and you see no loss, yet in fact you are injuring yourself.” Pressing the tongue to the Heavenly Pool has three benefits: first, closing this point keeps the true breath from leaking away; second, closing the Heavenly Pool opens the Mysterious Breast — the Mysterious Breast point is behind the “clever tongue” (the uvula), and when it is open, the coming and going of the True Breath flows unobstructed; third, closing the Heavenly Pool helps produce saliva — the two points that produce saliva are at the root under the tongue, the left called “Saliva Well” and the right “Stone Spring.” With the tongue against the Heavenly Pool, saliva is naturally produced under the tongue; this also shows that heart fire is descending and kidney water rising, digestion is strong, appetite good, and the body is naturally free of illness.

Regulate the Breathing: Deep, Fine, Long, Even
Original (Chinese)

深细长匀调呼吸。调理呼吸是静养功的重要一环，要求做到深、细、长、匀。所谓深，是行深呼吸，一吸一呼，息息归根。所谓细，是吸呼绵绵，微而且细，连自己也听不到呼吸声。所谓长，要把呼吸拉长。匀，是快慢均匀。初练功不要勉强追求深、细、长、匀，要像平日呼吸一样自然，由自然逐渐达到深、细、长、匀，心息相依，进而息不调而自调，甚至忘息。

“Regulate the breathing: deep, fine, long, even”: regulating the breathing is an important part of the quiet nurturing practice, and it should be made deep, fine, long and even. “Deep” means breathing deeply, each inhalation and exhalation, every breath returning to the root (the lower elixir field); “fine” means the breathing is soft and continuous, faint and fine, so that you cannot even hear your own breathing; “long” means drawing the breathing out long; “even” means the speed is uniform. When you first start practicing, do not force yourself to pursue deep, fine, long and even; let it be as natural as ordinary breathing. From the natural, gradually reach deep, fine, long and even, the heart and breath relying on each other; then, without your regulating it, the breathing regulates itself, and you even forget the breathing altogether.

The Heart Settled, Thoughts Stopped, Is the Right Practice
Original (Chinese)

心定念止是正功。功夫用得准确与否，其鉴别非常简单，即心定念止是对的，心动念驰是错的。收功后，浑身舒服，神清气爽，心明眼亮，如此功夫即是对的。倘坐完功后，身体感到劳累，精神疲备不堪，口干舌燥，就是错了。

“The heart settled, thoughts stopped, is the right practice”: telling whether the practice is right or not is very simple: if the heart is settled and thoughts stop, it is right; if the heart is moving and thoughts run wild, it is wrong. After closing the practice, if the whole body is comfortable, the spirit clear and the breath fresh, the heart bright and the eyes clear, the practice is right. If after sitting the body feels tired, the spirit is utterly exhausted (the original writes 疲备), and the mouth and tongue are dry, then it is wrong.

Sit Until Self and Others Are Both Forgotten
Original (Chinese)

如果真的坐到人我两忘，就像自己的身体不存在一样，甚至连外边的音响也听而不闻，一切都完全忘掉，这时眼前就会出一个圆光，此光即是修真养性的“性”，明心见性的“性”，是自己本来面目，又谓玄关。古云：“内观其心，心无其心。外观其形，形无其形。远观其物，物无其物。三者即悟，惟见于空。观空不空，是为真空，观空乃空，是为顽空”（有光是真空，无光是顽空）。又云：“常有欲以观其窍”⑨（即性窍与命窍），“常无欲以观其妙”⑩（妙即是性光）。以上这两段道家的名言，都是指静养功的观空不空而说的。

If you truly sit until self and others are both forgotten, as if your own body no longer existed, even hearing outside sounds without hearing them, everything entirely forgotten — then a round ball of light will appear before your eyes. This light is the “nature” of “cultivating the true and nourishing the nature,” the “nature” of “illuminating the heart and seeing the nature”; it is your own original face, also called the “Mysterious Pass.” The ancients said: “Contemplate your heart within, and in the heart there is no heart; contemplate your form without, and the form is no longer form; contemplate things afar, and things are no longer things. When these three are understood, one sees only emptiness. Contemplating emptiness that is not empty — this is true emptiness; contemplating emptiness and it is really empty — that is dull emptiness.” (Light present is true emptiness, light absent is dull emptiness. This passage comes from the Scripture of Clarity and Stillness; see the Translator’s Notes.) It is also said: “Always with desire, so as to contemplate its opening” (note 9; the opening is the opening of inner nature and the opening of life), “always without desire, so as to contemplate its wonder” (note 10; the wonder is the light of inner nature). (These two lines are from chapter 1 of the Daodejing, where the received text reads “contemplate its boundaries”; Niu Jinbao reads it as “opening,” following the alchemists’ understanding.) Both of these famous Daoist sayings describe the state of “contemplating emptiness that is not empty” in the quiet nurturing practice.

Getting Off the Seat, Stroke the Face and Warm the Eyes
Original (Chinese)

下座拂面熨双睛。在初练功时期，坐功时间至少不能少于二十分钟，如坐得舒服，时间越长越好。当下座收功之后，先搓热两手掌心，趁热两手分别捂住两眼，待热散后，两手同时向两侧分开，两眼随之用力一睁。如此接连用三遍，然后转眼球，左右各九圈。这谓熨眼法。此法能使两眼恢复疲劳，又能神清目明，不闹眼疾。

“Getting off the seat, stroke the face and warm the eyes”: in the first period of practice, each sitting should be no shorter than twenty minutes; if the sitting is comfortable, the longer the better. After getting off the seat and closing the practice, first rub the palms of both hands until hot, and while they are hot cover the two eyes with the two hands; when the warmth has gone, part the two hands to the sides at the same time, and open the two eyes firmly as they go. Do this three times in a row, then roll the eyeballs, nine circles to the left and nine to the right. This is called the “eye-warming method.” This method relieves the fatigue of the eyes (the original says “restores fatigue”), makes one’s spirit clear and eyes bright, and keeps one from eye disease.

Stretch the Arms and Lengthen the Waist, Qi and Blood Flow
Original (Chinese)

伸臂长腰气血通。用完熨眼功后，仍取坐势，两腿伸开，两脚尖向里勾，两脚跟向前蹬，两手按膝。以上是预备势。然后上身向后仰卧，两脚尖前绷，脚跟后收，同时两臂伸直，两手掌心向上，身躯仰卧，长腰伸直，平躺床上。在身躯向后仰卧的同时，鼻子吸气，两眼向上翻看。然后两手与头慢慢翘起，上身坐起前扑，两臂前伸，两手打两脚心，脚尖向里勾，脚跟后蹬。头靠拢两膝，同时鼻子呼气眼往下看，身躯后仰前扑，鼻子一吸一呼为一次。如此后仰前扑连做七次。此功法舒筋活血长腰增力。在盘膝时腿脚如感麻木，用完此功后立解。注意吸呼、仰卧、前扑必须协调一致。

“Stretch the arms and lengthen the waist, qi and blood flow”: after the eye-warming practice, still sitting, stretch out the two legs, the toes hooked inward, the heels pushing forward, the two hands on the knees — this is the ready position. Then lie back with the upper body: the toes pointed straight forward, the heels drawn back; at the same time the two arms are straight, the palms facing up, the body lying on the back, the waist lengthened and straight, lying flat on the bed. As the body lies back, inhale through the nose, the two eyes turned upward. Then slowly raise the two hands and the head, sit up and bend forward: the two arms stretch forward, the two hands pat the soles of the feet, the toes hooked inward, the heels pushing back, the head close to the knees; at the same time exhale through the nose, the eyes looking down. One lying back and one bending forward, one inhalation and one exhalation through the nose, count as once. Lie back and bend forward like this seven times in a row. This practice relaxes the sinews and quickens the blood, lengthens the waist and increases strength; if the legs and feet go numb from sitting cross-legged, the numbness goes at once after this practice. Note: the breathing and the movements of lying back and bending forward must be coordinated.

Rub the Whole Body from Top to Bottom Once
Original (Chinese)

浑身上下搓一遍，揉完两乳揉绛宫。浑身干搓，又谓干沐浴，是非常有效的健身法。其具体搓法，先从头部开始，两手从前额往后颈用力推搓三十六下，再顺序搓面部，继而太阳穴、迎香、脖颈、风池、风府、两臂内外侧（即阴维，阳维）、前小腹、脐轮、后腰眼、尾骶、两腿内外侧（即阴跻、阳跻）、膝盖、涌泉。最后两手揉两乳，两手分别捂住两乳，左转三十六，右转二十四，再揉搓绛宫（绛宫在心下一寸二分肝的根处）。在浑身搓时，必须以自己两手触及皮肤，并用数字控制，搓得皮肤发热为止。在搓时还可以伴之叩牙齿，鸣天鼓。叩牙三十六下，叩齿二十四下。叩时使上下牙齿撞击有声。这样能使牙齿括约筋收缩有力，保持牙根不拔，有防止患牙病的功能。另外在解大小便时，也要用力咬紧牙关，此法常用，既能控制炁不下泄，又能巩固牙齿，可一举两得。鸣天鼓，是保持两耳不聋的有效措施。用法两手分别捂严两耳，用两手食指压住两手中指。当两手中指挣脱时，两手食指借中指挣脱的弹力敲击玉枕两侧，并发出似击鼓般的咚咚声。每次敲击九下。此法也是一般练功的常识，但贵在坚持。

“Rub the whole body from top to bottom once, rub the two breasts, then rub the Crimson Palace”: dry rubbing of the whole body, also called “dry bathing,” is a very effective method of keeping fit. How to rub: begin with the head, the two hands pushing and rubbing firmly from the forehead back to the nape thirty-six times; then rub the face in order, followed by the temples, Yingxiang (beside the wings of the nose), the neck, Fengchi and Fengfu (the back of the neck), the inner and outer sides of the two arms (that is, the Yin and Yang Linking Vessels), in front the lower belly and the navel wheel (around the navel), behind the small of the back and the tailbone, the inner and outer sides of the two legs (that is, the Yin and Yang Heel Vessels; the original writes 阴跻、阳跻), the knees and the Bubbling Spring (the soles). Finally rub the two breasts with the two hands: cover each breast with a hand, turn thirty-six circles to the left and twenty-four circles to the right; then rub the Crimson Palace (the Crimson Palace is one cun two fen below the heart, at the root of the liver). When rubbing the whole body, your own two hands must touch the skin directly, and the number of strokes should be counted; rub until the skin is warm.

While rubbing, you can also add knocking the teeth and beating the heavenly drum: knock the front teeth thirty-six times and the back teeth twenty-four times, making the upper and lower teeth strike with a sound. This makes the muscles around the teeth contract strongly, keeps the roots of the teeth firm so they do not fall out, and helps prevent dental disease. Also, when passing stool or urine, clench the teeth firmly; used regularly, this method both keeps the breath from leaking downward and strengthens the teeth — two benefits at once. Beating the heavenly drum is an effective way to keep the ears from going deaf: cover each ear tightly with a hand, and press the index fingers of both hands onto the middle fingers; as the middle finger slides off, the index finger uses the spring to strike both sides of the Jade Pillow at the back of the head, making a “dong-dong” sound like a drum. Strike nine times each time. This method is also common knowledge in general practice, but what counts is persistence.

Persist and You Can Keep Your Looks
Original (Chinese)

持之以恒能驻颜，人老也能变年轻，好像有些过于夸张。但如果真能做到持之以恒，定能收到推迟衰老，延长寿命的效果。

我所介绍的妇女功法，最适合五十岁上下年龄采用。因为到这个年龄，抚养儿女的任务基本完成，应抓紧时间修补一下身体房屋。另外如果中年妇女有条件，练此功法也同样只有百益而无一害。

“Persist and you can keep your looks”: saying that by persisting you can keep your looks and even grow younger in old age may seem a little exaggerated. But if you can truly persist without slackening, you will certainly achieve the effect of delaying aging and lengthening life.

The women’s method I present is most suitable for people around fifty. For at that age the task of raising children is basically done, and one should seize the time to repair one’s own “house of the body.” In addition, middle-aged women who have the opportunity will also find this method entirely beneficial and without harm.

Niu Jinbao’s original notes:

Note 1: “The man captures the White Tiger” is the refining of essence into breath when setting to work on the Lesser Celestial Circuit (see Step Four of the main text).

Note 2: “The woman beheads the Red Dragon” is cutting off menstruation, transforming the red menstrual blood into a white quintessence that moistens the whole body (see the safety note).

Note 3: “Striking the bamboo and fighting the tortoise” is specifically for old men of seventy or eighty.

Note 4: “Striking the zither to lure the phoenix” is specifically for women of seventy or eighty.

Note 5: “Closing the yang gate” is a top-secret method of the Dragon Gate school, known only to those who have received the “Scroll of the Mandate of Heaven” (the formal certificate of transmission).

Note 6: “The method of returning the breath” is for Kun students (women), likewise a top-secret method of the Dragon Gate school.

Note 7: A woman’s “root life” is the Crimson Palace.

Note 8: “The four gates” are the eyes, ears, nose and mouth. Another explanation is: close the Heavenly Pool and open the Mysterious Breast, close the Conception Vessel and open the Governing Vessel, close the six pulses and open the Gate of Heaven, let go of the hands and hold the breath (four pairs of closing and opening).

Note 9: When there are thoughts and desires in the heart, use “contemplating the opening” (concentrating on illuminating the opening of inner nature and the opening of life) to stop them, and so reach the state where even the “contemplating” is forgotten.

Note 10: When there are no thoughts or desires, a wondrous state, vague and indistinct, appears naturally.

Key Terms

  • Beheading the Red Dragon (斩赤龙): a term of women’s alchemy, meaning to stop menstruation through practice and transform the menstrual blood into essence and breath. It is the counterpart of the men’s “capturing the White Tiger” (refining essence into breath).
  • Crimson Palace (绛宫): the place one cun two fen below the heart. Niu Jinbao holds it to be where a woman’s “root life” resides; in quiet sitting, women guard it with the intention.
  • Zi-wu formula (子午诀): a hand seal in which the thumbs pinch the tip of the middle finger or the base of the ring finger; women’s left and right are the reverse of men’s.
  • Heavenly Pool, Mysterious Breast (天池、玄膺): the Heavenly Pool is the palate; the Mysterious Breast is behind the uvula. Pressing the tongue to the Heavenly Pool closes the place where breath leaks, lets the True Breath flow freely, and produces saliva.
  • Dry bathing (干沐浴): a self-massage method of rubbing the whole body dry, rubbing it warm in order from head to foot.
  • Beating the heavenly drum (鸣天鼓): covering the ears with the hands and using the spring of the middle finger to strike the back of the head with the index finger, making a “dong-dong” sound.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The basic method is shared by men and women: relaxing, quiet sitting, the tongue against the palate, natural breathing, guarding with the intention, and closing with rubbing — these match the basic parts of the steps above. The differences lie mainly in the place guarded (women guard the Crimson Palace) and the reversed hand seal.
  2. Comfort is the standard: Niu Jinbao’s test of “right and wrong” is very practical: if after practice the whole body is comfortable and the spirit clear and fresh, it is right; if you are tired and your mouth and tongue are dry, it is wrong.
  3. Do not skip closing the practice: warming the eyes, stretching the arms and lengthening the waist, rubbing the whole body dry, knocking the teeth and beating the heavenly drum are all simple health methods, beneficial even practiced on their own.
  4. Who it suits: the author thinks it most suitable for women around fifty; middle-aged women can also practice it if they have the opportunity.
  5. Stay safe: advanced methods such as “beheading the Red Dragon” are given only by name — do not try on your own to change your menstrual cycle; rub the breasts only as far as is comfortable, and if there is a lump or pain in the breast, see a doctor promptly; people with lumbar or cervical spine problems should slow down and reduce the range when doing “stretching the arms and lengthening the waist.”

Translator's Notes

  1. “故耳不难”: 耳 is probably a mistake for 而; kept as in the original, and translated as “so it is not difficult.”
  2. “精神疲备不堪”: 疲备 should be 疲惫 (“exhausted”); kept as in the original.
  3. “This method can make the two eyes restore fatigue” (此法能使两眼恢复疲劳): it means to make the eyes recover from fatigue; translated as “relieves the fatigue of the eyes.”
  4. “阴跻、阳跻”: 跻 should be 跷 (蹻), referring to the Yin and Yang Heel Vessels; kept as in the original.
  5. The passage “Contemplate your heart within … that is dull emptiness” adapts the Scripture of Constant Clarity and Stillness Spoken by the Most High Lord Lao: “Contemplate your heart within, and in the heart there is no heart; contemplate your form without, and the form is no longer form; contemplate things afar, and things are no longer things. When these three are understood, one sees only emptiness.” The lines that follow, such as “contemplating emptiness that is not empty,” differ slightly from the received text; translated following Niu Jinbao’s original. In “三者即悟,” 即 appears where the received text has 既.
  6. “常有欲以观其窍”: chapter 1 of the Daodejing reads 徼 (“boundaries”) in the received text; alchemists often read it as 窍 (“opening”); kept as in the original.
  7. The trigram symbols are given in the order of the original; in the narration only the names of the trigrams are read.
  8. “In men inner nature and life are eight cun four fen apart, in women one cun two fen,” “a woman’s whole body belongs to yang, with only one point in the middle belonging to yin,” and the like are traditional Daoist sayings relayed by Niu Jinbao; this site only presents them.
  9. Qiu Ling’s afterword is not included, in keeping with this site’s conventions.

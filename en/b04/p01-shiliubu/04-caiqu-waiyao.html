The Sixteen Steps of Method Formulas · 第四步 采取外药

Step Four · Gathering the Outer Medicine

The formula of Step Four, gathering the outer medicine — the outer medicine has form but no fixed appearance; the Governing Vessel opens, and the tongue joins the Conception and Governing vessels

About This Chapter

This is the longest of the sixteen steps and the one with the most figures (thirteen in the original). It deals with the core of the stage of refining essence into breath: using the “Lesser Celestial Circuit” to gather back the essence that is about to leak out, transform it into original breath, and make good the losses.

  1. Laying the foundation and the Three Origins: first it explains why one must “lay the foundation” — the Original Spirit races outward, the original breath scatters, the original essence decays, and the three treasures of essence, breath and spirit must be refined together; then the “Diagram of the Three Origins” and the “Diagram of Wondrous Union” explain the relation between body, heart and intention (the “three families”) and essence, breath and spirit (the “three treasures”), as well as “the five breaths return to the origin.”
  2. The outer medicine and regulating the medicine: the “outer medicine” is the essence that is about to go out; instead of letting it go, you set to work, gather it back and refine it into the elixir. Before gathering you must first “regulate” — using the method of reining in the yang gate — until “when the medicine is produced, the spirit knows” (when the moment comes, the heart and spirit know by themselves); medicine that is too tender or too old cannot be used.
  3. The method of gathering: when the essence and breath are about to burst out, lie slightly on your side, press the Opening of Life and Death (the perineum) with the middle finger, and with inhalation and exhalation through the nose, turn the mind and the eyes along zi, mao, wu and you to draw the essence back — “as many degrees as the essence comes, so many degrees you draw back.”
  4. Decoding the terms: Zhao Bichen explains one by one the Wind of Xun, the bellows, closing and opening, the six periods, advancing the yang fire, withdrawing the yin tally and the ablutions of mao and you, and says that the “three hundred and sixty circuits” is only a figure of speech — in practice it is one inhalation and one exhalation, one turn from back to front.
  5. Turning the River Chariot empty is useless: without the true yang stirring, merely closing the eyes and counting circuits is “turning empty,” and you will still have emissions at night.

Qiu Ling’s note organizes Zhao’s account into workable steps (the method of turning the eyes, the circuit of one exhalation and one inhalation, the four-word formula “inhale, lick, contract, close”) and stresses that the root lies in reaching the utmost emptiness and abiding in deep stillness, and in turning the gaze back to illuminate within; in moving the circuit one should properly avoid leading the breath with the intention, and instead ride the breath with the spirit and let it be natural.

Safety note (translator): The techniques in this step — pressing the perineum with the middle finger, lying on the side, turning the eyes with the breath — are used only when the true yang stirs; they need not be done in ordinary practice, and one should certainly not deliberately provoke sexual arousal in order to “practice” them. Turn the eyes gently, never with force; people with glaucoma, high myopia or retinal disease should not do the eye turning. People with high blood pressure, heart disease, or prostate or perineal conditions should not press or hold the breath. If you feel a swelling in the head, eye pain, or swelling or pain in the lower belly or perineum, stop at once.

Figure 5 · Gathering the Outer Medicine

The figure is a schematic from the crown to the outer kidney: at the very top is the “Muddy Pellet palace”; a double-lined channel runs down on both sides from the Muddy Pellet, curving into a large vase shape, and narrowing at the bottom into a single tube. Inside the vase, two straight tubes, “artery” and “vein,” branch downward, with the left and right inner kidneys on either side and “prostate” marked below; a round shape in the middle is the “bladder.” The point below the neck of the vase is the Opening of Life and Death; a horizontal line is marked “yinqiao vessel” on one side and “Opening of Life and Death” on the other, with “anus” beside it; at the very bottom is the “outer kidney,” with a “left leg tube” and “right leg tube” branching off on either side. The meaning of the figure: at the Opening of Life and Death, the essence either goes with the current out through the outer kidney, or turns back up the Governing Vessel to reach the Muddy Pellet.

Formula
Original (Chinese)

采外药有形无相，

督脉开舌接任督。

巽风坤火阖辟转，

六候橐籥转昆仑。

Gather the outer medicine: the medicine has form (essence), but no fixed appearance; the Governing Vessel opens, and the tongue (pressed against the palate) joins the Conception and Governing vessels. With the Wind of Xun (the breath) and the fire of Kun (the fire of the lower elixir field), turn it with closing and opening; following the “six periods,” and by means of the “bellows” (the inner opening and closing, like a bellows), turn it up to “Kunlun” (the Muddy Pellet at the crown).

§1
Original (Chinese)

在筑基之先，元神逐境外驰，元炁散，元精败，必用三宝合炼，精补其精，气补其气，神补其神，三者合一则基成矣。为出欲界升色界之基者以此，为十月神定之基者以此，而十月不昏睡者有此基也，不寒暑者有此基也，神不外驰得大定者有此基也。炼气而气即定，炼神而神即虚基此。所谓阳神之有基，由于阳精之无漏，名曰漏尽通，金丹成矣，筑基之功大矣哉（图6）！

Before laying the foundation, the Original Spirit chases outer objects and races outward, the original breath scatters, and the original essence decays; the three treasures (essence, breath and spirit) must be refined together: replenish essence with essence, breath with breath, spirit with spirit, and when the three become one, the foundation is laid. To pass beyond the “realm of desire” and rise to the “realm of form” (borrowing the names of the three Buddhist realms, meaning rising from a state with desires to a state of purity) — this has its foundation here; “ten months of settled spirit” (a long period of absorption, like ten months of pregnancy) — this has its foundation here; not sleeping for ten months is because of this foundation; not fearing cold or heat is because of this foundation; the spirit not racing outward and attaining great absorption is also because of this foundation. Refine the breath, and the breath settles; refine the spirit, and the spirit becomes empty — the foundation lies here. What is called “the Yang Spirit has a foundation” comes from the yang essence not leaking; this is called “the power of the exhaustion of leaks” (the supernatural power of essence no longer leaking out), and the Golden Elixir is complete. How great is the work of laying the foundation (see Figure 6)!

Figure 6 · Diagram of the Three Origins

The figure is a three-column table, read from right to left: Lower Origin · realm of desire — a circle on a black ground, inscribed “refining essence into breath,” “taking from Kan to fill Li,” “refining this yang within yin”; Middle Origin · realm of form — a circle with a dot in it, inscribed “refining breath into spirit,” “being and non-being enter into each other,” “Qian and Kun close and open,” “yang flourishes and yin is gradually removed”; Upper Origin · formless realm — a circle with a dot in it, inscribed “yin exhausted, yang pure,” “refining spirit to return to emptiness,” “non-action.” The three columns are the three stages of the elixir path: refining essence into breath (taking from Kan to fill Li, refining the yang within yin) → refining breath into spirit (Qian and Kun opening and closing, yang flourishing and yin receding) → refining spirit to return to emptiness (yin exhausted and yang pure, returning to non-action). In the original, the vertical text in each column is read from right to left, and the figure keeps that arrangement.

§2
Original (Chinese)

身、心、意谓之三家。精、气、神谓之三宝，又谓之三元。以身、心、意为主，以精、气、神为用，三元合一而成丹。摄三归一在于虚静，虚其心则神与性合，静其身则精与情寂，意大定则三元混一。情合性，谓之金木并。精合神，谓之水火交。意大定，谓之五行全。然而精化为炁者，由身之不动也。炁化为神者，由心之不动也。神化为虚者，由意之不动也。心若不动，则东三南二同成五也。身若不动，则北一西四共之也。意若不动，则戊己还从生数五也。身心意俱不动，所以三家相见结婴儿。

Body, heart and intention are called the “three families.” Essence, breath and spirit are called the “three treasures,” and also the “three origins.” With body, heart and intention as the masters and essence, breath and spirit as their functions, when the three origins become one, the elixir is formed. To gather the three back into one, the key lies in emptiness and stillness: when the heart is empty, the spirit unites with inner nature; when the body is still, essence and feeling are quiet and unmoving; when the intention is in great absorption, the three origins merge into one. Feeling united with inner nature is called “metal and wood joined” (metal of the west joined with wood of the east); essence united with spirit is called “water and fire meeting” (water of the north meeting fire of the south); the intention in great absorption is called “the five phases complete.” And the reason essence transforms into breath is that the body does not move; the reason breath transforms into spirit is that the heart does not move; the reason spirit transforms into emptiness is that the intention does not move. If the heart does not move, then “east three and south two” together make one “five” (wood’s number three plus fire’s number two makes five); if the body does not move, then “north one and west four” also make a “five” (water’s number one, metal’s number four); if the intention does not move, then “wu and ji” (the central earth) is itself the generating number “five.” Since body, heart and intention all do not move, “the three families meet” (the three fives come together), forming the infant (the holy embryo).

§3
Original (Chinese)

一炁初判而列二仪，二仪定位而分五行，五行异地而各守一方，故金得土则生，木得土则旺，水得土则止，火得土则息，惟圣人知回机之道，得还元之理，于是攒五簇四，会三合二，而归一也。盖身不动则精固而水朝元，心不动则气固而火朝元，真性寂则魂藏而木朝元，妄情忘则魄伏而金朝元，四大安和则意定而土朝元，此谓五气朝元皆至于顶也，即三宝合一为炼己也（图7）。

As soon as the one breath divides, it sets out the “two modes” (yin and yang); once the two modes are in place, the five phases separate out; the five phases each in their own place, each guarding one direction. Thus metal, obtaining earth, is born; wood, obtaining earth, flourishes; water, obtaining earth, stops; fire, obtaining earth, rests (earth is at the center and can harmonize the four directions). Only the sage understands the principle of “turning the mechanism back” (reversing the mechanism of creation) and obtains the principle of “returning to the origin,” and so “gathers the five and clusters the four, brings the three together and unites the two” (gathering the five phases, clustering the four images, the three families meeting, the two uniting), returning to one (see gathering the five phases). In fact: when the body does not move, essence is firm and water returns to the origin; when the heart does not move, breath is firm and fire returns to the origin; when true nature is quiet, the ethereal soul is hidden away and wood returns to the origin; when deluded feelings are forgotten, the corporeal soul lies down and metal returns to the origin; when the four great elements (earth, water, fire and wind, meaning the body) are at peace, the intention is settled and earth returns to the origin. This is called “the five breaths return to the origin”: the five breaths all gather at the crown, which is “the three treasures united into one,” and is called “refining the self” (see Figure 7).

Figure 7 · Diagram of Wondrous Union

The figure lays out the five phases and the numbers of the River Chart by direction: at the top, fire of the south, two small circles (the number two), annotated “spirit” and “breath”; on the left, wood of the east, three small circles (the number three), annotated “ethereal soul” and “inner nature”; on the right, metal of the west, four small circles (the number four), annotated “corporeal soul” and “feeling”; at the bottom, water of the north, one small circle (the number one), annotated “essence” and “body”; in the center, earth, five small circles in a cross (the number five), annotated “heart” and “intention.” A dotted line runs between south and north through the center; south fire and east wood, north water and west metal are each joined by dotted arcs — this is what §2 means by “east three and south two together make five,” “north one and west four likewise,” and “wu and ji is itself the generating number five”: the three fives combine into one.

§4
Original (Chinese)

太上有言：“贵以贱为本，高以下为基”。后天滋补，贱下之道也。贱也者，师所谓说着丑也。下也者，师所谓下而取也。又《性命圭旨》云：“神仙不肯分明说，说得分明笑煞人”。又丹经云：“性命根，生死窍。说的丑，行的妙，惹得徒儿嘎嘎笑”。夫勒阳关即是调外药，如石匠勒碑刻铭，手使钻子将碑字来回刻深，碑之石面渐渐磨下来，勒阳关与勒碑一理也。精若不调，则元炁不生，何时能采小药补还我的亏欠之精？及至调到药产神知，精炁要冲出之时，身微斜卧，中指点住生死窍，精来多少度，收回多少度。该精正在生死窍中分开，顺出阳关是阳精，能生人；逆回督脉是元炁，能作丹。《金仙证论》曰：“精为万物之美”。人身内有精则生，无精则死，所以精者即是养性命之根源。世人从前不知修炼，因其精已然耗枯，必得炼补精法以补之，又名还精补脑，故汉钟离祖师云：“晚年修持，先论救护命宝”。光明如来佛曰：“老僧会接无根树，能续无油海底灯”。余著此书虽皆白话，只要留心反复推明此卷之意，必能益寿延年，诚为卫生真宝。

The Most High Lord Lao said: “The noble takes the lowly as its root; the high takes the low as its foundation.” Posterior Heaven nourishment and replenishment is precisely this way of the “lowly” and the “low.” The “lowly” is what the teachers mean by “it sounds ugly when said”; the “low” is what the teachers mean by “taking from below” (gathering from the lower, reproductive region). The Jade Tablet of Inner Nature and Life also says: “The immortals will not say it plainly; said plainly, it would make people die laughing.” The elixir classics also say: “The root of inner nature and life is in the Opening of Life and Death. It sounds ugly when said, it is wondrous when done, and it sets the disciples cackling with laughter.” What is called “reining in the yang gate” is “regulating the outer medicine,” like a stonemason carving an inscription: chisel in hand, he cuts the characters deeper back and forth, and the stone face of the tablet is gradually worn down — reining in the yang gate is the same principle as carving the tablet (doing it over and over, little by little). If the essence is not regulated, original breath is not produced; when then could one gather the lesser medicine and repay the essence I owe? When regulation has reached “when the medicine is produced, the spirit knows,” and the essence and breath are about to burst out, lie with the body slightly on its side, press the Opening of Life and Death (the perineum) with the middle finger, and as many degrees as the essence comes, draw back so many degrees. It is precisely at the Opening of Life and Death that the essence parts ways: going out with the current through the yang gate, it is yang essence, which can produce a person; turning back up the Governing Vessel, it is original breath, which can be refined into the elixir. The Treatise on the Realization of Golden Immortality says: “Essence is the most beautiful thing among the ten thousand things.” With essence in the body a person lives; without it, he dies; so essence is the source that nourishes inner nature and life. People of the world, not knowing about cultivation before, have already exhausted their essence, and must replenish it by the method of refining essence to replenish essence, which is also called “returning the essence to replenish the brain.” That is why Patriarch Zhongli of the Han (Zhongli Quan) said: “Cultivating in old age, one must first speak of rescuing and protecting the treasure of life.” The Buddha Light Tathāgata said: “This old monk can graft the rootless tree, and can relight the lamp at the bottom of the sea that has no oil.” (He can bring a rootless tree back to life and relight an oilless lamp at the bottom of the sea — a metaphor for how even the old whose essence is dry can make it good again.) Though I have written this book entirely in the vernacular, whoever pays attention and works over the meaning of this chapter again and again until it is clear will surely lengthen his years; it is truly a real treasure for guarding life.

§5
Original (Chinese)

吾再详论调药功夫。药究竟生否？是当采取之候否？尚不觉知。不惟药有不生之时，且生而有迟速之异，徒采不足之炁，皆无成，不免于死，空劳岁月而已。是故必须调药。曹还阳真人云：“忙里偷闲调外药，无中生有采先天”。吕祖云：“调到药产神知”。所谓时至神知，是知其机之可用不可用耳。若觉其炁未甚足，不可急于采取。必确知其根本之炁真足，方可采取，精始化炁而补脑。然根本之炁本足，因妄欲而亏耗，故有不足。欲补之使足，所以必取此根本足处发生者，始能为补足之用。设非至清、至真、至足之药物，何以补之至足乎？设有妄念贪淫之心，则浊质微露，而后天淫媾之精，是为已老，不得真精用也。若气不足则为嫩，不足以成丹，所以必须调也。缘人本有无淫念之欲而阳举，是阴精在内妄动所致，均是平时火候功夫不到，即巽风武火升降之法不妥，故一调而阳即萎缩矣。此调之秘诀贵在一知字。调法须按其年龄，酌其亏损程度，不能胡采乱炼。有亏损多者，有亏损少者，有先天足与不足者，有先后二天未受亏者，有先后二天大亏者，故不能一律传之耳。

Let me speak in more detail about the work of regulating the medicine. Has the medicine been produced or not? Is it the time to gather it? (The beginner) cannot yet perceive this. Not only are there times when the medicine is not produced, but when it is produced, it comes sometimes slowly, sometimes quickly; to gather in vain a breath that is not sufficient will never succeed, and one cannot escape death — one has merely wasted the years. That is why the medicine must be regulated. The Perfected Cao Huanyang said: “Snatch leisure in the midst of busyness to regulate the outer medicine; produce being from non-being to gather the Prior Heaven.” Patriarch Lü said: “Regulate until, when the medicine is produced, the spirit knows.” What is called “when the time comes, the spirit knows” means knowing whether this moment can be used or not. If you feel its breath is not yet quite sufficient, do not rush to gather it; you must know for certain that the root breath is truly sufficient before you may gather it, and only then does essence transform into breath to replenish the brain. The root breath was originally sufficient; it was depleted by deluded desire, and so is no longer sufficient; to make it sufficient again, you must gather what arises from this place where the root is full, and only then does it have the power to replenish. If it is not the clearest, truest, fullest medicine, how could it replenish to the full? If there is a deluded thought or a lustful heart, its turbid substance faintly appears and becomes the essence of Posterior Heaven intercourse — this is “old,” and cannot be used as true essence. If the breath is not sufficient, it is “tender,” and not enough to form the elixir (see old and tender). That is why it must be regulated. In some people the member rises without any lustful thought; this is caused by the yin essence stirring wildly within, and in every case it is because the work of fire phasing has not been sufficient in ordinary times — that is, the method of raising and lowering with the Wind of Xun and the martial fire has not been right; so once it is regulated, the member shrinks. The secret of regulating the medicine lies in the single word “knowing.” The method of regulation must follow one’s age and weigh the extent of one’s losses; one must not gather and refine at random. Some have lost much, some little; some were born with sufficient Prior Heaven endowment and some not; some have suffered no loss in either Prior or Posterior Heaven, and some have suffered great losses in both — so it cannot be taught the same way to everyone.

§6
Original (Chinese)

问曰：进阳三十六，合乾爻二百一十六。退阴二十四，合坤爻一百四十四。总合三百六十周天，又不是数息三百六十回，皆譬喻之辞。然则应以何法炼之而后可？

答曰：此三百六十周天，乃是一吸一呼，前后一转为进阳退阴。鼻一吸气，心、神、意由尾闾关起，从子、丑、寅到卯一定为沐浴，又上至午，此为循督脉之六阳时上行，名为进阳（图8），此为进阳火。鼻一呼气，心、神、意由泥丸宫下降，从午、未、申到酉一定为沐浴，又下降至子，循任脉使元炁归根，名为退阴（图9），此为退阴符。进阳退阴，乃是呼吸升降之消息。真精炁一动，鼻内一吸为阖，同时眼睛由下往上一看（图10）。复又由上往下一看（图11）。眼睛要紧随精炁心意转一圈（图12），统名谓进阳火退阴符。阖吸升，辟呼降，皆一时之功法也。橐籥者，内里消息也。下手采药时，先天真炁系由橐处所发，而籥为收真炁之地，心肾相交之处。若精炁不动不为橐籥，内里坎离、巽风、进阳、退阴、炉鼎，全在精炁机动之后，而有其名。六候又有定位，若无定位不能作丹，由生死窍起子到卯至午为一二三日进，再由午、酉、子一二三日退，此为六候法。诀曰：采药下手，精炁如动时，用中指点住生死窍，精炁机动，鼻内一吸一呼气，为巽风，引动先天真精炁，后升前降转一回。此时手足头目，上下左右照顾接送。初学此法最难，然练习纯熟，临机之时，不知不觉而手足头目上下左右照顾适当，亦不知从何而主持。法轮转动，自然催逼真精炁摄归气穴，大有不期然而然之消息，有何难哉？切勿采空气，平日亏耗真阳之精，以空气假补身体耗损之精，焉有补足之理？至于左手降龙，右手伏虎，十八罗汉座法台，宝剑又在三江口，管使黄河水逆流，种种名词与事实无关。应了解何为巽风？何为橐籥？何为阖辟？何为六候？何为进阳？何为退阴？何为卯酉沐浴？今将最正确之小周天法诀绘图如下（图13）。

Question: Advancing the yang fire is thirty-six, which across the six lines of the Qian hexagram makes two hundred and sixteen in all; withdrawing the yin tally is twenty-four, which across the six lines of the Kun hexagram makes one hundred and forty-four; three hundred and sixty in all, called one Celestial Circuit. Yet it is not really counting three hundred and sixty breaths — these are all figures of speech. Then by what method should one refine to do it right?

Answer: These “three hundred and sixty circuits” are simply one inhalation and one exhalation, one turn from back to front, called advancing yang and withdrawing yin. As the nose inhales, the heart, spirit and intention set out from the Caudal Funnel pass, through zi, chou and yin to mao, pausing at mao — this is called “ablution”; then on up to wu — this is rising along the Governing Vessel through the six yang hours, called “advancing yang” (Figure 8), which is advancing the yang fire. As the nose exhales, the heart, spirit and intention descend from the Muddy Pellet palace, through wu, wei and shen to you, pausing at you — also called ablution; then down to zi, letting the original breath return to the root along the Conception Vessel, called “withdrawing yin” (Figure 9), which is withdrawing the yin tally. Advancing yang and withdrawing yin are the waxing and waning (the pattern of change) of the breath’s rising and falling. Once the true essence and breath stir, one inhalation in the nose is called “closing,” and at the same moment the eyes look once from below upward (Figure 10); then once from above downward (Figure 11). The eyes must follow the essence, breath, heart and intention closely around the full circle (Figure 12) — the general name for this is “advancing the yang fire, withdrawing the yin tally.” Closing is the inhalation rising, opening is the exhalation descending; both are methods done in a single moment.

The “bellows” are the waxing and waning within the body (the inner opening and closing, like a bellows). When you set to work gathering the medicine, the Prior Heaven true breath issues from the “tuo” (the bag of the bellows), while the “yue” (its pipe) is where the true breath is stored, the place where heart and kidneys meet. If the essence and breath do not stir, there is no question of bellows; Kan and Li, the Wind of Xun, advancing yang, withdrawing yin, and the furnace and tripod within the body all take these names only after the essence and breath have stirred. The “six periods” also have fixed positions; without fixed positions, no elixir can be made: starting from the Opening of Life and Death, from zi to mao to wu is “advancing on days one, two, three”; then through wu, you and zi is “withdrawing on days one, two, three” — this is the method of the six periods.

The formula says: when you set to work gathering the medicine, at the moment the essence and breath stir, press the Opening of Life and Death with the middle finger; as the essence and breath begin to move, one inhalation and one exhalation in the nose — this is the “Wind of Xun” — draws the Prior Heaven true essence and breath around one circle, rising behind and descending in front. At this time the hands, feet, head and eyes, above and below, left and right, must all look after it, receiving and sending it on. This method is hardest for beginners; but once it is practiced until fluent, when the moment comes, without your knowing it, hands, feet, head and eyes, above and below, left and right, all look after it just right, and you do not even know from where it is being directed. Once the Dharma Wheel turns, it naturally presses the true essence and breath to be drawn back into the Cavity of Breath, with very much the sense of “it is so without one’s meaning it to be so” — what is difficult about that? On no account “gather empty air”: what has been depleted day after day is the essence of the true yang; to use empty air as a pretend replenishment for the essence the body has lost — how could that ever make it full? As for “the left hand subdues the dragon, the right hand tames the tiger, the eighteen arhats sit on the Dharma platform, the precious sword is at the mouth of the Three Rivers, and it will surely make the waters of the Yellow River flow backward” and the like, all such terms have nothing to do with the reality. What one should understand is: what is the Wind of Xun? What are the bellows? What are closing and opening? What are the six periods? What is advancing yang? What is withdrawing yin? What are the ablutions of mao and you? Now the most correct formula of the Lesser Celestial Circuit is drawn as a diagram below (Figure 13).

The “Wind of Xun” is the Posterior Heaven breath breathed in the nose. One inhalation in the nose is “advancing,” and the Prior Heaven true yang breath rises once; one exhalation in the nose is “withdrawing,” and the Prior Heaven true yang breath descends once. Rising thus, it goes from the Caudal Funnel up to the Muddy Pellet; descending thus, it goes from the Muddy Pellet down to the Opening of Life and Death — this is the Wind of Xun.

The “bellows” are the waxing and waning within the body (the inner opening and closing); when there is no essence and breath, they are vague and boundless, and you cannot find where they are; once the mechanism of the true breath is set in motion, the waxing and waning of the bellows appear. The tuo is above and the yue below, eight cun four fen apart; this is the place of “inner nature above and life below.” When you breathe in the Posterior Heaven breath, the Prior Heaven breath rises — that is, the outer breath comes in from outside and descends, while the Prior Heaven breath comes out from inside and rises; this is called “closing.” At this moment all the vessels open, and life below unites with inner nature above; this is called the “tuo.” When you breathe out, the Prior Heaven breath descends — that is, the outer breath is breathed out and rises, while the true breath within descends; this is called “opening.” All the vessels open, and inner nature above unites with life below; this is called the “yue.” Compare it with the diagram of the Lesser Celestial Circuit and it becomes clearer still. The inner wind issues from the bellows of zi; outside, the eyes turn along zi, mao, wu and you; inside, inner nature and life close and open, drawing the true essence, which naturally transforms into true breath. One inhalation, one closing; one exhalation, one opening — nothing forced, wholly from nature.

Further, “moving, staying, rising, stopping” together with mao and you are taken as the divisions of the six periods: zi, mao, wu, you, one inhalation and one exhalation, one rising and one descending, are the six periods — from zi to mao to wu is “one, two, three,” and from wu to you and back to zi is also “one, two, three.” The elixir classics say: “Three threes in front, three threes behind; gather them up and carry them on one pole.” One inhalation in the nose goes up through zi, mao and wu: this is rising (Figure 15); one exhalation in the nose goes down through wu, you and zi: this is descending (Figure 16). Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§9
Original note (Chinese)

【注释】本节续前第二节，主要介绍炼精化炁，运行小周天采外药的古典原理及功法。作者虽说自己用白话解释，但仍多喻言，初学者不易懂得。

何谓外药？作者在本书后面名词解释中说：“内药是精炁合一之光、外药是采精化炁，能成舍利子者”。又说，外药是“将要走到外边的精，不令走出，下手采回为丹者”。这个精顺出于外能生育，采回逆运为元炁，能成丹，丹即舍利子。外药须先调后采，调外药就是用勒阳关之法，调到药产神知，即炼出精炁时，自知下手采取，以补足自身前亏欠之精。采药就是行小周天功法，使精逆回补脑。

柳华阳《慧命经》谈到炼好舍利子时说：“即真种所产，以得法轮之炼法数足，外肾不举，故名之舍利子矣。”

运行小周天功法，要在数足之时，数不足叫做嫩，数太过是为老，老嫩之药均不能成丹，一定要恰到好处。是以有时至神知之说，即暖信也。时指活子时。阳炁生，阳物举，为活子时，但采药最好在阳物举急，痒生毫窍之时。古人称这个时机为展窍，即下丹田暖气融融并有气上冲心府，感到周身毛窍清爽通透之时。

赵氏十分重视把阳举作为活子时到的征象，此时阳物急举的程度，甚至用第二步功口诀中的转法轮七周和无孔笛降龙法七次提降，阳物仍然不倒，不但不倒，反而更加跃起，元精突然欲破关而出。这千钧一发的时刻，就是药物不老不嫩之时，学者应不等念起，即不等思维有所活动，立即点住会阴穴，向左侧身而卧，行小周天炼精化炁法诀，把欲夺关而出的元精逆回，化成元炁。

赵氏的小周天炼精化炁法诀是，精来多少度，用六候、巽风、橐籥、阖辟收回多少度。六候即前三田子、卯、午、后三关午、酉、子，而子、卯、午、酉，即升、住、起、止为四正，是停息止火沐浴之处。巽风即鼻中呼吸之气。橐籥是气发精动的消息。阖辟为内外呼吸。起运小周天，须以鼻呼吸，头转眼随，协调一致。先行转眼法，由自身会阴子位开始，向自身左上方旋转，用鼻连吸三吸，眼随头循子、卯、午转，眼看头顶午位，从午位眼又随头转向自身右下方向，鼻连呼气三次，循午、酉、子转。这样一阖一辟，上下循环，真精自然化为真炁。然后，紧接着又从会阴开始，鼻一吸气，气从尾闾上升夹脊，在此一停为沐浴，然后上升到泥丸，此为进火。在泥丸稍停，然后鼻一呼气，又下降至绛宫一停为沐浴，再从绛宫下降回到会阴，此为退阴符。如此一转即为一周天。在这个前后一转中，眼光也要跟着转，即眼光也要循着子、卯、午、酉（会阴、夹脊、泥丸、绛宫）转，最后回到子位（会阴）。运转小周天过程中，还须行吸、舐、撮、闭四字诀。吸是吸气；舐是舌舐上颚；撮是紧撮谷道；闭是闭任开督。这样运转小周天，精来多少，就采炼多少，待到精不动时，即精已化炁，药已归炉，仍点着会阴穴，慢慢坐正，行第二步口诀转法轮生精法三次，加以封固。至于三百六十周天之说，乃譬喻之词，赵氏指出这只是一吸一呼，进阳火退阴符，前后一转的方法而言。

行小周天即炼精化炁之功，主要在虚极静笃和返观内照上用功。静极而动则一阳生。静极而生之阳才为真阳，所生之精才为真精。如何才能达到虚极静笃之境，就须用返观内照，即凝神入炁穴之功。

行小周天之时，本来切忌以意引气，应任其自然运行为妥。赵氏此功看似偏于有为，但他说：“余自得诀，亲自在任、督二脉中行过数百回，今身体强壮，才知此诀是宝也。”此中道理，伍冲虚、柳华阳在《伍柳仙宗》一书言之甚详。他们都主张以神驭炁，即行则神炁同行，住则神炁同住。柳华阳曰：“大凡临机之时，必须畅明其神，勇猛其志，外除耳目，内绝思虑，一点真神，领炁循环，切忌昏迷散乱，稍有他见，炁则散于别路，空转无益”。他们又都主张运行升降之际，意要主宰中宫。柳华阳又把转法轮譬喻为乡人织布，行似难，熟则容易。他说：“乡人织布，临机之时，手足头目，上下左右，照顾接送，初学其法最难。然而，熟者临机之时，不知不觉手足头目、上下左右、照顾接送，亦不知从何而主持，乃自然而然之消息，若有所执，则不能成乎物矣”！

This section continues from Step Two above, and mainly presents the classical principles and methods of refining essence into breath and moving the Lesser Celestial Circuit to gather the outer medicine. Although the author says he explains things in the vernacular, much of it is still metaphorical language, not easy for beginners to understand.

What is the “outer medicine”? In the “Explanation of Terms” later in this book, the author says: “The inner medicine is the light of essence and breath united; the outer medicine is what is gathered from essence and transformed into breath, and can become the śarīra.” He also says that the outer medicine is “the essence that is about to go outside; instead of letting it go out, you set to work, gather it back and refine it into the elixir.” This essence, going out of the body with the current, can produce offspring; gathered back and moved in reverse, it becomes original breath and can form the elixir, and the elixir is the śarīra. The outer medicine must first be regulated and then gathered. Regulating the outer medicine means using the method of reining in the yang gate until “when the medicine is produced, the spirit knows,” that is, when the essence and breath have been refined, one knows oneself to set to work gathering it, in order to make good the essence one owed before. Gathering the medicine means practicing the Lesser Celestial Circuit, so that the essence turns back to replenish the brain.

Liu Huayang’s Book of Wisdom and Life, speaking of the refined śarīra, says: “That is, after the true seed is produced, it is refined with the method of the Dharma Wheel until the count is complete, and the outer kidney no longer rises; therefore it is called the śarīra.”

The Lesser Celestial Circuit should be moved when “the count is complete”: an insufficient count is called “tender,” an excessive count is “old”; neither old nor tender medicine can form the elixir, and it must be just right. That is why there is the saying “when the time comes, the spirit knows,” which is the “warm signal” (the signal of warmth in the elixir field). “The time” refers to the Living Zi Hour. When the yang breath is born and the member rises, that is the Living Zi Hour; but the medicine is best gathered when the member rises urgently and the pores itch. The ancients called this moment “unfolding the opening”: the lower elixir field is warm and melting, breath surges up toward the heart, and one feels all the pores of the body clear and open.

Zhao attaches great importance to the rising of the yang as the sign that the Living Zi Hour has come. At this point the member may rise so urgently that even after turning the Dharma Wheel seven times as in the formula of Step Two, and drawing up and sinking down seven times with the method of subduing the dragon with the holeless flute, it still does not go down; not only does it not go down, it leaps up even more, and the original essence suddenly threatens to break through the pass. This moment, when all hangs by a thread, is precisely when the medicine is neither old nor tender. The practitioner should, without waiting for a thought to arise — that is, before thinking has moved at all — immediately press the Huiyin point, lie on the left side, and practice the formula of the Lesser Celestial Circuit for refining essence into breath, turning back the original essence that is about to force the pass and transforming it into original breath.

Zhao’s formula of the Lesser Celestial Circuit for refining essence into breath is: as many degrees as the essence comes, draw back so many degrees with the six periods, the Wind of Xun, the bellows, and closing and opening. The six periods are the three fields in front, zi, mao, wu, and the three passes behind, wu, you, zi; zi, mao, wu and you are rising, staying, rising and stopping, the “four cardinal points,” the places for pausing the breath, stopping the fire and ablution. The Wind of Xun is the breath breathed in the nose. The bellows are the waxing and waning of the breath issuing and the essence stirring. Closing and opening are the inner and outer breathing. To set the Lesser Celestial Circuit going, you must breathe through the nose, the head turning and the eyes following, all in coordination. First do the method of turning the eyes: starting from the zi position at your own perineum, turn toward your upper left, inhaling three times in a row through the nose, the eyes following the head along zi, mao, wu, looking at the wu position at the crown; then from the wu position, the eyes following the head turn toward your lower right, exhaling three times in a row through the nose, along wu, you, zi. Closing and opening thus, circulating up and down, the true essence naturally transforms into true breath. Then move the circuit immediately after: again starting from the perineum, with one inhalation through the nose the breath rises from the Caudal Funnel to the Spine-Straddling Pass, pausing there for the ablution; then it rises to the Muddy Pellet — this is advancing the fire. Pause briefly at the Muddy Pellet, then with one exhalation through the nose descend to the Crimson Palace, pausing for the ablution; then descend from the Crimson Palace back to the perineum — this is withdrawing the yin tally. One circle thus is one Celestial Circuit. Within this one turn from back to front, the light of the eyes must turn with it too, that is, the light of the eyes also turns along zi, mao, wu, you (the perineum, the Spine-Straddling Pass, the Muddy Pellet, the Crimson Palace), finally returning to the zi position (the perineum). While moving the Lesser Celestial Circuit, also practice the four-word formula “inhale, lick, contract, close”: inhale is drawing in the breath; lick is the tongue licking the palate; contract is tightly contracting the grain path (the anus); close is closing the Conception and opening the Governing. Moving the Lesser Celestial Circuit thus, as much essence as comes, so much is gathered and refined; when the essence no longer stirs, the essence has transformed into breath and the medicine has returned to the furnace; still pressing the Huiyin point, slowly sit upright, and do the method of turning the Dharma Wheel to produce essence from the formula of Step Two three times, to seal it. As for “three hundred and sixty circuits,” it is a figure of speech; Zhao points out that it refers only to the method of one inhalation and one exhalation, advancing the yang fire and withdrawing the yin tally, one turn from back to front.

In moving the Lesser Celestial Circuit, that is, in the work of refining essence into breath, the main effort lies in reaching utmost emptiness and abiding in deep stillness, and in turning the gaze back and illuminating within. When stillness reaches its utmost it moves, and the one yang is born. Only yang born from utmost stillness is true yang; only the essence so produced is true essence. How does one reach the state of utmost emptiness and deep stillness? By turning the gaze back and illuminating within, that is, by the work of concentrating the spirit and entering the Cavity of Breath.

In moving the Lesser Celestial Circuit, one should properly avoid leading the breath with the intention, and it is right to let it move naturally. Zhao’s method looks rather like deliberate action, but he says: “Since I received the formula, I have traveled the Conception and Governing vessels myself several hundred times; now my body is strong, and I know this formula is a treasure.” The principle behind this is explained in great detail by Wu Chongxu and Liu Huayang in The Immortal Lineage of Wu and Liu. Both advocate “riding the breath with the spirit,” that is: when moving, the spirit moves together with the breath; when staying, the spirit stays together with the breath. Liu Huayang says: “Whenever the moment comes, you must make the spirit bright and the will bold, outwardly put away the ears and eyes (their distractions), inwardly cut off thoughts and worries, and with one point of true spirit lead the breath around the circuit; above all avoid dullness and scattering — at the slightest other thought, the breath scatters onto another road, and you turn empty to no avail.” Both also hold that while moving it up and down, the intention should preside in the central palace. Liu Huayang also compares turning the Dharma Wheel to a country person weaving cloth: it seems very hard to do, but once familiar it is easy. He says: “When a country person weaves, at the right moment, hands, feet, head and eyes, above and below, left and right, look after it, receiving and sending on; this method is hardest for beginners. Yet for the skilled, when the moment comes, without their knowing it hands, feet, head and eyes, above and below, left and right, all look after it, receiving and sending on, and they do not even know from where it is being directed — this is the message of what happens of itself; if there is any clinging, it cannot be done!”

Key Terms

  • Outer medicine: the essence about to leak outward, not allowed to go out but gathered back and moved in reverse to become original breath; contrasted with the “inner medicine” (the light of essence and breath united).
  • Regulating the medicine, reining in the yang gate: before gathering, first “regulate,” reining in the yang gate over and over like a stonemason carving a tablet, until “when the medicine is produced, the spirit knows.”
  • When the medicine is produced, the spirit knows: when the medicine is produced, the heart and spirit know of themselves; Qiu Ling’s note says this is the “warm signal,” that is, the Living Zi Hour.
  • Old and tender: medicine that is insufficient is tender; excessive (or with lustful thought, having become Posterior Heaven essence) is old; neither can form the elixir.
  • Three families, three treasures, three origins: body, heart and intention are the three families; essence, breath and spirit are the three treasures, also called the three origins.
  • The five breaths return to the origin: body, heart, inner nature, feeling and intention all unmoving, the five breaths of water, fire, wood, metal and earth gather at the crown.
  • Metal and wood joined, water and fire meeting: feeling united with inner nature is metal and wood joined; essence united with spirit is water and fire meeting.
  • Power of the exhaustion of leaks: the “supernatural power” attained when the yang essence no longer leaks, borrowing the name from the Buddhist six supernatural powers.
  • Wind of Xun: the Posterior Heaven breath breathed in the nose.
  • Bellows (tuoyue): a metaphor for the opening and closing of the Prior Heaven breath in the body with the breathing; the tuo above is inner nature, the yue below is life, eight cun four fen apart.
  • Closing and opening: inhalation is closing (the Prior Heaven breath rises), exhalation is opening (the Prior Heaven breath descends); Qiu Ling’s note: closing and opening are the inner and outer breathing.
  • Six periods: zi, mao, wu are the front three (rising), wu, you, zi the back three (descending); moving, staying, rising, stopping, with the ablutions of mao and you, are the divisions of the six periods.
  • Ablutions of mao and you: in the circuit, pausing briefly at mao (on the way up) and you (on the way down), neither advancing nor withdrawing.
  • The Qian origin uses nine, the Kun origin uses six: advancing the yang fire takes nine breaths per hour, counted by fours to make thirty-six; withdrawing the yin tally takes six breaths per hour, counted by fours to make twenty-four; multiplied by six hours, two hundred and sixteen and one hundred and forty-four, three hundred and sixty in all.
  • Turning the River Chariot empty: closing the eyes and counting circuits without any true yang stirring.
  • Returning the essence to replenish the brain: gathering back the essence about to leak and moving it upward in reverse to replenish the brain.
  • The horse-like retraction: the outer kidney retracts like a horse’s and no longer rises, showing that the essence has been wholly transformed.
  • Śarīra: Zhao Bichen’s Buddhist name for the refined elixir.
  • Inhale, lick, contract, close: draw in the breath, the tongue licks the palate, tightly contract the grain path, close the Conception and open the Governing.
  • Unfolding the opening: the moment when the member rises urgently, the pores itch, the elixir field is warm and melting, and breath surges up toward the heart.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Stillness first, then gathering: as Qiu Ling makes very clear, the root of this step lies in utmost emptiness and deep stillness, turning the gaze back and illuminating within. Without true yang stirring naturally in stillness, every technique that follows is “turning empty.”
  2. Three hundred and sixty is only a figure of speech: one Celestial Circuit is simply one inhalation and one exhalation, one turn from back to front. Don’t sit on the bed counting hundreds of circles — Zhao Bichen says plainly that this is “turning the River Chariot empty, of no benefit at all.”
  3. Use it only at a real stirring: the whole set of pressing the perineum, lying on the side and turning the eyes is an “emergency” technique for the moment the true yang stirs and the essence is about to leak; it need not be done in ordinary practice, still less should that moment be artificially provoked.
  4. Ride the breath with the spirit, don’t lead it with the intention: in rising and descending, the spirit moves and stays with the breath, the intention resting in the central palace; do not push or strain.
  5. Turn the eyes lightly: the eyes follow the head and look gently; this is not forcefully rolling the eyeballs; those with eye problems should leave this out.
  6. Practice makes perfect: Liu Huayang’s weaving metaphor — fumbling at first is normal; with familiarity it becomes coordinated by itself, and there is no need to force getting it right the first time.

Translator's Notes

  1. In the original, this step has one formula, thirteen figures (Figures 5 to 17) and eight passages of explanation (including two questions and answers), with Wu Chongxu’s Song of the Formula for Gathering the Medicine in the Lesser Celestial Circuit inserted in the middle; we follow the original’s divisions and number them §1–§9, with the song set apart. Figures 8 to 12 and Figures 14 to 17 are small figures side by side in the original; this site combines each group into a single image; the direction of the arrows in Figure 8 follows the original.
  2. “Refine the spirit and the spirit becomes empty, the foundation lies here” (炼神而神即虚基此): so in the original; the gist is “refine the spirit and the spirit becomes empty; the foundation lies here.”
  3. “This old monk can graft the rootless tree, and can relight the lamp at the bottom of the sea that has no oil”: the original attributes it to “the Buddha Light Tathāgata”; its source is unknown, and it is reproduced as is.
  4. In the song, “when the breath says it is about to be exhausted, gather, seal and wait until sufficient” (气曰将尽兮采封候足): 曰 (“says”) is probably a mistake for 日 (“day by day”); and “how is it called fire” (如何云火) is probably a mistake for “how is the fire moved” (如何运火). Both are reproduced as is, and the translation follows the sense.
  5. “There are not really three hundred breaths” (非寔有三百息): 寔 is the same as 实 (“really”); reproduced as is.
  6. In §8 the quotation mark at the start of the question is not closed in the original; reproduced as is.
  7. Figure 5 in the original is labeled “yinqiao vessel” (阴跻脉); 跻 should be 跷, see the notes to Step Three. “Prostate,” “bladder,” “artery” and “vein” in the figure are Western medical terms borrowed by Zhao Bichen.
  8. “East three and south two,” “north one and west four,” “wu and ji … the generating number five”: from the numbers of the River Chart — water one, fire two, wood three, metal four, earth five; Zhang Boduan’s Awakening to Reality speaks of “three, five, one — just three words,” and Figure 7 expresses this idea.
  9. “The yang light appears three times”: a Wu-Liu school expression; light appears three times between the eyebrows, the sign that the Lesser Celestial Circuit should stop and one should prepare to gather the Great Medicine.
  10. “Like the moon of the fifteenth, full of light” (十五光盈): the full moon of the fifteenth as a metaphor for the medicine being sufficient; the “seven-day formula” refers to the “seven days of gathering work” when gathering the Great Medicine in the Greater Celestial Circuit.
  11. Qiu Ling’s note says that when gathering the medicine one should “lie on the left side”; Zhao Bichen’s original text says only “lie with the body slightly on its side.”

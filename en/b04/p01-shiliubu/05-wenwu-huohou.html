The Sixteen Steps of Method Formulas · 第五步 文武火候

Step Five · Civil and Martial Fire Phasing

The formula of Step Five, civil and martial fire phasing — one stick of true incense is natural in itself; in the furnace of the Yellow Court the incense smoke rises

About This Chapter

The elixir path has always said “the medicine is transmitted, the fire is not” — fire phasing is the hardest and most secret part. In this step Zhao Bichen speaks openly about fire phasing, in two parts:

  1. Outer martial fire and outer civil fire (gazing at the elixir ball): a supporting practice for driving out illness. The outer martial fire is called “moving fire”: after sitting quietly, open the eyes and gaze at an “elixir ball” in front of you (originally a lit incense tip) until tears flow; Zhao holds that this “draws out” the roots of illness not yet manifest in the five organs along with the tears. The outer civil fire is called “resting fire”: right after gazing, close the eyes and enter stillness, the tongue against the palate, and when the mouth is full of saliva, swallow it. The proportion of the two is thirty percent martial fire, seventy percent civil fire.
  2. The eighteen fires: following The Scripture of Wind and Fire in Liu Huayang’s Treatise on the Realization of Golden Immortality, the fires fall into three groups —
    • Fires of breathing (four): raising fire, drawing fire, fire pressing fire onward, stopping fire; they transform the essence of food and the five grains and help the original essence;
    • Fires of the spirit (seven): condensing fire, entering fire, descending fire, moving fire, resting fire, Li fire, heart fire; they are “the spiritual power within the eyes,” transform the original essence and help the original breath;
    • Fires of the Prior Heaven breath (seven): circulating fire, taking fire, lifting fire, Kan fire, Kun fire, fire within water, fire within the furnace; they transform the outer breathing and help the Original Spirit.

What must be distinguished with special care are Li fire (the member rises without thought, but the breath is not yet sufficient; it is “tender” and must not be gathered) and heart fire (the member rises because the eyes see form and a thought stirs; it is a deviant fire and must never be gathered).

Qiu Ling’s note first gives general knowledge about fire phasing (civil fire and martial fire, the two periods and the six periods), and praises Zhao for setting out the eighteen fire methods concretely, which is rare; he also gives a clear warning about the method of gazing at the elixir ball: like holding the attention on the Ancestral Opening, it is not suitable for people with high blood pressure or excess yang; prolonged gazing easily brings dizziness and blurred vision and harms the original breath, and the rule of “thirty percent martial, seventy percent civil” must be strictly kept.

Safety note (translator): Gazing at an incense tip or elixir ball is a strongly stimulating “martial fire”; do it for only two or three minutes at a time, and afterwards you must close your eyes, enter stillness and warm and nourish with civil fire for a longer time. People with high blood pressure, excess yang (flushed face, swelling in the head, irritability), glaucoma, fundus disease or epilepsy should not do it. When gazing, do not sit too close to an open flame; take care against fire and against smoke getting into the eyes. If you feel dizzy, light-headed, have eye pain or palpitations, stop at once. The original’s claim that tears can “draw out illnesses of the five organs not yet manifest” is Zhao’s own view and is no substitute for seeing a doctor.

Formula
Original (Chinese)

一柱真香本自然，

黄庭炉内起香烟。

空中结就浮云篆，

上柱本身寿万年。

One stick of true incense is natural in itself; in the furnace of the Yellow Court (the middle and lower elixir fields) the incense smoke rises. In the air the smoke forms cloud-seal patterns like writing; offering this one stick of incense in itself grants ten thousand years of life. (This verse comes from the Scripture of Daily Devotions, see §2; “true incense” hints at the true breath in the body, and “incense smoke” is the rising of the breath.)

§1
Original (Chinese)

昔我师语我云：外武火者是“移火”，外文火者是“以火”，乃专为提出五脏内之渣滓，化泪流出之法，又是炼四相和合、五行攒簇归根、回光返照、日月合并之法也。既然学道，身中有未现之病，速宜设法除去之，然后再学道，犹不为迟。圣人不治已病，能治未来病。若对世人表面观察，似乎无有何种病症发现，而内中确有未发出之病根，惜其不自觉耳。一旦病魔缠身，乞灵于树皮草根，抑已晚矣。故病未发之前，用外文武火提出，病无根矣。

Long ago my teacher told me: the outer martial fire is “moving fire,” the outer civil fire is “resting fire.” This is a method specifically for drawing out the dregs in the five organs and turning them into tears that flow away; it is also the method for refining “the four forms united, the five phases gathered and returned to the root (see gathering the five phases), turning the light around to shine back, sun and moon joined (the two eyes joined into one).” Since one is learning the Way, if there is illness in the body that has not yet shown itself, one should quickly find a way to remove it, and then learn the Way — it is not too late. The sage does not treat illness that has already arisen, but treats illness yet to come. Looking at people of the world from the outside, they seem to have no symptoms, but within the body there really are roots of illness not yet manifest; it is a pity they have not noticed them. Once illness has wrapped itself around the body, it is too late to turn to bark and grass roots (medicines). So before the illness breaks out, draw it out with the outer civil fire and outer martial fire, and the illness has no root.

§2
Original (Chinese)

人初结胎时，在母腹中，天一生水生黑睛而有瞳仁通肾，地二生火而有两眥通心，天三生木而有黑珠通肝，地四生金而有白珠通肺，天五生土而有上下胞胎通脾，故五脏精华皆聚於目，而人之真灵神在脑，亦发之於目也。人之一身皆属阴，惟有两眼属阳，仰仗这点真阳，方能不被群阴所剥。必须由此入手修炼，故立此诀，将平日积累之病，使之由此提出。外武火者，是将神火注於炉中，为火中火引也，古名“移火”。移者移动身内病管开涨，即是移动内里真火，二火相磨相激，阳火必胜乎阴精，精融灌溉周身，而渣滓亦出净，病从何来？《功课经》曰：“一柱真香本自然，黄庭炉内起香烟，空中结就浮云篆，上柱本身寿万年”。按新法用催眠凝视球，又名丹球，每日早晚各炼一次。先行打坐，俟心气沉静，再用丹球一枚，摆在两眼正中前面，以观丹球，高矮远近，务要适宜，久之由泪管流出泪液，其味臭而咸酸，名曰渣滓，即人因悲恸哭出之泪是也。

When a person first forms as an embryo in the mother’s womb: “Heaven’s one generates water,” producing the dark of the eye, with the pupil, connected to the kidneys; “Earth’s two generates fire,” producing the two corners of the eye, connected to the heart; “Heaven’s three generates wood,” producing the iris, connected to the liver; “Earth’s four generates metal,” producing the white of the eye, connected to the lungs; “Heaven’s five generates earth,” producing the upper and lower eyelids, connected to the spleen (this matches the numbers of the River Chart to the “five wheels” of the eye). Thus the essence of the five organs all gathers in the eyes, and the true numinous spirit of a person, which is in the brain, also issues from the eyes. The whole human body belongs to yin; only the two eyes belong to yang, and it is by this one point of true yang that one is not eaten away by the mass of yin. One must begin cultivation from here; that is why this formula was set down, so that the illnesses accumulated day by day may be drawn out from here.

The “outer martial fire” pours the fire of the spirit into the furnace; it is the trigger of the “fire within fire,” and in ancient times it was called “moving fire.” “Moving” means moving the channels of illness within the body so that they open up, that is, moving the true fire within the body; the two fires rub against and stir each other, the yang fire is sure to overcome the yin essence, the essence melts and irrigates the whole body, and the dregs are cleared out — where then could illness come from? The Scripture of Daily Devotions says: “One stick of true incense is natural in itself; in the furnace of the Yellow Court the incense smoke rises; in the air it forms floating cloud-seal script; offering this stick, the body itself lives ten thousand years.” In the new method one uses a “hypnotic gazing ball,” also called an “elixir ball,” and refines once each morning and evening: first sit, and when the heart and breath have settled, take an elixir ball and place it in front of you, midway between the two eyes, and look at it; its height and distance must be just right. After a while tears flow from the tear ducts, smelly, salty and sour, called “dregs” — the same kind of tears people shed when weeping in grief.

§3
Original (Chinese)

该火甫经炼毕，立即炼外文火之功，以防眼睛受伤。外文火者，合并已开涨内管将通身气息回归原位之法也，又名“以火”。以者以然不动之意也。斯时二目紧闭，意在正中，气降真炁穴，坐到无人无我之时，身空意空始为正功。舌顶天池穴，久之甘露必降，味甜如蜜，吞下入我十二重楼，再降至炁穴，化为阴精，入睾丸宫，名曰精虫，出输精管，此即阳精也。津液者，乃是助身之宝，须要格外爱惜。津液常足，则肾水足，而目光灼灼，而且黑白分明。若内里有病，黑眼球不清，白眼球呈露红黄线，必炼至如童年眼睛，黑中有光，白似青白之色，始可言内脏无病。内部有病，除非由鼻涕泪管提出，其他方法，皆不能出。每次如炼外武火需时三成，则外文火至少需七成，方保无虞。昔年我师了然、了空曰：这个“移火”、“以火”，最能强壮精神，消除五脏百病，如老人已逾周甲之年，久用此功，则精神矍铄。少年得此法诀，则精神倍增，灵性顿开。行外武火，原用香火，今改用丹球。行外文火时，须闭目正观，心气下降，存神定意，耳不闻声，一心内守，调息绵绵，似有似无，直至一念不生，万虑皆空，自然心火下降，肾水上升，而舌顶上腭，舌下二穴生出津液，在舌后存之，候其要喷出时再吞，咕噜一声，下降丹田，不仅味甜如蜜，而且腹响如雷，恍惚之间，心空意定，先由两腿两肘空起，杳杳冥冥，不知身在何处。迨物极必反，静极动生，忽然元炁自动，外阳勃举，此为定久真火自动。后之学者，其可不尽心而悟之乎！

As soon as this fire (the outer martial fire) is finished, you must at once practice the work of the outer civil fire, to keep the eyes from harm. The “outer civil fire” is the method of bringing the inner channels that have opened back together and letting the breath of the whole body return to its place; it is also called “resting fire” — “resting” (yi) meaning to be at peace and unmoving. Now close the two eyes tightly, the intention in the center, the breath descending to the true Cavity of Breath; when you sit until there is neither other nor self, the body empty and the intention empty, that is the true practice. With the tongue pressed against the Celestial Pool point (the palate), after a while sweet dew is sure to descend, sweet as honey; swallow it, it enters my Twelve-Storied Tower (the throat), descends further to the Cavity of Breath, transforms into yin essence, enters the testicles and is called “seminal essence”; coming out through the seminal duct, it is yang essence. Saliva is a treasure that helps the body and must be cherished all the more. When saliva is always ample, the kidney water is ample, the light of the eyes is bright and spirited, and the black and white are clearly distinct. If there is illness in the body, the dark of the eye is not clear, and red and yellow threads of blood show on the white; you must refine until they are like a child’s eyes, light within the dark and the white bluish-white, before you can say the inner organs are free of illness. Illness in the body cannot be expelled by any method except by drawing it out through the nasal mucus and the tear ducts. Each time, if the outer martial fire takes thirty percent of the time, the outer civil fire must take at least seventy percent, to guarantee there is no danger.

Long ago my teachers Liaoran and Liaokong said: this “moving fire” and “resting fire” are the best for strengthening the spirit and removing the hundred illnesses of the five organs. If an old person past sixty uses this practice for a long time, he will be hale and vigorous; if a young person obtains this formula, his spirit will double and his intelligence will open at once. For the outer martial fire, incense was originally used; now the elixir ball is used instead. In practicing the outer civil fire, close the eyes and look straight (gaze straight inward), let the heart and breath descend, keep the spirit and settle the intention, the ears hearing no sound, the whole heart guarding within, the breath regulated, soft and continuous, as if present and as if absent, until not a single thought arises and all worries are empty; naturally the heart fire descends and the kidney water rises; now, with the tongue against the palate, saliva is produced from the two points beneath the tongue and kept behind the tongue; when it is about to spurt out, swallow it with a “gulp,” and it descends to the elixir field — not only is it sweet as honey, but the belly rumbles like thunder. In the dim and indistinct, the heart empty and the intention settled, first the two legs and two elbows begin to empty, and in the deep and obscure you no longer know where the body is. When things reach their extreme and reverse, and stillness at its utmost gives rise to movement, suddenly the original breath stirs by itself and the outer yang rises vigorously — this is the true fire stirring by itself after long absorption. How could later students not put their whole heart into realizing this!

§4
Original (Chinese)

问曰：丹经道书皆云，仙佛传药不传火，请将火候明传，以接后学。

答曰：火候是一总名。火是火，候是六候也。火有十八般名称，各有次第节序，故其功用亦皆不同，且药物与火候均有次序先后，不可乱用。比如砖窑烧砖，先须将土和泥脱胚，然后入窑经火锻炼，始能成砖；未经火炼，一迂雨天，仍化为泥。故采药必经此番火候，方能结丹；否则有坏。《金仙证论·风火经》云：凡云起火、引火，火逼行火、止火，皆为呼吸气之火也。凡云凝火、入火、降火、移火、以火、离火、心火，皆属神之火也。凡云运火、取火、提火、坎火、坤火、水中火、炉中火，皆先天炁之火也。凡吸呼之火，能化饮食五谷之精，而助元精。凡神火，能化元精，而助元炁。凡元炁之火，能化吸呼，而助元神。元神之火，能化神以还虚，而合道成，始终不离火之力。故此十八种火候，非得真师，不能彻底明瞭。

Question: The elixir classics and Daoist books all say “the immortals and buddhas transmit the medicine but not the fire.” Please transmit the fire phasing plainly, to guide later students.

Answer: “Fire phasing” is a general name. “Fire” is the fire, and “phasing” is the six periods (see Step Four). The fire has eighteen names, each with its own order and sequence, and so their functions also differ; moreover, the medicine and the fire phasing each have their order, and must not be used at random. It is like firing bricks in a kiln: first the earth is mixed into clay and shaped into blanks, then put into the kiln and forged by fire, and only then do they become bricks; without the fire, at the first rainy day they turn back to mud. So the gathered medicine must pass through this fire phasing before it can form the elixir; otherwise it will spoil. The Scripture of Wind and Fire in the Treatise on the Realization of Golden Immortality says: whatever is called raising fire, drawing fire, fire pressing fire onward and stopping fire is the fire of the breath of breathing; whatever is called condensing fire, entering fire, descending fire, moving fire, resting fire, Li fire and heart fire belongs to the fire of the spirit; whatever is called circulating fire, taking fire, lifting fire, Kan fire, Kun fire, fire within water and fire within the furnace is the fire of the Prior Heaven breath. The fire of breathing can transform the essence of food and the five grains and help the original essence; the fire of the spirit can transform the original essence and help the original breath; the fire of the original breath can transform the breathing and help the Original Spirit; the fire of the Original Spirit can transform the spirit to return to emptiness, uniting with the Way to completion — from beginning to end, nothing is apart from the power of the fire. Therefore, without a true teacher, these eighteen kinds of fire phasing cannot be thoroughly understood.

§5
Original (Chinese)

今日详其所以，幸亏当年我师，传我甚为详细。夫起火者，使鼻内吸呼气一起一伏，如采小药是一升一降，为巽风。缘鼻内一吸气，曰进，心意由生死窍一升。鼻内一呼气，曰降，心意由顶降到生死窍，即是转法轮。吸呼之气火本自有形，而用之无形。若着意用之则长邪火。似有似无，乃是二气之妙用，药自回也。一起一伏者，又名小阖辟，无孔笛颠倒两头吹。因无念，而真阳自举，鼻内心意气由生死窍一吸，气升到真炁穴，又一呼，气由绛宫降到真炁穴，如此数次，外阳自回，顿觉身内异常舒畅，故精如顺出能生人，逆回能作丹，就在中间颠倒颠。

Today I explain the principles in detail; fortunately my teacher transmitted them to me in great detail in those days.

Raising fire: let the breathing in the nose rise and fall, just like the one rising and one descending in gathering the lesser medicine; this is the Wind of Xun. For one inhalation in the nose is called “advancing,” and the heart and intention rise from the Opening of Life and Death; one exhalation in the nose is called “descending,” and the heart and intention descend from the crown to the Opening of Life and Death — this is turning the Dharma Wheel. The “fire” of the breath of breathing has form in itself, but in use it must be formless; if you use it deliberately, it breeds deviant fire. As if present and as if absent — that is the wondrous function of the two breaths (the breath of breathing and the Prior Heaven breath), and the medicine returns by itself. “Rising and falling” is also called “lesser closing and opening,” that is, “blowing the holeless flute at both ends in reverse” (see Step Two). For when the true yang rises by itself without thought, the breath of the nose and of the heart and intention is drawn up with one inhalation from the Opening of Life and Death to the true Cavity of Breath; then with one exhalation the breath descends from the Crimson Palace to the true Cavity of Breath. After a few times like this, the outer yang retracts by itself, and at once you feel unusually at ease within. So if the essence goes out with the current, it can produce a person; turned back against the current, it can make the elixir — the key lies in this “reversal” in between.

§6
Original (Chinese)

何谓引火？鼻内吸呼气，一升一降，心意一上一下，微微转动法轮，以数记之，由一记十，由十记百，摄心在数，务令意念不散不乱，心息相依而同行，此是锁心猿，拴意马之法。丹经云：“真意往来无间断，知而不守是真工”，“着意头头错，无为又落空”。从有心化为无心，使心内空空洞洞，虚虚灵灵，无生灭心。欲驱除妄念，先持正觉，自然无念可生，如皓月当空，洁白无滓。积习纯熟，永无梦幻之境，则心死神活。盖神不定，则性光不献。意不定，则情不死。虚极静笃，然后命根自动。因其自动，可以采小药，此之谓引火。又问曰：吸呼一升一降，神气一上一下，前谓之封固呼吸，今又言引火，实在不明。答曰：一升一降转法轮，乃是将念住於法轮之内，实止念耳。鼻内呼吸一升一降，神气亦随吸呼一升一降，淫根一缩，神气转一回，是谓之封固呼吸。采完药后，始用封固。两种吸呼之法，是勾引内里起火之意。念动转法轮，否则意念不升耳。

Drawing fire: the breathing in the nose rises and falls, the heart and intention go up and down, gently turning the Dharma Wheel, keeping count: from one to ten, from ten to a hundred, gathering the heart onto the count, making sure the intention is neither scattered nor confused, heart and breath depending on each other and moving together — this is the method of “locking up the monkey of the heart and tethering the horse of the intention” (locking up the heart that leaps about like a monkey, tethering the intention that runs off like a horse). The elixir classics say: “The True Intention goes back and forth without interruption; knowing without holding on is the true work.” They also say: “Fix the intention and every step is wrong; do nothing at all and you fall into emptiness.” You must transform from having a heart into having no heart, making the heart empty and hollow, empty and numinous, without a heart that arises and ceases. To drive away deluded thoughts, first hold to right awareness, and naturally no thought will arise, like a bright moon in the sky, white and flawless. When the habit is thoroughly formed and there are no more dream states, that is “the heart dies and the spirit lives” (the deluded heart dies and the Original Spirit comes alive). For if the spirit is not settled, the light of inner nature does not appear; if the intention is not settled, feeling does not die. With utmost emptiness and deep stillness, the root of life (the true yang) then stirs by itself; because it stirs by itself, the lesser medicine can be gathered — this is called drawing fire.

Another question: breathing rises and falls, spirit and breath go up and down; before, this was called “the breathing of sealing,” and now it is called “drawing fire” — I really don’t understand. Answer: turning the Dharma Wheel with one rising and one descending is stopping the thoughts within the Dharma Wheel; in fact it is stopping thought. The breathing in the nose rises and falls, the spirit and breath rise and fall with the breathing, the root of desire retracts once, the spirit and breath turn one circle — this is called “the breathing of sealing”; sealing is used only after the medicine has been gathered. Both breathing methods are meant to coax the fire within the body to rise: as soon as a thought stirs, turn the Dharma Wheel; otherwise the intention cannot rise.

§7
Original (Chinese)

何为止火？止火者，不行吸呼之气也。又问：因何不行吸呼之气为止火？答曰：止火，是精管内肾足满，当不行吸呼之气也。正阳祖师曰：“丹熟不须行火候。更行火候必伤丹”。冲虚真人曰：“有止火之景到，才能采大药，服食过关。”止火景者，是金光二现之时，采药不行吸呼，正在西南路上月华明，若等金光三现，再行止火，实乃失去之时，大丹有伤。止火在采大药过关之前，金光二现之后，精炁神要足未足之时。采药补到十足，采时若用吸呼之气，夜内准失大丹，因吸呼之气有振动精炁之患，故采药不行吸呼之气，为止火。又问曰：采药不行吸呼之气，精炁如何有升降？答曰：虽是不行鼻内吸呼之气，内里神意气要行升降，微微一升一降，神气随真意转动法轮，才是止火采小药之工。

Stopping fire: stopping fire means no longer moving the breath of breathing. Another question: why is not moving the breath of breathing called stopping fire? Answer: stopping fire is when the seminal ducts and the inner kidneys are already full, and one should no longer move the breath of breathing. Patriarch Zhengyang (Zhongli Quan) said: “When the elixir is ripe, there is no need for further fire phasing; further fire phasing is sure to harm the elixir.” The Perfected Chongxu (Wu Chongxu) said: “Only when there is the scene of stopping fire can one gather the Great Medicine, ingest it and pass the gates.” The scene of stopping fire is the moment of “the golden light appearing a second time” (golden light appearing before the eyes for the second time): in gathering the medicine, the breathing is no longer moved, just when “on the southwest road the moonlight is bright” (see §22). If you wait until the golden light appears a third time to stop the fire, you have in fact already missed the moment, and the great elixir will be harmed. Stopping fire comes before gathering the Great Medicine and passing the gates, after the golden light has appeared a second time, when essence, breath and spirit are about to be full but are not yet full. That is why not moving the breath of breathing when gathering the medicine is called stopping fire. Another question: if the breath of breathing is not moved when gathering the medicine, how can the essence and breath rise and fall? Answer: although the breath of breathing in the nose is not moved, the spirit, intention and breath within the body still rise and fall, gently, one rising and one descending, the spirit and breath following the True Intention in turning the Dharma Wheel — that is the work of stopping fire and gathering the medicine.

何为炉中火？以形言之，神炁动在丹田曰炉。神炁上升于顶曰鼎，无神炁即无炉鼎之可言，故老祖冲虚子曰：“鼎鼎原无鼎。”又吕祖曰：“真炉鼎，真橐籥，知之真者而后用之真”。炼真炉火者，须得师之真传采外药。今再细论，以便后学，可知下手途境。夫采外药者，外阳举时，认清是否身中活子时到，速勒阳关调外药，及至调到药产神知，精炁撞出之时，身体斜卧，中指点住生死窍，赶快用巽风、六候、橐籥、阖辟收回。采毕恐其有漏，故用封固呼吸，摄住精炁，然后再用周天沐浴火候，元精皆化为元炁，此即谓炼精化炁之渐法也。精炁补足，乃与童身无别。《翠虚篇》云：“西南路上月华明，大道还从此处生”。此是丹田内药足，阳气上达丽于目，行住坐卧，眼前不离白光，久之由白光变为金光，此为金光一现，若至二现，当可止火，稍不谨慎，危险立至，则前功尽弃，此其难也。

Fire within the furnace: speaking in images, when spirit and breath stir in the elixir field, it is called the “furnace”; when spirit and breath rise to the crown, it is called the “tripod.” Without spirit and breath, there is no question of furnace and tripod. That is why the old patriarch Chongxuzi said: “The tripod of tripods was never a tripod.” And Patriarch Lü also said: “The true furnace and tripod, the true bellows — know them truly, and then you can use them truly.” Those who refine the true furnace fire must obtain the method of gathering the outer medicine truly transmitted by a teacher. Let me now explain it in detail once more, so that later students may know the way to set to work: gathering the outer medicine is, when the outer yang rises, first to recognize clearly whether the Living Zi Hour in the body has come; quickly rein in the yang gate and regulate the outer medicine; when regulation has reached “when the medicine is produced, the spirit knows” and the essence and breath are about to break out, lie with the body on its side, press the Opening of Life and Death with the middle finger, and quickly draw it back with the Wind of Xun, the six periods, the bellows, closing and opening. When the gathering is done, fearing it may still leak, use the breathing of sealing to hold the essence and breath; then use the fire phasing of the circuit and the ablutions so that the original essence is wholly transformed into original breath — this is the “gradual method” of refining essence into breath. When the essence and breath are replenished to the full, there is no difference from the virgin body. The Writings of Cuixu says: “On the southwest road the moonlight is bright; the great Way is born again from here.” This is when the medicine within the elixir field is sufficient and the yang breath reaches upward and attaches to the eyes; walking, standing, sitting or lying, white light is always before the eyes; in time the white light turns to golden light — this is “the golden light appearing once”; if it comes to a second appearance, you should stop the fire (see §7). At the slightest lack of care, danger arrives at once and all previous work is lost — this is precisely its difficulty.

§23
Original (Chinese)

此七火者，是先天元炁之火，能化外呼吸而助元神。元神者，不神之神也。余得了然、了空禅师，又得彭茂昌儒师、敲蹻道师、理门金山派谭至明理师所传。历拜三十余位老师，惟上列五位有性命双修法诀，今我全泄漏于世。

These seven fires are the fires of the Prior Heaven original breath; they can transform the outer breathing and help the Original Spirit. The Original Spirit is the spirit that is not spirit-like. I received transmission from the two Chan masters Liaoran and Liaokong, and also from the Confucian teacher Peng Maochang, the Daoist teacher Qiaoqiao, and Tan Zhiming, teacher of the Jinshan branch of the Li Sect. I have bowed to more than thirty teachers in all, and only these five had the formula of the dual cultivation of nature and life; now I make all of it public to the world.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§23
Original note (Chinese)

【注释】金丹之法，火候最难最秘，古有“圣人传药不传火，从来火候少人知”之说。一般有文火武火之分。文火主温养，曰烹；武火主采药，曰炼。又有真火、凡火之分。只有真火能炼丹。而火工之运用，其法诀严密而细致，更是不传之秘。

丹法火候素有二候、六候之分。二候者，阳生为一候，药产为一候。因其即可采药，故又曰二候采牟尼。六候者，即采外药、采内药，为二候；进阳火、退阴符，为二候；卯酉沐浴为二候，共称六候。但火候之说亦随道门各派而异，故尚有七候、八候、十二候之分等等。

伍冲虚《天仙正理》一书中有《火候经》，柳华阳《金仙证论》中有《风火经》，集前人各家火候之说，详加注解，诚为历代丹经道书中罕见之作。但虽有理论，却无火工法诀，学者付诸实践尤难。赵避尘得乃师真传，在火工之运用上，开列十八种火工法诀，具体而微，虽略见繁琐，但有助于学者入门，诚足珍贵也。古人认为，火候诀法甚多，景亦各异，难以用文字语言指点，此说不为无理。学者欲知火候火工，贵在虚极静笃中自领自会，自生自得。

本章除详论十八种火候法诀外，又介绍了凝视丹球法。此法专为提出五脏未现之病，化泪流出而设，乃是继第三口诀开通八脉法之后，又介绍的一种祛病补助功法。此法须在初步静坐之后进行，夜间可手持燃着的线香，日间亦可用红头筷子，距离目前三寸许，高低与两目平齐，二目睁开凝视香头或筷子头约二、三分钟。运用外武火凝视久之，确能使眼眶由润湿而出泪，但与意守祖窍一样，恐非高血压及阳亢患者所宜习练。而久久凝视丹球，功后易抬致头昏目眩，又易使元炁受损。为此，学者须严格执行修习本功武火三成、文火七成之规定，以文火来温养恢复所受的损失。学者于凝视香头或丹球结束，擦净眼泪后，应即入静，坐到无人无我、身心两忘之境，久之口中津液产生，亦应倍加珍惜吞下。入静时间越长越好。千峰门人牛金宝，对本功又加上熨眼、转睛、眼呼吸等法，可在收功时行之。熨眼法是将两手搓热，趁热捂往眼睛，热散后两手猛然向两侧分开，两眼同时使劲一睁，如此行三至五次。转睛法为二眼睁开，头颈不动，独转眼珠，眼看下方，由极下方向左上方转到极上方，再由极上方向右转到极下方为一周，如此转九周一停，左转三十六周，右转二十四周。此法亦可于每天早晨行之。眼呼吸法为两眼与鼻根部同时吸气，徐徐将气吸足，然后两眼与鼻根同时呼气，连做九次呼吸。此法亦可在室外空气清新之处进行。

如此实行文火入静和其他补助方法，才能发挥凝视丹球法的功效，方保无虞。

In the method of the Golden Elixir, fire phasing is the hardest and most secret part; in ancient times there was the saying, “The sages transmit the medicine but not the fire; since time began, few have known the fire phasing.” Generally it is divided into civil fire and martial fire: civil fire is for warming and nourishing, called “cooking”; martial fire is for gathering the medicine, called “refining.” There is also the distinction between true fire and ordinary fire; only true fire can refine the elixir. And the use of the fire work, with its strict and minute formulas, is all the more a secret never transmitted.

The fire phasing of the elixir method has always been divided into “two periods” and “six periods.” Two periods: yang being born is one period, the medicine being produced is another; because at this point the medicine can be gathered, it is also called “gathering the mani in two periods” (mani meaning the precious pearl, the elixir medicine). Six periods: gathering the outer medicine and gathering the inner medicine are two periods; advancing the yang fire and withdrawing the yin tally are two periods; the ablutions of mao and you are two periods; together they are called the six periods. But the accounts of fire phasing vary with the different Daoist schools, so there are also divisions into seven, eight, twelve periods and so on.

Wu Chongxu’s Correct Principles of Celestial Immortality contains The Scripture of Fire Phasing, and Liu Huayang’s Treatise on the Realization of Golden Immortality contains The Scripture of Wind and Fire; they gather the sayings of earlier masters on fire phasing and annotate them in detail, and are truly rare works among the elixir classics and Daoist books of all ages. Yet though they have the theory, they lack concrete formulas for the fire work, which makes it all the harder for students to put into practice. Zhao Bichen received his teacher’s true transmission, and on the use of the fire work he sets out eighteen formulas for the fire methods, concrete and detailed; though somewhat cumbersome, they help students to enter the gate, and are truly precious. The ancients held that the formulas of fire phasing are many and the scenes vary, so that they are hard to point out in written words, and this is not without reason. For students who wish to understand fire phasing and the fire work, what matters is to grasp it for themselves within utmost emptiness and deep stillness, so that it arises naturally and is obtained by oneself.

Besides explaining the eighteen formulas of fire phasing in detail, this chapter also introduces “the method of gazing at the elixir ball.” This method is designed specifically to draw out illnesses in the five organs that have not yet manifested and turn them into tears that flow away; after the method of opening the eight vessels in Step Three, it is another supporting practice for driving out illness. This method must be done after an initial period of quiet sitting: at night one may hold a lit stick of incense, and by day one may use a red-tipped chopstick, about three cun in front of the eyes, at the level of the two eyes, and with the eyes open gaze at the incense tip or chopstick tip for about two or three minutes. Prolonged gazing with the outer martial fire does indeed make the eye sockets go from moist to tearful; but like holding the attention on the Ancestral Opening, it is probably not suitable for people with high blood pressure or excess yang. Moreover, prolonged gazing at the elixir ball easily brings on dizziness and blurred vision after practice, and easily harms the original breath. For this reason, students must strictly follow this method’s rule of “thirty percent martial fire, seventy percent civil fire,” using the civil fire to warm and nourish and restore what has been lost. After finishing the gazing at the incense tip or elixir ball and wiping away the tears, the student should immediately enter stillness and sit until reaching the state where there is neither other nor self and body and mind are both forgotten; when after a while saliva is produced in the mouth, it too should be cherished and swallowed. The longer the time in stillness, the better.

Niu Jinbao, a disciple of the Qianfeng school, adds to this method the methods of warming the eyes, turning the eyes and eye-breathing, which can be done when closing the practice. Warming the eyes: rub the two hands until hot, and while they are hot cover the eyes; when the heat has gone, suddenly draw the two hands apart to the sides, and at the same moment open the two eyes wide with force; do this three to five times. Turning the eyes: with the eyes open and the head and neck still, turn only the eyeballs — looking down, turn from the very bottom to the upper left up to the very top, then from the very top to the right down to the very bottom; that is one circle; turn nine circles like this and pause; turn thirty-six circles to the left and twenty-four to the right. This method can also be done every morning. Eye-breathing: breathe in with the two eyes and the root of the nose together, slowly filling with breath; then breathe out with the two eyes and the root of the nose together; do nine breaths in a row. This method can also be done outdoors where the air is fresh.

Only by practicing civil-fire stillness and the other supporting methods in this way can the method of gazing at the elixir ball show its effect without danger.

Key Terms

  • Fire phasing: “fire” refers to the force applied in practice (breathing, spirit and intention, the Prior Heaven breath); “phasing” refers to timing and measure (the six periods).
  • Civil fire, martial fire: civil fire is for warming and nourishing, called “cooking”; martial fire is for gathering the medicine, called “refining.”
  • Outer martial fire (moving fire): after quiet sitting, gazing at the elixir ball or an incense tip until tears flow, moving the true fire in the body and drawing out the roots of illness.
  • Outer civil fire (resting fire): after gazing, closing the eyes and entering stillness, bringing the opened inner channels back together and letting the breath return to its place; “resting” means at peace and unmoving.
  • Elixir ball: what Zhao Bichen calls a “hypnotic gazing ball,” replacing the incense tip of the old method as the object of the gaze.
  • The five wheels matched to the five organs: the pupil connects to the kidneys, the corners of the eye to the heart, the iris to the liver, the white of the eye to the lungs, the eyelids to the spleen.
  • Fires of breathing: raising fire, drawing fire, fire pressing fire onward, stopping fire; they transform the essence of the five grains and help the original essence.
  • Fires of the spirit: condensing fire, entering fire, descending fire, moving fire, resting fire, Li fire, heart fire; the spiritual power within the eyes, they transform the original essence and help the original breath.
  • Fires of the Prior Heaven breath: circulating fire, taking fire, lifting fire, Kan fire, Kun fire, fire within water, fire within the furnace; they transform the outer breathing and help the Original Spirit.
  • Breathing of sealing: after gathering the medicine, one rising and one descending, the spirit and breath turning one circle, so that the root of desire retracts and the essence and breath are sealed.
  • Stopping fire: when essence and breath are about to be full, no longer moving the breath of the nose, only rising and descending gently with spirit and intention; its scene is “the golden light appearing a second time.”
  • Li fire: the spirit of the heart stirs and the member rises without thought, but the breath is not sufficient; it is tender and must not be gathered.
  • Heart fire (sovereign fire): the member rises because the eyes see form and a thought stirs; it is a deviant fire and must never be gathered — gathering it makes an “illusory elixir.”
  • Closing the yang gate: the master formula for keeping the essence from leaking; “once the yang gate is closed, every one lives long.”
  • The mutual completion of water and fire: the heart fire shines down on the kidney water, fire enters water, the water does not flow down and the fire does not flare up.
  • The golden light appearing once, twice: when the medicine in the elixir field is sufficient, the white light before the eyes turns to golden light; at the second appearance, stop the fire.
  • Two periods, six periods: yang being born and the medicine being produced are the two periods; gathering the outer and inner medicine, advancing the yang fire and withdrawing the yin tally, and the ablutions of mao and you are the six periods.

Putting It into Practice

  1. The root of fire phasing: Qiu Ling’s summary is that “what matters is to grasp it for oneself within utmost emptiness and deep stillness.” The eighteen fires are Zhao’s classification, for understanding; there is no need to “practice” them one by one.
  2. Distinguish three kinds of rising: only when, in stillness and without thought, the true yang swells (the true Living Zi Hour) may it be gathered; without thought but with insufficient breath (Li fire) it is tender — do not gather; rising because of seeing form or a stirring thought (heart fire) is deviant fire — never gather it, just let it pass.
  3. Breathing as if present and as if absent: Zhao says that the fire of breathing “used deliberately breeds deviant fire” — breathe gently and naturally, without deliberate force.
  4. Be cautious with gazing at the elixir ball: do it only briefly after quiet sitting (two or three minutes), followed by at least twice as long or more of stillness with the eyes closed; people with high blood pressure, excess yang or eye conditions should not do it. If using incense, take care against fire.
  5. Niu Jinbao’s three methods for the eyes: warming the eyes (cover them with hands rubbed warm), turning the eyes, eye-breathing — all should be gentle, and can serve as health care when closing the practice.
  6. Swallow the saliva: saliva produced in the mouth during stillness should be swallowed unhurriedly and sent by the intention to the elixir field.

Translator's Notes

  1. In the original, this step has one formula and one figure, and Zhao Bichen’s explanation falls into three large parts: the outer martial and civil fires, questions and answers, and the eighteen fires; we number the original’s natural paragraphs §1–§23, with §9–§13 being the “seven fires of the spirit” and §15–§22 the “seven fires of the Prior Heaven breath.”
  2. “The two corners of the eye connect to the heart” (两眥通心): 眥 is the same as 眦, the corner of the eye. Matching the five wheels of the eye to the five organs is a teaching of Chinese ophthalmology (the pupil belongs to the kidneys, the two corners to the heart, the iris to the liver, the white to the lungs, the eyelids to the spleen); Zhao adds the numbers of the River Chart, “Heaven’s one generates water … Heaven’s five generates earth.”
  3. “At the first rainy day” (一迂雨天): 迂 should be a mistake for 遇 (“to meet”); reproduced as is.
  4. “Upper and lower baotai connected to the spleen” (上下胞胎通脾): baotai here refers to the upper and lower eyelids.
  5. “Turn the two eyes from wu to the left, through mao, to zi, to you” (两眼由午向左转至卯到子至酉): so in the original; the direction differs from the descent “wu, you, zi” in Step Four; reproduced as is.
  6. “The water of Caoxi” (漕溪水): usually written 曹溪水; reproduced as is.
  7. “The Daoist teacher Qiaoqiao” (敲蹻道师): a personal name, reproduced as is. The “Li Sect” (理门) is a popular religious organization (the Zaili Sect) widespread in North China from the Qing dynasty on.
  8. In Qiu Ling’s note, “easily lifts on dizziness after practice” (功后易抬致头昏目眩): 抬 should be a mistake for 招 (“to bring on”); “while hot cover toward the eyes” (趁热捂往眼睛): 往 should be a mistake for 住. The original text is reproduced as is, and the translation follows the correct characters.
  9. Niu Jinbao’s methods of warming the eyes, turning the eyes and eye-breathing described in Qiu Ling’s note are Qiu Ling’s own retelling, reproduced as is.
  10. Zhao’s statements that tears can “draw out illnesses of the five organs not yet manifest,” that swallowed saliva can turn into seminal essence, and so on, are his own views; the translation renders his meaning and does not represent the conclusions of modern medicine.
  11. Patriarch Zhengyang is Zhongli Quan; Hunranzi is the Yuan dynasty Daoist Wang Daoyuan; the Writings of Cuixu is a work by Chen Nan of the Southern Song.

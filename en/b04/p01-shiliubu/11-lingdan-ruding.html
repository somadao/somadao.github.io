The Sixteen Steps of Method Formulas · 第十一步 灵丹入鼎

Step Eleven · The Numinous Elixir Enters the Tripod

The formula of Step Eleven, the numinous elixir enters the tripod — the embryo is refined to roundness by the fire ball; the empty room produces whiteness, shining on ten thousand things

About This Chapter

This step enters a higher level of refining and nourishing the śarīra (the inner elixir). Zhao Bichen says: the first six steps refine the “light of inner nature” (spirit), the last six steps refine the “light of life” (the true breath within essence); when spirit and breath unite and nourish the śarīra, the light before the eyes is refined from the “light of wisdom” into the “toad light,” and only then is the śarīra fully nourished.

  1. The numinous elixir drops down: when essence, breath and spirit have been refined to sufficiency and gathered at the crown, and have been pressed long enough by the true breath and true spirit, the “numinous elixir” drops down; swallowed into the mouth, it turns into saliva, the belly rumbles like thunder, and it nourishes the śarīra; then the light of the śarīra is born from the Cavity of Breath — “the empty room produces whiteness.”
  2. The scenes: a feeling of bending at the crown, the dragon’s chant and the tiger’s roar in the ears, the body as if in the clouds, the whole face as if covered by a cobweb or crawled over by ants, saliva more than one can swallow, the breath stopping and the pulse ceasing, “when the breath is full one does not think of food,” “when the spirit is full one does not think of sleep,” and a moonlike brightness constantly at the Hall of Impression (between the eyebrows).
  3. The ten harms and the six don’ts: long walking harms the sinews, long standing harms the bones, long sitting harms the blood … nothing should be overdone; in practice, don’t let thoughts arise, don’t let the intention scatter, don’t look outward, don’t listen outward, and let the breathing be neither hurried nor stopped.
  4. The test: place an oil lamp in front of you, turn the two eyes nine circles facing the flame, then close the eyes; if you see a full, round great moonlight, the śarīra is sufficient; if there is only an empty ring of light, dark within, it is not yet sufficient. This method (taking fire, lifting fire) consumes true breath and must not be used often.
  5. The light of wisdom and the toad light: when one’s virginity has been lost and not yet made good, the light is like moonlight and is called the light of wisdom; when one has been restored to a virgin body and the horse-like retraction has occurred, the light is golden yellow and is called the toad light (borrowing the three-legged golden toad as a metaphor for the three treasures of essence, breath and spirit united into one).

Qiu Ling’s note stresses that, strictly speaking, “the six scenes appearing before you” is the sign that the śarīra has been refined to sufficiency; that refining the śarīra “takes utmost stillness as the main thing,” guarding it with care like a dragon nourishing its pearl or a young woman in her first pregnancy; and he relates Niu Jinbao’s test using a flashlight instead of an oil lamp.

Safety note (translator): The original’s test is to stare straight at a lamp flame and turn the head nine circles; the improved method related in Qiu Ling’s note is to stare straight at a flashlight bulb. Staring directly at bright light damages the retina, and turning the head round easily causes dizziness; Zhao himself says this method brings “dizziness and blurred vision and consumes the breath of the true light in your body” and must “on no account be used often” — this site recommends not doing it. The original’s “when the breath is full one does not think of food” and “one may even be able to abstain from grain” are ancient accounts; please do not diet or fast. Someone must watch over you in absorption; the “ten harms” principle that nothing should be overdone applies to everyone.

Original Text and Translation

Figure 26 · The Numinous Elixir Enters the Tripod

On the left is a three-legged incense burner (a tripod), from which a long wisp of smoke rises; in the smoke there is a small round pearl, and the top of the smoke curls like a flame with another pearl inside it; on the right a long-bearded Daoist sits upright cross-legged, his two hands joined in front of the belly. The meaning: the elixir is refined in the tripod and rises, and is then drawn into the “tripod” within the body.

Formula
Original (Chinese)

胎因火球炼得圆，

虚室生白照万千。

圆圆陀陀金光现，

百脉通合大药成。

流珠烁烁照昆仑，

九转丹成只自然。

一粒自后吞入腹，

始知世有活神仙。

The Embryo of the Way is refined to completeness by the “fire ball” (the light of the elixir); “the empty room produces whiteness” (the empty, bright heart gives rise to light), shining on ten thousand things. A perfectly round golden light appears; the hundred vessels are all open and joined, and the Great Medicine is complete.

The flowing pearl (the lively light of the elixir) glitters and flashes, shining on Kunlun (the crown); the elixir of nine reversions is complete, simply of itself. A grain of elixir comes up from behind (the Governing Vessel) and is swallowed into the belly; only then does one know that there really are living immortals in the world.

§1
Original (Chinese)

前段将精炁神炼足，聚于顶内，总得以真炁真神炼之，催逼既久，灵丹脱落，吞入口中，化为津液，腹响如雷，滋养舍利子，后由炁穴生出舍利子之光，能虚室生白，圆圆陀陀，此即是舍利子成也，百脉吸呼气长停。这段功夫全以至静为主，如龙养珠，如幼女初怀孕，要自知静心养之，舍利子方足也。

When essence, breath and spirit have been refined to sufficiency as above and gathered within the crown, they must always be refined with the true breath and the true spirit; when they have been pressed long enough, the numinous elixir drops down, is swallowed into the mouth, turns into saliva, and the belly rumbles like thunder, nourishing the śarīra; then the light of the śarīra is born from the Cavity of Breath, which can make “the empty room produce whiteness,” perfectly round — this is the śarīra formed, and the breath of breathing in the hundred vessels stops for a long time. This stage of practice takes “utmost stillness” as the main thing: like a dragon nourishing its pearl, like a young woman in her first pregnancy, one must know for oneself how to still the heart and nurture and protect it, and only then can the śarīra become full.

§2
Original (Chinese)

欲修大道者，理无别诀，无非神炁而已。神即是性，炁即是命。神从炁化，炁从精生，欲望成其道者，先当保守炼其精，精满然后炁生，以炁养精，精足成为舍利子。丹经道书千名万喻，不能出性命二字。任尔千变万化说法，不外炼精化炁，炁化养神，神足还虚而已。前六步功炼的性命双修真性光，即是神也。后六步功炼的性命双修真命光，即是精中真炁也。神炁合一，滋养舍利子，虚室生白，金机飞电，耳现龙吟虎啸之声。若丹光不圆不明，用取火提火提出神火之法，才得蟾光发现，丹光自圆明也。然取火提火之法不可久用，久用则头晕眼花。若舍利子足而蟾光现；舍利子不足还得加工细炼。每日参禅打坐，两眼归并合一，神炁下照丹田，是助养舍利子足满之法。斯时其顶有颠弯之状，耳发龙吟虎啸之声，其身如在云端，通身酥麻发痒，如凭虚御风，快乐无边，满面如蛛网罩住，又如蚁行，痒痒欲搔，散之印堂，次到鼻柱眼眶，两颧两腮牙关口中，津液升满，咽纳不尽，口闭懒开，身沉懒动，入于混沌，化为无有，并不知身在何处，自然息住脉停，真炁充满，故曰炁满不思食。谷不绝而阴气难消，阴气不消则阳气不纯。若犹思食则是舍利子还不足，真炁还是少，不得谓之炁满。直至寂照功勤，自然神满不思睡，炁足不思食。功夫至此，常寂常照，息无出入，不来不往，只觉一团蟾光，在不有不无之中，此乃是要入定未入定之时，如在母腹相似，虽有鸣锣响鼓，并不知耳。用功到此，印堂自有月光长明，只要死心守中抱一，此光自然常明于两眉中间，似电光闪灼，此时舍利子自长矣。从此谨防走失元炁，方能培养舍利子足也。

For those who wish to cultivate the great Way, there is in principle no other secret than spirit and breath. Spirit is inner nature; breath is life. Spirit is transformed from breath, and breath is born from essence; to attain the Way, one must first preserve and refine one’s essence; when the essence is full, breath is born; nourish the essence with the breath, and when the essence is sufficient, it becomes the śarīra. The elixir classics and Daoist books have a thousand names and ten thousand metaphors, but none goes beyond the two words “inner nature and life.” However you vary the telling in a thousand ways, it comes down to refining essence into breath, transforming breath to nourish the spirit, and the spirit, being full, returning to emptiness. What the first six steps refine is the “true light of inner nature” of the dual cultivation of nature and life, that is, spirit; what the last six steps refine is the “true light of life” of the dual cultivation of nature and life, that is, the true breath within essence. When spirit and breath unite and nourish the śarīra, “the empty room produces whiteness, the golden mechanism flies like lightning” (light arises in the empty brightness, like golden lightning flashing past), and the sound of the dragon’s chant and the tiger’s roar appears in the ears. If the light of the elixir is neither round nor bright, use the method of “taking fire and lifting fire” (see Step Five) to draw out the fire of the spirit; only then can the “toad light” appear, and the light of the elixir naturally becomes round and bright. But the method of taking fire and lifting fire must not be used for long; used for long, it brings dizziness and blurred vision. When the śarīra is sufficient, the toad light appears; when the śarīra is not sufficient, one must still work at refining it with care.

Every day when practicing Chan and sitting, bring the two eyes together into one, and let spirit and breath shine down on the elixir field; this is a method that helps nourish the śarīra to the full. At this time there is a feeling of bending at the crown, the sound of the dragon’s chant and the tiger’s roar comes forth in the ears, the body seems to be in the clouds, the whole body tingles and itches, as if riding the wind in the air, with boundless joy; the whole face feels as if covered by a cobweb, and again as if ants were crawling over it, itching so that one wants to scratch; it spreads to the Hall of Impression, then to the bridge of the nose, the eye sockets, the cheekbones, the cheeks, the jaw and the mouth; the saliva rises and fills the mouth, more than one can swallow; the mouth stays closed, too lazy to open, the body heavy, too lazy to move; one enters the primordial chaos, transformed into nothing, not knowing where the body is; naturally the breath stops and the pulse ceases, and the true breath is full — hence it is said “when the breath is full one does not think of food.” Without cutting off grain food, the yin breath is hard to dispel; while the yin breath is not dispelled, the yang breath is not pure. If one still wants to eat, it shows that the śarīra is not yet sufficient and the true breath still scant; it cannot be called the breath being full. Only when the work of quiet illumination is diligent does it naturally come about that “when the spirit is full one does not think of sleep, when the breath is sufficient one does not think of food.”

When practice reaches this point, constantly quiet and constantly illuminating, the breath neither going out nor coming in, neither coming nor going, one feels only a mass of toad light, between being and non-being — this is the time when one is about to enter absorption but has not yet entered it, as if in the mother’s womb, so that even if someone beat gongs and drums, one would not hear. When practice reaches this point, a moonlike light naturally shines long and bright at the Hall of Impression; just set your heart wholly on guarding the center and embracing the One, and this light naturally shines constantly between the two eyebrows, flashing like lightning — at this time the śarīra naturally grows. From now on one must guard carefully against losing the original breath, and only then can the śarīra be nourished to the full.

§3
Original (Chinese)

培养舍利子时，要谨戒十损：久行损筋，久立损骨，久坐损血，久睡损脉，久听损精，久看损神，久言损气，久思损脾，久淫损命，食足损心。总而言之，凡事不可过劳，劳多受伤。用功时，一不可起念，念起则火炎；二不可意散，意散则火冷，三不可目外视，外视则神驰而伤魂；四不可耳外听，外听则精散而伤魄；五不可吸呼骤，骤则散漫无归；六不可吸呼停，停则断续无力。忽断忽续，或燥或寒，种种弊端皆为害於舍利子，若不小心谨防危险，万无一成。然起念时，当起阳火转法轮，二目不可外视，要闭目而内睁，看正中有白光，方是正功。若是有惊动，眼开外视，意散神散，舍利子便受其害。耳不可外听，得有道侣护持。如有人来与响动，全得道侣经心，命来人远离，不可惊动。耳猛一听动，心内一惊身一抖，舍利子不但受害，且会散去。滋养舍利子，心不可急，心想舍利子速成，则舍利子不生长。要自然而然用功，勿忘勿助，安神于炁穴内，知而不守，使自然之吹嘘，绵绵不绝，念兹在兹，先存后忘，而入于混沌杳冥者也。至此真阳缩回，淫根涨动，若是舍利子足，当止火采大药；若是不足，就此炁动，可转法轮，养我舍利子。如何知道舍利子足不足？法曰：置一油灯于面前，二目直看灯火苗，两眼由左向右转，如此转九回，一闭目看正中有个大月光，圆满如电光一般，不增不灭，此是舍利子光足之兆。若是眼对灯转九回，有个虚光圈，圈边光亮，圈内黑暗，此是舍利子不足，还得加工细炼。以上即是取火提火，提出神火，才得蟾光发现。千万不可常用此法，若是常用，头晕眼花，耗你内里真光之炁。修者修的是此炁，炼者炼的是此光。光者即神也，神者即在二目也，千修万炼，不出神炁而已。

In cultivating the śarīra, one must carefully guard against the “ten harms”: long walking harms the sinews, long standing harms the bones, long sitting harms the blood, long sleeping harms the vessels, long listening harms the essence, long looking harms the spirit, long talking harms the breath, long thinking harms the spleen, long indulgence in sex harms life, and eating to fullness harms the heart. In short, nothing should be overdone; overwork brings harm.

In practice: first, do not let thoughts arise — when thoughts arise, the fire flares upward; second, do not let the intention scatter — when the intention scatters, the fire grows cold; third, do not let the eyes look outward — looking outward, the spirit races off and harms the ethereal soul; fourth, do not let the ears listen outward — listening outward, the essence scatters and harms the corporeal soul; fifth, the breathing must not be hurried — when hurried, it is diffuse and has nowhere to return; sixth, the breathing must not stop — when it stops, it is broken and without strength. Now broken, now continuing, now dry, now cold — all such faults harm the śarīra; if one is not careful to guard against danger, not one in ten thousand will succeed. But when a thought has arisen, one should raise the yang fire and turn the Dharma Wheel; the two eyes must not look outward but, with the lids closed, open inward and look at the white light in the center — that is the right practice. If one is startled and opens the eyes to look out, the intention and spirit scatter and the śarīra is harmed. The ears must not listen outward, and there must be a Daoist companion to watch over one: if someone comes or there is a noise, everything depends on the companion’s care to keep the visitor far away and not startle one. If the ears suddenly hear a disturbance, the heart is startled and the body jerks; the śarīra is not only harmed but scattered.

In nourishing the śarīra, the heart must not be impatient; if the heart thinks of the śarīra being refined quickly, the śarīra will on the contrary not grow. Practice naturally, neither forgetting nor helping it along (neither forgetting it nor pulling the sprouts to help them grow), set the spirit within the Cavity of Breath, knowing it without holding on, let the natural blowing and puffing (the stirring of the breath) go on softly and continuously, keep it in mind, first keeping it and then forgetting it, and enter the realm of primordial chaos and dark obscurity. At this point the true yang draws back and the root of desire throbs and swells: if the śarīra is sufficient, one should stop the fire and gather the Great Medicine; if it is not yet sufficient, take advantage of this stirring of breath to turn the Dharma Wheel and nourish my śarīra.

How does one know whether the śarīra is sufficient? The method: place an oil lamp in front of you, look straight at the flame with both eyes, and turn the two eyes from left to right, nine circles like this; then close the eyes, and if you see a great moonlight in the center, full and round like lightning, neither increasing nor decreasing — this is the sign that the light of the śarīra is sufficient. If after turning the eyes nine circles facing the lamp there is only an empty ring of light, bright at the rim and dark within, the śarīra is not sufficient, and one must still work at refining it with care. The above is “taking fire and lifting fire,” drawing out the fire of the spirit so that the toad light can appear. On no account use this method often; if used often, it brings dizziness and blurred vision and consumes the breath of the true light in your body. What those who cultivate the Way cultivate is this breath; what those who refine the elixir refine is this light. The light is the spirit, and the spirit is in the two eyes; a thousand cultivations and ten thousand refinings do not go beyond spirit and breath.

§4
Original (Chinese)

盼蟾子刘名瑞道师曰：灰心圣火养真元。灰心之功，乃心寒如死灰，毫无挂碍。若有挂碍，则舍利子光不足；若念动，则舍利子寒火上炎。大抵至静为要。将舍利子养足，有千变万化之景到，须防危虑阴，保守元阳。用真火无候之天机，其舍利子之光自出。师云：有火无候勿抽添，忘机忘时有妙玄，若忘原是无忘妙，不忘之中是忘禅，忘到纯阳盈月现，不可以忘睡昏禅。此言用功时不可睡觉，若有昏睡，走失元阳，前功罔费，学者戒之。

彭茂昌师曰：炼舍利子无他诀，心目合并下照坤脐，若有动机，速速运炼，一不留神，丹必出炉走失而前功废矣。阳物初缩回，纵有外光发现，非是马阴藏相，此是舍利子不足也。以后勤加锻炼，龟头缩入腹里，外光发现，云中掣电，有虚室生白之状，初发现于眉前，再加功细炼，炼得丹田之内丹光上涌，外达于目而生辉，两眼光耀闪灼，一连二三次而后已，丹光涌出，明如金钱，亦如火珠，从两眼发出，是舍利子足耳。

刘云普老师曰：要知养足舍利子之法，神不入定，则舍利子不生而丹不结；息不藏丹田炁穴，则舍利子光不现。心息俱要蛰藏丹田之内，纵息有时出，而心则无时离，心力眸光，守定丹田，直守至后天吸呼之气归于丹田之内，隐伏不动，则先天真一之炁滋养舍利子，自然凝结成丹光，状如火球，大如弹子，发生于丹田之中，于是丹田发热，淫根之根发动真炁，用真意将真炁由后督脉上升泥丸宫，下降于淫根，再将炁散于周身，通于四肢，炁血流通，养我身体，滋生舍利子也。

谭至明真人曰：舍利子之光是真阳之精，此是性命双修真阳之炁足也。若不留神，夜内走失真宝，其光无有，便失去真阳之精。学者要细细悟炼，内里养足舍利子，以保守外边真阳之精。即性命之光。《南华经》云：“至道之精，窈窈冥冥”。《道德经》云：“窈兮冥兮，其中有精，其精甚真。”惟此真精，乃吾身中之真种子是也。以其入于混沌，故名曰“太极”；以其为一身造化之始，故名曰“先天”；以其阴阳未分，故名曰“一炁”，又名“黄芽”，又名“玄珠”，又名“阳精”。此精若凝结于天地之间，即是舍利子之光也。邱祖长春云，阳精虽是房中得之，而非御女之术，内非父母所生之躯，外非山林所产之宝，但着在形体上摸索，皆不是，亦不可离形体而向外寻求。此各位祖师隐语，如同水中捞月，镜里攀花，真正智过颜闵，实难强猜，其实是舍利子足，其光发于目前，其光窈窈冥冥，内里有先天真一之精，无此真精，发不出真光，外有真光，内里舍利子足也。此即是上性下命合一之慧光也。

The Daoist teacher Liu Mingrui, Panchanzi, said: “With a heart of ashes, the holy fire nourishes the true origin.” The work of the heart of ashes is the heart as cold as dead ashes, without the slightest hindrance. If there is hindrance, the light of the śarīra is insufficient; if a thought stirs, the śarīra suffers “cold fire flaring upward” (the fire that should warm and nourish becomes an empty fire rising upward). On the whole, utmost stillness is the essential. When the śarīra has been nourished to the full, scenes of a thousand changes and ten thousand transformations will come; one must guard against danger, beware of yin deviance and preserve the original yang. Use the mechanism of heaven of “true fire without phasing” (true fire has no fixed phasing), and the light of the śarīra naturally comes forth. My teacher said: “When there is fire without phasing, do not withdraw or add; forget the mechanism, forget the time, and there is wondrous mystery; forgetting is in fact the wonder of forgetting nothing; within not-forgetting is the true Chan of forgetting; forget until pure yang and the full moon appear, but do not take forgetting for the Chan of drowsy sleep.” (When there is true fire without fixed phasing, do not add or subtract any more; forget scheming, forget time, and therein lies wondrous mystery; what is called forgetting is in fact the wonder of “nothing forgotten”; only within not-forgetting is there the true Chan of forgetting; forget until pure yang and the full moon appear — but you must not take “forgetting” for a Chan of dozing sleep.) This means that one must not fall asleep during practice; if one dozes, the original yang is lost and all previous work wasted; students must guard against this.

Teacher Peng Maochang said: there is no other secret to refining the śarīra: bring heart and eyes together and shine down on the navel of Kun (the area of the navel); if there is a stirring, quickly move and refine it; at the slightest lapse the elixir is sure to leave the furnace and be lost, and the previous work is wasted. When the member has only just drawn back, even if an outer light appears, it is not the horse-like retraction; the śarīra is not yet sufficient. Afterwards, refine diligently, until the glans withdraws into the belly and the outer light appears like lightning in the clouds, in the manner of “the empty room produces whiteness,” appearing first in front of the eyebrows; refine further with care, until the light of the elixir within the elixir field surges up, reaches outward to the eyes and gives forth radiance, the rays of the two eyes flashing two or three times in a row before stopping; the light of the elixir surges out, bright as a gold coin, or like a pearl of fire, issuing from the two eyes — only then is the śarīra sufficient.

Teacher Liu Yunpu said: know the method of nourishing the śarīra to the full: if the spirit does not enter absorption, the śarīra is not born and the elixir does not form; if the breath is not stored in the Cavity of Breath of the elixir field, the light of the śarīra does not appear. Both heart and breath must hibernate within the elixir field; even if the breath sometimes goes out, the heart does not leave for a moment; guard the elixir field with the power of the heart and the light of the eyes, guarding it until the breath of Posterior Heaven breathing returns into the elixir field and lies hidden and unmoving; then the breath of the Prior Heaven true One nourishes the śarīra and naturally condenses into the light of the elixir, shaped like a ball of fire, the size of a pellet, appearing within the elixir field. Then the elixir field grows hot, true breath stirs at the root of the root of desire, and with the True Intention one raises the true breath from behind up the Governing Vessel to the Muddy Pellet palace, then lowers it to the root of desire, then disperses the breath through the whole body to the four limbs, breath and blood flowing, nourishing my body and giving rise to the śarīra.

The Perfected Tan Zhiming said: the light of the śarīra is the essence of the true yang; it means that in the dual cultivation of nature and life the breath of the true yang has become full. If one is careless and loses the true treasure at night, that light is gone, and the essence of the true yang is lost too. Students must realize and cultivate this carefully, nourishing the śarīra to the full within so as to preserve the essence of the true yang without — that is, the light of inner nature and life. The Scripture of Southern Florescence (the Zhuangzi) says: “The essence of the utmost Way is deep and dark.” The Daode jing says: “Deep and dark, within it there is essence; its essence is very true.” Only this true essence is the true seed within my body. Because it enters the primordial chaos, it is called the “Supreme Ultimate”; because it is the beginning of the creation of the whole body, it is called “Prior Heaven”; because yin and yang are not yet divided in it, it is called “the one breath,” and also “Yellow Sprouts,” and also the “mysterious pearl,” and also “yang essence.” If this essence condenses between heaven and earth, it is the light of the śarīra. Patriarch Qiu Changchun (Qiu Chuji) said: although the yang essence is obtained “in the bedchamber,” it is not the art of managing women; within, it is not the body born of father and mother; without, it is not a treasure produced in the mountains and forests; groping about in the bodily form is all wrong, nor may one leave the bodily form to seek it outside. These veiled words of the patriarchs are like scooping the moon from the water or plucking flowers in a mirror; even one whose true wisdom surpasses Yan Hui and Min Ziqian would find them hard to guess. In fact, when the śarīra is sufficient, its light issues before the eyes; that light is deep and dark, and within it is the essence of the Prior Heaven true One; without this true essence, no true light could issue; when there is true light without, the śarīra within is sufficient. This is the light of wisdom of “inner nature above and life below” united into one.

§5
Original (Chinese)

问曰：这丹光因何发光？是什么生的光？即生出光来，因何光又不圆？如何能使之圆而且明？

答曰：这光又名曰慧光，养足曰蟾光。精不足不能生慧光；舍利子不足，不能生蟾光。慧光如月光，蟾光如金光。这舍利子是阳精虫生的。真精足，用二目下照丹田，二目之阳光气属火，下照丹田属水，火入水中蒸发出阳炁，发放于目前。其光乃是精中阴炁，又用二目之光合并下照属阳，二光阴阳一合，发出宝光，如同现在电灯一样，阴阳二线一合，电灯发亮；电力不足，电灯不亮。未炼到童身，还是破身，发的光不亮，为慧光，其色如月光；补还到童身，下身马阴藏相，阴阳二炁一合，发的光明亮，为蟾光，其光金黄之色。慧光者，身不足，内有阴气，会发现一切魔障景相。蟾光者，是佛祖借蟾名三足之意，象征精炁神三品合一。三足金蟾是活动物，世界稀有，精炁神三品合一的舍利子，亦是活物，发现蟾光者亦是少有之人。这慧光、蟾光不圆不明，是夜里走失真宝之故。

Question: Why does this light of the elixir shine? From what is the light born? Since light is born, why is it sometimes not round? How can it be made round and bright?

Answer: This light is also called the “light of wisdom,” and when nourished to the full it is called the “toad light.” If the essence is not sufficient, the light of wisdom cannot be born; if the śarīra is not sufficient, the toad light cannot be born. The light of wisdom is like moonlight, the toad light like golden light. This śarīra is formed from the yang seminal essence. When the true essence is sufficient, shine down on the elixir field with the two eyes: the breath of the yang light of the two eyes belongs to fire, shining down on the elixir field, and the elixir field belongs to water; fire enters water, steaming forth the yang breath, which is sent out before the eyes. This light is the yin breath within the essence; then the light of the two eyes, joined together and shining downward, belongs to yang; when the two lights, yin and yang, unite, a precious light is emitted — just like the electric lamps of today: when the two wires, yin and yang, are connected, the lamp lights up; when the current is insufficient, the lamp does not light. For those not yet refined back to a virgin body, still with broken bodies, the light emitted is not bright; it is the light of wisdom, colored like moonlight; for those restored to a virgin body, with the horse-like retraction below and the two breaths of yin and yang united, the light emitted is bright; it is the toad light, golden yellow in color. The light of wisdom means the body is not yet sufficient and there is yin breath within, and all kinds of demonic obstructions will appear. The toad light is the Buddha borrowing the meaning of the “toad” with three legs (legend says there is a three-legged golden toad in the moon) to symbolize the three treasures of essence, breath and spirit united into one. The three-legged golden toad is a living creature, rare in the world; the śarīra of essence, breath and spirit united into one is also a living thing; those who can see the toad light are also few. When this light of wisdom or toad light is neither round nor bright, it is because the true treasure has been lost at night.

§6
Original (Chinese)

问曰：取火提火提出神火，才得蟾光发现，又不可久用此法，若是久用，头晕眼花，耗散自己真炁，因何用此法会耗损自己真炁？

答曰：提火功法千万不可多用，若是多用，头晕眼花，伤耗真炁。眼一转动，提升命窍真炁，所散的口眼耳鼻七孔，全是耗散真炁之所，所以修道者行立坐卧不离这个，即是以舌倒顶上腭天池穴，不教真炁泄出。学者要明白，上窍不闭天池穴，玄膺穴不开；下窍不闭任脉，督脉不开；中窍六脉不闭，天灵窍不开，胎炁出不来。

Question: Only by drawing out the fire of the spirit with taking fire and lifting fire can one see the toad light, and yet this method must not be used for long; if used for long, it brings dizziness and blurred vision and dissipates one’s own true breath. Why does using this method consume one’s own true breath?

Answer: The practice of lifting fire must on no account be used much; if used much, it brings dizziness and blurred vision and wears away the true breath. As soon as the eyes turn, they lift the true breath of the opening of life, and the seven openings through which it disperses — mouth, eyes, ears and nose — are all places where true breath is dissipated. So those who cultivate the Way never part from this, walking, standing, sitting or lying — that is, pressing the tongue back against the Celestial Pool point on the palate, so as not to let the true breath leak out. Students must understand: if the upper opening does not close the Celestial Pool point, the Mysterious Breast point does not open; if the lower opening does not close the Conception Vessel, the Governing Vessel does not open; if the six pulses of the middle opening are not closed, the “Opening of the Celestial Spirit” (the crown) does not open, and the embryonic breath cannot come out.

§7
Original (Chinese)

问曰：行功时面上如虫行蚁走，又好似蛛网罩住，左耳内有风声，右耳内有嗷嗷之声，口内津液生多，咽纳不尽，此理难明，乞示知。

答曰：人用功时面如蚁行，是炼的真炁穿通周身面部，这面上毛细管教真炁开通，故面如虫食作痒，又如蚁行，又如蛛网罩住，是用功的好景象。又耳内闻声，更是好景象。左耳内听见如同风声，此是虎啸之声，右耳听见嗷嗷之声，此是龙吟之声，龙吟者精足也，虎啸者炁足也。口内津液上升，此是脾经真炁足，向上发生津液，要吞咽下降丹田，滋养精炁，此是用功真象。还有一个好景象，尔也许忘问，也许未见着，因道功师傅不肯先说景象，若是先说，恐尔生景念起，此念属阴。余今说出，眼前有光无光？是何样的光？若是白光，内暗而不明，尔还要加工细炼，见着阳光好景，再来询问。

Question: During practice my face feels as if insects were crawling and ants walking on it, and again as if covered by a cobweb; in my left ear there is the sound of wind, in my right ear a sound like “ao ao”; my mouth produces a great deal of saliva, more than I can swallow. The principle of this is hard to understand; please instruct me.

Answer: When a person practicing feels as if ants were crawling on the face, it is the refined true breath penetrating the whole body and the face; the fine blood vessels of the face have been opened by the true breath, so the face itches as if bitten by insects, and again as if ants were crawling, and again as if covered by a cobweb — this is a good scene in practice. Hearing sounds in the ears is an even better scene: hearing something like wind in the left ear is the sound of the “tiger’s roar”; hearing “ao ao” in the right ear is the sound of the “dragon’s chant.” The dragon’s chant means the essence is sufficient; the tiger’s roar means the breath is sufficient. The saliva rising in the mouth means the true breath of the spleen channel is sufficient and produces saliva upward; it should be swallowed and sent down to the elixir field to nourish the essence and breath — this is a true sign of practice. There is one more good scene that you may have forgotten to ask about, or may not yet have seen; for teachers of the Daoist practice are unwilling to speak of the scenes beforehand — if they did, they fear you would give rise to a “thought of scenes” (a thought of wanting to see some scene), and such a thought belongs to yin. Now I will say it: is there light before your eyes or not? What kind of light? If it is white light, dark within and not bright, you must still work at refining it with care; when you have seen the good scene of the yang light (golden light), come and ask again.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§7
Original note (Chinese)

【注释】此步功与下一步功法进入炼养舍利子（内丹）的更高层次。舍利子是由性光（即神）和命光（即精中真炁）合一而成。前六步功炼的是性光，后六步功炼的是命光。把慧光炼成蟾光，才算练成舍利子。蟾光是舍利子之光。慧光淡明如月光，其色淡红，或光圈暗黑，或为白光；蟾光则为金色，光象圆明。

炼成舍利子的征象或景，还有眉间常放白光和马阴藏相。舍利子将出炉时，自丹田至目一路皆虚白晃耀，如月华之光；马阴藏相即阳物龟缩不举，如婴儿之“朘”成为自然的不漏之体。如仅阳物微动而不缩，虽有外光发现，则不能认为舍利子已成，必须转动法轮加工炼之。此外，炼成舍利子就有炁满不思食、神满不思睡、精满不思淫等感受，甚至能够辟谷。

真正说来，行功中六景现前才是舍利子炼足之象。六景即眼前有金光、脑后鹫鸣、耳听龙虎啸、丹田火炽、身涌鼻搐、马阴藏相。这是古人总结的经验。

但是，舍利子炼得足还是不足，还需要不需要加工细炼，最好临机之前使心中有数，免得临机时不知所措。千峰老人在本章中泄出了自古不传的断定舍利子足与不足的秘法，这就是取火提火之法。用此法虽损失自己一点真元，但也是值得的。牛金宝根据乃师所传，对此法作了补充。他说，古时用棉油灯一盏，现在可改用一号手电筒，将盖子去掉立起来，灯头朝上放在面前。二目高低与灯泡平，距离一尺三寸左右。心定念止，二目睁开注视灯光，头颈盘旋，二目仍视灯光，头转眼不转。头转时依然走子、卯、午、酉四正路线，由下子位向左上午位，由午位不停紧接着向右下子位，如此为一转，一气转九转。转时不一定配合呼吸，九转功完后闭目看光，若眼前光圆满晶莹，亮如秋月，这即是内丹已足。如果眼前之光不圆，或内有暗光，这是内丹不足之兆，应继续用功。

炼舍利子之法全以至静为主，故须常常觉照，谨慎护持，如龙之乍养珠，似幼女之初怀孕，牢关神室，不可使之渗漏。其法仍是打坐时两眼归并合一，神炁下照丹田。柳华阳《慧命经》云：“正法眼藏，此乃采舍利秘法天机，故曰正法矣。眼者神之所棲，眼之所至，神亦至焉”。又曰：“若不以心目取之，舍利不能出炉”，“盖采之时，专视不可须臾离也。离则火冷炁散，不成牟尼”。牟尼者，即舍利子所产而运过三关归于中宫者。伍冲虚者《仙佛合宗》中说：“双眸之光乃神中真意之所寄，眸光之所至，真意至焉，真意属土，土乃中宫之黄婆，黄婆即勾引之媒妁也。黄婆勾引于上，则大药自相随而出现于下矣。古云中宫胎息号黄婆，即此意也”。此处所指大药，即舍利子。

行功中产生的良好效应，如面上虫行蚁走及蛛丝罩面、耳听风声等等，本章已有谈及，但在马阴藏相之前，学者仍须十分注意走泄元阳真宝的危险。至于入定中必须有道侣护持，平时须遵守不可过劳等戒规，都须正确对待，方保无虞。

This step and the next enter a higher level of refining and nourishing the śarīra (the inner elixir). The śarīra is formed by the union of the light of inner nature (that is, spirit) and the light of life (that is, the true breath within essence). The first six steps refine the light of inner nature; the last six steps refine the light of life. Only when the light of wisdom has been refined into the toad light is the śarīra said to be refined; the toad light is the light of the śarīra. The light of wisdom is faintly bright, like moonlight, pale red in color, or a dark ring of light, or white light; the toad light is golden, its appearance round and bright.

Other signs and scenes of the śarīra being refined are white light constantly shining between the eyebrows and the horse-like retraction. When the śarīra is about to leave the furnace, all the way from the elixir field to the eyes there is an empty white radiance, like the light of moonbeams; the horse-like retraction is the member retracting and not rising, like the “zui” of an infant (a male infant’s genital organ, from the Daode jing: “he does not yet know the union of female and male, yet his zui stirs”), becoming a naturally non-leaking body. If the member merely stirs slightly without retracting, even if an outer light appears, one cannot consider the śarīra formed; one must turn the Dharma Wheel and work further at refining it. Besides this, when the śarīra is refined there are such sensations as “when the breath is full one does not think of food, when the spirit is full one does not think of sleep, when the essence is full one does not think of lust,” and one may even be able to abstain from grain.

Strictly speaking, only when the “six scenes” appear before one during practice is it the sign that the śarīra has been refined to sufficiency. The six scenes are: golden light before the eyes, the cry of a vulture behind the head, the dragon’s chant and tiger’s roar in the ears, fire blazing in the elixir field, surging in the body and twitching of the nose, and the horse-like retraction. This is a summary of the experience of the ancients.

But whether the śarīra has been refined to sufficiency or not, and whether it needs further careful refining, is best known in one’s heart before the moment arrives, so as not to be at a loss when it comes. In this chapter the Old Man of Qianfeng reveals the secret method, never transmitted since ancient times, for determining whether the śarīra is sufficient — the method of taking fire and lifting fire. Although using this method costs one a little of one’s true origin, it is worth it. Niu Jinbao, following what his teacher transmitted, supplements this method. He says that in ancient times a cotton-oil lamp was used; now one can use a size-one flashlight instead, removing its cap and standing it up, the bulb facing up, in front of one; the two eyes level with the bulb, about one chi three cun away. With the heart settled and thoughts stopped, open the eyes and gaze at the light; circle the head and neck, the eyes still looking at the light — the head turns, the eyes do not. As the head turns, it still follows the route of the four cardinal points zi, mao, wu and you: from the zi position below up to the left to the wu position, and from the wu position without stopping straight down to the right back to the zi position; this is one turn; turn nine turns in one breath. While turning, it need not be coordinated with the breathing. When the nine turns are done, close the eyes and look at the light: if the light before the eyes is full, round and crystalline, bright as the autumn moon, the inner elixir is sufficient; if the light before the eyes is not round, or there is a dark light within, it is a sign that the inner elixir is not sufficient, and one should continue to practice. (See the safety note.)

The method of refining the śarīra takes utmost stillness as the main thing, so one must constantly be aware and illuminate, guarding it with care, like a dragon just beginning to nourish its pearl, like a young woman in her first pregnancy, firmly closing the Chamber of the Spirit and not letting anything seep out. The method is still, when sitting, to bring the two eyes together into one and let spirit and breath shine down on the elixir field. Liu Huayang’s Book of Wisdom and Life says: “‘The treasury of the true Dharma eye’ — this is the secret mechanism of heaven for gathering the śarīra, and so it is called the ‘true Dharma.' The eyes are where the spirit dwells; wherever the eyes go, the spirit goes too.” It also says: “If one does not take it with the heart and eyes, the śarīra cannot leave the furnace.” “When gathering, gaze at it intently, never leaving it for a moment; if one leaves it, the fire grows cold, the breath scatters, and one cannot form the mani.” The mani is what, after the śarīra is produced, is moved through the three passes and returned to the central palace. Wu Chongxu says in The Common Origin of Immortals and Buddhas: “The light of the two eyes is where the True Intention within the spirit lodges; wherever the light of the eyes goes, the True Intention goes. The True Intention belongs to earth, and earth is the ‘Yellow Dame’ of the central palace; the Yellow Dame is the go-between who coaxes. When the Yellow Dame coaxes above, the Great Medicine naturally follows and appears below. The ancients’ saying ‘the embryonic breathing of the central palace is called the Yellow Dame’ means this.” The Great Medicine referred to here is the śarīra.

The good effects arising in practice, such as the face feeling as if insects were crawling and ants walking, as if covered by cobweb threads, the ears hearing the sound of wind and so on, have already been discussed in this chapter; but before the horse-like retraction, students must still pay close attention to the danger of leaking the true treasure of the original yang. As for the rules that there must be a Daoist companion to watch over one in absorption and that one must not overwork in ordinary times, they must all be taken seriously, and only then can one be sure there is no danger.

Key Terms

  • The numinous elixir enters the tripod: the numinous elixir (what drops down after essence, breath and spirit have gathered at the crown) is swallowed into the belly and turns into saliva that nourishes the śarīra.
  • Light of inner nature, light of life: what the first six steps refine is the light of inner nature (spirit); what the last six steps refine is the light of life (the true breath within essence).
  • Light of wisdom, toad light: the light of wisdom is like moonlight, pale red or white, the light of one whose lost virginity has not yet been made good; the toad light is golden yellow, round and bright, the light of the śarīra nourished to the full. The three-legged golden toad symbolizes the three treasures of essence, breath and spirit united into one.
  • The empty room produces whiteness: the empty, bright heart gives rise to light.
  • Ten harms: long walking harms the sinews, long standing harms the bones, long sitting harms the blood, long sleeping harms the vessels, long listening harms the essence, long looking harms the spirit, long talking harms the breath, long thinking harms the spleen, long indulgence in sex harms life, eating to fullness harms the heart.
  • Taking fire, lifting fire: turning the eyes facing a lamp, drawing out the fire of the spirit to test whether the śarīra is sufficient; it consumes true breath and must not be used often.
  • Dragon’s chant, tiger’s roar: an “ao ao” sound in the right ear is the dragon’s chant (essence sufficient); a sound of wind in the left ear is the tiger’s roar (breath sufficient).
  • Six scenes: golden light before the eyes, the cry of a vulture behind the head, the dragon’s chant and tiger’s roar in the ears, fire blazing in the elixir field, surging in the body and twitching of the nose, the horse-like retraction (Qiu Ling’s note).
  • Zui: an infant’s genital organ, from the Daode jing.
  • Mani: the precious pearl, the śarīra moved through the three passes and returned to the central palace.
  • Yellow Dame: the True Intention, the “go-between” who coaxes the Great Medicine.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Stillness above all: the root of this step is still stillness — “like a dragon nourishing its pearl, like a young woman in her first pregnancy”; no hurry, no seeking.
  2. Guard against the ten harms: nothing should be overdone, and sitting too should not be too long; this is a principle of health that benefits anyone.
  3. The six don’ts: no arising thoughts, no scattered intention, no looking outward, no listening outward, breathing neither hurried nor stopped.
  4. Don’t crave scenes: an itching face, sounds in the ears, abundant saliva and seeing light may all occur, but Zhao says the teachers do not describe scenes beforehand precisely for fear that once a student’s “thought of scenes” arises, it will spoil things.
  5. Don’t do the bright-light test: the test of turning the head and eyes facing a lamp or flashlight harms the eyes and the spirit; this site does not recommend it.
  6. Don’t restrict eating: “not thinking of food” describes a natural state; it does not mean starving oneself.

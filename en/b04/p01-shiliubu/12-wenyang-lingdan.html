The Sixteen Steps of Method Formulas · 第十二步 温养灵丹

Step Twelve · Warming and Nourishing the Numinous Elixir

The formula of Step Twelve, warming and nourishing the numinous elixir — empty yet not empty, the four forms unite; cooking with civil fire and refining with martial fire is the work of lead and mercury

About This Chapter

Step Eleven refined up to “the six scenes appearing before you,” and the śarīra was first formed; this step is about warming and nourishing — letting the śarīra gestate and grow in stillness, and preparing for the next step, “the Great Medicine passes the gates.”

  1. The method of warming and nourishing: join the two eyes and, with the True Intention, shine down on the elixir field, “neither forgetting nor helping”: let no thoughts or worries arise (not forgetting), and do not cling or apply force (not helping). By this point in practice the foundation is laid and all the joints of the body are wide open; the greatest danger is that feelings arise at the sight of beauty and the spirit races off with circumstances — “one false step,” and the savings of many years pour out in a moment.
  2. The seven emotions, the ten harms, the nine breaths: much joy harms the heart, much anger harms the liver …; long walking harms the sinews, long standing harms the bones …; anger makes the breath rise, fear makes the breath sink … — all must be carefully guarded against. Zhao says: having refined up to Step Twelve, even without gathering the Great Medicine and passing the gates, one can surely lengthen one’s years and increase one’s life.
  3. The five breaths return to the origin: only when the breaths of the five organs and the breaths of the five viscera — “five yin and five yang, ten breaths” — unite into one, and golden light appears before the eyes, is it the five breaths returning to the origin; “the three flowers gather at the summit” is not gold, silver and red flowers, but essence, breath and spirit gathered within the crown.
  4. The six scenes: golden light in the eyes, the cry of a vulture behind the head, the dragon’s chant and tiger’s roar in the ears, fire blazing in the elixir field (the two kidneys simmering as in hot water), surging in the body and twitching of the nose, and the horse-like retraction — the first sign that the śarīra is sufficient and the fire can be stopped.
  5. Cultivating the Way in old age: Zhao particularly encourages the old: most of those who attained the Way in ancient times did so in old age, because they had seen through fame and profit and their hearts had no distractions.
  6. The yang light appearing three times: when the yang light appears once, prepare the method, the means, the companion and the place; when it appears twice, stop the fire and gather the medicine; when it appears three times, gather the Great Medicine, ingest it and pass the gates.

Qiu Ling’s note sums up: having practiced up to these twelve steps, one can already lengthen one’s years and increase one’s life; from Step Thirteen on there are many conditions and limitations, “not at all easy to meet”; and comparing Wu Chongxu’s account, he makes “the yang light appearing once, twice, three times” clearer.

Safety note (translator): The original says that in preparing the “ritual implements” one presses a soft seat against the grain path (the anus) and plugs the nostrils tightly with an object, and that in “stopping the fire” one “does not move the breath of breathing” — please do not plug your nostrils or hold your breath; there is a danger of suffocation. Going into the mountains for a closed retreat and having one’s food brought by others for long periods are ancient ways of practice and are not recommended. If in quiet sitting there are repeated, intense visions such as “the beauties of paradise or the horrors of hell,” or you cannot tell the illusory from the real, stop practicing and seek professional help, and do not rely on practice methods alone.

Original Text and Translation

Figure 27 · Warming and Nourishing the Numinous Elixir

In the figure an old man with a shaven head and a long beard, in a wide-sleeved robe, sits upright cross-legged, his two hands in front of the belly, a knot tied at his waist. From each ear a wisp of breath rises and turns into a cloud: the cloud on the left is marked “tiger’s roar,” the cloud on the right “dragon’s chant” (in the original figure a tiger and a dragon are drawn in the clouds; this site has simplified). The meaning: when the śarīra is nourished to the full, the sound of the dragon’s chant and the tiger’s roar is heard in the ears.

Formula
Original (Chinese)

空以不空四相合，

文烹武炼铅汞功。

龙吟虎啸昆仑顶，

片时黄芽白云升。

With a state of heart that is “empty yet not empty,” let the four forms (the four images) unite; cooking with civil fire and refining with martial fire — this is the work of refining lead and mercury (breath and spirit). The dragon’s chant and the tiger’s roar resound at the summit of Kunlun (the crown); in a moment the Yellow Sprouts (the sprouts of the elixir) rise like white clouds.

§1
Original (Chinese)

前第十一步功炼到六景现前，金机飞电，淫根缩如童子，忽然正子时至还不能采取，因精与炁合为舍利子，当再用温养之功使舍利子孕育长大。温养之法是用二目合并真意双眸，下照丹田，将气质之性化为天命之性，将通身混元之炁合而为一，胸中一片太和之炁现于目前，色似月光，内外一体，天地同春，真息绵绵若存，元神得见，则炁源源而生矣。切忌意动炁不生而神亦不续，极须遵守勿忘勿助之戒。若稍存念虑，即失去虚灵之体，这便是“忘”；一心不二，须臾不离，就是“不忘”。稍有固执，即滞其活泼之圆机，这便是“助”；活活泼泼毫不执意，就是“勿助”。“忘”必神昏，“助”必散乱，皆为学人之大忌。凡气散漫于形骸之间，皆尸气也。操则存，舍则亡，道与不道，一转眼间耳。功到此时，筑基已成，关节俱已洞开，周身毫无阻滞。倘心志不坚，见色而情生，迁境而神驰，一经失足，纵使储积多时，难逃片刻之间倾囊倒箧而出。精本无形无质之物，在内是炁，出外是精，内被七情牵心之伤，外受十损九气之害，则一本之身散于万株之脉络；若逆而行之，聚万株而归一本，仙道可期矣。现将七情受伤处列后：喜多伤心；怒多伤肝；哀多伤肺；惧多伤胆；爱多伤神；恶多伤情；欲多伤脾。又有十损，修道者谨戒：久行伤筋；久立损骨；久坐损血；久睡损脉；久乐损精；久看损神；久言损气；久思损脾；食饱损心；久淫损命。又有九气列后：怒则气上；恐则气下；喜则气缓；悲则气消；惊则气乱；思则气结；劳则气耗；寒则气收；热则气泄。修功至此，若采大药过关，仅戒七情、十损、九气之害。若不采大药过关，仅炼十二步功，准能延年益寿。也有下手采药者，但多不知修炼，不知火候，采药白采，反被七情六欲耗去，成不了道。只有会用火候者才能长生。修者火候用得对，没有七情、十损、九气之害，准得舍利子也。

When the practice of Step Eleven above has refined up to the six scenes appearing before one and “the golden mechanism flying like lightning” (golden light flashing before the eyes), and the root of desire has retracted like a boy’s, suddenly the true zi hour arrives, yet one still cannot gather — because essence and breath have only just united into the śarīra, and one should further use the work of warming and nourishing so that the śarīra gestates and grows. The method of warming and nourishing is to join the two eyes and, with the twin pupils of the True Intention, shine down on the elixir field, transforming the “nature of breath and constitution” (habits formed after birth) into the “nature endowed by heaven” (the original nature), and uniting the primordial breath of the whole body into one; a breath of great harmony in the chest appears before the eyes, colored like moonlight, inner and outer one body, heaven and earth sharing the same spring; the True Breath is soft and continuous, as if barely there, the Original Spirit is able to appear, and the breath is then born in an endless stream. Above all avoid the slightest movement of the intention — then the breath is not born and the spirit cannot keep up; one must keep the precept of “neither forgetting nor helping.” Keeping even a little thought or worry loses the empty, numinous substance — this is “forgetting” (so in the original, see the Translator’s Notes); a single undivided heart, never leaving it for a moment, is “not forgetting.” The slightest stubbornness stalls its lively, round mechanism — this is “helping”; lively and free, with no insistence at all, is “not helping.” “Forgetting” is sure to make the spirit dull, “helping” sure to make it scattered — both are great taboos for students. Every breath scattered loosely through the body is “corpse breath” (dead breath). Hold on and it is kept; let go and it is lost; having the Way and not having it lie in the turn of an eye.

When practice reaches this time, the foundation is complete, the joints are wide open, and there is no obstruction anywhere in the body. If the will is not firm, and feelings arise at the sight of beauty and the spirit races off with circumstances, then at one false step, even what has been stored up long cannot escape being poured out in a moment, bags and boxes emptied. Essence is fundamentally without form or substance: within, it is breath; going out, it is essence; within, harmed by the seven emotions tugging at the heart, without, suffering the ten harms and the nine breaths, the one root of the body is scattered into ten thousand vessels; if one goes against the current, gathering the ten thousand back into the one root, the way of the immortals can be hoped for. Now the places harmed by the seven emotions are listed below: much joy harms the heart; much anger harms the liver; much grief harms the lungs; much fear harms the gallbladder; much love harms the spirit; much hatred harms the feelings; much desire harms the spleen. There are also the “ten harms,” which those who cultivate the Way must carefully guard against: long walking harms the sinews; long standing harms the bones; long sitting harms the blood; long sleeping harms the vessels; long pleasure harms the essence; long looking harms the spirit; long talking harms the breath; long thinking harms the spleen; eating to fullness harms the heart; long indulgence in sex harms life. And the “nine breaths” are listed below: anger makes the breath rise; fear makes the breath sink; joy makes the breath slacken; sorrow makes the breath dwindle; fright makes the breath disordered; thought makes the breath knot; toil makes the breath wear out; cold makes the breath contract; heat makes the breath leak.

When practice reaches this point, if one wishes to gather the Great Medicine and pass the gates, one need only guard against the harm of the seven emotions, the ten harms and the nine breaths. Even without gathering the Great Medicine and passing the gates, just practicing these twelve steps, one can surely lengthen one’s years and increase one’s life. There are also those who set to work gathering the medicine, but most do not understand cultivation or fire phasing; they gather the medicine in vain, and it is worn away by the seven emotions and six desires, and they do not attain the Way. Only those who know how to use fire phasing can live long. A practitioner who uses fire phasing correctly, without the harm of the seven emotions, the ten harms and the nine breaths, will surely obtain the śarīra.

§2
Original (Chinese)

问曰：何为舍利子？如何养法？舍利子不足如何知道？

答曰：精升与性交，即得慧光，色似月光，光圆足满为八两。炁降与命交，即得金光，色似红黄，光圆足满为半斤。二光合一，炼成一斤，而为舍利子。真种灵宝归根后，回光返照久之，必由眉间白光内，发现金光，此是舍利子苗，即我精炁神合而为一，充满于内发现于外之光。此慧光、金光比为电灯阴阳二线，用户接电局总线；精炁神比同发电所之电气，电局不发电，纵有线接连，任用户开闭电灯绝不放光明，此是阴阳二线不合之故。若是二光不合，发不出光来，若合者还得有身内五炁合一，比为电气有无，无电气灯不明，无五炁朝不了元。五炁者是养舍利子原料也，尔养舍利子得二目合并下照坤脐，此是聚五炁归一养舍利子也，即是电灯局聚电之法，无电无光，如无五炁无金光。

Question: What is the śarīra? How is it nourished? How does one know if the śarīra is insufficient?

Answer: When essence rises and meets inner nature, one obtains the light of wisdom, colored like moonlight; when the light is round, full and complete, it is called “eight ounces”; when breath descends and meets life, one obtains golden light, reddish yellow in color; when the light is round, full and complete, it is called “half a pound.” When the two lights unite and are refined into “one pound,” it becomes the śarīra (under the old system sixteen ounces made a pound; “eight ounces and half a pound” is a metaphor for the two being of equal weight). After the true seed, the numinous treasure, has returned to the root and the light has been turned around to shine back for a long time, golden light is sure to appear within the white light between the eyebrows; this is the sprout of the śarīra, the light of my essence, breath and spirit united into one, filling within and appearing without. This light of wisdom and golden light are like the two wires, yin and yang, of an electric lamp, which the user must connect to the main line of the power company; essence, breath and spirit are like the electricity of the power station; if the power company does not generate electricity, then even with the wires connected, however the user switches it on and off, the lamp will never light — because the two wires, yin and yang, do not meet. If the two lights do not unite, no light can be emitted; even if they unite, there must still be the five breaths within the body united into one, which is like whether there is electricity or not: without electricity, the lamp does not light; without the five breaths, there can be no return to the origin. The five breaths are the raw material for nourishing the śarīra. When you nourish the śarīra, join the two eyes and shine down on the navel of Kun (the area of the navel); this is gathering the five breaths into one to nourish the śarīra, which is the power station’s method of gathering electricity: no electricity, no light — just as without the five breaths there is no golden light.

§3
Original (Chinese)

问曰：养舍利子用五炁朝元之功，如何考察？

答曰：五炁者五脏之炁也。炁在炁穴之中，而流通于五脏之间，于肺则为金炁，于心则为火炁，于肝则为木炁，于脾则为土炁，于肾则为水炁，是为五行之炁，平日间行于五脏，各有衰旺，过衰则病，过旺则病，五炁瘀塞不通，则有瘫痪痈疽之病。功到息息归元之后，炁穴中之炁蓬蓬勃勃，从尾闾上透泥丸，与脑中之髓相映，下至重楼，遍薰诸脏，如一轮炯月照耀潇湘洞庭之间。心中之炁属阳中阴气，又为无根树，心火太旺则血枯，日中宜寡言少思，自然心逸而志气如神，色身强健。元神恶动而好静，恶实而好虚，静虚之至则心中阴神气自灵。小肠之炁属阳，其炁与心中之气合而为一炁，以共朝元之用。肺气属阴，有吸氧气呼碳气之能力，能使血液清鲜。大肠发出真炁属阳，其炁与肺中之炁合而为一炁，以共朝元之用。肝气属阴，当人盛怒之际，或烦闷而气不舒，积久在内易生郁病，每见妇女烦闷只要一哭，心中苦衷，溢于言表，肝气即散。胆气属阳，其汁入胃，可防饮食腐坏，其炁与肝中之气合为一炁，以共朝元之用。脾气属阳，有舒缩推动之能，将胃内饮食化成糜粥，吾人之生存，全赖谷食以养脾胃，土气充盈分输四体，已土左旋，谷气归于心肺，戊土右转谷精归于肾肝。胃气属阳，其精气化糟粕于大肠，大肠得其津液以润下，又为六腑之总司，居中藏而主土也，谷气入胃运于脾输于肺，以滋润全身经络，运化于脏腑表里，是以气和四达，长养而不病。脾胃之气相为表里，此脾胃阴阳气合而为一炁，以共朝元之用。肾气属阴，动属阳，非指两肾而言，此乃是生尿之器，系指生精之内肾。膀胱属阳，人之饮水均归肾，气化上行则为津液，其所剩余之质，乃下通膀胱而为溺。肾与膀胱相为表里，二气合而为一炁，以共朝元之用。此心、肝、脾、肺、肾之炁统名为五炁，实则五阴、五阳、十炁而成。设问凝神聚五炁在何处？丹经云：“生身受炁初”。此是丹经书上隐语。这凝神聚五炁，指你全身真炁发动，其炁升，五脏之炁皆升，其炁降，五脏之炁皆降，既降之后，五炁合而为一，又为攒簇五形。丹经所谓金木交并、性情合一，即是二目合并归一，丹田五炁发生金光，现于目前性光之内，全身万脉归宗，而阴阳之炁化成纯阳之光，似这个〇，才为五炁朝元之功。没有三花聚顶，发不出五炁朝元之景。盖行此功，真意动为火，定为土，土能生金，金能生水，水能生木，木能生火，火能生土。真意大定谓之五行全，生生之机不息，则灵根得孕育源流，不难出矣。若妄以意动为是，意念知识俱是魔将魔兵，肝、脾、肺、肾俱是魔巢窟。若心地灵明，何惧舍利子光不献，而舍利子光献，即是五炁朝元。

Question: How does one examine the work of the five breaths returning to the origin used in nourishing the śarīra?

Answer: The five breaths are the breaths of the five organs. The breath is in the Cavity of Breath and flows among the five organs: in the lungs it is the breath of metal, in the heart the breath of fire, in the liver the breath of wood, in the spleen the breath of earth, in the kidneys the breath of water — these are the breaths of the five phases. In ordinary times they move through the five organs, each with its waning and flourishing: too weak, there is illness; too strong, there is illness too; when the five breaths are blocked and do not flow, there are illnesses such as paralysis and abscesses. When practice has reached the point where every breath returns to the origin, the breath in the Cavity of Breath surges vigorously, passes up from the Caudal Funnel to the Muddy Pellet, shines together with the marrow in the brain, then descends to the Storied Tower and steams all the organs, like a bright moon shining between the Xiao and Xiang rivers and Lake Dongting.

(The breaths of the five organs and five viscera unite in this way:) the breath in the heart belongs to “yin within yang,” and is also called the “rootless tree”; when the heart fire is too strong, the blood dries up; by day one should speak little and think little, and naturally the heart is at ease, the will spirit-like and the body strong. The Original Spirit dislikes movement and loves stillness, dislikes fullness and loves emptiness; when stillness and emptiness reach their utmost, the yin spirit-breath in the heart naturally becomes numinous and bright. The breath of the small intestine belongs to yang; its breath unites with the breath in the heart, and together they are used to return to the origin. The breath of the lungs belongs to yin; it has the ability to take in oxygen and breathe out carbon breath (carbon dioxide), refreshing the blood. The true breath issuing from the large intestine belongs to yang; it unites with the breath in the lungs, and together they are used to return to the origin. The breath of the liver belongs to yin; when a person is in a great rage, or vexed so that the breath does not flow freely, if it accumulates long within it easily breeds illness of depression; one often sees that when a woman is vexed, as soon as she weeps and speaks out the bitterness in her heart, the liver breath disperses. The breath of the gallbladder belongs to yang; the bile enters the stomach and can keep food from spoiling; its breath unites with the breath in the liver into one breath, and together they are used to return to the origin. The breath of the spleen belongs to yang (so in the original); it has the function of contracting, expanding and pushing, turning the food in the stomach into gruel; our human existence depends wholly on grain food to nourish the spleen and stomach; when the earth breath is full, it is distributed to the four limbs; the ji earth (the original writes 已土) turns to the left, and the breath of grain returns to the heart and lungs; the wu earth turns to the right, and the essence of grain returns to the kidneys and liver. The breath of the stomach belongs to yang; it turns the essence-breath into dregs and sends them to the large intestine, which receives its fluids and is moistened to move downward; it is also the chief of the six viscera, residing in the center and governing earth; the breath of grain enters the stomach, is transported to the spleen and conveyed to the lungs, moistening the channels and collaterals of the whole body, transforming between the organs and viscera, inside and outside, so that the breath is harmonious and flows freely in all directions, nourishing growth without illness. The breaths of the spleen and stomach are inside and outside to each other, and the yin and yang breaths of the spleen and stomach unite into one and are used together to return to the origin. The breath of the kidneys belongs to yin, and in movement belongs to yang; this does not refer to the two kidney organs — those are the organs that produce urine — but to the “inner kidney” that produces essence. The bladder belongs to yang; all the water a person drinks returns to the kidneys; transformed by breath and moving upward, it becomes saliva and fluids, and what remains passes down to the bladder and becomes urine. The kidneys and bladder are inside and outside to each other, and the two breaths unite into one and are used together to return to the origin. These breaths of heart, liver, spleen, lungs and kidneys are together called the “five breaths,” but in fact they are made up of ten breaths, five yin and five yang.

If you ask where one concentrates the spirit and gathers the five breaths, the elixir classics say: “At the beginning, when the body received breath.” This is a veiled expression in the elixir books. Concentrating the spirit and gathering the five breaths refers to the true breath of your whole body stirring: when its breath rises, the breaths of the five organs all rise; when its breath descends, the breaths of the five organs all descend; after descending, the five breaths unite into one, which is also called “gathering the five forms” (the five phases). What the elixir classics call “metal and wood joined, inner nature and feeling united” is the two eyes joined and returned to one, the five breaths of the elixir field producing golden light that appears within the light of inner nature before the eyes; the ten thousand vessels of the whole body return to their source, and the breaths of yin and yang turn into the light of pure yang, like this one “〇” — only this is the work of the five breaths returning to the origin. Without the three flowers gathering at the summit, the scene of the five breaths returning to the origin cannot appear. For in doing this practice, when the True Intention moves, it is fire; when it settles, it is earth; earth produces metal, metal produces water, water produces wood, wood produces fire, fire produces earth. When the True Intention is in great absorption, it is called “the five phases complete”; the mechanism of ceaseless generation does not stop, and the numinous root can gestate its source and stream, which is not hard to bring forth. If one rashly takes the movement of the intention as right, then thoughts and knowledge are all demon generals and demon soldiers, and liver, spleen, lungs and kidneys are all demons’ lairs. If the ground of the heart is numinous and bright, why worry that the light of the śarīra will not appear? And the appearing of the light of the śarīra is the five breaths returning to the origin.

§4
Original (Chinese)

问曰：医书云，五脏六腑俱通气，此是五朝元之炁否？

答曰：五脏六腑之气，是养后天之身之气，非是五炁朝元之先天炁。先天炁能养命，后天气能养身。五炁足，温养舍利子，舍利子之光射于目，为五炁朝元。此五炁聚，是五脏六腑之炁聚。学者不可以道听途说，三花聚顶非是金花、银花、红花三花，实则是精、炁、神聚于顶内。恍恍惚惚，金光发献，为三花聚顶。

Question: The medical books say the five organs and six viscera all have breath flowing through them; is this the breath of the five breaths returning to the origin?

Answer: The breath of the five organs and six viscera is the breath that nourishes the Posterior Heaven body, not the Prior Heaven breath of the five breaths returning to the origin. The Prior Heaven breath can nourish life; the Posterior Heaven breath can nourish the body. When the five breaths are sufficient, they warm and nourish the śarīra; when the light of the śarīra shines into the eyes, that is the five breaths returning to the origin. This gathering of the five breaths is the gathering of the breaths of the five organs and six viscera. Students must not believe hearsay: the three flowers gathering at the summit is not three flowers, a gold flower, a silver flower and a red flower; in fact it is essence, breath and spirit gathered within the crown; when golden light appears in the dim and indistinct, that is the three flowers gathering at the summit.

§5
Original (Chinese)

问曰：何为六景？

答曰：六景现前，眼有金光，脑后鹫鸣，右耳龙吟，左耳虎啸，丹田火炽，身涌鼻搐，马阴藏相，此是身中六景出现，乃是舍利子足之象。眼有金光，乃是性光下照丹田，久则性光内发现金光，是精、炁、神光足之力。龙吟者精中真炁足，故作隐隐之声。虎啸者真炁发生之声。脑后鹫鸣者，精、炁、神内之真火力也，非是邪火耳鸣。丹田火炽、两肾汤煎者，皆由精炁满足，内里发生真火。马阴藏相者，即阳物自缩回，如同童子阳物外形，肾囊如核桃皮纹。若淫根略有微动，功尚缺欠，不可止火。故六景为止火之首步。有位弟子自云，小药未采，居然马阴藏相。此为年老精髓枯，阳物常不举，睾丸下坠，证明精不资生，若无口诀，其精不能复生，寿亦难延。应速行炼精化炁口诀，身内精炁生机渐可挽回。晚年修道先论救护延命，再求长生不迟。莫谓吾老矣不能有成，就是八十岁，能修得与少年人一样，甚至要比少年人多一层好处，只恐老年人不立志耳。历观古之成道者，大半在年老之时，如汉钟离、刘海蟾、吕洞宾均在老年修道，修一位成一位。盖年老之人大都阅尽世情，把名利恩爱已看透了，荣华富贵不再想了，心中空空洞洞，早有出世之想，一经名师指点，便专心修道，故修道皆成道，不比少年人，易进易退，这正是老年人之好处。

Question: What are the six scenes?

Answer: The six scenes appearing before you: golden light in the eyes, the cry of a vulture behind the head (like the call of a vulture), the dragon’s chant in the right ear, the tiger’s roar in the left ear, fire blazing in the elixir field, surging in the body and twitching of the nose (the body surging, the nose twitching), and the horse-like retraction — these are the six scenes appearing in the body, the scene of the śarīra being sufficient. Golden light in the eyes is the light of inner nature shining down on the elixir field; in time golden light appears within the light of inner nature — it is the power of the light of essence, breath and spirit being sufficient. The dragon’s chant is the true breath within essence being sufficient, so that it gives forth a faint sound. The tiger’s roar is the sound of the true breath arising. The cry of a vulture behind the head is the power of the true fire within essence, breath and spirit, not ringing in the ears caused by deviant fire. Fire blazing in the elixir field and the two kidneys simmering as in hot water both come from essence and breath being full, so that true fire arises within the body. The horse-like retraction is the member retracting by itself, like the outer form of a boy’s member, the scrotum wrinkled like a walnut shell. If the root of desire still stirs slightly, the practice is still lacking, and the fire must not be stopped. So the six scenes are the first step toward stopping the fire.

A disciple once said of himself that the lesser medicine had not yet been gathered, yet he already had the horse-like retraction. This is old age, the essence and marrow dried up, the member often failing to rise and the testicles hanging low — proof that the essence can no longer be produced; without the formula, his essence cannot be reborn and his life is hard to lengthen. He should quickly practice the formula of refining essence into breath, and only then can the mechanism of life of the essence and breath in his body be gradually retrieved. In cultivating the Way in old age, first speak of rescuing and prolonging life; seeking long life afterwards is not too late. Do not say “I am old and can achieve nothing” — even at eighty one can cultivate to be like a young person, and even have one advantage more than the young; the only fear is that the old do not set their will to it. Looking over those who attained the Way in ancient times, most did so in old age; Han Zhongli, Liu Haichan and Lü Dongbin all cultivated the Way in old age, and every one who cultivated succeeded. For the old have mostly seen all there is to see of the world, have seen through fame, profit, love and affection, no longer think of glory and wealth, their hearts empty and hollow, long thinking of leaving the world; once a famous teacher points the way, they devote themselves wholly to cultivating the Way, and so all who cultivate attain it; unlike the young, who advance easily and retreat easily — this is precisely the advantage of the old.

Question: In refining these twelve steps, what should one do after the golden light has appeared once?

Answer: All day long let the two eyes look down at the elixir field; the opening of inner nature (the Ancestral Opening) gives forth lightning; shine it gently inward and guard it softly and carefully; after a further long stillness, golden light is born within the lightning — this is “the yang light appearing once,” and one should prepare “the method, the means, the companion and the place.” The “method” is the ritual implements: one shaped like a steamed bun, wrapped in cotton wadding to make a soft seat, pressed against the grain path (the anus); above, a “mulainian” (a wooden implement, see Step Thirteen) plugging the nostrils tightly, shaped like a rod nose-plug (see the safety note). The “means” is money, for the daily food of the Daoist companion. The “companion” is fellow disciples of one heart and mind. The “place” is a secluded spot; a mountain temple not far from a town is most suitable. Only when all four are ready does one dare to go into the mountains to refine the great work; if one of the four is lacking, one must on no account use the work of stopping the fire, but keep gathering the lesser medicine, staying in the world to lengthen one’s years and waiting for the right time to complete the great matter.

问曰：请老师将止火之法诀传出，日后如有法、财、侣、地齐备者，俾可安心修炼。

答曰：前之阳光一现，便当法、财、侣、地具备，入山静养，不离性光命光合一。养之日久，忽然金光明亮，名曰阳光二现，当用止火功采药。最末后一次与前大不相同。止火者，是不行吸呼之气也，一行一止皆以心神意代之，由督而升，由任而降，每日时刻不可离火，离则炁冷，日日凝照，即是温养。道侣侍候，食水调和，久之必生魔，眼内或现天堂美景，或现地狱恶像，或真或幻，愈出愈奇，任他千变万化，总以收心为主，法曰：见如不见，听若未闻，一心内守，目视正中，用第七步子、卯、午、酉法诀，一切魔障立即全消。要知魔障源头，乃是脏腑馀阴所致，即明阳气微小，阴气壮大，故将眼一转，阳气入内，而魔障化为乌有。究其原因，若真心邪念一动，阳气立化阴气，阳光无三现之希望，大药仍不足，不为纯阳之体，即不能过关。昔年我师了空曰：阳光一现，预备法、财、侣、地；阳光二现，急止火采药；阳光三现，采大药服食过关。炼炁化神，超凡入圣，皆以过大关服食为起首。

Question: Please, teacher, transmit the formula for stopping the fire, so that in future anyone who has the method, the means, the companion and the place ready can cultivate with peace of mind.

Answer: When the yang light appeared once, as above, one should have prepared the method, the means, the companion and the place, gone into the mountains to nourish in stillness, never departing from the union of the light of inner nature and the light of life. When this has been nourished for a long time, suddenly the golden light becomes bright; this is called “the yang light appearing twice,” and one should use the work of stopping the fire to gather the medicine. This last time is very different from before: stopping the fire means not moving the breath of breathing; each movement and each stop is replaced by the heart, spirit and intention, rising by the Governing Vessel and descending by the Conception Vessel; every day at every moment one must not leave the fire, for if one leaves it, the breath grows cold; concentrating the spirit and shining on it day after day is warming and nourishing. The Daoist companion waits at one’s side, with food and drink adjusted. In time “demons” are sure to arise: in the eyes there may appear the beauties of paradise or the horrors of hell, true or illusory, ever more strange; let them change in a thousand ways — always take gathering in the heart as the main thing. The method: “see as if not seeing, hear as if not hearing,” guard within with a single heart, the eyes looking at the center, and use the zi, mao, wu, you formula of Step Seven (turning the eyes to draw in the light), and all demonic obstructions vanish at once. Know that the source of demonic obstructions is the remaining yin of the organs, which also shows that the yang breath is slight and the yin breath strong; so with one turn of the eyes, the yang breath enters the body and the demonic obstructions turn to nothing. Looking into the cause: if a true deviant thought of the heart stirs, the yang breath at once turns into yin breath, the yang light has no hope of appearing three times, the Great Medicine is still insufficient, a body of pure yang is not formed, and one cannot pass the gates. Long ago my teacher Liaokong said: “When the yang light appears once, prepare the method, the means, the companion and the place; when it appears twice, quickly stop the fire and gather the medicine; when it appears three times, gather the Great Medicine, ingest it and pass the gates.” Refining breath into spirit and transcending the ordinary to enter the holy both begin with passing the great pass and ingesting.

问曰：何为阳光三现？

答曰：止火之后，若不知前采药法诀，阳光绝不三现。得真传者知采法，止火景到后，丹田内真阳之炁发生舍利子之光，祖窍内之性光因凝神集于脐下，炁穴内之阳光上达丽于目前，故祖窍性光与脐下命光，二光合一，终日当觉由目至脐一路皆是电光，其色红黄，二光比如花内雌雄二蕊，二蕊一合花即成胎结果（图27），即是采大药景现。每日将此二光温养于内，二炁光足，自然合一，永定不散，有华光而无形，悬于太空中。学者至此，口鼻无气，六脉皆停，方为真命实足，万株归于一本，五炁朝元，即是采药过关之时。《慧命经》集说云：或放白光，或放金光，本性有所见，当求明师指点收光之法，如若不收其光，则驰散矣，错过其机，其光再无有也。收光法曰：二目子、卯、午、酉一转，将光收回，速用采大药六根震动口诀。无此口诀，舍利子不能升动，大药过不了关。学者已到破而补圆，百尺竿头，再进一步可矣！

Question: What is “the yang light appearing three times”?

Answer: After stopping the fire, if one does not know the formula for gathering the medicine above, the yang light will certainly not appear three times. One who has received the true transmission knows the method of gathering: after the scene of stopping the fire has come, the breath of the true yang within the elixir field gives forth the light of the śarīra; the light of inner nature within the Ancestral Opening, because the spirit is concentrated below the navel, and the yang light within the Cavity of Breath, rising up to reach and attach before the eyes — so the light of inner nature of the Ancestral Opening and the light of life below the navel, the two lights unite into one, and all day long one feels lightning all the way from the eyes to the navel, reddish yellow in color. These two lights are like the female and male stamens of a flower: when the two stamens unite, the flower forms an embryo and bears fruit (Figure 28; the original text says “Figure 27,” see the Translator’s Notes) — this is the scene of gathering the Great Medicine appearing. Every day warm and nourish these two lights within the body; when the light of the two breaths is sufficient, they naturally unite into one and settle forever without scattering, radiant yet formless, hanging in the great void. When students reach this point, there is no breath in mouth or nose and the six pulses all stop; only then is true life truly full, ten thousand stems returned to one root, the five breaths returned to the origin — this is the time to gather the medicine and pass the gates. The “Collected Sayings” of the Book of Wisdom and Life says: whether it emits white light or golden light, it is one’s original nature showing itself; one should seek an enlightened teacher to point out the method of drawing in the light; if one does not draw in this light, it races off and scatters, the moment is missed, and this light never comes again. The method of drawing in the light is: turn the two eyes one circle through zi, mao, wu and you to draw the light back, and quickly use the formula of “the six roots quaking” for gathering the Great Medicine. Without this formula, the śarīra cannot rise and move, and the Great Medicine cannot pass the gates. Students who have reached the point of “broken and made whole again” (a broken body restored to completeness) need only advance one more step from the top of the hundred-foot pole!

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§8
Original note (Chinese)

【注释】此步功与前十一步功同为温养舍利子。温养之法，主乎至静，两目合并下照丹田，勿忘勿助，把性光与命光连成一片，五炁朝元，三花聚顶，炼成一粒赤金丹，为下一步功大药服食过关打好基础。

练到迄此为止的十二步功法，已能延年益寿。古人认为这是“采小药住世延年，等待时机以了大事”。为什么说要等待时机？因为若要续练第十三步大药过关之功，有许多条件和限制，并非容易做到。千峰老人就认为，法、财、地、侣齐备，才能续练第十三步功。

六景出现是舍利子充足的标志。六景是：一、眼前出现金光，关于此点，下面再加叙述；二、脑后鹫鸣，像奏仙乐一样；三、左耳龙吟，右耳虎啸，龙吟像隐隐气笛之声，虎啸像呼呼的风声；四、丹田火炽，两肾汤煎；五、身涌鼻搐；六、马阴藏相，阳物缩如儿童之“朘”。此外，还有珠网罩面，浑身奇痒等征兆。

伍冲虚著《仙佛合宗》一书云：阳光二现止火景到，阳光二现乃采大药之时；阳光发现恍如眉间掣电，虚室生白。其说不详，学者仍难捉摸按之行功。千峰老人则说得较明白具体，从慧光到蟾光之转变，首先是在慧光白光中发现金光，此为阳光一现，是舍利子之苗，是精炁、神足的表现。在未现金光之前，先有眉间掣电之景，这就是有时（不一定在练功中）眼前发现刷的闪过一道紫金光芒，道家叫作“金机飞电”。慧光是性光，金光是命光，性光来自祖窍；因凝神集于脐下，金光来自下田炁穴，上达丽于目前。此两光合一，由目至脐一路皆是电光金光，此为阳光二现三现。阳光一现，便当备法，财、地、侣。

前第八步功蛰藏炁穴一章注释中曾言及神与炁、日与月相交出现的一片祥和境界，本步功中这种境界在光的亮度和色泽上当有不同。二光合一，混然粹然，胸中一片太和之炁现放目前，内外一体，天地同春，真息绵绵，真炁源源而生，舍利子（内丹）日臻满足，此是正功。

《慧命经》说：“不得慧命漏尽，不能了道，直入于如来之太空，而慧命漏尽不得风火炼法，不能和合凝集而成大道”。慧命者，先天真炁也；漏尽者，无漏也，漏尽通也。得此二者，即得舍利子和马阴藏相，而性如虚空；风火漏尽并用，自然和合凝集而成大道。行动至此，此段话可作结语。

This step and Step Eleven before it are both about warming and nourishing the śarīra. The method of warming and nourishing takes utmost stillness as the main thing: join the two eyes and shine down on the elixir field, neither forgetting nor helping, linking the light of inner nature and the light of life into one, the five breaths returning to the origin, the three flowers gathering at the summit, refining one grain of “red-gold elixir,” laying a good foundation for the next step, ingesting the Great Medicine and passing the gates.

Having practiced up to these twelve steps, one can already lengthen one’s years and increase one’s life. The ancients considered this “gathering the lesser medicine to stay in the world and lengthen one’s years, waiting for the right time to complete the great matter.” Why speak of waiting for the right time? Because to go on to practice the work of Step Thirteen, the Great Medicine passing the gates, there are many conditions and limitations that are not easy to meet. The Old Man of Qianfeng held that only when the method, the means, the place and the companion are all ready can one go on to practice Step Thirteen.

The appearance of the six scenes is the sign that the śarīra is full. The six scenes are: first, golden light appearing before the eyes (more on this below); second, the cry of a vulture behind the head, as if immortal music were playing; third, the dragon’s chant in the left ear and the tiger’s roar in the right ear — the dragon’s chant like a faint steam whistle, the tiger’s roar like the whooshing of wind; fourth, fire blazing in the elixir field, the two kidneys simmering as in hot water; fifth, surging in the body and twitching of the nose; sixth, the horse-like retraction, the member retracted like a child’s “zui.” Besides these there are signs such as the face as if covered by a net of pearls and an extraordinary itching all over the body.

Wu Chongxu’s The Common Origin of Immortals and Buddhas says: when the yang light appears twice, the scene of stopping the fire has come; the yang light appearing twice is the time to gather the Great Medicine; when the yang light appears, it is like lightning flashing between the eyebrows, the empty room producing whiteness. His account is not detailed, and students still find it hard to grasp and practice by. The Old Man of Qianfeng puts it more clearly and concretely: in the change from the light of wisdom to the toad light, first golden light appears within the white light of the light of wisdom; this is “the yang light appearing once,” the sprout of the śarīra, a sign that essence, breath and spirit are sufficient. Before golden light appears, there is first the scene of “lightning flashing between the eyebrows,” that is, at times (not necessarily during practice) a purple-gold ray “flashes” past before the eyes, which the Daoists call “the golden mechanism flying like lightning.” The light of wisdom is the light of inner nature, the golden light is the light of life: the light of inner nature comes from the Ancestral Opening; because the spirit is concentrated below the navel, the golden light comes from the Cavity of Breath of the lower elixir field and rises to reach and attach before the eyes. When these two lights unite, there is lightning and golden light all the way from the eyes to the navel — this is the yang light appearing twice and three times. When the yang light appears once, one should prepare the method, the means, the place and the companion.

In the note to the chapter “Hibernating in the Cavity of Breath” of Step Eight above, we spoke of the realm of auspicious harmony that appears when spirit and breath, sun and moon meet; in this step, that realm should differ in the brightness and color of its light. The two lights unite, merged and pure; a breath of great harmony in the chest appears before the eyes, inner and outer one body, heaven and earth sharing the same spring, the True Breath soft and continuous, true breath born in an endless stream, the śarīra (the inner elixir) coming day by day to fullness — this is the right practice.

The Book of Wisdom and Life says: “Without wisdom-life exhausting its leaks, one cannot complete the Way and enter directly into the great void of the Tathāgata; and with wisdom-life having exhausted its leaks, without the method of refining with wind and fire, one cannot unite, condense and gather to form the great Way.” Wisdom-life is the Prior Heaven true breath; exhausting leaks is having no leaks, the power of the exhaustion of leaks. Obtaining these two, one obtains the śarīra and the horse-like retraction, and inner nature is like empty space; using wind and fire together with the exhaustion of leaks, one naturally unites, condenses and gathers to form the great Way. When practice reaches this point, these words can serve as a conclusion.

Key Terms

  • Warming and nourishing: after the śarīra is first formed, letting it gestate and grow through stillness, with the True Intention of the two eyes shining down on the elixir field.
  • Neither forgetting nor helping: not forgetting (a single undivided heart, never leaving it for a moment), not helping (not clinging, not applying force); from the Mencius.
  • Nature of breath and constitution, nature endowed by heaven: habits formed after birth and the original nature (terms of the Song Confucians).
  • Seven emotions: joy, anger, grief, fear, love, hatred and desire, each harming one organ or faculty.
  • Ten harms: long walking harms the sinews, long standing harms the bones, long sitting harms the blood, long sleeping harms the vessels, long pleasure harms the essence, long looking harms the spirit, long talking harms the breath, long thinking harms the spleen, eating to fullness harms the heart, long indulgence in sex harms life.
  • Nine breaths: anger makes the breath rise, fear makes it sink, joy makes it slacken, sorrow makes it dwindle, fright makes it disordered, thought makes it knot, toil makes it wear out, cold makes it contract, heat makes it leak (from the Basic Questions of the Yellow Emperor’s Inner Classic).
  • Eight ounces, half a pound: the light of wisdom (essence rising to meet inner nature) and the golden light (breath descending to meet life) are each one half; together they make “one pound,” the śarīra.
  • The five breaths return to the origin: the ten breaths of the five organs and five viscera unite into one, and golden light appears before the eyes.
  • The three flowers gather at the summit: essence, breath and spirit gather within the crown, and golden light appears in the dim and indistinct.
  • Six scenes: golden light in the eyes, the cry of a vulture behind the head, the dragon’s chant and tiger’s roar in the ears, fire blazing in the elixir field (the two kidneys simmering as in hot water), surging in the body and twitching of the nose, the horse-like retraction; the first sign for stopping the fire.
  • Method, means, companion, place: the four conditions for cultivating the great work — ritual implements, money, a Daoist companion, a place for cultivation.
  • The yang light appearing once, twice, three times: once, prepare the method, means, companion and place; twice, stop the fire and gather the medicine; three times, gather the Great Medicine, ingest it and pass the gates.
  • Stopping the fire: not moving the breathing of mouth and nose, replacing fire phasing with heart, spirit and intention, concentrating and shining day after day to warm and nourish.
  • Broken and made whole again: a broken body restored to completeness.

Putting It into Practice

  1. Twelve steps are enough: both Zhao and Qiu Ling say that practicing up to this step “can surely lengthen one’s years and increase one’s life”; the conditions from Step Thirteen on are demanding, and ordinary people need not force them.
  2. Neither forgetting nor helping: this is the heart method that recurs throughout the book — don’t drop it, and don’t force it.
  3. Guard against the seven emotions, ten harms and nine breaths: an even temper and a measured daily life are more basic than any practice method.
  4. The old are even better suited to cultivate: Zhao particularly encourages the old to set their will on quiet cultivation; having seen through fame and profit, with an undistracted heart, is precisely their advantage.
  5. Don’t be alarmed by demons: if beautiful or horrible scenes appear in stillness, “see as if not seeing, hear as if not hearing”; if they appear repeatedly and strongly, stop practicing first and seek help.
  6. Don’t plug the nose or hold the breath: the ritual implements, nose plugs, stopping the fire without breathing and so on are records of ancient methods; people today should not follow them.

Translator's Notes

  1. In the original, this step has one formula, two figures, one passage of explanation and seven questions and answers; we number them §1–§8.
  2. “Keeping even a little thought or worry loses the empty, numinous substance — this is ‘forgetting’”: from the sense, keeping thoughts and worries would seem to belong to “helping”; so in the original, reproduced as is.
  3. “Much grief harms the lungs … much desire harms the spleen” and “long pleasure harms the essence” among the “ten harms”: slightly different from “long listening harms the essence” in Step Eleven; each is translated as in the original.
  4. “已土 turns to the left”: 已 should be 己; reproduced as is, and translated as “ji earth.”
  5. “The breath of the spleen belongs to yang”: Chinese medicine generally assigns the spleen to yin (as an organ) and the stomach to yang (as a viscus); so in the original, reproduced as is.
  6. “Above, a 木来年 plugging the nostrils tightly”: mulainian is the name of an implement for plugging the nostrils; see Figure 29 in Step Thirteen.
  7. “When the two stamens unite, the flower forms an embryo and bears fruit (Figure 27)”: the original text says “Figure 27,” but the illustration referred to here is labeled “Figure 28” in the original; reproduced as is.
  8. Zhao says “the dragon’s chant in the right ear, the tiger’s roar in the left ear,” and the questions and answers of Step Eleven also put the dragon’s chant in the right ear; Qiu Ling’s note has “the dragon’s chant in the left ear, the tiger’s roar in the right ear”; each is translated as in the original.
  9. “At the beginning, when the body received breath”: a common veiled expression in the elixir classics, referring to where the Prior Heaven breath resides when a person is first conceived, that is, the lower elixir field.
  10. The text in Figure 28 is horizontal in the original, read from right to left; this site sets it from left to right.

The Sixteen Steps of Method Formulas · 第六步 采取内药

Step Six · Gathering the Inner Medicine

The formula of Step Six, gathering the inner medicine — move sun and moon to turn the Dharma Wheel; revolving left and turning right, the breath returns to the center

About This Chapter

Step Four used the “zi-wu circuit” (the Lesser Celestial Circuit, rising behind and descending in front) to gather the outer medicine; this step uses the “mao-you circuit” — the two eyes revolving left and turning right — to transform the outer medicine in the furnace into true breath, so that a “glorious light” appears before the eyes; this is called “gathering the inner medicine.”

  1. The method: with the eyes, shine down on the navel of Kun (the area of the navel), then shine up from the left to the summit of Qian (the crown), and from the right back down to the navel of Kun; returning to the center is one degree; thirty-six turns like this are advancing the yang fire. Then open the eyes and turn twenty-four times in the opposite direction: this is withdrawing the yin tally.
  2. Outer union and inner union: the one rising and one descending of the zi-wu circuit is the “outer union,” which keeps the body free of illness; the one lifting and one settling of the mao-you circuit is the “inner union,” which preserves life (the original breath). Gathering only the outer medicine without drawing in the inner medicine is like “a cart without wheels, a boat without a rudder”; medicine left in the furnace and not transformed into breath by fire will leak away sooner or later.
  3. Metal and wood joined: the left eye belongs to the east and is the nature of wood; the right eye belongs to the west and is the nature of metal; as the two eyes turn, metal and wood are joined, “life returns to inner nature,” and inner nature and life become one.
  4. The principle of ablution: zi (the Opening of Life and Death), wu (the Muddy Pellet), mao (the Spine-Straddling Pass) and you (the Crimson Palace) are the positions of birth and death of the five phases; here one pauses the breath and steams them with the intention, so that the original essence quickly transforms into breath.
  5. A review of the six steps: Zhao sums up the first six steps as one continuous whole: guarding the Ancestral Opening to refine inner nature → refining the union of inner nature and life → opening the eight vessels → refining essence to replenish inner nature → gathering the five phases → the mao-you circuit, refining with fire to transform essence.

Qiu Ling’s note adds: in the elixir classics generally, the mao-you circuit is a closing method for drawing in the Great Medicine, whereas Zhao places it after the Lesser Celestial Circuit as a way of gathering the inner medicine; he also quotes the detailed description of the route and inner scenery of the mao-you circuit in Perfected Yin’s Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall, stressing that “the mechanism lies in the eyes” and that one must “move in stillness, not push in a hurry.”

Safety note (translator): This step relies entirely on turning the two eyes; be sure to do it gently and slowly — it is “shining,” not forcefully rolling the eyeballs; beginners can start with fewer circles, with no need to make up the full counts of thirty-six and twenty-four at once. It is not suitable for people with glaucoma, high myopia, retinal disease or vertigo. If you feel swelling or pain in the eyes, dizziness or nausea, stop at once and sit quietly with the eyes closed to rest.

Figure 19 · The Mao-You Circuit

In the figure a person sits upright cross-legged, both hands raised, each holding up a circle: the left hand holds the “moon,” the right hand the “sun.” Around the moon are marked “wu one” (top), “mao two” (right), “zi three” (bottom, on the forearm) and “you four” (left); around the sun, “zi one” (bottom, on the forearm), “mao two” (right), “wu three” (top) and “you four” (left). A line runs down from the chest to the furnace in the belly. Sun and moon are the two eyes; the four numbers mark the order in which the eyes revolve through the four cardinal positions zi, mao, wu and you — one side in the order wu, mao, zi, you, the other in the order zi, mao, wu, you: precisely the two directions, one advancing and one withdrawing.

Formula
Original (Chinese)

行动日月转法轮，

左旋右转炁归中。

三十六转进阳火，

二十四旋退阴符。

Turn sun and moon (the two eyes) to turn the Dharma Wheel; revolving left and turning right, let the breath return to the central palace. Turning thirty-six circles is advancing the yang fire; revolving twenty-four circles is withdrawing the yin tally.

§1
Original (Chinese)

此法是采内药也。左旋右转，一起一伏，用目下照，从左上照乾顶，再从右下照坤脐，至中心为一度。如此三十六转是为进阳火。开关从午、卯，子、酉转一回，如此二十四转为退阴符。

This method is gathering the inner medicine. Revolving left and turning right, lifting and settling: shine downward with the eyes, then shine up from the left to the summit of Qian (the crown), then down from the right to the navel of Kun (the area of the navel), and back to the center — that is one degree (one circle). Turning thirty-six circles like this is advancing the yang fire. Then “open the pass” (open the eyes) and turn one circle through wu, mao, zi, you; turning twenty-four circles like this is withdrawing the yin tally.

§2
Original (Chinese)

前第四步口诀言采外药，纯粹还精补脑真功，后升为进阳火，又为入火三十六数。前降为退阴符，又为降火二十四数。此是一吸一呼，神炁同时行住于赤黄两道之意。神是眼睛转动，炁是送精真炁转动。同行同住谓之采外药。外药保身无病，内药保命。命者元炁也。此卷所言乃是卯酉周天之火炼内药之法也，亦为内交媾。采足外药之后，彼时因风火相激，真炁上升于脑，于是眼睛左旋右转，一起一伏，脑髓充满发涨，顿觉荣光发现，有形无相采内药，俗名炼丹是也。内药之荣光，即为玄关。昔我师曰：“阴阳转动和脑髓，发长精神玄关出。”陈泥丸云：“真阴真阳是真道，只在眼前何远讨。”《火候歌》云：“欲透玄玄须谨独，谨独工夫机在目”。日月转动合阴阳，脑髓总部发荣光。只晓乾坤交媾采外药，不知卯酉周天收内药，如有车而无轮，有舟而无舵，欲望远载岂可得乎？且药归炉，非行卯酉周天火候，不能将精炁化为荣光，以为金丹之苗。不然，任外药在炉，不以火化炁，便有下漏。漏则精囊虚空。例如茶盅为炉，精炁为水，若不加火，水不化炁而上升，反而加水，盛满茶盅，超过最高部位，不能容纳，势必溢出无疑。此精满足，迟早仍复自泄之铁证。故卯酉周天阳火、阴符之功用大矣。盖眼睛部位虽分两边，其根则一，总得眼睛转动，才有斗柄之机斡旋。左眼属东为木性，右眼属西为金性，水性转西爱金，金性转东恋木，此为金木交并，脑气筋因感受混合，遂发涨而归一，引入䯕髓内，将采得外药化为保命真炁，收归我有，即是命归性真功，性命合而为一，才是性命双修之大法。兹再以理喻之，假设母鸡孵雏，蛋壳为身，黄为性，迨至雏鸡孵成，蛋壳弃地，倘在未成雏以前，偶遭损伤，雏鸡决难孵成。故修道者保身如宝玉一般，深知无身不成道。今偏重性轻视命，以为当然，是皆知其一，不知其二也。神炁即性命，性命即心肾，心肾即铅汞，铅汞即龙虎，龙虎即坎离，坎离即鼎炉，诸般名词，千名万喻，不出性命二字。

The formula of Step Four above speaks of gathering the outer medicine, which is purely the true practice of returning the essence to replenish the brain: rising behind is advancing the yang fire, and also the count of thirty-six of “entering fire”; descending in front is withdrawing the yin tally, and also the count of twenty-four of “descending fire.” This means that with one inhalation and one exhalation, spirit and breath move and stay together on the two roads of the “celestial equator and the ecliptic” (metaphors for the Governing and Conception vessels): the spirit is the turning of the eyes; the breath is the turning of the true breath that sends the essence along; spirit and breath moving and staying together is called gathering the outer medicine. The outer medicine keeps the body free of illness; the inner medicine preserves life — and life is the original breath.

What this chapter speaks of is the method of refining the inner medicine with the fire of the mao-you circuit, also called the “inner union.” Once the outer medicine has been gathered to the full, the wind (the breath) and the fire (the spirit) stir each other and the true breath rises to the brain; then the eyes revolve left and turn right, lifting and settling, the brain marrow fills and swells, and suddenly one feels a “glorious light” appear — this is gathering the inner medicine, with form but no fixed appearance, commonly called “refining the elixir.” The glorious light of the inner medicine is the Mysterious Pass. Long ago my teacher said: “Yin and yang turning harmonize the brain marrow; vitality grows and the Mysterious Pass comes forth.” (The two eyes, yin and yang, turn and harmonize the brain marrow; vitality grows, and the Mysterious Pass appears.) Chen Niwan (Chen Nan) said: “True yin and true yang are the true Way; it is right before your eyes — why seek it far away?” The Song of Fire Phasing says: “To penetrate the mystery of mysteries you must be watchful when alone; the mechanism of watchfulness when alone lies in the eyes.” (To see through the mystery upon mystery, you must keep the practice of watchfulness in solitude; the key to that practice lies in the eyes.) When sun and moon (the two eyes) turn, yin and yang unite, and the headquarters of the brain marrow emits a glorious light.

To know only the union of Qian and Kun (the zi-wu circuit) for gathering the outer medicine, and not to know the mao-you circuit for drawing in the inner medicine, is like having a cart without wheels or a boat without a rudder — how could one travel far? Moreover, once the medicine has returned to the furnace, if the fire phasing of the mao-you circuit is not carried out, the essence and breath cannot be transformed into the glorious light that is the first sprout of the Golden Elixir. Otherwise, if the outer medicine is left in the furnace and not transformed into breath by fire, it will leak downward; once it leaks, the seminal vesicles are empty. It is like a teacup that is the furnace, with the essence and breath as the water: if no fire is applied, the water does not turn to vapor and rise, and if instead one keeps adding water until the cup is full and beyond the brim, it cannot hold it and is sure to overflow. This is iron proof that, once full, the essence will sooner or later leak out by itself. So the function of the yang fire and yin tally of the mao-you circuit is very great.

Although the eyes are placed on two sides, their root is one; only when the eyes turn is there the mechanism of rotation of the “Dipper’s handle” (the handle of the Northern Dipper, a metaphor for the mechanism that presides over the rotation). The left eye belongs to the east and is the nature of wood; the right eye belongs to the west and is the nature of metal. The nature of wood (the original has “the nature of water,” see the Translator’s Notes) turns to the west and loves metal; the nature of metal turns to the east and longs for wood — this is “metal and wood joined.” The brain-breath sinews (the nerves), affected, merge, then swell and return to one, and are drawn into the brain marrow, transforming the outer medicine that has been gathered into the true breath that preserves life and taking it into my possession — this is the true practice of “life returning to inner nature”; when inner nature and life unite into one, that is the great method of the dual cultivation of nature and life.

Let me give another comparison: suppose a hen is hatching a chick; the eggshell is the body, the yolk is inner nature; only when the chick has hatched is the shell discarded on the ground. If it is accidentally damaged before the chick has hatched, the chick will certainly not hatch. So those who cultivate the Way should protect the body as they would protect precious jade, knowing deeply that without the body the Way cannot be attained. Nowadays some people favor inner nature and slight life, and think it only natural; all of them know the one but not the other. Spirit and breath are inner nature and life; inner nature and life are the heart and kidneys; the heart and kidneys are lead and mercury; lead and mercury are the dragon and tiger; the dragon and tiger are Kan and Li; Kan and Li are the tripod and furnace — all these names, a thousand names and ten thousand metaphors, do not go beyond the two words “inner nature and life.”

§3
Original (Chinese)

余有诗曰：“怀抱全诀觅知音，日月神火转法轮，吸升呼降精化炁，阳火阴符保命根”。本书功法虽曰有步骤，实则一贯而下：首步守祖窍，由静炼性；第二炼性命和合；第三开通八脉却病，亦壮性命；第四炼精补性，实是性命同修之法诀；第五炼性和命、五行攒簇之功；第六卯酉周天，炼火化精之功。以上乃是修性炼命，性命合一，成为真阳之精，真精化炁成为这个〇，即先天真一之性光也。若非阴阳和合，不能保命。只是闭目静坐，目前有光，纯是阴神之性，修它何用？任汝阅看经书万卷，不会精化炁法诀，绝难保其长生。丹经云：“顺成人逆回仙，就在中间颠倒颠”。若回光返照将此真炁炼成舍利子如〇，舍利子足，六根振动可采大药。

I have a poem: “Holding the whole formula, I seek a kindred spirit; the spirit fire of sun and moon turns the Dharma Wheel; inhaling it rises, exhaling it descends, essence turns to breath; the yang fire and yin tally guard the root of life.” (I carry the whole formula, seeking someone who understands: turning the Dharma Wheel of the spirit fire of sun and moon, rising on the inhalation and descending on the exhalation, so that essence turns into breath; guarding the root of life with the yang fire and yin tally.) Although the practice in this book is said to be divided into steps, in fact it runs through as one: the first step is guarding the Ancestral Opening, refining inner nature through stillness; the second step is refining the union of inner nature and life; the third step is opening the eight vessels to drive out illness, which also strengthens inner nature and life; the fourth step is refining essence to replenish inner nature, which is in fact a formula for cultivating inner nature and life together; the fifth step is the work of refining inner nature and life and gathering the five phases; the sixth step, the mao-you circuit, is the work of refining with fire to transform essence. All of the above is cultivating inner nature and refining life, inner nature and life uniting into one to become the essence of true yang; when the true essence transforms into breath, it becomes this one “〇,” which is the light of inner nature of the Prior Heaven true One. Without the union of yin and yang, life cannot be preserved. Merely sitting quietly with the eyes closed and seeing light before the eyes is purely the inner nature of the “yin spirit” — what use is cultivating that? Even if you read ten thousand scriptures, without the formula for transforming essence into breath it is very hard to preserve long life. The elixir classics say: “Going with the current, one becomes a person; turning back, one becomes an immortal; it lies in the reversal in between.” If you turn the light around to shine back and refine this true breath into a śarīra like “〇,” then when the śarīra is full and the six roots quake, you can gather the Great Medicine.

§4
Original (Chinese)

问曰：对卯酉周天火候已悉用法，但子、午、卯、酉沐浴之真理不明，请师示知。

答曰：子为生死窍，午为泥丸宫，卯为夹脊，酉为绛宫。此四位适当五行生死之位，且通五脏六腑，必停其息，以意薰蒸之，脏腑方得滋润，否则易生病患，是以丹经以沐浴为火候之秘机，为炼精炼炁之要法。丹诀以人生死大事之机在沐浴，故引以为喻也。子、午、卯、酉为五行四正之位，子在正北，午在正南、卯在正东、酉在正西，《入药镜》所谓看四正是也。火生于寅，沐浴在卯，死在酉。水生于申，沐浴在酉，死在卯。金生于巳，沐浴在午，死在子。木生于亥，沐浴在子，死在午。是沐浴为死而不动之位，当洗心涤虑，以真炁薰蒸，使二炁不动，以阴符合其火候之机，以臻其薰蒸之效。故沐浴之用，惟在绵密寂照，使元精速于化炁而已。

Question: I already know how to use the fire phasing of the mao-you circuit; but I still do not understand the true principle of the “ablutions” at zi, wu, mao and you. Please instruct me, teacher.

Answer: Zi is the Opening of Life and Death (the perineum), wu is the Muddy Pellet palace (the crown), mao is the Spine-Straddling Pass, you is the Crimson Palace (below the heart). These four positions are precisely the positions of birth and death of the five phases, and they connect to the five organs and six viscera; one must pause the breath here and steam them with the intention, and only then are the organs moistened; otherwise illness easily arises. That is why the elixir classics treat ablution as the secret mechanism of fire phasing and as the essential method of refining essence and breath. The elixir formulas hold that the mechanism of the great matter of human life and death lies in ablution, and so use it as a metaphor. Zi, wu, mao and you are the “four cardinal” positions of the five phases: zi due north, wu due south, mao due east, you due west — what the Mirror for Compounding the Medicine calls “watching the four cardinal points” is this. Fire is born at yin, has its ablution at mao, dies at you; water is born at shen, has its ablution at you, dies at mao; metal is born at si, has its ablution at wu, dies at zi; wood is born at hai, has its ablution at zi, dies at wu (this is the teaching of the “twelve palaces of growth” of the five phases). So ablution is the position of “dead and unmoving”: one should wash the heart and cleanse the thoughts, steam with the true breath so that the two breaths do not move, and use the yin tally to match the mechanism of fire phasing, achieving the effect of steaming. So the function of ablution lies only in being soft, continuous and close, quietly contemplating and illuminating, so that the original essence quickly transforms into breath — that is all.

Qiu Ling's noteon §1–§4
Original note (Chinese)

【注释】本章虽说是介绍卯酉周天，但叙述过于简单，须在这里作一些说明和补充。

卯酉周天一般作为收取大药的收功法，是纯乎自然升降的天然真火候。《尹真人寥阳殿问答编》谈到卯酉周天时云：“时至发动，寂体以随，中无后天参杂”。赵氏所传丹功，将卯酉周天按次序排在小周天即子午周天之后，作为将所得的外药，化为真炁，并使内药之荣光在眼前呈现，称之为玄关，亦即作为采内药之法。赵氏功法中大药之概念乃外药与内药相合而成，又与一般丹经所说内药即大药的概念不同。本书十六步功法，一、三、五、七、九步是单练，其他各步是紧密相连练的。因此，本功法紧接着第四步口诀采取外药进行。子午周天为外交媾，后上前下，一升一降；卯酉周天为内交媾，左旋右转，一起一伏。两者循环状似璇玑。卯酉周天之法是以真意主宰，下照坤脐（肚脐），良久，徐徐闭目转眼从左上照乾顶（头顶），少停，从右降下坤脐，是为一度。又从坤脐而升上乾顶，又从乾顶而降下坤脐，如此三十六转，是为进阳火。三十六转毕，开关（开眼）以退火，亦下照坤脐，从右上至乾顶，左边放下坤脐，是为一度。如此二十四转，是为退阴符。在以上行功过程中，两眼按子午卯酉正位上下交相送，须向静里行，莫向忙里送，这就是两眼行斗柄斡旋之机。

《尹真人寥阳殿问答编·秘授篇》有一段文字，描绘卯酉周天的运行路线及内景，比较清楚。它说：“用目守住泥丸，下照坤脐。良久，自从气穴中透出火珠一粒，自左边升起，至脐左边，次到绛宫，从绛宫之左，忽折入左胁下，而透入左肩，上左耳根，入左目，到山根，略存一顷，即转右目，从右耳根后，下右肩，绕而前转心之右，下至脐，仍还丹田。”又说，初时入手，未免略略着意，到纯熟地步，便不知其然而然，目到气到，气穴中自然元气升起，如喷泡入于脐轮，后通命门，忽然两肾汤煎，热气徐徐滚上泥丸，此时下而尾闾，中而二十四骨节，都不经历；更有浓液如雀卵，从鹊桥入舌下，徐徐咽入中宫；若尚有阴火之处，则觉微微作痛。此种化景，虽然人各不同，但这是集合了前人行卯酉周天的体验，并由此可知，卯酉周天之功还在于战退身上群阴，使成乾健之体。在这里，两眼之功特别重要，所谓“机在目”，所谓“含光便是长生药”，两眼神光随真气之运行而从下至上，从左至右旋转不离，就能使阳道日长，阴道日消，达到阳纯阴剥的地步。

顶者性之根，脐者命之蒂。卯酉周天功采取药物于脐下，聚火载金于乾顶之上，乾顶即泥丸，《黄庭经》云：“泥丸经过交媾、凝结、抽添，百节皆有神”。又云：“脑神精根字泥丸”。故此功使精炁神起到还精补脑的作用。

Although this chapter is said to present the mao-you circuit, its account is too brief, and some explanation and supplement are needed here.

The mao-you circuit is generally a “closing method” for drawing in the Great Medicine; it is the natural true fire phasing of purely spontaneous rising and descending. Perfected Yin’s Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall, speaking of the mao-you circuit, says: “When the time comes it stirs of itself; the quiet substance moves along with it, with nothing of Posterior Heaven mixed in between.” The elixir practice transmitted by Zhao places the mao-you circuit in sequence after the Lesser Celestial Circuit (the zi-wu circuit), using it to transform the outer medicine obtained into true breath and to make the glorious light of the inner medicine appear before the eyes — which he calls the Mysterious Pass — that is, as a method for gathering the inner medicine. In Zhao’s practice, the concept of the “Great Medicine” is the outer medicine and inner medicine combined, which differs from the concept in the elixir classics generally that “the inner medicine is the Great Medicine.” Of the sixteen steps of practice in this book, Steps One, Three, Five, Seven and Nine are practiced separately, while the other steps are practiced in close succession. Thus this practice follows directly on Step Four, gathering the outer medicine. The zi-wu circuit is the “outer union,” up behind and down in front, one rising and one descending; the mao-you circuit is the “inner union,” revolving left and turning right, one lifting and one settling. The two cycle together, in a shape like the xuanji (the ancient armillary sphere).

The method of the mao-you circuit is presided over by the True Intention: first shine down on the navel of Kun (the navel); after a while, slowly close the eyes and turn them, shining up from the left to the summit of Qian (the crown), pausing briefly, and descending from the right to the navel of Kun — that is one degree. Again rise from the navel of Kun to the summit of Qian, and again descend from the summit of Qian to the navel of Kun; turning thirty-six circles like this is advancing the yang fire. When the thirty-six circles are done, “open the pass” (open the eyes) to withdraw the fire: again first shine down on the navel of Kun, rise from the right to the summit of Qian and let it down from the left to the navel of Kun — that is one degree; turning twenty-four circles like this is withdrawing the yin tally. Throughout the practice above, the two eyes receive and send each other up and down along the cardinal positions zi, wu, mao and you; it must be done in stillness, not pushed along in a hurry — this is the mechanism of the two eyes performing “the turning of the Dipper’s handle.”

A passage in the “Secret Teachings” of Perfected Yin’s Questions and Answers in the Liaoyang Hall describes the route and inner scenery of the mao-you circuit rather clearly. It says: “Keep the eyes on the Muddy Pellet and shine down on the navel of Kun. After a while, a pearl of fire emerges from the Cavity of Breath, rises on the left to the left of the navel, then to the Crimson Palace; from the left of the Crimson Palace it suddenly turns in under the left ribs, passes into the left shoulder, rises to the root of the left ear, enters the left eye, reaches the Mountain Root (between the two eyes), pauses for a moment, then turns to the right eye, goes down behind the root of the right ear to the right shoulder, comes round to the front past the right side of the heart, down to the navel, and returns again to the elixir field.” It also says: when one first begins, one cannot avoid applying a little intention; when one has reached fluency, it happens without one knowing how — where the eyes go, the breath goes; original breath naturally rises in the Cavity of Breath, like bubbles spurting into the navel wheel and passing back to the Gate of Life; suddenly the two kidneys feel as if simmering in hot water, and hot breath slowly rolls up to the Muddy Pellet — at this time it does not pass through the Caudal Funnel below or the twenty-four vertebrae of the spine in between; and there is also thick saliva, like sparrow’s eggs, entering beneath the tongue from the Magpie Bridge (where the tongue meets the palate), to be slowly swallowed into the central palace; if there are still places of yin fire in the body, one will feel a slight pain there. Although such transformations differ from person to person, this gathers the experience of earlier practitioners of the mao-you circuit; from it we can see that the function of the mao-you circuit also lies in driving off the mass of yin in the body and making the body one of “Qian’s strength” (pure yang and vigor). Here the work of the two eyes is especially important — what is meant by “the mechanism lies in the eyes,” and by “holding in the light is the medicine of long life”: the spiritual light of the two eyes follows the movement of the true breath, from bottom to top and left to right, revolving without leaving it, so that the way of yang grows day by day and the way of yin wanes day by day, until yang is pure and yin is stripped away.

The crown is the root of inner nature; the navel is the stem of life. The work of the mao-you circuit gathers the medicine below the navel and gathers the fire to carry the metal up to the summit of Qian — the summit of Qian is the Muddy Pellet. The Scripture of the Yellow Court says: “Through union, condensing, withdrawing and adding at the Muddy Pellet, all hundred joints have their spirits.” It also says: “The spirit of the brain is named Essence Root, styled Muddy Pellet.” That is why this practice enables essence, breath and spirit to return the essence and replenish the brain.

Key Terms

  • Mao-you circuit: the circuit in which the two eyes revolve left and turn right, lifting and settling, also called the “inner union”; contrasted with the zi-wu circuit (rising behind and descending in front, the outer union).
  • Inner medicine: for Zhao, the true breath into which the outer medicine is transformed by the mao-you circuit, appearing as a “glorious light” before the eyes; Qiu Ling’s note points out that Zhao’s “Great Medicine” is the outer and inner medicine combined, unlike the usual “the inner medicine is the Great Medicine” of the elixir classics.
  • Summit of Qian, navel of Kun: the crown and the area of the navel.
  • Opening the pass: opening the eyes (Qiu Ling’s note).
  • Glorious light: the radiance of the inner medicine, which Zhao calls the Mysterious Pass.
  • Metal and wood joined: the left eye belongs to wood (east), the right eye to metal (west); as the two eyes turn, inner nature and feeling unite.
  • Turning of the Dipper’s handle: the rotation of the handle of the Northern Dipper as a metaphor for the eyes presiding over the turning of the circuit.
  • Yin spirit: the “inner nature” of merely sitting quietly and seeing light, without refining essence into breath; Zhao considers it useless.
  • Four cardinal points: zi (north), wu (south), mao (east), you (west); in Zhao’s practice, respectively the Opening of Life and Death, the Muddy Pellet, the Spine-Straddling Pass and the Crimson Palace.
  • Ablution: pausing the breath at the four cardinal positions and steaming them with the intention, so that the original essence quickly transforms into breath.
  • Twelve palaces of growth: twelve stages of the five phases — birth, ablution, death and so on — matched to the twelve earthly branches, as in “fire is born at yin, has its ablution at mao, dies at you.”
  • Xuanji: an ancient instrument for observing the heavens (the armillary sphere), a metaphor for the cycling of the circuit.

Putting It into Practice

  1. First the medicine, then turning the eyes: the mao-you circuit is done after the outer medicine has been gathered to the full in Step Four; without that foundation, merely turning the eyes means nothing.
  2. Shine gently, don’t spin hard: what is used is “shining” — the intention and the light of the eyes go from the area of the navel up the left side to the crown and down the right side back to the navel. Slow, light, even.
  3. The counts can be reduced: thirty-six and twenty-four are the rule; beginners can first do nine circles each or fewer, as long as it is comfortable.
  4. Move in stillness: Qiu Ling quotes “move in stillness, not push in a hurry” — turning in a flurry is worse than not turning at all.
  5. Ablution is pausing and steaming: pause briefly at zi, wu, mao and you, neither advancing nor withdrawing, closely and quietly illuminating.
  6. Don’t chase only “seeing light”: Zhao says plainly that merely seeing light with the eyes closed, without knowing how to transform essence into breath, is the “yin spirit,” and cultivating it is useless.

Translator's Notes

  1. In the original, this step has one formula, one figure, three passages of explanation and one question and answer; we number them §1–§4. The original’s title for this step is “Gathering the Inner Medicine,” and the figure is titled “The Mao-You Circuit.”
  2. “The nature of water turns west and loves metal” (水性转西爱金): the text above says “the left eye belongs to the east and is the nature of wood,” so “the nature of water” should be a mistake for “the nature of wood”; reproduced as is, and translated as “the nature of wood.”
  3. “Drawn into the brain marrow” (引入䯕髓内): the character 䯕 is the same as in Step Three, meaning roughly the brain marrow; reproduced as is.
  4. “The two roads of the celestial equator and the ecliptic” (赤黄两道): the astronomical equator and ecliptic, used here for the Governing and Conception vessels (the two roads along which spirit and breath move and stay).
  5. The numbers around Figure 19 were read by this site from the original figure: beside the moon, “wu one, mao two, zi three, you four”; beside the sun, “zi one, mao two, wu three, you four.”
  6. In Qiu Ling’s note, “let it down from the left to the navel of Kun” is as in the original.
  7. “Fire is born at yin … wood is born at hai”: the teaching of the twelve palaces of growth of the five phases; metal being born at si, wood at hai and so on are traditional; Zhao uses them to explain the positions of ablution.
